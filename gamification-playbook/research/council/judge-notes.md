# What the judges said about each design


## B0

- **S1-r1** holistic launch 82 noticed 55 lasting 40: Union co-design, pay-for-process with no penalty for holds, dispatch-based denominators, paper fallback and a no-pressure pause clause make it very approvable, but 25 per month is small, so awareness and motivation are modest, and habit persistence depends on the retained paid time and prompt rather than the reward.
  - rubric codex 75: worst flaw: Without a comparison group that receives the same workflow fixes and private feedback but no bonus, the pilot cannot tell whether the bonus improves safe checklist completion.
  - rubric claude 72: worst flaw: The pilot judges success by comparing before and after on one group, with no comparison group, test of plain support without money, uncertainty handling or cost-benefit decision rule, so it cannot show that the cash reward added value beyond the access and workflow fixes.
- **S1-r2** holistic launch 82 noticed 66 lasting 55: Union co-design, equal credit for stop-work, dispatch-based denominators and sampled quality review make it safe and credible, but a one-hour-pay award is modest and lasting change rests on retained app prompts, paid time and fallbacks rather than the incentive itself.
  - rubric codex 75: worst flaw: The award launches alongside paid checklist time, app prompts, and private feedback, with no comparison group receiving the same support without the award, so the pilot cannot tell whether the award adds enough value to justify its cost.
  - rubric claude 74: worst flaw: The learning design has no comparison group or isolation of the award's added value over plain workflow support (paid time, devices, app prompt), so the 85% before/after result cannot be attributed to the incentive, and no cost or harm decision thresholds are set in advance.
- **S1-r3** holistic launch 88 noticed 55 lasting 66: Union co-design, paid check time, equal credit for stopping work, and no individual rankings or discipline use make it safe and credible, but a one-hour-pay bonus is modest and weakly salient, and durability depends on fixing app and scheduling barriers rather than on the reward.
  - rubric codex 76: worst flaw: Without a comparison group receiving the same workflow support and private feedback but no bonus, the pilot cannot tell whether the bonus improves verified checklist completion.
  - rubric claude 71: worst flaw: The learning design is a single before/after comparison with no control or staggered rollout, so the pilot cannot separate the bonus's added value from the barrier fixes in weeks 1-2, novelty effects or seasonal case mix, and the 80% retain rule has no stated uncertainty or sample-size reasoning.
- **S2-r1** holistic launch 82 noticed 62 lasting 66: It is carefully designed for sceptical engineers (no points or rankings, protected reviewer time, quality audits, clear rules for what counts, a stop date and a post-pilot check), which makes approval easy. The reward is modest and the metrics and audits add some admin, so visibility and motivation are only moderate. Keeping the rotation as normal work gives decent lasting effect.
  - rubric codex 71: worst flaw: The pilot introduces the reward alongside the reviewer rota, protected time, and queue alerts, so even a large improvement would not show whether the reward adds value beyond those changes.
  - rubric claude 70: worst flaw: The learning design is a six-week team before/after against a four-week recalculated history, with no comparison to plain rotation-and-protected-time support, so the reward's added value can't be told apart from the workflow changes, seasonality or regression.
- **S2-r2** holistic launch 72 noticed 55 lasting 62: It is carefully built, with team-level metrics, a quality audit, protected time and no individual stats, which suits sceptical engineers, but the reward is modest, the rules are complex, and it depends on the manager actually protecting review time and cutting sprint scope.
  - rubric codex 72: worst flaw: The pilot introduces the reward alongside protected review time, queue ownership, and deadline feedback, so without a workflow-support-only comparison it cannot tell whether the reward adds any value.
  - rubric claude 71: worst flaw: Success is judged by a before/after comparison with no comparison condition that separates the effect of the reward from the protected rotation time, and the 90% target is fixed before the recalculated baseline is known, while the measurement has no replayable event history and no appeal or correction-ownership process.
- **S2-r3** holistic launch 82 noticed 55 lasting 50: The proposal is carefully designed with team-level reward, staffing support, quality audits, a reward-free exit test and stop conditions, but the reward (one cancelled meeting) is modest and low-visibility, so engagement and carry-over beyond the rotation are uncertain.
  - rubric codex 75: worst flaw: The pilot introduces the reward alongside staffing and feedback, then removes it only after exposure, so it cannot reliably tell whether the reward adds value beyond those workflow changes.
  - rubric claude 69: worst flaw: The learning design is only a single-team before/after comparison with the reward later removed: it has no comparison group, no plan for uncertainty with 25 engineers over two-week blocks, and does not separate the reward's effect from the effect of the added review windows. It also has no appeal or correction process for disputed clock or quality judgments.
- **S3-r1** holistic launch 80 noticed 45 lasting 55: Unusually careful design that separates logging from hygiene, avoids perverse incentives and has clear exit rules, but a team thank-you is a weak, low-salience motivator and the measurement and audit burden is heavy for a three-ward pilot.
  - rubric codex 72: worst flaw: All three wards receive the same package, so the pilot cannot determine whether the team thank-you improves accurate logging beyond plain feedback and workflow support.
  - rubric claude 67: worst flaw: The pilot runs on all three wards at once with only a pre-pilot baseline and no comparison ward or plain-support arm, so it cannot separate the effect of recognition from logging support, the effect of being audited, or changes over time.
- **S3-r2** holistic launch 72 noticed 38 lasting 40: Well-designed, safety-conscious, and measurement-sound with a small non-gameable team recognition, but the reward is weak and low-visibility, and the heavy audit and reconciliation burden plus extra logging work may fade once recognition ends.
  - rubric codex 69: worst flaw: The reward should not launch until the five-minute matching rule is validated, because repeated actions and excluded ambiguous matches could change which wards qualify.
  - rubric claude 71: worst flaw: The learning design is a single before/after sequence across three wards with no comparison to plain support or logging without recognition, so any change in coverage or compliance can't be credited to the incentive, and secular trends or audit effects could explain it.
- **S3-r3** holistic launch 82 noticed 55 lasting 30: Safety-conscious design with independent audits, no individual stakes and a clear exit plan is approvable, but the modest team reward and a four-week scoring window are unlikely to build a durable logging habit once incentives end.
  - rubric codex 69: worst flaw: All three wards receive the reward alongside training, access fixes and feedback, so the pilot cannot tell whether the reward improves logging beyond what plain support achieves.
  - rubric claude 62: worst flaw: Recognition is earned for a weak proxy: one entry of any kind per nurse-shift counts, so a ward can qualify while logging almost none of its real hand-hygiene moments, and with all three wards incentivised and no comparison, the pilot cannot show whether the kiosk or the reward adds anything over the existing observer audits.
- **S4-r1** holistic launch 82 noticed 62 lasting 55: Well-designed with verified outcomes, protected writing time, no individual pay or ranking effects, service guardrails and a handoff plan, but the reward is a modest shared learning session, the 70% target is demanding, and durability rests on workflow changes (closeout prompt, editor, paid time) rather than on the incentive itself.
  - rubric codex 69: worst flaw: Without a group receiving the same workflow support and feedback but no reward, the pilot cannot tell whether the reward adds value.
  - rubric claude 65: worst flaw: The design has no comparison group and no predefined decision at a feasible sample size, so it cannot tell how much of any coverage gain comes from the shared reward rather than from the closeout prompt, the paid writing time and the editor support.
- **S4-r2** holistic launch 72 noticed 62 lasting 55: Well-guarded design with quality checks, protected time, and honest exit plan, but the team-wide all-or-nothing reward is modest and diffuse, and the QA and measurement overhead is heavy for a 60-person org.
  - rubric codex 67: worst flaw: The org-wide pilot introduces the reward alongside protected drafting time, coaching, and feedback, so it cannot tell whether the reward adds value over workflow support alone.
  - rubric claude 65: worst flaw: The design reports only one before/after result for the whole org, with no comparison group or plain-support arm. Because the 8-week coaching, protected time and editor support all start together, it cannot tell whether the group reward added anything beyond the workflow support.
- **S4-r3** holistic launch 80 noticed 62 lasting 55: Well-guarded design (verified coverage, QA sampling, no per-agent ranking, service guardrails, planned retirement and follow-up check), but a one-hour team reward is modest and collective so individual motivation may be thin, and durable habit depends on search friction and steward capacity staying funded.
  - rubric codex 63: worst flaw: Without a comparison group that gets the same workflow support and private feedback but no reward, the pilot cannot tell whether the reward improves verified coverage.
  - rubric claude 67: worst flaw: The pilot has no comparison group or plain-feedback arm, and no definition of what success or failure means for the decision, so it cannot show whether the reward added anything beyond the protected time, the steward and the search fixes; it also lacks an appeals process and replayable event and policy versions.

## C2

- **S1-r1** holistic launch 82 noticed 55 lasting 62: Unusually well-designed for a unionised workforce, with pilot fixes, steward agreement, rewards for stop-work, quality sampling and a default-to-checklist exit, though a 30-minute paid release is a modest incentive and may be low-salience, and lasting effects depend on the app-default change actually removing friction.
  - rubric codex 71: worst flaw: Launch should wait until an independent check confirms that dispatch records and offline device timestamps reliably establish whether the checklist was completed before work actually began.
  - rubric claude 74: worst flaw: The staggered-crew comparison has no predefined sample size, uncertainty handling, cost/harm decision thresholds or spillover controls. A 30-minute paid release is the only reward, and it is a binary 70% threshold with no stated rationale or test that it adds value beyond the app fixes and the default first-screen change.
- **S1-r2** holistic launch 80 noticed 62 lasting 55: Unusually thorough on union co-design, safety-stop protection, gaming safeguards and a default-based exit, but the reward is a modest 30-minute pooled break that may be weakly salient, and durability rests mostly on app fixes and the default screen rather than the reward.
  - rubric codex 69: worst flaw: Using the approved safety fallback during an outage lowers the crew’s chance of earning the break, creating peer pressure to avoid the fallback.
  - rubric claude 74: worst flaw: The crew-level 70% milestone gates everyone's break on peers' completion without addressing low-volume technicians, case mix across job types, or a staffed appeal path, and the learning design relies on an optional comparison with no predefined sample size, uncertainty handling or cost/harm decision thresholds.
- **S1-r3** holistic launch 80 noticed 62 lasting 62: Union-co-designed, hazard-hold-safe, outage-tolerant counting rules and a baseline-verified pilot with a comparison group and friction removal make it approvable, but a team meal is a modest reward and the 'noticed' and 'lasting' effects depend on the default-screen redesign and technician buy-in.
  - rubric codex 71: worst flaw: Launch should wait until the programme can independently verify that critical hazards are checked before work starts and has a defined safety response when an audit finds a missed hazard.
  - rubric claude 69: worst flaw: The credit rule counts a timestamp-valid checklist without real-time verification that the answers reflect the site, and the weekly audit of 10 random checklists across roughly 300 technicians is too thin to catch backfilled or rote ticking before rewards are paid.
- **S2-r1** holistic launch 72 noticed 55 lasting 62: It fixes the queue and protects reviewer time before adding any reward, uses a team-level goal with no individual penalties and has a quality check, so sceptical engineers should accept it. But the counting rules are dense, the visible feedback is only a weekly channel line, and a modest lunch or time-off reward may not excite people, although the standing defaults should help the change last.
  - rubric codex 73: worst flaw: The pilot introduces the team reward alongside workflow changes without a reward-free comparison, so it cannot determine whether the reward improves review turnaround beyond what routing, protected time, and private feedback achieve.
  - rubric claude 68: worst flaw: The evaluation is a four-week before/after comparison that launches the workflow fixes and the team reward together, so it cannot show whether the reward added anything beyond plain queue support, and it sets no uncertainty or cost thresholds.
- **S2-r2** holistic launch 82 noticed 62 lasting 66: It fixes the actual queue bottlenecks first, uses a modest reward tied to a quality-audited metric, and plans a handoff to automatic routing, but the reward is low-salience and mostly private, and noticing depends on weekly channel posts and staying free of audit disputes.
  - rubric codex 68: worst flaw: The trial introduces the reward alongside better routing, protected review time, and private feedback, so it cannot tell whether the reward adds any value.
  - rubric claude 67: worst flaw: The trial changes the queue setup and adds the reward at the same time, then judges them only against a four-week before-and-after baseline, so it cannot tell whether the reward added anything beyond the routing fixes; it also has no appeal process or versioned record of the scoring rules.
- **S2-r3** holistic launch 78 noticed 62 lasting 55: Fixes root-cause friction first, uses private feedback and a team-level reward with quality sampling, and plans to make routing defaults permanent, but the weekly sampling and manager visibility add overhead and some surveillance risk for sceptical engineers.
  - rubric codex 66: worst flaw: The pilot bundles workflow fixes, private feedback, and paid time off, so a higher on-time rate cannot show whether the reward adds value over the same workflow support and feedback alone.
  - rubric claude 63: worst flaw: The learning design is a four-week before/after comparison with no control or way to separate the gain from routing and protected review time from the gain from credits and reward, while managers can open individual cases and there is no morale check and no staffed appeal path, even though the engineers are already sceptical.
- **S3-r1** holistic launch 62 noticed 55 lasting 40: The design is thoughtful (workflow fixes, shared ward reward, audit reconciliation, pause rules), but it is complex to run, the reward depends on small audit samples, the privacy-sensitive code matching may erode trust, and the reward ends after only two cycles with no mechanism beyond the kiosk default to sustain logging.
  - rubric codex 71: worst flaw: Without a concurrent ward using the same kiosk and feedback without the meal, the pilot cannot separate the reward’s effect from changes in staffing, workload, or familiarity with the kiosk.
  - rubric claude 66: worst flaw: The design judges success by comparing each ward with its own pre-reward month. There is no comparison ward or plain-feedback arm, and the observers know about the reward, so a gain from the reward cannot be separated from seasonal change or from staff behaving differently while watched. It also lacks an event history, permissions and an appeal process for disputed ward results.
- **S3-r2** holistic launch 82 noticed 55 lasting 50: Unusually careful design that fixes workflow first, audits independently, separates recording from clinical compliance and has pause triggers, but the modest team-meal reward on a monthly audit cadence is low-salience, and lasting change depends on whether the kiosk becomes the default workflow rather than on the reward.
  - rubric codex 69: worst flaw: The reward should wait until representative shadow scoring confirms that kiosk entries saved at a later break can be reliably matched to individual observed actions.
  - rubric claude 70: worst flaw: The design compares each ward only with its own before-and-after audits, with no comparison ward or plain-support arm and only a 30-action minimum, so it cannot separate the reward's effect from the workflow fixes, observer effects or secular trends.
- **S3-r3** holistic launch 72 noticed 62 lasting 55: The design is unusually rigorous on gaming risks, safety pauses, and separating kiosk logging from audited compliance, but sampling-based verification adds admin burden, the team-meal incentive is modest, and the eight-week plan hinges on the kiosk becoming the default workflow to sustain behavior after rewards end.
  - rubric codex 70: worst flaw: A credible report that kiosk logging delayed patient care triggers a reward hold, with no rule requiring the ward to suspend logging while Infection Prevention investigates.
  - rubric claude 68: worst flaw: The pilot has no comparison condition, such as plain feedback versus the team meal or a non-pilot ward, so it cannot tell what the reward adds beyond the workflow fixes; it also has no staffed appeal process and no replayable record of events and policy versions.
- **S4-r1** holistic launch 72 noticed 66 lasting 55: Well-designed with fair per-agent targets, quality sampling, CSAT guardrails and a pre-launch pilot, but the 30-minute paid-time reward is modest against a 12% baseline, and the 50% target is a big jump that may rely on generic-link gaming and fade once rewards end.
  - rubric codex 64: worst flaw: The before-and-after rollout bundles paid time with training, private feedback, and workflow changes, so it cannot establish whether the reward adds value.
  - rubric claude 67: worst flaw: There is no comparison group or incremental-value test. Success is judged by a before/after coverage rise against the baseline, so plain workflow fixes and the reward's own effect can't be separated, and no cost or harm decision thresholds are set ahead of time.
- **S4-r2** holistic launch 80 noticed 62 lasting 55: It fixes tool friction first, uses a clear per-ticket rule with audits and CSAT safeguards, and ends by making the workflow the default, but the reward is a small 45-minute paid block that agents may barely notice and goals may feel complex, so habit lasts only if the resolution-form defaults really cut the effort.
  - rubric codex 64: worst flaw: The pilot lacks a feedback-only comparison, so any increase in coverage cannot establish whether the reward adds value beyond protected time, training, and workflow fixes.
  - rubric claude 61: worst flaw: The design has no comparison group or predefined decision criteria at a usable sample size, so a coverage rise from 12% can't be separated from the workflow fixes, the novelty effect or seasonal changes in ticket mix. On top of that, posting coverage team-wide and in public channels risks peer pressure, and nothing checks for that.
- **S4-r3** holistic launch 82 noticed 62 lasting 55: It fixes workflow friction first, rewards only real credit-worthy outcomes with a quality-sampling circuit breaker and a CSAT/reopen guard, but the one-hour time-off reward is modest, a 50% goal may feel distant from the 12% baseline, and lasting effect depends on the default-in-panel change surviving.
  - rubric codex 68: worst flaw: The pilot introduces the reward alongside workflow improvements and private feedback, so its before-and-after comparison cannot show whether the reward adds value.
  - rubric claude 68: worst flaw: There is no comparison group or predefined decision criteria with costs, so the design cannot separate the reward's added value from the workflow fix, and it relies on a before/after comparison against baseline.
