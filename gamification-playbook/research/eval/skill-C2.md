# Gamification playbook (compact)

A workplace programme to get people to adopt a tool or habit works when the new way is
genuinely better for them, the reward follows real work, people notice it and want it,
and the new way stays the easy default after the prizes stop. Design for those.

- **Build it with the people who'll do it.** Ask a few of them what makes the new way
  slow or risky, and give them protected or paid time to learn it.
- **Fix the work first.** If the new way is blocked or costs the worker more time or
  risk than the old one, fix that before adding any reward, and make the fix visible
  ("you reported it, we fixed it"). Rewards shift behaviour only while they run.
- **Reward finished, real work, not activity.** Count the outcome (the quote sent, the
  ticket resolved with an article), at most once, never logins or clicks. People
  produce whatever is cheapest to count. Check a small sample for quality in the
  background; tell people the rule in one sentence. Before launch, try the rule on a few
  awkward cases (a duplicate, an undo, a case done the old way) and write down which
  quality number pauses rewards, and who decides.
- **Make it noticeable and worth wanting.** Prompt, specific confirmation after each act;
  a reachable personal goal sized to each person's volume; the team's progress shown
  where people already look; and a real, modest reward at a reachable milestone (paid
  time, a team meal, a leader's specific thanks, a small gift card). Earned recognition
  and team bonuses have raised performance in field trials.
- **Don't make people feel watched.** Individuals see their own numbers; managers see
  team totals unless there's a specific coaching or correction reason. No named
  leaderboard by default; if people want a contest, make it opt-in and winnable.
  Monitoring tends to add stress without improving performance.
- **Make missing easy to recover from.** A missed week is just the next case. No streak
  that a single absence wipes out.
- **Plan the ending.** Time-box it, and before prizes stop make the new way the default
  in the tool (pre-filled, first in the queue). Defaults persist without payment;
  incentive lifts usually fade after rewards stop. Check the behaviour for a few weeks
  after the end, against a team that didn't get the programme or at least the
  pre-launch baseline, and decide then whether to keep, change or drop anything.

Keep it light enough that one person can run it in under an hour a week.
