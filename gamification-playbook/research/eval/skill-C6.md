# Gamification playbook

A workplace programme to get people to adopt a tool or habit works when the new way is
genuinely better for them, the reward follows real work, people notice it and want it,
and the new way stays the easy default after the prizes stop. Design for those.

- **Build it with the people who'll do it.** Ask a few of them what makes the new way
  slow or risky, what reward they'd actually want, and give them protected or paid time
  to learn it.
- **Fix the work first.** If the new way is blocked or costs the worker more time or
  risk than the old one, fix that before adding any reward, and make the fix visible
  ("you reported it, we fixed it"). Reward lifts usually fade once rewards stop.
- **Reward finished, real work, not activity.** Count the outcome (the quote sent, the
  ticket resolved with an article), at most once, never logins or clicks. People
  produce whatever is cheapest to count. Count an event a system already records and,
  where possible, one the workplace already checks for quality (an audit, reopen rate,
  rework). A correct "not needed" (a safety hold, nothing to link) counts as done. Tell
  people the rule in one sentence. If the records can't tell real work from a tick
  without new tracking of people, run the fixes and feedback without a prize first.
- **Make it noticeable and worth wanting.** Prompt, specific confirmation after each act;
  a reachable personal goal sized to each person's volume and case mix; the team's
  progress shown where people already look; and a reward people will talk about,
  named in the launch message and handed over in person (paid time off, a meal with a
  senior leader, a leader's specific public thanks). Each person earns it from their own
  verified work within a week or two; never make it all-or-nothing on peers' numbers.
  Earned recognition and team bonuses have raised performance in field trials.
- **Don't make people feel watched.** Individuals see their own numbers; managers see
  team totals, never a per-person drill-down, and the data never feeds performance
  reviews. No named leaderboard by default; if people want a contest, make it opt-in
  and winnable. Monitoring tends to add stress without improving performance.
- **Make missing easy to recover from.** A missed week is just the next case. No streak
  that a single absence wipes out.
- **Plan the ending.** Time-box it, and before prizes stop make the new way the default
  in the tool (pre-filled, first in the queue). Defaults change behaviour without any
  payment, while incentive lifts usually fade. Give everyone the fixes and feedback
  first, then start the reward a few weeks apart by team, so everyone gets it but you
  can see what it adds. Name the one number that decides keep, change or drop, and check
  it again a few weeks after prizes stop.

**Write it so a sponsor can approve it in two minutes.** Lead with the fix, what the
worker sees and the reward. Put the operating details in a short owner's note at the
end: the quality check and the number that pauses payouts, who fixes a wrong count,
and the comparison. One person should run it in under an hour a week.
