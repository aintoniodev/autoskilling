## Six-week pre-job safety checklist programme

**Before launch.** Give technicians from different shifts and routes, including union stewards, paid time to try the app on real jobs. Ask where it slows them down or puts them in a bind. Test weak-signal sites, shared devices and jobs that need to be stopped. Fix the main blockers before adding a reward, then tell crews what changed and give everyone a paid practice session. Agree with union representatives on the reward, who can see the data, and how technicians can correct a record. Confirm that checklist time is paid and that an app failure has a workable fallback.

**What earns credit.** “Your crew gets one credit per job when you record the safety checklist before work begins, including when the checklist leads you to stop the job.” A documented safety hold earns the same credit as a job cleared to proceed. If work later resumes, follow the normal safety procedure and keep the original single credit. A late submission earns zero. A duplicate earns no extra credit, and a voided checklist loses credit unless a valid replacement was recorded before work began. A job cancelled before the technician reaches the safety check is excluded. An approved fallback checklist recorded before work begins earns the same credit during an app outage.

**How it’s measured.** Match checklist records to eligible jobs by job ID using existing app and dispatch records. Use the time the checklist was recorded on the device for offline submissions. Before launch, verify that the existing job record reliably marks when work begins, and run a duplicate, a void, a safety hold and an offline submission through the scoring rule. If the records cannot establish the order reliably, fix that before rewards begin. Report two weekly rates over the same eligible jobs: on-time digital checklists and on-time checklists by either approved method. Use the first to measure app adoption and the second for the crew reward. Confirm the baseline using those definitions.

**What technicians see and earn.** Show immediate, private confirmation in the app, including when a checklist is saved offline and awaiting sync. Give each technician a private goal of one more on-time checklist per ten eligible jobs than their own baseline, capped at 100%. Show progress over the full six weeks so a quiet week or an absence has no lasting penalty. Put crew totals in the existing weekly huddle or dispatch channel. Managers see crew totals, with individual records used only for a specific correction, coaching need or safety review. For a crew starting at 35%, set the milestone at 45% over six weeks. Use the same ten-point improvement for other crews, capped at 100% and adjusted for any difference between the digital and reward baselines. Each member of a crew that reaches its milestone gets 30 minutes of paid time they can schedule, including members who were on leave during the pilot.

**What could go wrong.** Rushed answers, backdating and pressure to proceed can make completion look better while making the job less safe. Each week, the safety lead checks ten records across crews against existing job notes, including holds or fallback records when available. Two material defects in ten records, or one credible report that someone was pressured to skip a check or proceed despite a hazard, pauses reward scoring for the affected crew. The safety lead decides when to resume after reviewing the cause and remedy with the union steward. Keep the weekly tally and sample review under an hour for one owner. Use the existing safety process for any incident that needs investigation.

**How it ends.** In week six, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job. Pre-fill job details and leave safety answers blank. Keep the approved fallback available. Pay out the earned reward and end scoring. Track the on-time digital rate, fallback use and sample quality for four more weeks against the pre-launch baseline, using a comparable crew without rewards if a staggered rollout is feasible. Keep the app default if digital completion stays above baseline and quality holds. If completion drops or quality worsens, review the affected jobs with technicians and fix the source of friction.
