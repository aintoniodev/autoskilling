## Hand hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward observed hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one recognition milestone during an eight-week pilot. A ward earns it when its pooled compliance rate across two routine monthly audits reaches its preannounced target and clears the quality checks below. The reward is a team meal available to every shift, with specific thanks from the nursing director. Every ward can earn it. Kiosk entries carry no points and have no minimum quota.

**Make the kiosk easy to use before launch.** The infection prevention lead walks through the workflow with nurses from each ward, including night shift, during paid time. Ask where the kiosk adds delay, creates a queue, or is easy to forget. Fix those problems before starting and post what changed in the ward’s usual huddle channel. Keep the kiosk ready on the entry screen with the ward prefilled. Patient care and hand hygiene take priority. If the kiosk is unavailable or using it would interrupt care, nurses skip the entry and resume at the next opportunity.

**Measure with the existing monthly audits.** Compliance is the number of observed hand-hygiene opportunities completed according to the hospital’s existing audit protocol, divided by all eligible opportunities observed. Count each observed opportunity once. Pool the last two pre-pilot audits for each ward’s baseline. Set the target five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Use the usual observer schedule and check that shift coverage and observation counts are comparable with baseline. Agree on a minimum usable observation count with infection prevention before launch, using the existing audit volumes. Review weekly kiosk totals separately to see whether the tool is being used and to flag faults or possible duplicate entries.

**Show nurses immediate confirmation and ward progress.** After a submission, the kiosk says “Hand hygiene entry saved.” Each nurse’s first goal is to use the kiosk after their next eligible moment when care allows. After a missed shift or week, the same goal applies. Use the existing huddle board or channel to show the ward’s baseline, target, and latest audited result with its observation count. Managers see ward totals. Keep names and patient identifiers off the progress display.

**Set the pause rule before launch.** Walk through a duplicate tap, an incorrect entry, a kiosk outage, and an observed hand-hygiene moment that was never logged. The observed moment still counts in the audit. A duplicate or corrected kiosk entry has no effect on the reward. The infection prevention lead pauses the award if either audit falls five percentage points or more below baseline, if observation coverage falls short of the agreed minimum, or if a reported kiosk delay interferes with care. The lead reviews the cause with ward nurses and checks the next routine audit before releasing a paused award. If kiosk use rises while audited compliance falls, investigate the workflow and audit coverage. Keep routine observer visits on their usual schedule so the pilot does not turn into preparation for an announced visit.

**End after eight weeks.** Give earned recognition after the second audit review and end the reward period. Keep the prefilled kiosk and the entry shortcut in place. At the next two monthly audits, compare each ward’s compliance with its baseline and pilot results, and check whether kiosk use holds up. Keep the workflow if nurses can use it without delaying care and audited compliance holds. If use falls, ask the affected shifts where the workflow broke down. If compliance falls, address it through the hospital’s usual infection prevention process. The ward lead uses existing kiosk totals and huddles for a short weekly review, with a one-hour weekly cap across all three wards.
