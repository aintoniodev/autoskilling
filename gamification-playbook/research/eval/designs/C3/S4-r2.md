# Six-week knowledge-base adoption programme

**Before launch.** Give six agents across shifts 30 paid minutes to resolve representative tickets in the new workflow. Ask where finding, linking, or writing an article slows them down or creates risk. Fix the biggest blockers before rewards start. For example, put article search in the resolution panel and open a prefilled draft from the ticket when search comes up empty. Show agents what changed because of their feedback and give everyone 30 paid minutes to practise.

**What earns credit.** “A resolved ticket counts once when you link an article that helps solve it or publish a reusable article that does.” A link can be internal if the article is for agents. A draft earns credit once published, with credit assigned to the original ticket. Give agents an “Article doesn’t apply” option so they can close tickets that have no reusable answer.

**How it’s measured.** Use the existing ticket and knowledge-base records to divide credited tickets by all distinct resolved tickets, including those marked “Article doesn’t apply.” Use the same calculation to verify the reported 12% baseline over the four weeks before launch. Before launch, test three cases: two links on one ticket earn one credit, reopening removes credit until the ticket is resolved again with a valid article, and a ticket closed through the old workflow without an article earns zero.

**What agents see and earn.** Confirm credit in the ticket as soon as both conditions are met. If an article is awaiting publication, show it as pending. In the queue view, each agent sees their own progress toward a personal goal of one credited ticket per five resolutions, based on the tickets they actually handle. Progress accumulates across the programme, so time off or a quiet week carries no penalty. The same view shows the team’s aggregate rate and its **20% goal**. If the team reaches 20% across weeks five and six and clears the quality checks, everyone gets **30 minutes of paid time to use as they choose**, scheduled around queue coverage. Managers see team totals and use individual records for specific coaching or corrections. The existing CSAT bonus keeps its current rules.

**What could go wrong.** Agents may attach irrelevant links, publish duplicate articles, or rush a resolution to get credit. Each week the support QA lead samples ten credited tickets in the existing QA queue, including new articles when available, and checks that the article supported the resolution, was accurate and reusable, and contained no private customer details. The QA lead pauses reward eligibility if fewer than eight of ten pass or if any article exposes customer information. The lead removes invalid credits, fixes the cause with agents, and resumes eligibility after a passing sample. The lead also checks the existing CSAT report against the pre-launch baseline. A drop of five percentage points over a two-week window with at least 30 responses pauses the reward decision while the lead reviews ticket mix and customer feedback. Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, checking the dashboard, and posting the team update.

**How it ends.** Make article suggestions and the prefilled draft available by default in the resolution panel by week five. End the reward period after week six and deliver any earned paid time. Keep the resolution workflow in place. Four weeks later, compare the article coverage rate, sampled quality, and CSAT with the pre-launch baseline. Keep the workflow if coverage is still above baseline and quality holds. If coverage falls back, use agent feedback and sampled tickets to identify what still makes the workflow hard to use.
