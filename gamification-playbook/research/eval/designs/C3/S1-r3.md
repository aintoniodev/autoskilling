# Pre-job safety checklist incentive

**Two weeks to prepare, six weeks to earn the reward, four weeks to check whether the habit sticks.** Start with the current estimate of 35% of jobs with a digital checklist. Verify that baseline against the same counting rule the programme will use.

## Before launch

Bring union stewards and a few technicians from different shifts and routes into a paid walkthrough. Try the app with gloves, poor reception and a last-minute dispatch change. Give every technician 20 minutes of paid practice. Fix anything that makes the app slower or less reliable than the existing procedure, then tell crews what changed because they raised it. Start the incentive once the checklist can be completed offline and technicians know the approved fallback.

Agree with union representatives on the counting rule, the reward, who can see the data and when individual records may be reviewed for coaching or safety correction. Keep programme scores out of performance rankings and discipline.

## What earns credit

**One credit per dispatched job when its required digital safety checklist is completed before work starts.** Use the existing job ID, the app’s completion timestamp and the existing job-start record. For offline work, use the completion time captured on the device once it syncs. Count a shared job once and give the technician who submits the checklist private confirmation.

Before launch, test a duplicate, a withdrawn checklist, a stopped job and a job completed under the approved fallback. A duplicate earns one credit. A withdrawn submission earns credit only if a valid replacement was completed before work began. A technician who identifies a hazard and stops work can still earn credit for a completed checklist. A fallback earns no digital credit and carries no individual penalty. Keep fallback jobs in the adoption denominator so app failures stay visible. Flag jobs with missing start times as unverifiable and show their count alongside the rate. If existing records cannot establish completion before work starts, fix that during setup before offering the reward.

## What crews see and earn

After submission, show **“Checklist saved for job 4821”** or **“Saved offline. Credit pending sync.”** In the app, each technician sees their own credited jobs and an achievable goal based on their assigned volume. For someone with 10 eligible jobs, the first goal is five. A missed week leaves the next job available.

Post the company-wide rate weekly in the existing safety huddle or dispatch channel. If the verified baseline is close to 35%, award the milestone at **45% across weeks three through six**, subject to the quality check below. If the verified baseline differs materially, set the milestone 10 percentage points above it before launch. Everyone in the technician group earns one **30-minute paid break**, scheduled across shifts without reducing existing breaks. Managers see team totals. There are no named rankings, and a technician’s personal goal does not affect anyone’s eligibility.

## What could go wrong

Rushed checklists can hide hazards. Each week, the safety lead spends up to 45 minutes reviewing 10 credited jobs across shifts using existing checklist and job records. Look for missing required checks, start-time discrepancies and recorded hazards with no disposition. **Two material failures in the sample pause reward credit pending review.** A credible report of a bypassed critical safety check triggers an immediate pause. The safety lead owns the pause and restart decision, with a union steward reviewing the findings. The checklist and stop-work procedures remain in effect throughout.

Poor reception, sync delays and dispatch pressure can distort the numbers or push people to start early. Check fallback and unverifiable counts alongside adoption. Fix recurring causes, correct credits once records sync, and make clear at every huddle that raising a hazard or using the approved fallback never costs someone the reward.

## How it ends

Before week six, make the checklist the first action when a technician opens a dispatched job, with job details prefilled and safety answers left blank. End the incentive on schedule and honour the earned break. For four more weeks, track the same adoption rate, fallback counts and quality sample. Compare them with the verified pre-launch baseline and review the results with technicians and union stewards. Keep the app default if adoption holds above baseline and quality holds steady. If either slips, use the records and crew feedback to identify what got harder and fix that before considering another incentive.
