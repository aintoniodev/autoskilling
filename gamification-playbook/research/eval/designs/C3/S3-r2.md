## Eight-week kiosk pilot for three wards

**Set up and baseline.** Give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts on each ward 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk on their usual route. Fix queues, slow sign-in and awkward placement before starting. Put the logging screen first, prefill the ward and use a one-tap save after sign-in. Tell staff what changed because of their feedback. Run the improved workflow for one monthly audit cycle without prizes to establish each ward’s baseline.

**What earns the reward.** Each ward earns one staff meal, with an equivalent option for night and weekend staff, if it reaches its logging goal across the two monthly audits and clears the quality checks below. Set the goal at five percentage points above that ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at 95% or higher aim to maintain their baseline.

The rule nurses see is **“After you clean your hands, record that moment once when patient care allows.”** During the existing audit sessions, observers tally how many observed hand-hygiene actions are followed by a kiosk entry they can confirm. Divide that count by the number of observed actions for which observers could determine whether an entry followed. Record how many actions could not be assessed, and use comparable audit windows across shifts. Use the tally to award the meal only if at least 20 actions per ward could be assessed across the two audits. If the sample is smaller, report it as insufficient and give the ward a paid huddle to review what got in the way.

**What people see.** After each save, the kiosk confirms “Hand-hygiene moment recorded” and offers an undo. Nurses can view their own recent entries privately. Each nurse picks a personal practice goal that fits their workload, such as logging the first feasible moment on each shift they work this week. A missed shift or week carries no penalty. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s baseline, goal, latest audited logging rate and sample size. Managers receive ward totals. The kiosk collects no patient identifiers and publishes no individual rankings.

**Quality checks and failure modes.** Infection Prevention continues to measure clinical compliance as observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Kiosk entries are self-reports and never enter that calculation. Before launch, test a duplicate submission, an undo and an action completed without a kiosk entry. A duplicate counts once, an undone entry is removed, and an unlogged action still counts toward clinical compliance. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

- **Inflated or inaccurate entries.** The pilot lead reviews ten recent kiosk entries per ward each week using the existing export, looking for clear duplicates or invalid records. If two or more are confirmed in a ward’s sample, Infection Prevention pauses that ward’s reward decision and reviews the cause. The export flags record problems. The observer sample checks whether real actions are being logged.
- **Care delays or falling compliance.** Nurses skip the kiosk during urgent care and resume at the next feasible moment. Infection Prevention pauses the incentive immediately after a credible report of a care delay, or when a comparable monthly audit with at least 20 observed opportunities shows compliance falling five percentage points or more below baseline. Infection Prevention decides when the workflow is safe to resume.
- **Unequal access or misleading samples.** Use each ward’s own baseline, include off-shifts in the usual audit rotation and report kiosk outages alongside results. If observers miss an entire shift type or cannot assess logging for more than a quarter of observed actions, hold that ward’s reward decision until the existing audit process yields a usable sample.

**Ending.** The prize window closes at week eight. Keep the fast logging screen and private confirmation as kiosk defaults. Use the next two routine monthly audits to compare logging coverage and clinical compliance with each ward’s pre-prize baseline. Keep the workflow if logging gains hold without added care burden or a drop in compliance. Fix and reassess it if access or sampling problems obscure the result. Drop the kiosk requirement if it adds work without a sustained gain. One pilot lead uses the kiosk export, existing audit and huddle board to run the programme in under an hour a week.
