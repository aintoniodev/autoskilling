# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Before launch, have a union steward and technicians from different shifts try the checklist on paid time, including on a low-signal site and during a job handoff. Ask what slows them down or makes it harder to flag a hazard. Fix the issues they find and post the changes in the dispatch briefing. Agree with union reps on the counting rule, the reward, who can see the data, and how technicians can correct a record. Start the pilot once technicians can complete the checklist on paid job time and have an agreed fallback when the app is unavailable.

**Reward a completed safety check.** Give the crew credit once per dispatched job when the required checklist is completed in the app before work starts. A valid checklist still earns credit if it identifies a hazard and the technician delays or stops work. If a crew reaches **45% of eligible jobs across weeks 5 and 6**, offer everyone on that crew a paid team lunch, with an equivalent option for anyone who cannot attend. Confirm the target against each crew’s own baseline before launch. For a crew already above 45%, agree on a modest increase from its baseline. Individual participation levels do not affect access to the reward.

**Measure it with dispatch and checklist records.** Match checklist submissions to dispatch job IDs and compare the completion time with the existing work-start event. Include jobs where a technician reaches the site and performs the pre-job assessment, even if work stops there. Exclude cancellations before arrival. Check these cases before launch:

- A duplicate submission or reopened job earns at most one credit. A withdrawn checklist counts only if a valid replacement was completed before work began.
- A checklist completed offline can count when it syncs if the app preserves the original completion time.
- An approved paper fallback during an outage is recorded separately and excluded from the digital adoption rate. Paper used when the app was available stays in the denominator. Publish the number of fallback exclusions alongside the rate.

**Show progress in the tools people already use.** On submission, show “Checklist saved for job 418” or “Saved offline. Credit will show after sync.” In the app, technicians see their own eligible jobs and completions, with a private goal based on their workload. For someone who usually handles 10 jobs a week and completes checklists on three or four, the initial goal could be five. A missed week carries no penalty. Put the crew’s aggregate rate and milestone in the weekly dispatch briefing. Managers see crew totals and access individual records only for a specific coaching or correction need agreed with the union.

**Run for six weeks and check quality.** Each week, the safety lead reviews 10 credited checklists across crews using existing job records, looking for missing required checks, late completions, or answers that conflict with the job record. The safety lead pauses rewards for the affected crew if two of the 10 contain substantive defects tied to that crew, or immediately if a record indicates work started despite an unresolved stop-work hazard. The safety lead and union steward review the cause, communicate the fix, and confirm when counting resumes. Keep the weekly review and progress update under an hour. Watch for rushed answers, early work-start timestamps entered late, outages concentrated on certain routes, and pressure to start a job to help the crew hit its target.

**End the reward and keep the checklist on the job path.** By week 5, make the checklist the first action on the dispatched job screen, with job details prefilled and the agreed fallback visible. Close reward counting at the end of week 6 and deliver any earned lunch on paid time. Track adoption, fallback use, and sampled quality for four more weeks. Compare with the four weeks before launch and, if rollout is staggered, with a similar crew using the same app workflow before its incentives begin. At the end of follow-up, the safety lead and union reps decide whether to keep the workflow as configured or fix what still gets in technicians’ way.
