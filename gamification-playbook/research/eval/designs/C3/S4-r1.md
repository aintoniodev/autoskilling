## Six-week support knowledge-base incentive

**Goal and reward.** Raise the share of resolved tickets with a useful knowledge-base article from 12% to 20%. If the team reaches 20% across any two consecutive weeks and passes the quality checks for both weeks, everyone earns one paid hour for learning or a project of their choice. Agents can schedule it around queue coverage. Fund the reward separately from the existing CSAT bonus.

**Before launch.** Give six agents across shifts and queues 30 paid minutes to walk through recent tickets, including ones they resolved without an article. Use those sessions to fix the biggest source of friction, such as poor search, missing permissions, or slow article approval. Give all 60 agents 30 paid minutes to practise on real cases. Show the team which fixes came from their feedback. Start the six-week clock once agents can find and attach an existing article from the ticket and have a clear route to get a new one approved.

**What counts.** Tell agents: “A resolved ticket earns one credit when its linked article directly helps solve the issue or prevent it from recurring.” An existing article qualifies, as does a new one once it’s approved and linked. Use existing ticket and KB records to divide qualifying tickets by all resolved tickets. Multiple links, repeat closures and merged duplicates earn at most one credit for the underlying ticket. A reopened ticket drops out until it’s resolved again. A ticket closed without a qualifying link stays in the denominator. If the team hits a genuine content gap, agents can flag it through the existing KB request process and finish helping the customer. Before launch, run these cases through the report and verify that the counts match the rule.

**What people see.** Show a specific confirmation in the ticket after resolution, such as “Password reset article linked. This ticket counts toward the team goal.” For an article awaiting approval, show its pending status and confirm credit when it qualifies. Each agent sees their own coverage and a personal goal of five percentage points above their recent baseline, roughly one extra qualifying ticket per 20 resolutions. Show progress over a rolling four-week window so agents with lower volume can build toward the goal and a missed week carries no penalty. Post the team rate and milestone progress in the existing support channel each week. Managers see team totals and access individual tickets for routine QA, coaching or corrections.

**What could go wrong.** The support lead uses the existing QA review to check 10 randomly sampled credited tickets each week for relevance, accuracy and whether the article helped resolve the issue. Two failures in 10, or one article with materially harmful guidance, pause milestone approval. Also pause if team CSAT falls by three percentage points or more against the four-week pre-launch baseline over a two-week window with at least 30 survey responses. The lead reviews the affected cases for rushed closures, forced links or delays from article writing, fixes the cause, and resumes milestone eligibility after a passing review. Use existing reports and budget 45 minutes per week for the sample, CSAT check and team update.

**How it ends.** Before week six ends, make article suggestions and one-click linking part of the default resolution screen. Keep a clear path to resolve tickets where no article fits. Close the incentive at week six and schedule any earned paid time. Track coverage, sampled article quality and CSAT for four more weeks using the same measures and pre-launch baseline. Keep the workflow if coverage stays above baseline and quality holds. If coverage falls back, use the sampled tickets to find and fix the remaining friction. Rework the close flow if quality or CSAT deteriorates.
