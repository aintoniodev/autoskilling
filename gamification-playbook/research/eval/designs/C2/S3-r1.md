## Hand hygiene kiosk pilot

**Set up the workflow.** Give day, night and float nurses from all three wards 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk during a normal shift. Ask where logging would cause a detour, a queue or an interruption. Before launching rewards, fix those issues and show staff what changed at shift huddles. Put the kiosk on the existing work route, open it directly to the logging screen and prefill the ward and time. Nurses clean their hands when clinically indicated and log as soon as care safely allows.

**Reward accurate logging.** Tell staff, “Log each completed hand-hygiene action once, using the applicable moment or moments.” Each ward independently earns one team meal, with equivalent options for all shifts, when its monthly sample shows that at least 80% of observed completed actions were accurately logged and at least 90% of reviewed entries were valid. Everyone on the ward shares the reward. Extra entries earn nothing. Before launch, test a double submission, an undone entry, one action that covers two indications, and an entry on the fallback form during an outage. A valid fallback entry can be transferred once after recovery.

**Measure adoption and patient safety separately.** During routine monthly rounds, observers sample day, night and weekend shifts. In short audit windows, use a temporary code and approximate event time to match observed actions to kiosk entries, then delete the matching key after reconciliation. Kiosk adoption is the share of observed completed actions that have one accurate record. Entry validity is the share of reviewed kiosk entries that match a completed action with the correct moment. Aim for at least 20 observed completed actions and 10 reviewed entries per ward before deciding on a reward. If the sample falls short, roll it into the next round. Keep the clinical compliance measure from the regular audit: observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Give people feedback they can use.** After saving, show a private confirmation such as “Saved · Before touching a patient,” with an undo button. Offer a private getting-started goal of recording the first eligible action on each shift worked. Every shift is a fresh start. At existing huddles, share each ward’s latest adoption rate and sample size alongside the separately labeled clinical compliance rate. Managers see ward totals. Keep patient identifiers out of kiosk records and individual results out of performance reviews.

**Run it for eight weeks.** Capture one reward-free month of kiosk and observer data first. Run the pilot for two monthly audit cycles, with one reward available per ward across the entire pilot. At week eight, end rewards and keep the kiosk as the default, with the fallback available for downtime. At four and eight weeks after rewards stop, compare each ward’s adoption and observed compliance with its baseline using comparable shift coverage. Keep the workflow where adoption holds and compliance is stable. Fix and retest where logging declines. The pilot lead uses an automated summary and spends up to 45 minutes a week on exceptions and feedback, using the existing audit rounds.

**Pause when the programme creates risk.** Infection Prevention pauses a ward’s reward if more than 10% of reviewed entries are invalid or if staff report that logging delayed care or hand hygiene. A decline in observed compliance triggers a review of the sample and workflow before any payout. Check for duplicate or backfilled entries, selective logging during audit rounds, and missed coverage on busy or understaffed shifts. Investigate access, workload and kiosk placement with nurses before resuming rewards.
