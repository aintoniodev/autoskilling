## Eight-week kiosk pilot for three wards

**Start with the workflow.** Ask day, night and weekend nurses on each ward to try the kiosk during paid time. Find out when logging would interrupt care, require a detour or create a queue. Before launch, fix the biggest barriers and post what changed. Put the log screen first, prefill the ward and time, and make entries easy to correct. Nurses log at the first safe pause after the completed action. During urgent care or a kiosk outage, they carry on with care and flag the barrier when they can.

**Reward accurate logging as a ward.** Publish one rule: “Log each completed hand-hygiene action once, at the first safe pause, using the applicable moment or moments.” One action that covers multiple indications counts once. Each ward can earn one team meal, available across all shifts, if at least 80% of sampled completed actions have one accurate kiosk record at the end of the pilot and the safety and data-quality checks pass. There is no reward per entry. Give each nurse a private goal tied to their workload: log the next eligible action on the shifts they work. A missed shift or week carries no penalty.

**Measure logging and hand hygiene separately.**

- **Logging adoption.** Run a two-week, reward-free kiosk baseline after fixing the workflow. During the baseline and pilot, use brief, rotating spot checks with consenting nurses across wards and shifts, including nights and weekends. For each sampled action, check whether it has one valid entry by the next safe pause. Use a temporary nurse code, time window and moment category to match records, with no patient identifiers. Check kiosk entries in the same samples for duplicates, wrong categories and entries without a matching completed action. Report the numerator and denominator and keep ambiguous matches visible. Before launch, test a double tap, an undo, an action recorded only in the old workflow and an outage. A corrected record counts once, a voided record is removed, and an old-workflow-only entry shows up as a missed kiosk log.
- **Clinical compliance.** Keep the existing monthly observer audit independent. Calculate completed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities and compare each ward with its own pre-pilot audit baseline using comparable shift coverage. Kiosk totals measure use of the kiosk. The observer audit measures clinical compliance. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/infection-prevention-control/hand-hygiene/monitoring-tools)
- **Pause rules.** The Infection Prevention lead pauses a ward’s reward if more than 10% of sampled entries are invalid, monthly observed compliance falls five percentage points or more below baseline, or a credible report shows that logging delayed care. Agree on minimum sample sizes before launch and hold the payout if coverage is too thin to judge. Treat those thresholds as pilot decision rules. The lead reviews the cause and resumes eligibility after the fix and a fresh check.

**Show progress where nurses already look.** The kiosk confirms the saved moment immediately and offers an undo. At the weekly huddle or on the existing ward board, show the ward’s sampled logging rate, sample size and one workflow fix or next action. Nurses see their own recent entries privately. Managers see ward totals. Temporary matching codes are deleted after reconciliation, and individual records stay out of rankings and performance reviews. One coordinator uses an automated summary and a short exception review to keep programme administration under an hour a week, alongside the existing audit.

**End at week eight.** Confirm earned meals once the final audit clears. By then, make the kiosk the default logging route in the ward workflow and remove any redundant logging step that Infection Prevention confirms can be retired. Keep the quick entry and confirmation. For eight more weeks, track sampled logging and the next two monthly audits against each ward’s baseline and pilot results. Keep the workflow if accurate logging holds and observed compliance remains stable. Fix access or workload barriers if logging falls. Rework the kiosk workflow if it interferes with care.

**Watch for predictable failure modes.** Duplicate or invented entries can inflate adoption, so use sampled verification and a single team milestone. Busy shifts can look worse if spot checks favor weekdays, so rotate coverage and report outages alongside results. Nurses may change their behavior when observed, so keep audit methods consistent and treat a small monthly swing as a signal to investigate. Keep the public display focused on the ward’s progress and the problems staff helped fix.
