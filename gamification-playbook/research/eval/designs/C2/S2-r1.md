# One-working-day code review pilot

**Goal.** Get at least 85% of review-ready PRs a substantive first response within one working day. The current median is 2.6 days. Track the median alongside the target so the team can see whether the longest waits are improving too.

**Fix the queue first.** In week 1, spend 30 minutes with five engineers across time zones and seniority levels. Ask them to walk through a recent slow review and identify what got in the way. Give everyone paid time to try the new workflow. Set up a rotating primary reviewer and backup, with a protected 30-minute review block each working day and a handoff before planned absences. Route review-ready PRs to the primary and sort the shared queue oldest first. Publish a short “you reported it, we fixed it” note. If the queue regularly exceeds the protected time during the trial, adjust coverage before rewards begin.

**What counts.** Tell the team in one sentence: “A PR counts once when a teammate reviews it within one working day and either approves it after checking the change or explains what needs to change.”

Start the clock at the first review request on a non-draft PR. One working day means eight hours on the team’s published review calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. The backup covers individual absences. Use repository events to capture request and response timestamps. Count approvals and submitted reviews with actionable feedback. An acknowledgment, bot comment, or self-review leaves the clock running. Duplicate requests, reassignments, and draft toggles leave the original clock intact. An approval withdrawn because the review was incomplete loses credit unless a qualifying replacement arrives by the original deadline. A PR genuinely withdrawn before its deadline is excluded and logged. A PR closed after its deadline stays in the denominator. Review feedback given in chat counts when the reviewer records it on the PR by the deadline.

**What people see and earn.** Post a single weekly line in the existing engineering channel with the on-time count and denominator, the median response time, and the number of PRs still overdue. Calculate the weekly rate from PRs whose first-response deadline fell that week, including those still unanswered. The queue shows reviewers which PR they own and when it is due. A private confirmation after each qualifying review shows the elapsed time. Each reviewer’s goal is to clear their assigned queue by the deadlines or hand it off early, so the goal scales with their review load. Managers see team totals. The team earns one modest reward, chosen up front from a team lunch or an hour of paid time back, if it reaches 85% across the final two reward weeks combined and clears the quality check. A missed week carries no individual penalty.

**Quality and failure modes.** Before launch, run the rule against a duplicate request, a withdrawn approval, a draft toggle, and a review given in chat. Each week, a rotating senior engineer checks five randomly selected on-time reviews for evidence that the reviewer addressed the change’s main risks and made any requested changes clear. The engineering lead pauses reward eligibility if two or more of the five fail. The team fixes the cause and resumes eligibility after a fresh sample passes. If reviewers rush approvals, address the quality findings before asking for more speed. If work piles up on a few people, rebalance the rotation. If PRs are split or withdrawn to improve the number, inspect the flagged cases and correct the count. The rotation owner spends up to 45 minutes a week on the sample, exceptions, and team update.

**How it ends.** Run one setup week and four reward weeks. Before the reward ends, make automatic routing, oldest-first sorting, and protected review time the standing defaults. Then track the same measures for four more weeks and compare them with the four weeks before launch, noting any major changes in PR volume or staffing. Keep the defaults if at least 85% of PRs are answered on time across the follow-up and review quality holds. If performance slips, use the overdue cases to identify where coverage or routing failed.
