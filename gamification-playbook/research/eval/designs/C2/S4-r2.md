## Six-week knowledge-base adoption programme

**Before launch.** Give six agents across shifts and queues 30 minutes of paid time to walk through recent tickets. Find out where search fails, which tickets lack a usable article, and how long writing one takes. Fix the biggest blockers before starting rewards. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected practice, reserve paid queue time for writing, and post the changes under “You reported it, we fixed it.”

**What earns credit.** Use one rule agents can remember. “Earn one credit per resolved ticket when you link an article that supports the fix or write a reusable article that passes review.” Count each ticket ID once. Merge duplicates under the surviving ticket, reverse credit when a ticket reopens, and restore it once the ticket qualifies again. An article linked after resolution can earn credit if it supports the actual fix. A draft stays pending until approved. Give agents a short article template and assign a reviewer each shift so drafts get a decision within one business day. For sensitive or genuinely non-reusable cases, allow an exception with a reason. Track exceptions separately and include them in the overall coverage denominator.

**What agents see and earn.** On resolution, show “Article linked · 1 credit” or “Draft submitted · credit pending review.” Each agent sees their own weekly coverage and a personal goal expressed as a ticket count, sized to their typical volume and prorated for leave and part-time schedules. For the first two weeks, set the goal at 20% of their volume or five percentage points above their own baseline, whichever is higher, capped at 100%. At the end of week two, use the pilot results to set a reachable goal for weeks three through six and publish it before those weeks begin. Meeting the goal in any three of the six weeks earns one 45-minute paid block away from the queue, scheduled with shift coverage. A missed week leaves another chance to earn it. Post team-wide coverage and progress in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals and access individual records only for specific coaching or corrections. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula in place.

**Measurement and safeguards.** Use all resolved tickets as the coverage denominator so the result can be compared with today’s 12%. Report the share with a qualifying link or approved article and track exceptions separately. Before launch, test a duplicate, a reopened ticket and a ticket resolved without an article. Each week, the KB lead audits 10 credited tickets across queues and shifts for relevance, accuracy and reusable content, plus three exceptions for misuse. If two or more credited tickets fail, the support operations lead pauses new credits, publishes the issue and resumes after a fresh sample has at most one failure. Monitor reopen rates, median handle time and CSAT against the four-week baseline by queue. A sustained rise in reopens or handle time, or a CSAT decline across two weeks with enough responses to interpret it, triggers a workflow review before goals rise. This gives agents a way to flag when documentation time or rushed resolutions are putting their CSAT bonus under pressure. Use an automated report and one weekly review of under an hour for programme administration. Schedule article review as part of normal KB maintenance.

**Ending.** Before week six ends, put suggested articles and the draft template directly in the resolution form. Keep the exception option available there. End credit earning after week six and schedule any outstanding paid blocks. For four more weeks, compare coverage, audit quality, reopens and CSAT with the four-week pre-launch baseline, using the same definitions throughout. Keep the workflow default if coverage stays above baseline without a sustained quality or service decline. If gains fade, use agent feedback and exception reasons to identify what still makes the workflow hard to use.
