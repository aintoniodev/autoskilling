## Pre-job safety checklist programme

Reward technicians for completing a usable digital safety checklist before each job. Aim to raise pre-job digital completion from about 35% to 75% and sustain it after the reward ends.

### Set up with technicians and union reps

Use two weeks to test the app with technicians across shifts and routes, including sites with poor reception. Give them 20 minutes of paid practice and make room for the checklist in scheduled job time. Agree with union reps on the counting rule, reward, data access and correction process. Fix blockers such as unreliable offline saving or repeated data entry before launch. Tell crews what they reported and what changed.

### What earns credit

**One credit per job when the assigned technician completes the required checklist before work starts. A checklist that flags a hazard and puts the job on hold earns the same credit.**

The app matches the checklist to the dispatch job ID and compares its completion timestamp with the recorded work-start time. Offline entries count once synced, using the saved completion time. Duplicate submissions earn one credit. A checklist withdrawn before work starts earns credit only if a valid replacement is completed in time. Late checklists and paper fallbacks earn no digital credit. When the app is unavailable, technicians use the approved fallback and can flag the outage for review.

The adoption measure is the share of dispatched jobs that reached the pre-work safety check and had a qualifying digital checklist. Include jobs held for hazards and report paper fallbacks separately. Exclude cancelled jobs that never reached the check. For the reward calculation, remove verified outage jobs from the denominator so crews can use the fallback without losing ground. Keep those jobs in the adoption measure so outages stay visible.

### What people see and earn

After submission, the app shows “Checklist counted for job 4821” or “Saved offline. Credit pending sync.” Technicians see their own completed and eligible job counts and a personal goal of 75% of their eligible jobs over the programme. A missed week carries no penalty. Dispatch can correct a missing or misassigned job, with a union rep available if the issue is disputed.

Show team progress at the regular weekly huddle. Each team that reaches 75% across the programme earns a company-paid meal during paid shift time, with equivalent options across shifts. Every team can earn it. Managers see team totals and open individual records only for a specific coaching or correction need agreed with union reps.

### Run it for six weeks, then check what sticks

Pilot with several crews and use comparable crews on the same app as a comparison group. Use the two setup weeks to verify the baseline in both groups. Before rewards end, make the checklist the first screen on opening a job and prefill the job details. Leave safety answers for the technician to enter. End the meal incentive at week six and track weekly adoption, fallbacks and checklist quality for four more weeks.

Keep the app default if digital completion stays at or above 75% over those four weeks and quality holds. If adoption falls, review where it fell with technicians and fix the friction before considering another incentive.

### What could go wrong

- **Rushed or backfilled checks.** Each week, the safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected checklists for credible answers, timing and follow-through on flagged hazards. One missed critical hazard or two materially deficient checklists pauses reward decisions. The safety lead makes the call with a union rep and tells crews what needs to be fixed before rewards resume.
- **Pressure to start work or hide a hazard.** Make clear at launch and in huddles that a hazard hold earns full credit and that technicians retain the authority to stop work. Review a sample of holds for follow-through.
- **Connectivity or dispatch errors leave people short.** Show pending syncs, give technicians a simple correction route, and resolve exceptions before calculating the final reward.

Automate the counts and weekly sample selection. Budget 30 minutes for the safety lead’s sample and 15 minutes for exceptions and the huddle update.
