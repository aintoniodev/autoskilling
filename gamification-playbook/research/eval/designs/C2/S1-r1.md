# Pre-job safety checklist incentive

**Goal.** Raise verified, on-time digital checklist completion from about 35% to 75% of eligible jobs by week 6, and sustain at least 70% four weeks after rewards end. Recalculate the baseline using the rules below before launch so the comparison is consistent.

**Before launch.** Give a small group of technicians from different shifts and service areas, including union stewards, paid time to try the app on real jobs. Ask where it adds time or makes it harder to flag a hazard. Fix the barriers they find, test the fixes on site, and show crews what changed. Agree with union representatives on paid training, reward access, who can see individual records, and how technicians can correct them. Give every technician 20 minutes of paid practice before their crew starts.

**What earns credit.** One credit per assigned job when the technician completes the required checklist before work begins and records what they did about any hazards they found. A checklist that results in a stop-work or escalation earns the same credit as one that clears the job to start. The rule technicians see is “Complete the checklist before work starts, record any hazards and what you did about them, and earn one credit for the job.”

**How it’s measured.** Match checklist records to dispatch job IDs and the recorded work-start event. For a job stopped before work starts, match the checklist to the dispatch stop-work or escalation record. Let offline submissions sync later using the original device event time. Count each job once. Before launch, test duplicate submissions, a valid offline submission, an undone or corrected checklist, a pre-start cancellation, a stop-work, and a job done on paper. Keep jobs with missing timing evidence in the denominator and flag them as unverified. When the app fails, use the agreed safety fallback. Log fallback jobs separately, keep them in the digital adoption denominator, and remove verified outage jobs from personal reward calculations.

**What people see and earn.** After submission, the app confirms the job and whether credit is recorded or pending sync. Technicians see their own progress and can flag a missing credit. The existing crew huddle shows team totals and the completion rate. Managers see team totals, with individual records available for a specific correction or coaching case under the agreed rules. A technician earns one 30-minute paid release from duties by completing 70% of their eligible jobs during the six-week programme, with scheduling agreed up front so every shift can use it. Set the required count from each technician’s actual job volume and agree a reduced target for very low-volume assignments. Progress accumulates across the six weeks, so the next job is a chance to recover after leave or a missed week.

**Quality and failure modes.** The safety lead reviews eight credited jobs per week across shifts, including a stop-work or offline case when available. Check timing, whether the answers fit the job, and whether recorded hazards have a documented response. If two or more of the eight fail, the safety lead pauses new reward credits for the affected crew and reviews the cause with its union steward. A single critical omission, such as an unaddressed hazard followed by work starting, triggers immediate safety follow-up and a reward pause for that crew. Resume after the cause is fixed and a fresh sample passes. Preserve valid earned credits. Tell crews explicitly that hazard reports and stop-work decisions protect their ability to earn. Have the programme owner use an automated report and reserve up to one hour per week for sampling, corrections, and the crew update.

**Ending.** Stagger the six-week programme so comparable crews awaiting their start provide a comparison. Give all crews the app fixes and paid practice up front. Before rewards end, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job, prefilled with the job details and with a clear route to log a hazard or stop work. Honour earned rewards and track weekly completion, sample quality, and fallback use for four more weeks. Keep the default if completion holds at 70% or higher and quality is at least as good as at launch. If it slips, review the missed jobs with technicians and stewards and fix the most common barrier before deciding whether to run another incentive.
