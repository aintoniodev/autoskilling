# Knowledge-base adoption programme

**Reward one useful article per resolved ticket.** A ticket earns one credit when the resolving agent links an approved article that addresses the issue, or submits a reusable article that is subsequently approved. An internal article qualifies when it helps agents solve the same problem again. Give agents a short list of valid exceptions, such as tickets containing only account-specific details, and let them select a reason when they resolve the ticket. Track exceptions so they remain visible.

## Before launch

Give six agents from different shifts 30 minutes of protected time to resolve real cases using the tool. Ask where search, permissions, or article review makes the workflow slower or riskier. Fix the biggest blockers, name an article reviewer and backup, and aim to review drafts within two business days. Show agents what changed based on their feedback. Give all 60 agents protected practice time before rewards begin.

Test the counting rule against a duplicate, a reopened ticket, a ticket resolved through the old workflow, and a draft awaiting review. Duplicates share one credit, reopening makes the credit provisional again, and a ticket resolved through the old workflow can qualify if a relevant article is attached by final resolution. Drafts count after approval and are credited back to the week the ticket was resolved.

## Six weeks of rewards

Each agent earns **30 minutes of paid time for an activity of their choice** when useful articles cover 50% of their eligible tickets in any two weeks. Schedule the time during a staffed shift. Set each agent’s goal from their own ticket volume. For someone with 20 eligible resolutions, the weekly target is 10. Combine weeks with fewer than five eligible resolutions so low-volume agents have a fair shot. A missed week costs nothing.

At resolution, show private feedback in the ticketing tool, such as “Article linked. You’re at 7 of 10 this week” or “Draft awaiting review.” Post a weekly team total in the existing support channel. Agents see their own progress, and managers see team totals. Review individual cases privately when coaching or correcting a credit. Keep the reward separate from the existing CSAT bonus.

## Measurement and safeguards

Use the ticketing report to count distinct tickets at final resolution. Show two rates each week: credited tickets as a share of **all** resolutions, which can be compared with today’s 12% after verifying the baseline definition, and credited tickets as a share of **eligible** resolutions, which drives rewards. Also track exception rates, reopen rates, and CSAT. Pull four weeks of pre-launch data using the same definitions.

Each week, the programme owner checks eight randomly selected credits and two exceptions for relevance, reusability, and sensitive information. If two of the eight credits fail, the support lead pauses new awards, fixes the cause, and restarts after a fresh sample passes. Route any exposure of sensitive data through the existing incident process immediately. Budget 45 minutes per week for the report, sample, and team update. Handle draft approvals through the knowledge-base review queue.

The likeliest failure is an agent attaching a generic article to a ticket they’re trying to close quickly. The sample catches weak links, the reopen rate flags rushed resolutions, and the exception rate flags skipped work. If CSAT drops by five percentage points across two weeks with at least 30 responses, the support lead pauses new awards and checks whether the workflow is delaying or hurting customer resolutions. If draft reviews routinely miss two business days, fix the queue and honor credits for drafts submitted during the programme once they’re approved.

## Ending

Before week six ends, put suggested articles first in the resolution form and prefill a draft with non-sensitive fields when an agent chooses to write one. End awards after week six and honor earned time. Keep tracking coverage, CSAT, and reopens for four more weeks. Keep the workflow defaults if coverage stays at least 10 percentage points above the verified baseline and quality holds. If adoption falls back or quality worsens, use the ticket samples and agent feedback to identify what to fix.
