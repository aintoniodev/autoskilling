## Eight-week hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Set up the workflow.** On each of the three wards, give day, night and weekend nurses 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk during a normal shift. Ask where recording would interrupt care, add walking or force duplicate documentation. Put the kiosk on the existing staff route, prefill the ward and shift, and let nurses save or correct an entry in a few taps. Require no patient identifiers. Fix access, queueing and duplicate documentation before offering a reward. Post the fixes at handover so staff can see what changed.

**Reward accurate recording of completed care.** Tell staff, “Clean your hands when care calls for it, then record the completed action once at the next safe break in care.” Each ward earns one team meal, with equivalent options for every shift, if at least 80% of independently observed, completed hand-hygiene actions have a matching kiosk entry by shift end across the two monthly audits, and the ward passes the quality checks below. One action can count only once, even if it meets several indications. Use the ward’s own target so a higher-volume ward has no advantage. Leave, emergencies and missed entries carry no individual penalty. The next eligible action is a fresh start.

**Measure recording and clinical compliance separately.** Use one monthly audit before rewards start to establish a ward baseline for both measures. During the pilot, observers follow the existing audit protocol and record opportunities and actions independently of the kiosk. For the recording check, pair completed actions with entries using a temporary staff code, approximate event time and moment type. Delete the matching data after review. Schedule audit samples across shifts and keep exact times off the reward dashboard. Show denominators and require at least 30 observed completed actions per ward across the two audits before awarding the meal. If coverage falls short, infection prevention reviews the result before any payout.

Clinical compliance is the share of observed opportunities where hand hygiene was performed according to hospital protocol. Keep the existing audit and feedback process in place. Observation can change behavior and can miss some shifts, so report coverage alongside the rates. A rise in kiosk entries alone cannot establish a rise in compliance. [WHO hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Give immediate, private feedback.** After saving, the kiosk says “Recorded for this shift” and offers Undo. Each nurse can see and correct their own recent entries. The ward’s existing huddle board shows the latest aggregate recording rate, sample size and progress toward the meal. Managers get ward totals. Limit access to identifiable entries to staff authorized to investigate a specific correction. Keep individual rankings out of the programme.

**Pause when the data or workflow signals trouble.** Before launch, test a duplicate, an undo, one action with two indications, a kiosk outage and a case documented through the old route. A duplicate adds no credit, an undo removes the entry, and old-route records stay outside the kiosk numerator. Track downtime separately so it is visible when interpreting results. Infection prevention pauses the ward’s reward if more than 10% of entries sampled within fully observed windows are duplicate, contradicted by observation or cannot be matched unambiguously, or if observed compliance drops by 5 percentage points or more from baseline at either monthly audit. These are pilot review thresholds. Pause immediately if staff report that logging delayed care. The ward lead fixes the workflow and infection prevention reviews a fresh sample before releasing the reward. One coordinator spends up to 45 minutes a week reviewing exceptions and posting ward totals, using the existing monthly audits.

**End the reward at week eight.** By week six, make the prefilled kiosk screen the first option in the existing recording workflow. At week eight, award qualifying wards and end the meal offer. Keep the quick entry, private confirmation and routine audits. Compare both measures with each ward’s pre-reward baseline at the next two monthly audits. Keep the workflow if recording remains above baseline and observed compliance holds. If recording falls, revisit the barriers with nurses. If compliance falls or logging still interrupts care, change or drop the kiosk workflow.
