## Six-week safety checklist programme

**Goal.** Raise the share of jobs with a digital safety checklist completed before work starts from about 35% to 70% by the final two weeks, and check whether that level holds four weeks after the reward ends.

**Before launch.** Give a small group of technicians from different shifts and routes, plus union stewards, paid time to try the app on real jobs. Test it with poor signal, shared devices, and last-minute dispatch changes. Fix the problems that make the app slower or less reliable than the current process. Tell crews what they reported and what changed. Agree with union representatives on the reward, who can see individual records, how technicians can correct errors, and which existing safety procedure to follow during an outage. Give every technician paid time to practise.

**What earns credit.** Tell crews: “One job counts when you complete its digital safety checklist before starting work, including when the checklist leads you to stop the job for safety.” Credit goes to the technician responsible for the assessment, once per work order. A safety stop remains eligible if the job is later cancelled. A dispatch cancelled before an assessment is due is excluded. A duplicate earns no extra credit. A checklist voided as erroneous loses credit, and a replacement counts only if completed before work starts. A checklist completed through the approved fallback keeps the job in the safety process and is reported separately from digital completions. Test these cases with technicians and stewards before launch.

**Measurement.** Use dispatch records to count all jobs that reached the point where an assessment was due, including jobs stopped for safety. Match the checklist to the work order and verify its completion time against the independently recorded work start or safety hold. Offline submissions use the time saved on the device when they sync. Flag missing or conflicting timestamps for correction before closing the weekly count. Keep outage and fallback totals visible alongside the adoption rate so an app failure shows up in the results.

**What people see and earn.** The app confirms credit immediately, or shows “Saved offline” until it can verify the submission. Each technician sees their own completed and eligible jobs and a private weekly goal sized to their workload. For example, a technician with six eligible jobs aims for four timely checklists. Each week starts fresh, and there is no minimum job volume to share in the reward. The existing crew huddle shows only pooled progress. If the participating crews collectively reach 70% across two consecutive weeks, everyone in those crews earns one 30-minute paid break, scheduled with coverage and available across shifts. Managers see pooled results. Individual records are available for a specific correction or coaching need under the agreed rules.

**Quality and failure modes.** Each week, the safety lead checks 10 records across shifts, including a safety stop or fallback when available, against the job record for timing and whether hazards and actions fit the job. If two or more sampled records show a material safety omission or an assessment completed after work began, the safety lead pauses milestone accrual and reviews the cause with a union steward before restarting. Any evidence that the incentive pressured someone to start unsafe work triggers an immediate pause. Watch for rushed answers, shifted start times, and pressure to hide safety stops. Supervisors explicitly reinforce that reporting hazards and stopping work preserve eligibility. Use a single weekly report and cap routine administration at 45 minutes.

**Ending and follow-up.** By week five, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job, with job details filled in and hazard answers left blank. End reward accrual after week six and honour the earned break. Track adoption, quality, and fallback use for four more weeks. Where feasible, compare with similar crews that receive the same app fixes and default without the reward during the trial. Otherwise use the pre-launch baseline. Keep the default if adoption holds and quality remains acceptable. If adoption falls or quality slips, review the affected jobs with technicians and fix the cause before considering another reward.
