## Six-week knowledge-base adoption programme

**Before launch.** The support lead spends 30 minutes with six agents across queues and shifts to watch them resolve tickets and identify where finding or writing an article slows them down. Fix the biggest blocker before starting the reward. For example, put article search inside the resolution panel and carry the ticket topic into the search. Show agents the change and credit their feedback. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and reserve paid queue coverage for drafting articles when no suitable one exists.

**What earns credit.** “Earn one credit per resolved ticket when you link a current article that supports the resolution or publish an approved article that documents it.” Agents can attach an article draft and close the ticket immediately. Credit posts when the draft is approved and published. Agents leave the article field blank on tickets with no reusable resolution, such as spam, duplicates, or account-specific exceptions, and select a reason. Customer details stay out of articles.

**How it’s measured.** Use unique resolved ticket IDs. Multiple links earn one credit. Reopening a ticket removes its credit until it is resolved again with a valid article. An eligible ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the denominator and earns credit if the agent adds a qualifying article during the programme. Before launch, test those cases and a late draft approval. Use the same eligibility rules to recalculate the pre-launch baseline alongside the reported 12%.

Each agent has a goal of **50% of their own eligible resolutions over six weeks**, with no minimum ticket count or weekly quota. Agents who reach it earn one hour of paid discretionary time with queue coverage. Leave and lighter queues reduce the number of tickets needed, and a missed week carries no penalty. The existing CSAT bonus continues under its current rules.

**What people see.** On resolution, show a private confirmation such as “Article linked. 12 of 28 eligible tickets counted toward your goal.” Show “Draft awaiting approval” when credit is pending. Agents can see their own totals and pending drafts. Post the team’s weekly coverage and example of a helpful article in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals and look at individual records only to resolve a credit dispute or coach on a specific case.

**Quality and failure modes.** The support lead samples 10 tickets per week, including credited tickets and exclusions. Check that articles actually support the resolution, contain no customer details, and that exclusions are justified. If **two or more of 10** fail, the support lead pauses new reward credit, fixes the issue, and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Tickets handled during the pause can qualify after correction. Remove any article that exposes customer information immediately. Check existing CSAT and reopen-rate reports for signs that agents are rushing closures. Pay particular attention to generic links, overused exclusions, and drafts awaiting review. If reviews back up, give the designated knowledge reviewer paid coverage and count qualifying drafts submitted by the deadline once approved. Automate tallying and confirmations so the support lead can run the weekly programme in under an hour.

**How it ends.** By week six, make article suggestions the default in the resolution panel. Keep a one-click path for legitimate exceptions and allow agents to close tickets while drafts are pending. End earning after week six, settle pending credits, and schedule earned time off. Keep tracking eligible-ticket coverage, sampled quality, CSAT, and reopens for four more weeks against the pre-launch baseline. Keep the default if coverage stays above baseline and quality holds. If coverage falls back or quality declines, use the sampled tickets and agent feedback to decide what to change or drop.
