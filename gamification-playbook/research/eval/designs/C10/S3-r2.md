## What nurses hear at launch

Perform hand hygiene when it’s due. When patient care allows, use the ward kiosk to record the moment once. If you’re pulled away, log it later in the same shift if you can identify the moment accurately. If you’re unsure, leave it unlogged and pick up with the next moment. The kiosk will confirm each saved entry.

Your ward earns a catered meal, offered on paid breaks across all shifts, if at least 80% of the completed, eligible hand-hygiene moments sampled in its final monthly audit have a matching kiosk entry and observed hand-hygiene compliance meets the pilot’s safety standard. Everyone on the ward is included. The ward huddle board will show the latest audit result and the number of moments sampled. Managers see ward totals only. Kiosk records stay out of performance reviews. Contact the infection prevention lead if an entry or count looks wrong.

## How it runs

- **Make logging easy first.** Ask nurses from day and night shifts to try the kiosk during paid time and confirm that the meal is a reward they’d value. Fix access, queues and duplicate submissions before launch. Make the ward prefilled and the log form the first screen. Report back at huddles on what changed. If a typical entry still takes more than 10 seconds or interferes with care, fix the workflow before starting rewards.
- **Verify one event.** During existing monthly rounds, observers add one tally for each completed, eligible moment they can match unambiguously to a kiosk entry by ward, moment type and time. Give each observed moment credit once. Ambiguous entries stay unverified. Before launch, test whether the kiosk can reliably match deferred entries without patient or staff identifiers. The infection prevention lead sets the minimum audit sample before launch and uses comparable day and night coverage across rounds. If matching is unreliable or the sample is too small, the ward’s result stays pending. Observers continue measuring compliance as hand-hygiene actions performed divided by all observed opportunities. [WHO hand hygiene technical reference manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)
- **Run an eight-week reward window per ward.** Give all three wards the fixes and one monthly audit before rewards begin. Randomly pick one ward to start first and start the other two two weeks later. Use any comparable audit coverage in that gap to look for an early reward effect, and treat it as directional with only three wards. The infection prevention lead resolves disputed counts privately and updates the huddle board within an hour a week outside routine audit rounds.
- **Pause if care suffers.** Pause rewards on a ward if observed compliance drops five percentage points below its pre-reward baseline, falls below the hospital’s existing minimum, or staff report the kiosk delaying care. Record outages and urgent deferrals, and keep those opportunities in the compliance audit. Review clusters of unmatched or duplicate entries before awarding the meal. Keep observation windows consistent and unannounced because staff may behave differently when they know they’re being observed. [Hand hygiene observation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25903555/)
- **End the reward and check what sticks.** Close each ward’s reward window after eight weeks. Keep the kiosk defaults and check the verified logging rate in the next monthly audit, about four weeks later. Keep the workflow if it still reaches 80% and clears the safety standard. If verified logging slips below 80% with compliance intact, fix the friction nurses report and recheck at the next audit. If the workflow interferes with care or compliance remains below the safety standard after fixes, drop the kiosk requirement.
