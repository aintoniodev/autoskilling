## What technicians hear at launch

The safety checklist will open first when you open a job in the mobile app. It will already have the job details, and you can complete it offline. You’ll get paid practice time during a crew briefing. Technicians from different shifts and job types and your union reps will try the workflow first. At launch, we’ll show you what changed because of their feedback.

**Complete the checklist before work starts on at least 80% of your eligible jobs during your four-week reward window and earn two hours of paid time off.** A completed checklist that correctly puts a job on safety hold counts too. Each job counts once for its assigned checklist owner. Your goal is based on the jobs you actually handle. If you handle two to four eligible jobs, you can miss one and still qualify. If you handle one, completing it qualifies. A missed week leaves your earlier credit intact.

The app will confirm each qualifying checklist as soon as you submit it, with a clear status when an offline checklist is waiting to sync. You’ll see your own count and goal privately. The existing crew briefing will show the weekly crew completion rate. Managers get crew totals only, and individual programme data stays out of performance reviews. If a count looks wrong, contact the programme coordinator privately. You can bring a union rep into the conversation.

Your crew will get its start and end dates at launch. At the end of your four weeks, the coordinator will confirm your paid time off in person and arrange it through the usual scheduling process. The checklist will stay first in the app after the reward ends. If the app is unavailable, follow the existing safety fallback. A verified outage job is excused from your reward calculation.

## How it’s run and checked

- **Fix the workflow before the clock starts.** Test it with technicians and union reps on paid time, including jobs with poor signal and jobs that need a safety hold. Agree on checklist ownership for shared jobs, the reward terms, and who can access programme data. Fix any recurring delays or safety blockers before launching. Give every crew the improved app at the same time.
- **Count one record per dispatch job.** Use the existing dispatch job ID and work-start record to check whether the app recorded a valid completion before work started. For safety holds, use the recorded hold before any work begins. Use the original completion time for offline submissions. Exclude jobs cancelled before the checklist was due. The coordinator uses one weekly report for counts, crew totals, and corrections, with a budget of under an hour a week.
- **Give crews equal reward time.** Pair crews with similar baseline completion and job mix, then randomly start one crew in each pair two weeks later. Both get four weeks to earn the same reward. Compare their completion rates during the first two weeks, when both have the improved workflow and only one has the reward.
- **Watch for rushed checks, late entries, and pressure to clear holds.** Use existing safety spot checks to catch those failures. A confirmed missed critical hazard, falsified timing, or pressure to proceed on a valid hold pauses new awards in the affected crew while the safety lead and union rep review it. Previously earned time stays earned. Keep small-crew results private if a total would reveal someone’s record.
- **Decide what to keep before launch.** Track the share of all jobs requiring a checklist that have a valid digital checklist before work begins or a correctly recorded safety hold. Include outage jobs in this measure so reliability problems stay visible. Use the same measure to verify the roughly 35% baseline, compare the reward groups, and check again four weeks after the last reward window closes. At **85% or above**, keep the workflow as configured. At **70–84%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck. Below **70%**, close the incentive programme and have the safety lead and union reps revisit the workflow. Rewards end on schedule in every case.
