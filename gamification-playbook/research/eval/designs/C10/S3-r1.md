## What nurses hear at launch

Do hand hygiene at the point of care and log the moment once when it’s safe to use the kiosk. If care is urgent, the kiosk is down, or you miss a shift, pick up with the next opportunity. There’s nothing to make up.

Your ward earns a catered meal across all shifts if at least 80% of the hand-hygiene actions verified in the milestone audit have a matching kiosk entry and the ward passes its usual hand-hygiene safety check. Everyone on the ward’s nursing team shares the meal. The director of nursing will come to handover to thank the team and deliver it.

The kiosk confirms what you saved and lets you correct an entry privately. The staff handover board shows your ward’s progress toward 80% and the latest monthly compliance result. Managers see ward totals only. Individual entries stay out of performance reviews, and there are no rankings.

## How it runs

- **Fix the workflow first.** The infection prevention lead spends paid time with day and night nurses from each ward to walk through real shifts, test kiosk placement, and confirm the meal is wanted. Give staff protected practice time. Before launch, fix access or queueing problems, show staff what changed, and make the one-tap entry the kiosk’s opening screen. If logging still pulls nurses away from care, delay the reward.
- **Count one verified match per action.** During the existing monthly audit, match completed hand-hygiene actions to kiosk records using a temporary staff code, the type of moment, and a five-minute window. Count each observed action and each entry at most once. The ward score is verified matches divided by all completed actions the observer saw, including actions with no entry. Multiple taps, unmatched entries, and ambiguous matches earn no credit. The infection prevention lead resolves disputed matches privately and deletes the temporary matching data after corrections close. The kiosk collects no patient identifiers. The regular compliance audit still measures completed actions divided by all observed opportunities. [WHO hand-hygiene observation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)
- **Stagger the reward.** Give all three wards the fixes and training in weeks 1–2, and take a baseline using the regular audit. Randomly choose one ward to start rewards in week 3 and start the other two in week 5. Each ward has an eight-week reward window, and all three use the week 8 audit for the meal. Use the week 4 audit to compare early and delayed wards. With three wards and one comparison audit, treat any difference as a signal. Sample comparable day, night, and weekend shifts without announcing exact audit times. Require at least 30 observed completed actions per ward for a reward decision. If the sample is smaller, carry the decision to the next scheduled audit.
- **Pause if care suffers.** Pause the reward if a ward’s audited compliance falls five percentage points below its baseline, breaches the hospital’s existing minimum, or staff report a credible care delay or pressure to fabricate entries. The infection prevention lead checks the cause and corrects it before restarting. Keep the lead’s weekly work under an hour outside the existing audits, with one ward view and one reward.
- **End the reward and check what sticks.** Keep the one-tap kiosk flow and retire the prize scoreboard after the reward window. In week 16, use the verified logging rate to decide ward by ward. Keep the workflow at 80% or above, fix and retest once at 60–79%, and drop the kiosk logging requirement below 60%. A safety breach overrides those thresholds. Monthly hand-hygiene audits continue.
