## What agents hear at launch

When you resolve a ticket, link the article that helped, write a reusable article if there’s a gap, or choose **No article needed** if the case has no reusable guidance. Each ticket counts once. The KB step will be in the ticket close screen, with suggested articles you can confirm or replace. You’ll get 30 minutes of paid practice before the programme starts.

You’ll see confirmation when a ticket counts, along with your own progress toward a weekly goal based on your ticket volume and case mix. If you miss a week, pick up on your next ticket. The support dashboard shows your team’s progress. Managers see team totals, and individual counts stay out of performance reviews. Your CSAT bonus stays as it is.

If your team records a valid KB disposition on **60% of resolved tickets across its four reward weeks**, everyone on the team earns **one hour of paid time off**, announced at the next team huddle and scheduled through normal coverage planning. If a ticket is missing from your count, send its ID privately to the Support Ops lead.

## How it runs

- **Fix the close flow first.** Ask agents from different queues to walk through real cases and confirm that paid time off is worth earning. Fix the friction they surface, such as missing permissions, slow search, or duplicate article drafting. Show agents what changed. Give every team the fixes, paid practice and the same close-screen default before rewards begin.
- **Count the resolved ticket.** Use the ticketing system’s resolution event and saved KB disposition. A linked article, a submitted draft tied to the ticket, or a valid no-article reason qualifies. If a ticket reopens, update the same record on final resolution. Use existing QA samples to check relevance, draft usefulness and no-article reasons. Remove confirmed invalid credits. Support Ops owns corrections and uses the existing dashboard and QA report to run the programme in under an hour a week.
- **Watch for shortcuts and customer harm.** Irrelevant links, duplicate drafts and overuse of **No article needed** are the main ways to inflate the count. Review the disposition mix by queue and inspect suspicious shifts in the existing QA sample. Pause reward accrual and payouts for a team if its rolling two-week ticket QA pass rate or CSAT falls **5 percentage points below its pre-launch baseline**, using the usual reporting minimums. Support Ops checks case mix and reviews sampled tickets with QA before resuming. Agents can resolve urgent tickets even when the KB tool is unavailable.
- **Stagger the reward and end it on schedule.** Randomly assign half the teams to start in week 1 and half in week 3, pairing similar queues where possible. Each team gets four reward weeks. Compare the two groups in weeks 1–2 to estimate what the reward adds once everyone has the improved workflow. Rewards end after week 6. The close-screen default, personal feedback and team dashboard stay in place.
- **Decide four weeks after the last reward ends.** The decision metric is the **share of article-eligible resolved tickets with a relevant link or useful draft**, estimated from routine QA. Measure it before rollout and again four weeks after rewards end. The reported 12% is the starting recorded usage rate. Keep the workflow as designed if the QA measure reaches **50%** and the quality guardrails hold. If it improves but stays below 50%, fix the most common remaining obstacle. If it shows no improvement, retire the incentive and revisit the close flow with agents.
