## What technicians hear at launch

Before you start a job, open the safety checklist from your work order and record the actual site conditions. **Your crew gets one credit per job when you complete a valid checklist before work starts or record a justified safety hold before any work goes ahead.** A safety hold earns the same credit even if the job never goes ahead. If it resumes, follow the normal safety clearance process. The job still earns one credit.

You’ll get confirmation in the app as soon as your checklist or hold is saved. If you’re offline, the app will show that it’s saved on your phone and confirm credit after it syncs. If you can’t use the app, follow the existing safety procedure and tell dispatch. Take the time you need to check the site and stop work when conditions call for it.

Each crew has four weeks to reach **75% of eligible jobs**. When your crew reaches that mark, everyone on the crew earns **one paid hour off**, scheduled with dispatch. The pilot lead will confirm the milestone at your crew huddle. Your app shows your own count and a weekly goal based on your scheduled jobs and job mix. The dispatch board shows crew totals. Managers see the same crew totals, and incentive data stays out of performance reviews. If you miss a week, pick up with your next job. If a count looks wrong, tell the pilot lead, who will check it with dispatch and correct it before rewards are finalized.

## How we run and check it

- **Before launch**, agree the rules, paid time off, and data access with union representatives. Give technicians across shifts and routes paid time to try the workflow and identify what slows it down. Fix the barriers they surface, such as re-entering job details or losing a checklist in a dead zone, and tell crews what changed. Make the checklist the first action on every eligible work order for all crews before rewards begin. Keep that default after the pilot.

- **Use the work order as the unit of measure.** The denominator is every job where work began or a safety hold was called. Use dispatch records to include jobs that started without a usable app record. Exclude jobs canceled before any work or safety hold. Match the app’s original checklist or hold timestamp to the recorded start or hold, deduplicate by work-order ID, and use the existing safety audit to check that entries reflect site conditions. Recalculate the baseline using this definition so the reported 35% is comparable.

- **Give every crew the same four reward weeks.** Match crews by baseline completion and job mix, then randomly assign one crew in each pair to start in week one and the other in week three. Compare their completion rates during the first two weeks, when both groups have the workflow fixes and only one group has rewards. Four weeks after each crew’s rewards end, measure the same rate again. Agree the decision rule before launch. Keep the app workflow as the standard if at least **75%** of eligible jobs still qualify and safety quality holds. Change the workflow if completion is below 75% but at least **10 percentage points above baseline**. Drop the incentive if it adds less than **5 percentage points** during the staggered start or if the follow-up gain is under **10 points**. Rewards end after four weeks per crew, and earned time off is honored.

- **Watch for rushed checks, late entries, and pressure to skip holds.** The pilot lead spends up to an hour a week reviewing counts, corrections, and findings from the existing safety audit with the safety lead. Pause new rewards immediately if the audit confirms an incentive-related unsafe start or restart, falsified checklist, or pressure to suppress a hold. Also pause if the audited checklist pass rate drops **5 percentage points or more** below its pre-pilot baseline. Fix the cause with technicians and union representatives before restarting rewards.
