## What agents hear at launch

For four weeks, you can earn **one paid hour off** by completing the knowledge-base step on 60% of the tickets you resolve during your pilot. If you work in a queue where cases routinely require writing a new article, your goal is 50%. Your private tracker shows your target and how many tickets you’ve completed. A missed week carries no penalty. Pick up with your next case.

**A resolved ticket counts once when you link an article that solves the issue, write one that passes the usual review, or correctly record that there’s nothing useful to add.** Reopening a ticket won’t earn a second credit. Close the customer’s ticket when it’s ready. If you’ve submitted an article for review, your credit can clear afterward.

You’ll get a private confirmation in the ticket tool showing what counted or what’s pending. The team will see its combined progress in the usual support dashboard. Managers see the same team total. Your individual tally stays out of performance reviews. Your CSAT bonus and the way customers receive surveys stay as they are. The support ops lead handles missed credits privately and will confirm earned time off with you in person.

## How we run and check it

- **Fix the workflow first.** Sit with agents from routine and complex queues to see where search, permissions or writing slow them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and provide queue coverage for writing during shifts. Before either group starts earning, put article suggestions and a prefilled draft in the resolution screen, with a visible “No article needed” option. Show agents which problems they raised and what changed.
- **Use the existing ticket record.** Read the article link, draft review status or “No article needed” reason from the resolved ticket and deduplicate by ticket ID. Use existing QA samples to catch irrelevant links, duplicate drafts, incorrect exceptions and premature closes. Reverse incorrect credits and tell the agent why. Keep one team view and have the support ops lead spend up to an hour a week checking totals, handling disputes and reviewing the existing QA and CSAT reports.
- **Stagger the reward.** Pair teams by queue mix and baseline adoption, then randomly start one team in each pair two weeks later. Each team gets four reward weeks, for six weeks total. Compare the groups during the first two weeks, when everyone has the same workflow fixes and only the early teams can earn the reward.
- **Pause if service slips.** Before launch, record each team’s baseline QA pass rate and CSAT. Pause new earning for a team if either drops by five percentage points in its rolling two-week report, using at least 20 QA reviews or 30 CSAT responses for the affected measure. Honor credits already earned. Check whether agents are rushing customers, spending too long searching, or using “No article needed” to finish faster. Fix the cause before restarting.
- **End the prize and check what sticks.** Turn off earning after each team’s fourth week and leave the resolution-screen defaults in place. Use one decision measure: **the share of sampled resolved tickets where QA finds an article was warranted and the agent linked a suitable article or wrote one that passed review.** Establish that baseline before launch using the same QA rule. The current 12% is the raw adoption rate. Recheck the decision measure in weeks three and four after each team’s reward ends. Keep the workflow as configured if it’s at least 15 percentage points above baseline. Revise it if it’s 5 to under 15 points higher. Drop the blanket requirement to record a knowledge-base action on every ticket if the gain is under 5 points.
