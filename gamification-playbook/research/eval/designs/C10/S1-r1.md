## What technicians hear at launch

Before every job, complete the safety checklist in the mobile app. A job counts once when you record a completed checklist before work starts or correctly record a safety hold through the existing procedure before any work begins.

Your crew can earn **one paid hour off per technician** by counting at least 75% of eligible jobs over its four-week reward period and meeting the usual safety audit standard. Everyone on the crew earns the hour, including people who were on leave or had few jobs. We’ll confirm it at the paid crew huddle and schedule it during paid shifts. The checklist is expected on every job throughout the pilot. If the app is unavailable, follow the existing safety procedure and flag the issue at the next sync.

After you save, the app tells you whether the job counted, is waiting to sync, or needs a correction. You can see your own progress against a goal based on your scheduled workload and job mix. The existing crew board shows one crew percentage. Managers see crew totals only, and pilot data stays out of performance reviews. If a count looks wrong, send the job ID to the pilot coordinator through the existing support channel. Taking leave or missing a week doesn’t reset your progress. Your next eligible job still counts.

## How it runs

- **Before launch.** Give technicians from different shifts and job types paid time with union stewards to try the workflow, identify barriers, and confirm that the paid hour and milestone are worth pursuing. Fix reported issues such as slow sign-in or poor offline access before rewards start. Show crews what changed and give everyone paid practice time. Agree on the counting, privacy and reward rules with union representatives before launch.
- **Count from existing records.** Use the dispatch job ID and the app’s saved checklist or hold record. Compare the recorded completion time with the existing job-start record, using the device’s saved time for offline submissions that sync later. Divide qualifying jobs by dispatched jobs that reached the pre-job safety decision. Include jobs held for safety and exclude jobs cancelled before that point. Keep jobs with app failures in the denominator so outages stay visible. The coordinator resolves disputed counts using existing records and keeps the weekly work under an hour.
- **Run a six-week pilot.** Give every crew the fixes first and record two weeks of post-fix baseline. Pair comparable crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly pick one in each pair to start rewards in week 1. The other starts in week 3. Each crew gets four reward weeks. Compare the change in qualifying job rates between the two groups during weeks 1 and 2 to estimate what the reward added.
- **Guard against rushed checks and pressure to proceed.** Use the existing safety audit to spot inaccurate sign-offs, checklists filled after work started, and questionable holds. Pause affected crew payouts if the audit finds any critical safety miss or the share of audited jobs with a checklist defect rises more than five percentage points above baseline. The safety lead and a union steward review the cause and explain the fix before payouts resume.
- **End with the checklist in the job flow.** Make the checklist open first when a technician opens a job in every crew’s app before the first reward window closes. End payouts after each crew’s fourth week. Four weeks after the final payouts, use the company-wide share of eligible jobs with an on-time checklist or valid hold as the decision metric. Keep the workflow at **75% or higher**, revise it at **60–74%**, and drop the reward programme at **below 60%** while keeping the safety requirement. Use the staggered comparison to decide whether the reward is worth using in another rollout.
