### What engineers hear at launch

We want every ready pull request to get a useful review within one working day. A review counts once per PR when an assigned reviewer approves it after checking the change, requests specific changes, or identifies a concrete blocker that the author can act on. Acknowledgements and automated comments don’t count. Approval is never required to meet the deadline.

Before launch, engineers from each review group will help choose the reward and identify what slows reviews down. We’ll use paid work time to fix those issues and show what changed. The starting setup is a shared review queue ordered by due time, a named backup for each group, and 20 minutes reserved each workday for reviews.

Your review queue shows what’s due, and a private confirmation tells you when your review counted. Your personal goal is to answer the requests assigned within your available review time. Flag a complex change or a full queue early so the backup can help. If you miss a deadline or take a week off, pick up the next request.

If your review group answers at least 80% of its eligible PRs within one working day during its two-week reward window, everyone in the group gets two paid hours off, scheduled around coverage. The engineering lead will thank the group at its next team meeting. The team channel shows aggregate progress. Personal counts stay private, and programme data stays out of performance reviews.

### How it runs

- **Count from ready to answered.** Use existing PR timestamps. Start the clock when a ready, non-draft PR first requests human review. One working day means eight hours on the review group’s published working calendar. Requests outside those hours start at the next opening. Reassignments and revisions keep the original clock. Once the deadline passes, unanswered requests count as misses. The engineering lead owns corrections, and engineers can send a disputed PR link privately.
- **Run for six weeks.** Use the prior four weeks of PR records to calculate the baseline share answered within one working day. Give everyone the workflow fixes in weeks 1–2. Randomly assign half the existing review groups to earn the reward in weeks 3–4 and the rest in weeks 5–6. Compare the groups during weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward added. Treat the comparison as directional if review volume is low or groups frequently cover for one another.
- **Protect review quality.** A speed target can encourage rubber-stamping, perfunctory blockers, or cherry-picking. The lead checks disputed reviews and uses existing rollback and incident reviews to spot quality problems. Pause rewards if an existing incident review confirms that a rushed approval let a defect through, or if two disputed reviews in a week are confirmed as superficial. Use one automated team view and keep the lead’s weekly administration under an hour.
- **End rewards after week six.** Keep due-time ordering, backup routing, and protected review time as defaults. Four weeks later, use the same on-time share to decide: keep the setup at 80% or above, adjust routing or capacity if it’s below 80% but above baseline, and retire the pilot setup if it’s at or below baseline.
