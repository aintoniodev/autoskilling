## What agents hear at launch

For four weeks, you can earn **one extra paid hour off** by meeting your personal knowledge-base goal. Your lead will hand you the award at a team huddle, and you can book the hour with normal coverage. Your CSAT bonus stays in place under its existing rules.

**A resolved ticket counts once when you link an article that answers the customer’s issue, publish and link a reusable article if one is missing, or record an approved reason why no article is appropriate.** For example, an account-specific refund correction may have no reusable guidance. A hard-to-find or missing article is a gap we need to fix. Resolve the customer’s issue on time, and add the article link afterward if it needs editorial review.

You’ll get a private confirmation when a ticket counts and see your progress against your goal in the support tool. Your goal is set before you start, based on your expected ticket volume and case mix. A missed week just means you pick up with your next ticket. The team will see its combined progress in the usual support channel. Managers see team totals only, and programme data stays out of performance reviews. If a count looks wrong, message the support operations lead with the ticket ID.

## How it runs

- **Fix the workflow first.** Give a few agents across queues and shifts paid time to walk through difficult cases and confirm they value the paid hour. Fix the friction they identify, such as poor search, missing permissions or a slow publishing queue. Tell agents what changed and give everyone paid practice time. Put article search and linking first in the resolution form before either group starts earning rewards. Keep an easy way to close urgent tickets and add the link later.
- **Use the ticket as the record.** Count unique resolved ticket IDs from the existing resolution record, using the linked article or an approved exception recorded on the ticket. Reopened tickets still count once, and a new article earns credit through the ticket it helps resolve. Use the existing QA sample to check article relevance, accuracy, reuse and whether exceptions are justified. The support operations lead corrects invalid credits and runs the weekly team update and reward tally in under an hour. Set goals from recent workloads. For example, an agent expected to resolve 40 tickets with a typical case mix might aim for 24 qualifying resolutions. Scale goals for part-time schedules, leave and queue differences.
- **Stagger the reward.** Once everyone has the workflow fixes, randomly assign half of the existing teams to start in weeks 1–4 and the rest in weeks 3–6, balancing queue mix where possible. Compare the groups in weeks 1–2 to estimate what the reward adds. End rewards after each team’s four weeks and keep the resolution form default in place.
- **Decide what to repeat.** Use one decision metric, the **share of resolved tickets handled correctly under the rule**, estimated from the existing QA sample. Measure it before launch and again four weeks after each team’s rewards end. The current 12% is the raw article-link-or-write rate, so establish a comparable starting point that includes valid exceptions. At **50% or more** after rewards, keep the workflow and repeat the incentive only if the staggered comparison showed a lift. At **30–49%**, use QA findings to fix the main barrier before any repeat. Below **30%**, drop the incentive.
- **Protect service quality.** Watch for irrelevant links, duplicate articles, overused exceptions and delayed resolutions. Use the existing QA review and CSAT reporting. Pause new reward accrual if the QA pass rate falls **5 percentage points** or CSAT falls **3 points** from its pre-launch baseline across a two-week window with enough observations under the team’s usual reporting rules. The support operations lead checks the affected queues, corrects the cause and preserves valid credit already earned.
