## Launch message

When you mark a pull request ready for review, you should get a useful answer within one working day. That can be an approval after inspection, a request for changes, or a specific blocker that says what’s needed and who can unblock it. The pull request counts once, when the first useful review is submitted on time.

The review queue puts the oldest deadlines first and routes new requests to the engineer on review duty. If you’re on duty, use the review block already reserved on your calendar. Hand off anything you can’t cover. If you miss a day, pick up the next review when you’re back.

You’ll get a private confirmation when your review counts and can see the deadlines for your assigned work. The team will see one weekly number in our existing engineering channel: the percentage of ready PRs that got a useful answer on time. Managers see the team total. Individual programme counts stay private and stay out of performance reviews.

Each review group has four weeks to hit **85%**. If your group hits it, everyone in the group earns **two paid hours off**, announced at the next team meeting and scheduled around coverage. The reward ends after four weeks for each group. The queue, review rotation, and protected review time stay in place.

## How we run it

- **Clear the bottlenecks first.** Ask five engineers across review loads what makes reviews slow and whether they’d value two paid hours off. Fix the blockers they identify, such as unclear ownership or interruptions during review duty, and tell the team what changed. Start once both groups have coverage and protected review time.
- **Use the code host’s timestamps.** Start the clock at the first ready-for-review event. One working day means eight hours inside the team’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Reassigning a PR or moving it back to draft doesn’t reset the clock. Include unanswered PRs once their deadline passes. Use submitted review events for the count. The engineering lead corrects disputed counts privately from the PR history and runs the programme in under an hour a week.
- **Stagger the reward.** Give both review groups the workflow fixes and two weeks to settle in. Randomly pick one group to start earning the reward, then start the other two weeks later. Compare each group’s on-time rate during the stagger against its own rate after the fixes. Use that comparison as a directional read on what the reward added.
- **Watch for rushed reviews and overloaded reviewers.** Acknowledgments and placeholder comments don’t count. Use the existing revert and hotfix review to catch rubber-stamping. Pause rewards after two reverts or hotfixes attributed to missed review issues in any 20 consecutive merges. If a group falls behind, check coverage and PR size before asking reviewers to move faster.
- **Make the decision after the reward ends.** Four weeks after the last reward window, check the team-wide on-time rate. Keep the workflow at **85% or above**. Below 85% but above the pre-programme rate, fix the remaining bottleneck and recheck. If the rate returns to baseline or worse, retire the incentive and redesign review coverage.
