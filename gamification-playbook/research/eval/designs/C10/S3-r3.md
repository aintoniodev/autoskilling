## What nurses hear at launch

“Clean your hands as usual. When you have a safe pause, log the completed moment once at the kiosk. If patient care takes priority or the kiosk is down, carry on with care. Leave any entry you can’t accurately recall. The kiosk will confirm when it saves your entry.

“Your ward earns a hospital-funded team meal if it reaches the target on your ward board during its eight-week pilot and maintains its hand-hygiene audit result. The target is based on how often the monthly audit can confirm that a completed hand-hygiene moment was also logged. Everyone on the ward is included, with meal times and staffing cover for every shift.

“The ward board shows our last audit result, how many opportunities were sampled, and how far we are from our target. Staff and managers see the same ward totals. The pilot keeps no individual scores, and its data stays out of performance reviews. If you miss a shift or a week, just pick up with your next case. Tell the infection-prevention nurse if a kiosk entry is wrong or the workflow gets in your way.”

## How it runs

- **Fix the workflow first.** Give nurses from day and night shifts protected time to try the kiosk during real work and confirm the meal is worth earning. Fix placement, queues, or slow entry before launch. Open the kiosk directly to the ward’s logging screen with the ward prefilled. Post what changed in response to staff feedback.
- **Count one verified outcome.** During the existing monthly audit, the observer adds one anonymous tick when they can confirm that a hand-hygiene action they observed was logged once during a safe pause in the same observation session. The score is confirmed logs divided by observed completed actions in those sessions. Keep unconfirmed actions in the denominator and flag sessions disrupted by outages or urgent care so the ward can see where the workflow failed. A safety deferral has no individual penalty. Duplicate entries and entries the observer cannot match earn no credit. The infection-prevention nurse handles corrections and posts only ward totals. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)
- **Set the target before launch.** Give all three wards the improved workflow for one baseline audit cycle. Set each ward’s target at 10 percentage points above its baseline verified logging rate, capped at 90%. Wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain baseline. Pool two routine audits during each ward’s eight-week reward period. Randomly start one ward first and the other two two weeks later. Compare any routine observations that fall in that gap. Three wards and monthly sampling make this a rollout check with limited evidence of the reward’s effect.
- **Protect care and the audit.** Pause rewards on a ward if its routine hand-hygiene compliance drops five percentage points below baseline or staff report that logging delayed care. Infection prevention reviews the issue before restarting. Keep the usual audit schedule and shift coverage. A jump in kiosk entries without a corresponding rise in verified logging, or gains confined to one sampled shift, calls for checking duplicates, observer effects, and kiosk access before treating the increase as adoption.
- **End the reward on schedule.** At week eight, the ward leader hosts the earned meal and thanks staff for specific workflow improvements. Keep the prefilled kiosk screen and remove reward tracking. At the next routine audit, about four weeks later, use the same verified logging rate to decide what happens to the workflow. Keep it if the ward still meets its target and the safety check passes. Fix and retest it if logging falls below target. Suspend the workflow if it continues to interfere with care.
