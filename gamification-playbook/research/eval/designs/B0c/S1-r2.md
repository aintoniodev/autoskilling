## Eight-week pre-job safety checklist programme

**Reward.** Give technicians a one-time hour of paid release time if they complete a valid pre-job checklist on at least 90% of their eligible jobs in weeks 5–8. Technicians can take the hour within the following 60 days. Agree the reward, eligibility, data access and appeals with union representatives before launch. Provide paid training and schedule time for the checklist on every job. Technicians with fewer than five eligible jobs in the reward window can qualify through a paid, observed walkthrough of the same safety steps. There are no team quotas or rankings.

**What counts.** A technician completes the checklist at the job site before work begins. They record any hazards found and what they did about them. If a hazard delays or stops work, the completed checklist still earns credit. Measure start time from the existing job status record and retain cancelled or paused jobs that reached the on-site assessment stage. Use the dispatch roster to count eligible jobs so a missing checklist remains visible in the denominator.

Offline submissions earn digital credit if the app records completion before work starts and syncs later. If a device or app failure makes that impossible, technicians use the approved fallback checklist. A verified fallback earns the same individual credit and is reported separately from digital adoption. Resolve disputed timestamps, outages and job assignments before calculating awards. Keep the existing stop-work and hazard-reporting procedures in effect throughout.

**What people see.** At dispatch, the app shows the checklist beside the job details. On submission, it confirms completion and shows whether the record has synced. Each technician sees their own eligible jobs, credited checklists, unresolved exceptions and progress toward the reward. A weekly aggregate update shows digital completion, fallback use and the app problems being fixed. Give supervisors individual information where they need it to resolve an exception or provide support. Agree a retention period and give technicians a private route to correct records or appeal through a joint union-management review.

**Timeline and exit.** Before launch, check the reported 35% baseline against two weeks of dispatch and app records. In weeks 1–2, train technicians on paid time and fix barriers such as missing devices, poor coverage and unrealistic job scheduling. In weeks 3–4, share private progress and resolve recurring exceptions. Weeks 5–8 determine the award. Each week, the safety team reviews a random 5% sample across shifts and job types against job records and occasional field observations. Look for whether the check happened before work and whether hazards received an appropriate response.

End the reward after week 8 and settle appeals before issuing paid time. The adoption target is at least 90% digital completion across eligible jobs in each of the final two weeks. Keep the checklist, paid time to complete it and the fallback procedure as standard practice. Check adoption and checklist quality again at 30 and 60 days. If adoption misses the target or drops after the reward ends, review the causes with the union and fix the barriers before considering another incentive.

**What could go wrong.** Technicians could tap through or submit after starting. Review suspicious patterns alongside sampled records and give technicians a chance to explain before changing credit. Dispatch pressure could make people rush or hide hazards. Count safety stops toward the award, give technicians a private way to report pressure and review any drop in hazard reporting. Poor coverage, shared devices or tightly packed schedules could make some assignments harder to qualify on. Compare results by shift and job type, verify fallback credit and fix the underlying access or scheduling problem.
