# Knowledge-base adoption programme

**Goal.** Make a useful knowledge-base link or article part of every resolved ticket where the answer can be reused. The current link-or-write rate is 12%. Set an eight-week target of 85% verified coverage across eligible tickets, with 100% as the ongoing workflow expectation.

## What earns a reward

At resolution, the agent links the article that supports the answer. If coverage is missing or outdated, they draft a new article or update the existing one and attach it to the ticket. Several tickets can point to the same draft. The agent can resolve the customer’s issue while the draft is in review. Give agents 30 minutes of protected writing time each week and assign a rotating editor to review drafts within two business days.

Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula. If the whole support org meets the coverage, quality, and service targets for the final two weeks, each agent earns one additional paid hour for learning or a support improvement of their choice, scheduled within the next month. In the weekly huddle, highlight one article that helped another agent resolve a case, with the author’s consent.

## How it is measured

- **Coverage.** Divide unique eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article by all eligible resolved tickets. Count each ticket once and attribute it to its final resolution. An existing link qualifies if the article supports the actual resolution. A new or updated article qualifies once approved, with credit applied to the original resolution week. Track pending drafts separately. Personalized account actions, duplicates, spam, and cases whose resolution cannot safely be generalized can be marked exempt using a reason code. An ordinary ticket with a missing article stays eligible.
- **Quality.** Each week, QA randomly samples 30 linked tickets across queues and checks whether the article is accurate, relevant, and reusable. QA also samples 10 exemptions, or all of them if fewer than 10, to catch misuse. Require at least 90% of sampled links to pass. Estimate verified coverage by multiplying recorded coverage by the sampled pass rate, weighting by queue if sampling rates differ. Review drafts for duplicate content, sensitive data, and accuracy before publication.
- **Service.** Compare the final two weeks with the four weeks before launch, using a fixed queue mix. The reward requires CSAT to stay within 2 percentage points of baseline and median time to resolution within 10%. Show CSAT response counts alongside the score. If there are fewer than 50 responses in the final two weeks, use the final four weeks for the CSAT check. If that still yields fewer than 50, record the result as inconclusive and base the reward on the other measures.

## What people see

At resolution, agents see suggested articles and three actions: link an article, attach a draft, or select an exemption reason. Each agent has a private list of tickets needing a link or draft revision, with QA feedback on sampled cases. The shared dashboard shows weekly coverage, quality, exemptions, draft backlog, and the service measures. Managers use individual results for coaching. There is no public ranking.

## How it runs and ends

In week 1, establish the baseline under the new eligibility and QA rules, train agents on real tickets, and check that the workflow fits inside normal ticket handling. Aim for 50% verified coverage by week 3, 70% by week 5, and 85% in both weeks 7 and 8. Close scoring two business days after week 8 so editors can clear the final drafts. Publish the results and award the paid time if the targets are met. End the reward on schedule either way. Keep the resolution prompt, editorial rotation, and a monthly QA sample.

## What could go wrong

Agents may add generic links or overuse exemptions. Review sampled failures with agents, correct the affected records, and check exemption spikes by queue. Drafts may pile up or duplicate each other. Surface existing drafts in search and shift editor time toward the backlog if reviews miss the two-day target. Agents may delay closing tickets to wait for approval or rush replies to make time for writing. Track resolution time and CSAT weekly, and move writing into protected time when either deteriorates.
