## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

Run a four-month pilot across the three wards. Reward teams for logging hand-hygiene actions accurately and consistently. Keep the monthly observer audit as the measure of clinical compliance.

### What earns a reward

Each ward can earn one team reward at the end of the pilot. A ward qualifies when at least **80% of observed, completed hand-hygiene actions are logged** and at least **90% of kiosk entries in the sampled windows match an observed action**. Pool the final two monthly audits for scoring. Require at least 30 observed completed actions per ward, with coverage across day, evening and night shifts.

Every qualifying ward receives a €150 team recognition budget, or the hospital’s approved equivalent, to spend on an activity or shared item chosen by staff. Include night, agency and float nurses in the recognition. There are no individual points or rewards for the number of entries.

### How it is measured

Use the existing monthly audits, with unannounced observation windows spread across shifts. In each sampled area, the observer records hand-hygiene opportunities and completed actions under the existing audit protocol. For completed actions eligible for kiosk logging, the observer also records the time. Reconcile those actions one-to-one with timestamped kiosk entries from the same area, using a two-minute window after the action. Score only areas where the observer can see the full set of actions that feed the kiosk. Keep names and patient identifiers out of the scoring data.

An observed completed action without a matching entry lowers logging coverage. Duplicate entries and entries with no observed action lower the match rate. Flag ambiguous pairings and report them separately. If ambiguous pairings or insufficient observations could change the result, add a short, unannounced sampling session before deciding the award. Track kiosk outages separately and resample any affected windows.

Calculate **clinical compliance** separately as the share of observed opportunities where hand hygiene was performed. Infection prevention reviews any deterioration through its usual process and can pause the incentive if the kiosk or reward appears to be interfering with care.

### What staff see

The kiosk confirms each entry with a brief “Logged” message. A ward dashboard shows the latest audited logging coverage, entry match rate and clinical compliance, with sample sizes and the date of the last audit. It also shows kiosk downtime and one practical fix the ward is testing, such as moving a poorly placed kiosk. Ward staff see their own results. Infection prevention and pilot leads can see all three wards’ results. Avoid live counters, rankings and individual scorecards.

### Timing and closeout

Use **month 1** to introduce the kiosk, establish a baseline and fix access problems. Confirm that the audit can produce enough matchable observations before announcing the scoring rules for months 2–4. Use **month 2** for feedback and workflow fixes. Use **months 3 and 4** to determine awards.

At the end of month 4, issue the rewards once and retire the incentive dashboard. Infection prevention and ward leads review logging reliability, clinical compliance and staff feedback on workload. They decide whether to keep the kiosk in routine use, revise the workflow or stop using it. The monthly clinical audits continue either way.

### What could go wrong

- **Staff log extra events or log only when observed.** One-to-one matching catches duplicates and unsupported entries. Rotate unannounced audit windows and inspect results by shift so a strong day shift cannot hide poor coverage elsewhere.
- **Logging competes with patient care.** Tell staff to prioritize care and hand hygiene. Ask each shift about delays and queues during the pilot, and fix kiosk placement or pause the workflow if it interferes with care.
- **Ward scores reflect access or sampling differences.** Provide the same training to permanent, agency and float staff. Review downtime and shift coverage before deciding awards. Mark results as insufficient evidence if supplemental sampling still cannot support a decision.
- **A good logging score is mistaken for safer care.** Display observed clinical compliance beside logging results with its own denominator. Keep kiosk entries out of clinical compliance reporting.
