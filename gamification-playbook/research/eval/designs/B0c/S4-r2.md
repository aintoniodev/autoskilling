## Eight-week knowledge-base adoption programme

Keep the existing CSAT bonus. Run a separate, one-time team incentive for making ticket resolutions reusable. If the support org meets the adoption and quality targets in both weeks 7 and 8, every agent gets two hours of paid development time, scheduled around queue coverage. Give specific, opt-in recognition for articles that help other agents solve tickets. Linking the right existing article earns the same credit as writing one.

### What counts at resolution

The close screen asks for one of three outcomes.

- **Link an existing article** that accurately covers the fix, workaround, or policy used.
- **Submit a new article** when the knowledge base has a genuine gap. Save the draft against the ticket at closure. A reviewer checks it within two business days, and it earns credit once approved. If two agents draft the same fix, merge the drafts and link both tickets to the approved article.
- **Record an exception** using a short reason code, such as a privacy restriction or a one-off administrative action with no reusable guidance. The lead reviews exceptions and routes recurring ones into the knowledge backlog.

Build the step into the normal close flow. Let agents close tickets while drafts are in review, and provide a fallback during tool outages. Give agents scheduled queue time for writing and revisions.

### How it’s measured

The goal is a useful article on every ticket where one applies, with a recorded outcome on every resolved ticket. Set weekly coverage milestones of 40% by week 2, 60% by week 4, 75% by week 6, and 85% in each of weeks 7 and 8.

- **Coverage** is the share of unique resolved tickets with an existing article or an approved draft linked back to the ticket. Count each ticket once, including reopened tickets, and keep exceptions in the denominator. Recalculate the current 12% baseline using the same definition before launch.
- **Quality** is the share of sampled links that cover the actual resolution, are accurate and current, and are safe for the intended audience. Each week, leads randomly sample 60 credited tickets across queues, with at least 20 involving newly written articles when volume allows. The reward requires at least 90% to pass in both final weeks. Remove credit for confirmed failures and check for other tickets using the same bad link.
- **Customer guardrails** use the four weeks before launch as a baseline. Monitor CSAT with response counts, seven-day reopen rate, and median time to resolve by queue. A CSAT drop of 3 percentage points, a reopen increase of 2 points, or a resolution-time increase of 15% sustained for two weeks triggers a review of ticket mix and workflow friction. If the review finds the programme caused the deterioration, pause the incentive and fix the workflow before resuming.

### What people see

At closure, agents see suggested articles, the selected link, and any draft status. In a private view, they see their own coverage, pending reviews, and specific feedback they can act on. A weekly shared scoreboard shows org-wide coverage against the milestone, audit results, exception trends, and customer guardrails. It also names the most common missing articles so agents can see where a draft would help. Managers use individual results for coaching. There is no public individual ranking.

### How it ends

End scoring after week 8. Publish the result once the last drafts and seven-day reopen data have cleared, then schedule the earned time within 30 days. Keep the close-screen step, review ownership, and a monthly quality sample after the reward expires. Check coverage and service metrics 30 days later. If coverage falls, use the ticket sample and agent feedback to identify where the workflow is getting in the way.

### What could go wrong

- **Agents attach generic articles or write duplicates to get credit.** Audit against the actual resolution, search before drafting, and remove invalid credit. Article volume carries no reward.
- **Agents overuse exceptions or close tickets prematurely.** Keep exceptions in the coverage denominator, review spikes by reason and queue, and check reopen rates alongside coverage.
- **Busy or specialist queues get left behind.** Review coverage and exceptions by queue, assign leads to fill recurring gaps, and schedule protected writing time. The reward is shared across the org.
- **Review delays make results depend on reviewer capacity.** Give each queue a named reviewer and backup. Attribute approved drafts to the original closure week and hold final scoring until on-time submissions are reviewed.
