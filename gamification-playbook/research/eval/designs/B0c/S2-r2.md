# One-working-day code review pilot

## What earns the reward

The 25-engineer team earns a half-day of protected engineering time if at least 90% of eligible pull requests get a substantive first review within one working day in each of the final two weeks, and the quality and workload checks pass. The engineering manager commits the time before launch and clears sprint capacity for it. Engineers choose what to work on during the half-day.

The reward is team-wide. Two engineers share review coverage each workday on a rotating schedule, with time reserved in their sprint plans. They can review a PR themselves or find someone with the right expertise. The rotation gives every PR an owner even when its usual reviewers are away.

## How it is measured

- **Start the clock** when a human-authored PR first becomes ready for review in a team-owned repository. A ready PR enters the queue automatically, including when the author forgets to request a reviewer. Drafts and bot-authored PRs are excluded. A PR returned to draft and reopened keeps its original start time.
- **Count one working day** as eight hours on the team’s published working calendar. For example, on a 09:00–17:00 calendar, a PR marked ready at 15:00 on Friday is due at 15:00 on Monday, assuming Monday is a working day. Individual absences are handled through coverage.
- **Stop the clock** at the first substantive human review. That can be an approval after inspecting the change, a request for changes with a specific reason, or a blocking question tied to the diff. Acknowledgments, emoji, and automated checks do not count.
- **Report the weekly on-time rate** using all PRs whose deadlines fell that week. A PR still waiting at its deadline counts as late. PRs withdrawn before the deadline appear in a separate count, with withdrawals checked for signs of gaming. Also report volume and median first-review time. Recalculate the previous four weeks under these rules before comparing results with the reported 2.6-day median.

## What engineers see

The live review queue shows each waiting PR, its current reviewer, and its deadline. The coverage pair receives one reminder when two working hours remain. A weekly team post shows the on-time count and denominator, median, withdrawals, and recurring causes of delay. Individual performance scores stay out of the report. Use the existing pull-request queue and team channel.

## How long it runs and how it ends

Run the pilot for six weeks. In week one, verify the baseline, check that timestamps match real PRs, and start the rotation. Use weeks two through four to fix routing and capacity problems. Weeks five and six determine whether the team earns the reward. Require at least 20 eligible PRs across those two weeks. If volume is lower, report the result as inconclusive and end on schedule.

At the end of week six, publish the outcome and book the half-day if earned. End the incentive after one payout decision. Use the retrospective to decide whether the rotation and queue should become routine team practices.

## What could go wrong

- **Fast approvals replace real reviews.** Each week, two engineers outside the coverage pair audit 10 randomly selected on-time reviews, or all of them if fewer qualify. They check the diff and review together and ask the reviewer for context where needed. Confirmed superficial reviews are removed from the on-time count. The reward also requires at least 90% of audited reviews to pass in each scoring week.
- **The team shifts the wait upstream or downstream.** Track the weekly number of drafts open for more than two working days, withdrawals, and time from first review to merge. Investigate increases in the retrospective. Deliberately delaying the ready flag or withdrawing PRs to protect the score invalidates the affected scoring week.
- **The same specialists absorb the work or people review after hours.** The coverage pair flags overloaded specialties at the weekly check-in. The manager shifts sprint work or adds backup coverage when the queue exceeds available capacity. The workload check fails if confirmed recurring after-hours reviewing is needed to meet the target in either scoring week.
