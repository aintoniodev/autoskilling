## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

Run a four-month pilot across the three wards. Each ward can earn one €300 team improvement fund. Nurses on all shifts choose how to spend it on a shared workplace improvement. Set the same criteria for every ward, assess each ward independently, and publish the rules before launch.

### What earns the reward

A ward earns the fund if it meets both criteria across months 3 and 4 combined.

- **Logging coverage reaches 80%.** Of the hand-hygiene actions observers see nurses complete, at least 80% have a matching kiosk entry.
- **Observed compliance meets the ward’s target.** Compliance is the number of correctly completed actions divided by all eligible opportunities observers record under the hospital’s existing audit protocol. Wards below 90% at baseline must improve by five percentage points or reach 90%, whichever comes first. Wards starting at 90% or above must stay at or above 90%.

Pay the reward once, after the final audit. Raw kiosk volume carries no reward. Multiple entries for the same action count once.

### How measurement works

In month 1, measure baseline compliance and introduce the kiosk. In month 2, give wards practice and feedback. Keep the monthly observer audits throughout the pilot. Schedule observation blocks across day, night, and weekend shifts, with comparable coverage at baseline and during the reward period. Staff know how observation works, and the exact blocks vary.

Use at least 60 observed opportunities per ward for the baseline and 60 for the combined reward period, including at least 40 completed actions for the logging measure. Report the counts alongside percentages. If an audit falls short, schedule a make-up block before deciding the award.

Nurses log completed actions when care permits, by the end of the shift. The kiosk records the moment category and approximate action time, with a quick option for delayed entry. The audit lead matches observed actions to entries using ward, temporary staff code, category, and action time. Pair entries one-to-one. Review ambiguous pairs before scoring and count only confirmed matches in the numerator. Keep patient identifiers out of the kiosk and discard staff-level matching data once awards and any disputes are resolved.

### What nurses see

After logging, the kiosk confirms the entry and lets the nurse correct it. A ward-only dashboard shows its baseline, current audit counts and rates, both award targets, and the next feedback date. Indicate when the sample is too small to score. At the monthly huddle, the ward lead shares one practical barrier raised by staff and who is fixing it. Individual scores and cross-ward rankings stay off the dashboard.

### How it ends

At the end of month 4, the audit lead validates the results, resolves discrepancies, and announces each ward’s outcome. The incentive expires after that decision. Keep monthly compliance audits and check logging coverage eight weeks later. Use that follow-up and staff feedback on workload to decide whether to retain or revise the kiosk workflow.

### What could go wrong

- **Staff log extra actions or reconstruct a shift from memory.** Reward only independently matched entries. Check duplicate and late-entry patterns for workflow problems before drawing conclusions about staff conduct.
- **Logging competes with care.** Allow delayed entry, check whether kiosk queues or placement create friction, and pause scoring if logging interferes with care.
- **Audited shifts look better than routine practice.** Vary observation times and compare shift coverage. Treat results as estimates from sampled opportunities.
- **Outages or uneven access skew the award.** Track downtime by ward and shift. For verified outage periods, keep observations in the clinical compliance measure and use make-up blocks to restore comparable logging coverage.
- **Team rewards create pressure to hide misses.** Keep individual records restricted to audit matching. Discuss barriers in huddles and let nurses flag pressure privately to the audit lead.
