# Pre-job safety checklist incentive

**Reward a sound pre-job safety decision.** For six weeks, technicians earn an illustrative $15 weekly bonus when they complete the digital checklist before starting work on at least 90% of eligible jobs that week. Agree the amount, eligibility, data access and dispute process with union representatives before launch. Pay the same amount regardless of job volume. A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a pause, escalation or cancellation earns the same credit as one that clears the job. Technicians complete checklists on paid time, with time built into dispatch schedules.

**Measure against dispatched jobs.** Use the dispatch system to establish which jobs required a checklist, and compare the app’s completion timestamp with the existing job-start record. Jobs paused or cancelled because of a checklist finding stay in the denominator and count as compliant. Jobs cancelled before a technician has to assess the site are excluded. Offline checklists use the timestamp recorded on the device and sync when connectivity returns. Record approved paper fallbacks, app failures and emergency exceptions separately. Verified exceptions do not reduce bonus eligibility, and their volume remains visible in programme reporting. Give technicians a chance to correct missing or mismatched records before payroll closes.

**Show technicians what they can act on.** At dispatch, show the checklist and its completion status. After submission, show the decision recorded, any required follow-up, and whether the record has synced. In a private weekly view, show qualifying jobs, jobs requiring review and bonus status, with a correction link. Supervisors see operational exceptions they need to resolve. Management and union representatives review aggregate completion, fallback and quality data. There are no public individual rankings.

**Run for ten weeks and publish the end date up front.** In weeks 1–2, verify the roughly 35% baseline against dispatch records and test the workflow with technicians across shifts and connectivity conditions. Run the bonus in weeks 3–8. In weeks 9–10, keep the paid checklist time, prompts and support in place and measure completion after the bonus ends. Check again four weeks later. Aim for at least 85% on-time digital completion at the end of the bonus and at the follow-up, with sampled checklist quality at or above baseline. Pay all earned bonuses and resolve outstanding disputes. If adoption falls, review the barriers with technicians and union representatives before deciding whether another incentive is warranted.

**Watch for predictable failure modes.**

- **Rushing or backfilling.** Review a small random sample across shifts for site-specific answers and appropriate hazard follow-up. Investigate completion spikes and timestamp mismatches before drawing conclusions.
- **Pressure to clear unsafe jobs.** Keep bonus eligibility independent of incident counts, stop-work frequency and job speed. Give technicians a union-supported route to raise concerns about pressure to proceed.
- **Unequal access or insufficient time.** Review exceptions and completion rates by shift, job type and connectivity. Fix device, language, accessibility and scheduling barriers surfaced by the pilot.
- **App data used beyond the agreed purpose.** Set access and retention limits with the union. Keep programme scores out of disciplinary and productivity rankings.
