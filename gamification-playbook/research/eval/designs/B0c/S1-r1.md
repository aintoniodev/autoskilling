## Pre-job safety checklist programme

**Goal.** Raise verified pre-job checklist completion from roughly 35% to at least 85% of eligible jobs, then sustain it after the incentive ends. Agree the reward, exception rules, data access and appeals process with union representatives before launch. Give technicians paid training and schedule time for the check on every job.

### What earns the reward

During an eight-week rollout, technicians can earn up to the equivalent of **one hour of base pay per four-week block**. The payout is proportional to their verified completion rate across eligible jobs in the block. A technician who completes 18 of 20 checks earns 90% of the maximum. Each technician has the same cap, regardless of job volume. A block with no eligible jobs is skipped.

A check earns credit when it is job-specific, completed at the site and recorded before work begins. A technician who identifies a hazard and records a stop or escalation before work begins earns the same credit, including when the job is postponed. If using the phone would be unsafe, the technician can use the agreed paper or dispatch procedure and reconcile it later. Injury rates, hazard counts and job speed play no part in the payout.

### How it is measured

Use dispatch records to identify jobs that reached the pre-work decision point. This includes jobs stopped for safety and excludes jobs cancelled before arrival. Match each job to the checklist or documented safety stop and compare its recorded time with the job-start record. Count late checks separately and give technicians a way to correct inaccurate start times.

Allow offline completion and later sync using the original recorded time. Count verified fallback checks toward the reward, and report digital uptake separately so outages and paper use remain visible. Publish the number and reasons for exclusions, including jobs where a confirmed system failure prevented both recording and recovery.

Each week, a safety lead and a union representative review a small random sample across crews and shifts. They check whether the record reflects a real pre-job assessment and follow up on patterns such as identical answers across very different jobs. Give technicians five working days to dispute missing credit before payroll closes each block.

### What people see

Before work starts, the app opens the checklist from the job record and confirms whether it was saved or is waiting to sync. Technicians see a private running tally of eligible jobs, credited checks, unresolved records and estimated payout, with a way to flag errors. Crews see weekly company-wide completion and digital uptake, along with the problems being fixed. Managers see aggregate results. Individual records are available to the designated reviewers and payroll for their agreed roles. There are no individual rankings.

### How it runs and ends

- **Weeks 1–2.** Establish the baseline using the same eligibility rules. Pilot the app, fallback and credit calculation with a cross-section of technicians working different shifts and connectivity conditions.
- **Weeks 3–10.** Run the reward company-wide in two four-week blocks. Review missed checks and fix recurring app, dispatch and scheduling problems each week.
- **Weeks 11–14.** End the reward on the published date and pay the final block after appeals. Keep paid checklist time, the app prompt and the fallback. Check whether completion stays at or above 85% each week. If it slips, review the causes jointly with the union and address the barriers before considering another incentive.

### What could go wrong

| Risk | Programme response |
|---|---|
| Technicians tap through or fill out checks in advance | Keep the checklist short and job-specific. Use sampled field observations and record reviews to assess whether checks are meaningful. |
| Dispatch pressure makes skipping the check the easiest option | Build checklist time into job estimates and review missed checks alongside workload and start-time expectations. |
| Poor connectivity, shared devices or shift patterns skew payouts | Provide offline and fallback routes. Compare credit disputes and exclusions by crew and shift, then correct verified discrepancies before paying. |
| Workers feel pressure to hide hazards or view the app as surveillance | Give safety stops full credit. Agree limits on access, retention and use of incentive records with the union before launch. |
| Completion falls when the money ends | Use the four-week follow-up to identify which crews still face friction and fix the barriers in their normal workflow. |
