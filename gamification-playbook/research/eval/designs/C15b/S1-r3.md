# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward a completed safety check before work starts.** Each crew that completes a valid digital checklist before at least 80% of eligible job starts over four weeks earns a 30-minute paid early finish for every crew member. Schedule it across shifts so everyone can take it, including people who were on leave during the pilot. Agree the reward with union stewards and technicians before launch, name it in the launch message, and have the field-service lead thank each qualifying crew in person. A crew that flags a hazard still gets credit if its checklist is complete and the hazard is resolved before work starts.

**Make the app usable on a real callout.** Use paid time to walk through jobs with technicians from different shifts and routes, including low-signal sites. Fix the blockers they identify, show them what changed, and give everyone paid practice before measuring the reward. Make the checklist the first screen on opening a job, prefill job details, and let technicians save it offline. Keep the approved fallback available if the app fails. Technicians follow the same stop-work and escalation procedure throughout the pilot.

**Count each job once.** Use the existing work order, checklist completion, and job-start records. The crew’s score is the percentage of eligible started jobs with a complete digital checklist saved before the recorded start time. Agree eligibility with the union before launch. An offline checklist counts once synced if the app preserves its original save time. Duplicate submissions add no credit. A checklist completed after work starts earns no credit. Jobs cancelled before work starts stay out of the denominator. Started jobs checked on paper stay in the adoption denominator, and verified app outages are excluded from reward scoring so crews can use the fallback without losing ground. Track safety stops that prevent a start in the existing safety process, outside reward scoring. Before launch, have dispatch, safety, and a union steward test these cases, including a voided checklist and an incorrect start time. If the existing records cannot reliably establish which event came first, fix that before tying the count to pay.

**Show progress without ranking people.** After saving, the app confirms whether the job counted or is pending sync. Technicians see their own eligible jobs and counts, with a personal target based on their assigned workload. A missed week carries no penalty. Managers and crews see crew totals in the usual weekly briefing, with small crews grouped where necessary to protect privacy. Agree in writing that pilot counts stay out of performance reviews and discipline. Technicians can raise a disputed count privately with a union steward. Dispatch corrects verified record errors, and the safety lead resolves disputed eligibility with the steward.

**Pause if quality slips.** The safety lead samples 20 completed checklists each week across crews using existing job records. A review checks whether the answers are plausible for the job and whether flagged hazards have a documented resolution before work starts. If two or more of the 20 have a material defect, pause new reward accrual, fix the cause with technicians and the steward, and resume after a fresh sample clears the threshold. Keep valid credit already earned. Watch for rushed answers, hazards recorded as clear, and job starts entered late to make timing look right. Review verified outages and missing timestamps with dispatch so crews serving low-signal sites have a fair shot.

**End the reward after four weeks per crew.** Give all crews the improved app and feedback first, then stagger reward starts by two weeks across comparable crews. Use the overlap to see whether the reward adds adoption beyond the app fixes and feedback. When each crew’s reward period ends, keep the checklist first in the app and continue private confirmation. Four weeks later, use the company-wide share of eligible starts with a valid pre-start digital checklist to decide what to do. At **80% or more**, keep the workflow and retire the prize. At **60–79%**, fix the remaining barriers and recheck. Below **60%**, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and union stewards.
