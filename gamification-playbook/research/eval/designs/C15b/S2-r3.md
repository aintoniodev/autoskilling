# One-working-day code reviews

**Goal.** Give authors a useful first review within one working day. The current median is 2.6 days. Run this as a trial of a better review workflow, with a team reward for meeting the response target.

## Fix the queue first

Spend two weeks with a cross-section of authors and reviewers identifying what makes reviews wait. Give each reviewer a protected daily review block and name a backup for each ownership area. Make ready PRs appear oldest first in the existing review queue, with a suggested reviewer and backup. Ask authors for a short summary and any areas where they need particular scrutiny. Tell the team which friction they reported and what changed. If reviewers still lack time or ownership is unclear, resolve that before starting rewards.

## What earns the reward

Tell engineers at launch: “If at least 85% of ready PRs in your group get a substantive human review within one working day over the four-week trial, everyone in the group gets a paid half-day off.”

Agree the half-day with engineers and secure coverage before announcing it. The engineering lead confirms the result at the team meeting and thanks the group for specific improvements. Engineers can take the time off on a day that works for them.

Use existing PR events. Start the clock when a human-authored PR is ready and review is requested, including when a PR with an existing request leaves draft. Count eight hours on an agreed team working calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. A qualifying response is a submitted human review that approves after inspection, requests a concrete change, or asks a specific question the author needs to answer to move forward. Bot activity, acknowledgements and emoji reactions earn no credit.

Count each PR once against its original deadline. Re-requests, reassignment and reopening cannot reset the clock or earn another credit. A duplicate or genuinely withdrawn PR can be excluded with a documented reason. A PR merged without review or moved back to draft to buy time stays in the denominator. Include PRs whose deadline falls in the measurement window, even if they are still unanswered.

## What engineers see

After submitting a qualifying review, the reviewer gets a private confirmation in the existing workflow. Each engineer sees their assigned PRs and deadlines, with a personal goal of answering the cases assigned during their available review time. The backup picks up work during leave or overload. A missed deadline does not affect the next case.

Post the group’s weekly percentage and eligible PR count where the team already looks. Managers get aggregate programme figures only. There are no rankings, individual reporting or performance-review uses.

## Timing and decision

Apply the queue fixes and feedback to everyone in weeks 1–2. If the team has two existing ownership groups with largely separate review queues, give each four reward weeks, starting in weeks 3 and 5. Compare the groups during the stagger and report the PR counts. If reviewers are shared across queues, run one four-week trial and treat the comparison with weeks 1–2 as directional.

Keep the queue defaults in place when rewards end. Four weeks later, use the same measure, **the share of eligible PRs receiving a substantive review within one working day**, to decide:

- **85% or higher:** keep the workflow and close the reward programme.
- **70–84%:** adjust coverage or routing and recheck in four weeks.
- **Below 70%:** end the incentive experiment and revisit the main source of delay with engineers.

## What could go wrong

- **Rubber stamps.** A rotating senior engineer checks 10 credited reviews per week across PR sizes, or all reviews if there are fewer than 10. Two or more non-substantive reviews in the sample pause reward confirmation. The engineering lead decides when to resume, and the queue owner corrects disputed counts and reports the aggregate adjustment.
- **Easy PRs get picked first.** Order the queue by deadline and have the backup take aging cases. Check whether large or specialist PRs account for a disproportionate share of misses before changing the reward.
- **Reviews crowd out planned work.** Budget review blocks into sprint capacity and shift assignments when engineers are away. If the deadline routinely requires after-hours work, fix coverage before continuing.
