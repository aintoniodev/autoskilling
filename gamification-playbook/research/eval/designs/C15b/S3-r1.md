## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Make logging fit the work.** Give day, night and weekend nurses 20 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk on their ward. Ask where logging would interrupt care, require a detour or create an infection-control issue. Fix those barriers before rewards start. Put the kiosk on an existing staff route, with its ward prefilled and the entry screen ready to use. Agree with nurses on one use for the data, such as identifying which hand-hygiene moments are hardest to document. Post the fixes at handover under “You reported it, we fixed it.”

**Reward verified documentation once per ward.** Announce the rule at launch: “When at least 80% of completed hand-hygiene moments sampled in your monthly audit have one accurate kiosk entry, and observed hand hygiene holds steady, the chief nurse will bring a catered meal to every shift on your ward.” Each ward can earn the meal independently, including night and weekend staff. The chief nurse thanks the team in person and names a specific improvement the ward made.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** Infection Prevention calculates documentation coverage as the share of eligible, observer-confirmed completed moments with one matching kiosk entry. Use the kiosk’s existing time, location and moment fields where they permit an unambiguous match within the area the observer covered. Match each entry to at most one observed moment and collect no staff or patient identifiers for matching. Before launch, test a duplicate, a corrected or withdrawn entry, a completed moment with no entry, and two look-alike events. Duplicates earn one count, corrections replace the original, and withdrawn, missing or ambiguous entries earn zero. If existing fields cannot support reliable anonymous matching, hold the performance reward and fix the workflow.

Use at least 50 observed opportunities and 30 eligible completed moments per ward per audit cycle as pilot decision minimums. Spread existing observation sessions across shifts and keep the timing unannounced. A thin sample leaves the reward pending. Track clinical compliance independently as **observed hygiene actions ÷ observed opportunities**, including opportunities where kiosk logging was impractical. Pause the ward’s reward if compliance falls by more than five percentage points below its pre-reward baseline or if logging delays care. Infection Prevention owns the pause and restart decision. The ward kiosk lead handles disputed entries before results are posted. Observed behaviour can improve while an auditor is present, so treat the audit as a sample. [Multicentre study of direct observation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111925/)

**Show staff what they can act on.** The kiosk confirms each save immediately with the moment type and a correction option. At handover, show the ward’s latest verified result, such as “24 of 32 sampled moments documented accurately · 75% · goal 80%,” alongside the latest workflow fix. Staff and managers see ward totals only. Keep individual data out of rankings, performance reviews and incentive reports. The next eligible moment is a fresh chance after leave, a busy shift or an outage. During urgent care, skip the kiosk and carry on with care.

**Stagger the start and retire the prize.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback for one full audit cycle first. Randomly assign reward starts to the next three monthly cycles, one ward per cycle. Each ward gets two reward cycles and can earn the meal once. Compare changes with each ward’s own baseline and with wards still awaiting rewards. Three wards give a directional read. Before the last reward cycle ends, make the kiosk shortcut and the agreed logging cue part of normal ward orientation and workflow.

At four and eight weeks after each ward’s reward ends, use verified documentation coverage to decide the next step. Keep routine kiosk logging at **80% or above** if the safety guardrail passes. Below 80%, fix the workflow and retest if coverage is still at least 10 percentage points above baseline. Otherwise, drop the kiosk rollout in its current form. End prizes on schedule and continue the existing hand-hygiene audits.
