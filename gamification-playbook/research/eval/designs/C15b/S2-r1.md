# One working day for code reviews

**Give every review request a useful response within one working day.** The current median is 2.6 days. Set a pilot target of **80% of eligible pull requests receiving a useful first review within eight working hours**. A request for changes counts just as much as an approval.

## Fix the queue first

In week 1, give four engineers who regularly author and review PRs 30 minutes of protected time to walk through recent delays. Ask where requests get missed, what makes a review hard to start, and whether the reward below is worth earning. Set up a rotating reviewer and backup, reserve 30 minutes each working day for the rotation, and put the oldest ready PRs first in the team’s existing review queue. Share which friction points the team raised and what changed. Give everyone the queue and private feedback for two weeks before rewards begin. Start the reward phase once the rotation can handle normal review volume within working hours.

## What earns the reward

Use this rule in the launch message.

> If 80% of ready PRs get a useful first review within one working day over the four-week pilot, everyone on the team earns two hours of paid time off.

A useful response is a submitted approval after checking the change, a change request with a concrete issue, or a submitted review comment that explains a specific blocker and gives the author a next step. Acknowledgments, emoji, bot reviews, and self-reviews earn no credit. Arrange coverage so everyone can take the time, including people who were away during the pilot. The engineering lead announces the reward at launch and recognizes the team at its next regular meeting after results are verified.

## How it’s counted and what people see

Use existing PR and review events. Start the clock when a PR is ready and human review has been requested. Count eight hours on the team’s published working calendar, excluding evenings, weekends, and holidays. Assign the backup when the reviewer is away. Each PR counts once. Reassignment, reopening, or flipping back to draft preserves the original deadline. Unanswered requests count as late once the deadline passes. Exclude genuine duplicates and PRs withdrawn before the deadline, and check those exclusions in the weekly sample.

After a qualifying review, the reviewer gets a private confirmation in the tool they already use, such as “PR #412 received a review in 5 working hours.” Their private view shows assigned reviews still due and timely responses out of eligible assignments. The personal goal is four in five for people with at least five assignments and the next due review for people with fewer. Absences carry no penalty. The existing team channel shows the weekly and cumulative team rate and denominator. Managers see team totals, without individual drill-down. Programme data stays out of performance reviews.

## What could go wrong

- **Rubber stamps or vague blockers.** Each week, two rotating engineers sample ten credited reviews, or all if fewer, and check whether each met the rule. If two or more fail, pause reward accrual while the engineering lead fixes the cause. The lead also handles private count disputes and corrects the tally before the reward is awarded.
- **Easy PRs crowd out difficult ones.** The reviewer takes the oldest ready request first. A concrete blocker or change request earns full credit. Reviewers can give useful feedback within a day even when a PR needs another pass.
- **Work shifts into evenings or onto the same people.** Keep the eight-hour clock tied to working hours and route overflow to the backup. If the queue keeps exceeding the protected review time, adjust capacity with the team before resuming rewards.
- **People feel watched.** Use existing review events and private feedback. Keep recognition team-wide, with no named rankings.

## How it ends

End accrual after four reward weeks. Keep the review rotation, protected time, and oldest-first queue as the default, then track the same timely-response rate for four weeks without prizes. **Keep the workflow at 80% or above. If the rate is below 80% but above the two-week pre-reward baseline, fix the main remaining bottleneck and measure again. If it returns to baseline or worse, drop the incentive and revisit the review workflow.** If the team already reaches 80% during the fixes-only phase, keep that workflow and skip launching the reward.
