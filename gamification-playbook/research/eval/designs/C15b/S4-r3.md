# Make the next ticket easier

Run a six-week incentive in each support team to increase the share of resolved tickets backed by a useful knowledge-base article. Today, agents link or write an article on 12% of resolved tickets. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its calculation in place.

## What earns the reward

**A resolved ticket counts once when it has a published article that explains the fix or gives a colleague a usable next step.** Agents can link an existing article or draft a new one that the knowledge-base owner approves and publishes within seven days of resolution. Create articles for reusable gaps. Agents can use an existing disposition for tickets with no reusable guidance, such as spam or account-specific actions, and close the ticket promptly.

Announce the reward at launch: **everyone on a team that reaches 50% coverage across its six reward weeks earns one paid hour off**, scheduled around queue coverage. The support lead announces the result live and thanks the team for a specific article that helped. New hires, part-timers and people returning from leave share in the team reward. Each agent gets a private goal of covering half their eligible tickets, with a goal of one ticket when they have only one or two. A missed week has no penalty.

## How it’s measured and what people see

Use existing ticket resolution, disposition, article-link and publication records. Coverage is unique eligible tickets with a qualifying article divided by unique eligible resolved tickets. Recalculate the 12% baseline using that definition before launch. Also show the share of **all** resolved tickets with an article and the exception rate so rising exclusions are visible.

Test the rule before launch. Two articles on one ticket earn one credit. A ticket reopened within seven days loses provisional credit and can earn it again on re-resolution, once per ticket ID. An article that is drafted but never published earns no credit. A ticket resolved the old way stays in the denominator unless it has a valid exception.

After resolution, the ticket view tells the agent what counted or whether publication is pending. Agents see their own progress and can flag a wrong count to support operations, which corrects the source record or tally. Managers see team totals only. Post team progress in the existing weekly support update. Keep reward data out of performance reviews.

## How it runs and ends

Spend the first two weeks with agents from different shifts and queues. Give them protected time to try the workflow. Fix the friction they identify, such as weak article search or slow approvals, and tell them what changed. Give every team the fixes and feedback before introducing the prize.

Start teams in three waves, two weeks apart, with six reward weeks per team. Compare each wave with teams still receiving the same fixes and feedback without the prize. Before rewards end, make article search the first step in the resolution panel, prefilled from the ticket category, and leave an easy path for exceptions.

Four weeks after each team finishes, measure coverage over those four prize-free weeks. **Keep the workflow if coverage holds at 50% or above. Change it if coverage falls below 50% but remains above the recalculated baseline. Drop the programme if coverage returns to baseline or below.** Apply the quality and CSAT checks before expanding the workflow.

## What could go wrong

- **Agents attach irrelevant articles or overuse exceptions.** Each week, support QA samples ten credited tickets per team, or all credits if fewer than ten, plus a few exceptions. If 20% or more of sampled credits fail for relevance or accuracy, the support lead pauses reward progression while the knowledge-base owner fixes the articles and support operations corrects the tally. Any confirmed exposure of customer data through an article triggers an immediate pause.
- **The article step delays replies or hurts CSAT.** Agents can resolve tickets while publication is pending. The support lead checks team CSAT alongside response counts and time to resolution. A five-point CSAT drop against the team’s pre-programme baseline in a rolling four-week window with at least 30 responses pauses the reward for review. Review sampled tickets and queue mix before deciding whether to restart.
- **Some queues have fewer reusable cases.** During the baseline, check that the 50% milestone is reachable in each queue and that exclusions are applied consistently. If a queue has genuine coverage gaps, fix article availability or the workflow before starting its reward clock.
