# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Set up the work with technicians and the union.** In the first two weeks, give technicians from different shifts, routes, and job types paid time to try the app on real jobs. Agree with union representatives on paid checklist time, reward terms, data access, and a way to dispute counts. Give dispatch enough time in the schedule for the check, prefill job details from the work order, and make the form work offline. Show crews what changed because of their feedback. Start rewards once crews can reliably complete the check on paid time, including at sites without signal.

**Reward a completed pre-job safety check.** Announce at the launch huddle that each technician who completes a qualifying check on at least 60% of their eligible jobs during their six-week reward window earns two hours of paid time off. Confirm with technicians that the reward is worth earning and agree with the union on how it can be booked. The goal scales with assigned job volume. Leave and missed weeks carry no separate penalty. The company’s safety procedure still calls for a check before every job.

Use one rule in the huddle and the app. “One check counts per job visit when you finish it before work begins or record the hazard that makes you stop or defer the job.” A crew uses one shared checklist per visit. Each crew member already assigned to that visit gets credit for participating in the check.

**Measure from existing job records.** Match the checklist to the dispatch visit ID and the existing work-start or safety-hold record. Count visits that reached the point where a pre-job check was due, including visits stopped for safety. Exclude jobs canceled before arrival. Keep legacy-method jobs in the denominator. Duplicate submissions count once, a voided record loses credit, and a late entry cannot earn credit retroactively. Use the app’s original completion time for offline checks and reconcile them when they sync. During a verified outage, a contemporaneous paper checklist can earn reward credit through the existing safety process. Track it as paper in the digital adoption measure. Test these cases against real records before launch and verify that the event order can be established from the existing workflow.

**Give private feedback and show team progress.** After each check, show “Pre-job check recorded” or “Saved offline, pending sync,” then update the technician’s own progress. At the existing weekly huddle, share the team’s digital completion rate and fixes requested by crews. Combine small crews in the reporting view where individual results would be apparent. Managers see aggregate incentive data only. Use no named rankings, GPS tracking, or incentive data in performance reviews. A technician can flag a missing visit through a steward. The safety programme administrator resolves the count from the job record and confirms the correction privately.

**Watch for rushed checks and pressure to proceed.** Each week, the safety lead and union safety representative review 20 randomly sampled checklists against available job records and include both proceed and stop outcomes in the review. Look for site-specific hazards, appropriate controls, and work beginning before a hold is cleared. They pause new reward accrual if more than two sampled checks miss a required hazard assessment or control, or immediately if they verify pressure to bypass a check, suppress a report, or work through a safety hold. Preserve earned credit, fix the cause, and restart only after a joint review. Hazard, near-miss, and injury reports never reduce eligibility. Keep the existing reporting route and stop-work authority available throughout. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation)

**Stagger the launch and end the reward on schedule.** Give every team the app fixes, paid training, and private feedback in weeks 1–2. Randomly assign comparable teams to start their six-week reward window in week 3 or week 6. Use the three-week overlap to see whether rewards add uptake beyond the fixes and feedback. Before the first reward window ends, make the checklist the first screen when opening an assigned job, with the safety-hold and outage paths visible. At the closing huddle, thank the crews and confirm earned time off privately. End each team’s reward after six weeks.

Use **the percentage of eligible visits with a qualifying digital pre-job check** as the decision measure. Compare it with the roughly 35% starting rate, verify that baseline against the same denominator, and measure again four weeks after each team’s rewards end. If the rate holds at 75% or higher and quality clears the pause threshold, keep the workflow and feedback. If adoption improves but falls short of 75%, fix the remaining barriers. If rewards add no discernible lift or the gain disappears after they end, drop the incentive from future rollouts.
