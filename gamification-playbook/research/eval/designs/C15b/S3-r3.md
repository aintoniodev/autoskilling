# Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward.** Give each ward that meets the milestone one paid 30-minute break extension per nurse, scheduled with cover across all shifts. Announce it at launch and have the director of nursing thank the team in person at shift huddles. The rule staff hear is “Your ward earns the reward when the routine audit shows good hand hygiene and the kiosk records cover at least 80% of the hand cleaning observed in the audit sample.” Each ward can earn it independently. Missed shifts and leave carry no penalty.

**Make logging fit the work.** Before launch, ask nurses from days, nights and weekends to try the kiosk on paid time. Fix barriers they identify, such as a kiosk away from the normal walking route, repeated sign-ins or too many taps. Share the fixes at huddles. Put the logging screen first and prefill the ward. Nurses clean their hands at the point of care and log afterward when patient care allows. If the kiosk is down, the audit still counts hand hygiene and the affected window is excluded from the logging measure.

**Measure two things with the existing monthly audits.**

- **Hand hygiene.** Infection Prevention divides observed hand-hygiene actions by observed opportunities for nurses on each ward. To qualify, a ward must improve by five percentage points over its two-month baseline or reach 90%, and stay within five points of its baseline if it already exceeds 90%. Use comparable samples across shifts and keep observation times out of the reward communications. Set the minimum observation count before launch. Hold the decision for another routine audit if the sample falls short. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)
- **Kiosk coverage.** Use a small, comparable subset of routine audit windows where observers cover the same area and nursing group as the kiosk records. Compare counts of completed hand cleaning and valid kiosk entries by area and window, using existing timestamps and no nurse or patient identifiers. For each window, cap credited entries at the number of observed actions and divide the sum of credited entries by the sum of observed actions. This is an estimate of coverage. Before offering the reward, confirm that the existing records support comparable windows. If they do not, use audited hand hygiene alone for the reward and defer the kiosk coverage target until it can be measured credibly.

**Give feedback.** The kiosk confirms “Saved” immediately and offers Undo. Huddles and the existing ward board show the ward’s monthly audit rate, estimated coverage and progress toward the milestone. Managers see ward totals only. Keep kiosk data out of performance reviews and give nurses a private way to report a wrong count. The kiosk owner corrects records and Infection Prevention signs off on the totals.

**Run and end it.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback for two baseline months. Randomly assign wards to start the two-month reward window in months 3, 4 and 5. When each window closes, stop prizes and leave the fast logging screen and team feedback in place. Use the stagger to see whether coverage moved when rewards began, treating three wards as a pilot. The decision metric is **80% estimated kiosk coverage at the first routine audit at least four weeks after prizes end**, subject to the hand-hygiene safeguard. Keep the workflow if it meets that threshold. If coverage rose only during the reward window, change the workflow and retest without prizes. If coverage stays low after those fixes or cannot be measured credibly, drop the kiosk requirement.

**Watch for failure.** Before launch, test that a retry saves once, an undone entry contributes zero and observed cleaning without a log still counts toward clinical compliance. Infection Prevention pauses rewards if audited compliance falls more than five points below baseline or if raw kiosk entries exceed observed actions by more than 10% in comparable windows. Review duplicate submissions, late entries, kiosk downtime and sampling differences before deciding what to fix. Ask nurses during huddles whether logging is pulling them away from care, and fix that friction before resuming.
