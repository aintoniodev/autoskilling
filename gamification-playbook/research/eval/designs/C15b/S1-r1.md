### Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**What earns the reward.** Technicians earn one hour of paid release time when they complete the digital safety checklist before at least 80% of their eligible jobs during their six-week reward period. Announce the reward at launch and agree with union representatives how technicians can take the hour across shifts. The goal scales with each technician’s job volume and accrues over the full period. A missed week or time off never resets progress. A valid checklist that leads to a safety stop counts toward the goal. The safety checklist remains required on every applicable job.

**Fix the workflow before launch.** Ask technicians and union representatives from different shifts and routes to try the app on real jobs during paid time. Fix the issues they identify, such as poor connectivity, slow sign-in, or repeated data entry. Show crews which fixes came from their feedback. Agree on the counting rule, data access, and correction process with union representatives before launch. Keep the approved fallback available during outages so crews can complete the safety check without waiting for the app.

**How it’s counted.** Use the existing job ID, checklist submission time, and job-start record. Award one credit per job for a complete checklist recorded before work starts. Count a completed checklist linked to an existing safety-stop record even if the job never starts. An offline submission counts once synced if the app already preserves a reliable completion time. Confirm that these records can establish the order of events during the pilot setup. A duplicate earns no extra credit, a voided submission loses credit unless a valid replacement was recorded before work started, and a late or paper checklist earns no digital credit. Exclude cancellations before the safety check is due. Exclude verified outages from personal reward calculations, but keep those jobs in the programme’s adoption rate and report fallback use alongside it.

**What people see.** After submission, the app confirms credit or shows that it is pending sync. Technicians see their own eligible jobs, credits, and progress toward the paid hour. Use the existing shift briefing to share team totals and a reminder of the rule. Suppress small groups where totals could identify individuals. Managers see aggregate results only, and incentive data stays out of performance reviews. A designated programme administrator corrects disputed counts using existing job records, with a union representative available to support the technician.

**Timing and ending.** Give every crew the app fixes, paid practice, and private feedback first, then measure adoption for two weeks. Start the six-week reward period for half the crews and start the other half three weeks later. Assign the order by crew, balancing for starting adoption and job volume. Use the three-week overlap to estimate what the reward adds while both groups have the same workflow and feedback. Before each crew’s reward period ends, put the checklist first on the job screen and prefill known job details. The reward then ends and the simpler workflow stays in place.

Measure the company-wide share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-start digital checklist over the four weeks after the last reward ends. Set the decision thresholds before launch. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow without prizes. At **50–69%**, fix the main remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **50%**, retire the incentive and revisit whether the mobile workflow works in the field. Use the two-week baseline to interpret the change alongside the current estimate of 35%.

**What could go wrong.** Rushed checks and delayed job-start entries can make adoption look better than it is. Each reward week, the existing safety lead samples 20 credited jobs across shifts and compares timing and responses with available job records. If two or more have a substantive safety or timing error, the safety lead pauses new reward awards, investigates with a union representative, and corrects the cause before resuming. Keep credit already verified. Handle any immediate hazard through the existing safety process. Review outage and dispute patterns by crew so poor coverage, inconsistent counts, or pressure to skip a safety stop surface while there is time to fix them.
