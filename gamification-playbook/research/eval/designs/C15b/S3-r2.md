# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

Reward wards for **hand hygiene completed during routine independent audits**. Give nurses immediate feedback when they record a moment in the kiosk. Kiosk entries carry no points or prizes, so tapping twice or recording a missed action as completed earns nothing.

## Set up the workflow

Give day- and night-shift nurses on each ward paid time to try the kiosk during a normal shift. Ask where logging adds walking, queues, or interruptions, especially during urgent care. Fix those issues before launch and post what changed in the ward huddle. Make the current ward and shift the kiosk defaults, with a single confirmation after the nurse selects the moment. Hand hygiene and patient care take priority whenever logging would interrupt either.

## Reward and measurement

Use the hospital’s existing monthly observer audit. Each eligible opportunity counts once in the denominator, and each correctly completed action counts once in the numerator. A ward earns **30 minutes of paid break time per nurse, scheduled with staffing cover**, when its pooled compliance across the two reward-period audits reaches its target. Set the target at five percentage points above the ward’s run-in rate, capped at 95%. Wards starting at or above 95% earn the reward by staying within two percentage points of their baseline. Announce the reward and target at launch. The nursing director thanks the ward in person and confirms when staff can take the time. Each ward can earn it independently, and staff on leave or on nights share in it.

Use existing kiosk records to measure adoption. Where routine audits already record observation windows, compare valid kiosk entries with observed completed actions for the same ward, area, and time window. Use the ratio as an estimate of logging coverage. Similar totals can hide omissions and extra entries, so the infection prevention lead reviews a small sample of windows across shifts before interpreting the ratio. Use ward-level totals and collect no new identifiers. If existing audit records cannot support that comparison, treat kiosk totals as usage counts and leave coverage unestimated.

Before launch, test three cases. A repeat submission counts once when the kiosk can identify it as the same submission. A voided entry is removed from the usage count. Hygiene completed without a kiosk entry still counts in the observer audit. The kiosk owner corrects disputed entries and updates the ward total.

## What nurses see

The kiosk confirms the selected moment was saved and offers an undo. The existing ward huddle board shows aggregate kiosk usage, the latest audited compliance rate, and progress toward the ward target. Managers see ward totals only. Individual records stay out of performance reviews. A missed shift or week changes nothing about a nurse’s eligibility.

## Timing and ending

Run the improved kiosk and feedback on all three wards for four weeks before any rewards begin. Stagger eight-week reward periods by four weeks per ward, aligned to monthly audits. Use the stagger to see whether compliance changes when the reward starts, while noting changes in staffing or audit coverage. End the reward on schedule and keep the kiosk defaults and feedback in place.

At four and eight weeks after each ward’s reward ends, check **audited hand-hygiene compliance against its run-in baseline**. At eight weeks, keep the workflow if the ward still meets its original target and nurses can use it without disrupting care. If compliance is at or above baseline but below target, change the workflow with nurses and test again. If it is below baseline, end the incentive programme and investigate the workflow.

## What could go wrong

- **The kiosk gets in the way of care.** If an audit falls five percentage points or more below the ward’s baseline, the infection prevention lead pauses rewards and investigates with ward nurses before any payout.
- **Counts rise while hygiene stands still.** Investigate duplicates, delayed batches, and gaps between kiosk usage and audited behaviour. Keep the payout tied to the observer audit.
- **Some shifts are rarely observed.** Set minimum audit coverage using the hospital’s existing audit protocol before launch. If coverage falls short, defer the decision until the next routine audit supplies enough observations.
