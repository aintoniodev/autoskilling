## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** A ticket earns one credit when the agent links an existing article that accurately supports the resolution or writes a new article that passes the normal publishing review and is linked to the ticket. An internal article qualifies if it helps another agent solve the same problem. Reuse an existing article whenever it covers the case. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place.

**Launch message:** “Resolve the ticket, then link the article that helped or submit one that would help the next agent. If our team reaches 45% for two consecutive weeks during its four-week pilot and clears the quality check, everyone on the team earns one paid hour off. If there’s no useful article, close the ticket as usual.”

### Fix the workflow first

Give agents across shifts 30 minutes of paid time to work through recent tickets and identify what slows them down. Fix the biggest friction points before launch. Put article search in the closeout screen, make it easy to submit a draft from the resolution notes, and give article reviewers protected time and a two-business-day turnaround. Share what agents reported and what changed. Give every team these improvements and private feedback for two weeks before any rewards start.

### Count and show progress

- **Use existing ticket and article records.** Divide credited tickets by all distinct resolved tickets, excluding spam, test tickets, and merged duplicates under the same rules used for the baseline. Credit belongs to the canonical ticket, even if two agents worked it or several articles were linked. Attribute it to the week of resolution. A draft shows as pending and counts if published and linked within seven days. A reopened ticket loses its credit until it is resolved again. A removed link or withdrawn article voids the credit before payout. Verify that the reported 12% baseline uses the same definition.
- **Give agents immediate, private feedback.** Show “counted,” “pending review,” or “credit removed” with a reason. Give each agent a weekly goal based on their typical ticket volume, starting at roughly one useful link per three resolutions. Recalculate after leave or a low-volume week. The next ticket is a fresh opportunity, and personal goals don’t determine anyone’s pay.
- **Show team totals in the existing support huddle or channel.** Post the team’s credited-ticket rate, ticket volume, and progress toward 45%. Managers see team totals only. Keep individual counts out of rankings and performance reviews. The support operations lead corrects disputed counts. The support director announces the earned reward in the team huddle and makes paid time available across all shifts.

### Run it, then remove the reward

Start half the existing teams in week 3 and the rest in week 5. Give each team four reward weeks. Assign start dates before launch, balancing teams on baseline coverage and ticket mix where feasible. The two-week stagger gives an early read on what the reward adds to the workflow changes and feedback everyone already has.

Before each team finishes, make article suggestions the default in closeout, with a one-click skip. End rewards on schedule and keep search, review capacity, and feedback in place. Four weeks after each team’s rewards end, use its credited-ticket rate over the preceding two weeks as the decision metric. **Keep** the workflow at 40% or higher, **change** it at 25–39%, and **drop** the programme below 25%. Use the same counting rules and let the seven-day publication window close before reading the result.

### Catch problems early

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles.** Before launch, test a merged duplicate, a reopened ticket, a withdrawn article, and a valid resolution with no useful article. Each week, QA checks 20 randomly sampled credited tickets per active group through the existing review process. Pause the affected group’s reward if fewer than 90% pass for relevance and accuracy. The support director decides when to resume after the knowledge-base lead fixes the cause and a fresh sample passes.
- **Slower resolutions or pressure on CSAT.** Agents close tickets as soon as the customer’s issue is resolved, even when an article is pending. Review resolution time, reopen rate, and CSAT alongside response volume by team each week. Investigate a sustained decline by ticket type and shift before releasing the affected team’s reward.
- **Unequal opportunity.** Check credit rates and review delays by queue. If a team misses because its queue lacks article coverage or drafts are stuck in review, fix that bottleneck and give the team another full reward window.
