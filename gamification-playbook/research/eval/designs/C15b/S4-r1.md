## Reward a resolved ticket with a useful article

Give each support team four weeks to earn a **one-hour paid break per team member**, scheduled across shifts. The team earns it when at least **40% of eligible resolved tickets** have a qualifying article and the quality checks pass. Announce the break and the rule at launch. When a team earns it, have the support director thank the team in person and name an article that helped solve a recurring issue. Keep the existing CSAT bonus terms in place.

**Rule agents see:** “A resolved ticket counts once when you link a published article that helps solve the issue or write, publish, and link one that does.”

## Make the workflow fit the queue

Spend two weeks with agents from different shifts and queues. Give them protected paid time to try the workflow on real tickets. Ask where search fails, which issues need new articles, and how long drafting and publication take. Fix the biggest bottlenecks before rewards start and tell agents what changed in response to their feedback. Give teams paid writing time and assign an editor to clear drafts promptly.

Put article search and link suggestions directly in the resolution form for everyone. Let agents close promptly when no useful article exists, then add a link within two business days if an article becomes available. Keep those defaults after the programme ends.

## Count tickets using existing records

Use existing ticket IDs, resolution records, resolver assignments, and linked article IDs. Count every eligible resolved ticket in the denominator, including tickets closed through the old workflow. Exclude spam, tests, merged duplicates, and categories where policy bars a reusable article. A relevant existing article counts on each distinct ticket it helps resolve. Multiple links earn one credit per ticket. If a ticket reopens within two business days, reverse its credit and reassess it at final resolution. Test these cases before launch, including a generic link and an article still awaiting publication.

Show agents immediate confirmation when a ticket is provisionally counted and announce final credit after the two-business-day window. In their own view, show qualifying tickets out of eligible tickets and a personal goal of 4 in 10, scaled to actual volume. Agents returning from leave pick up with their next ticket. Put team totals and progress toward the break in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. Agents can flag a missing credit privately to Support Operations, which checks the existing record and corrects the count.

## Protect quality and CSAT

Each week, existing QA staff randomly sample credited tickets across queues and check that the article actually supports the resolution and follows privacy policy. Pause reward accrual for a team if **fewer than 18 of its latest 20 sampled tickets pass**. Also audit a small sample of uncredited tickets for improper exclusions or useful articles that search failed to surface. The support lead owns pause and restart decisions. Support Operations fixes counting errors, and the knowledge-base owner fixes article or search problems. Publish the team-level reason for a pause and the fix.

The existing CSAT bonus makes it especially important to check for rushed closures and customers being sent to an article instead of getting help. Review reopen rates and CSAT by queue alongside coverage. Pause rewards for investigation if team CSAT drops **5 percentage points or more** from its pre-programme baseline over a rolling two-week window with at least 30 responses. Use the same review to catch generic links, duplicate articles, and cherry-picking easy tickets.

## End the reward and measure what sticks

After the shared two-week setup, start half the teams on the reward and the other half two weeks later. Give each team the same four-week window. Compare coverage during the stagger to estimate what the reward adds to the shared fixes and feedback. Deliver earned breaks after the final quality check.

Use **quality-adjusted article coverage** as the decision metric: the share of eligible tickets credited multiplied by the QA pass rate. Measure it during the final reward period and again four weeks after each team’s rewards end. At **35% or above** after the break, keep the workflow and end the incentive. At **20% to under 35%**, fix the biggest remaining workflow barrier and remeasure without prizes. Below **20%**, drop the incentive and revisit where article linking or writing genuinely helps agents resolve tickets.
