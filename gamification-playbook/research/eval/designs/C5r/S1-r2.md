# Before the Job

Run a four-week team incentive for completing the digital safety checklist before work starts. A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a stop or escalation earns the same credit as one that clears the job.

### Set up with technicians and union reps

Bring together technicians from different shifts and routes, union reps, dispatch and safety. Give technicians paid time to try the checklist on real jobs. Fix the friction they identify, such as weak signal, repeated data entry or schedules that leave too little time for checks. Tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Agree on the scoring, data access, corrections and fallback with union reps before announcing the reward.

Put the prefilled checklist first when a technician opens an assigned job. Keep the approved fallback available for outages and devices that cannot sync. Give every crew the fixes, paid practice and feedback before any crew starts earning the reward.

### Reward and measurement

**Launch message:** “When our crew completes the app safety checklist before work starts on at least 75% of eligible jobs over four weeks, everyone on the crew earns a paid team lunch during their shift. A check that stops unsafe work counts.”

Use existing job and checklist records. Count one submission per job ID when the completed checklist is linked to the job and timestamped before the existing work-start event. Count a job stopped before work when the checklist precedes the recorded safety hold or escalation. Count an offline submission if the app preserves a reliable completion timestamp. Verify these records on real routes before launch. If they cannot establish the order reliably, fix the workflow before attaching a reward.

The denominator is all dispatched jobs that reach the point where a safety check is required, including jobs checked on paper during an outage. Report fallback use alongside the digital rate so a crew can see what affected its result. Exclude jobs cancelled before a check was required. Before launch, test a duplicate, a voided submission, a late completion, a paper fallback, an offline sync and a safety stop. For a voided submission, credit only a valid replacement completed before work starts or before the safety hold. If a verified outage materially affects a crew’s chance of earning the lunch, extend its earning window by the affected shifts and keep those jobs in the adoption measure.

### What people see

The app confirms a qualifying check as soon as the record can be verified. Each technician privately sees progress toward completing the checklist on three of their next four eligible jobs. The goal moves with their workload, and time off has no effect. At the usual crew huddle, share the team percentage, job count and progress toward lunch. Managers see team totals only. Programme data stays out of individual performance reviews.

### Timing and ending

Recalculate the reported 35% baseline using the agreed rule. After all crews have had two weeks with the improved workflow and feedback, start half the crews on the incentive and the rest two weeks later. Where scheduling allows, assign comparable crews to the two start dates at random. Each crew gets four earning weeks. Use the two-week overlap to estimate what the lunch adds to the improved workflow.

Hold the lunches during paid time, with equivalent sessions for different shifts. Keep the checklist first in the app when rewards end. Four weeks after each crew finishes, measure the share of eligible jobs with a valid digital pre-start checklist in the preceding two weeks. Agree on the decision rule before launch. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow and retire the prize. At **50–69%**, fix the most common remaining barrier and remeasure. **Below 50%**, drop the incentive approach and redesign how the required safety check fits into the job.

### What could go wrong

- **People rush through checks or delay recording the job start.** Each week, the programme coordinator samples 10 scored jobs across crews using existing records. Safety reviews whether the timing is credible and whether recorded hazards received the required follow-up. The safety lead pauses rewards if fewer than 9 of 10 pass, or immediately after a confirmed case of work proceeding despite a recorded stop condition.
- **Crews feel pressured to clear unsafe jobs.** Give safety stops full credit and give technicians a private route through their union rep or safety lead to flag pressure. The safety lead investigates and decides when rewards can resume after a pause.
- **A bad count costs a crew its lunch.** Technicians can flag the job ID privately. The coordinator corrects verified errors before closing the tally and reports the revised team total. Use an existing report, the weekly sample and the regular huddle to keep routine administration under an hour a week.
