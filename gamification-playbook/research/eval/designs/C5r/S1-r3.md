## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Make the app ready first.** Agree on the pilot with union representatives and technicians from different shifts and routes. Give technicians paid practice time on the phones they use in the field. Test weak signal, gloves, and urgent dispatches. Fix anything that makes the digital checklist harder to complete safely than the existing process, and tell crews what changed. Keep the approved fallback available from day one. A technician can stop or delay work for safety without losing credit.

**Reward a completed pre-job check.** The assigned technician earns one credit per job when they complete the required digital checklist before work starts. If the safety procedure requires each technician to complete a check, apply the same rule once per required technician per job. Announce the reward at launch. Anyone who completes the digital check on at least 80% of their eligible jobs during their four-week window earns one paid hour off, scheduled with normal coverage. Goals scale with actual assignments. Leave adds no missed jobs, a missed week resets nothing, and a technician with no eligible jobs gets a window when they return. The pilot owner confirms each award directly and privately.

**Use records the company already has.** Match the app’s completion record to the dispatch job ID and an independently recorded work-start time. Confirm those records are reliable before launching rewards. If the app already preserves the original completion time offline, use it when the record syncs. If timing cannot be verified, use the approved fallback and exclude the affected job from the technician’s reward calculation. An approved paper check during normal app availability satisfies the safety procedure and counts as a missed digital check. Track fallback use in aggregate so the pilot still shows where digital adoption is breaking down.

Before launch, walk through five cases with union reps and dispatch.

- A duplicate earns one credit.
- A check voided before work starts earns credit only if a valid replacement is completed before work starts.
- A valid check documenting a hazard that stops the job can earn credit through the existing safety-hold record.
- A canceled job with no work or safety hold is excluded.
- A paper check during a verified outage protects the technician’s reward eligibility and remains visible as fallback use in the company-wide results.

The pilot owner handles private disputes and corrects counts before awards are issued. The safety lead resolves questions about whether a check met the safety procedure.

**Show technicians their own progress.** After submission, the app confirms whether the check counted or is awaiting sync. Technicians can see their own tally, their goal, and how to dispute a count. Post team totals weekly in the usual crew briefing and combine small crews where a total could identify someone. Managers see team totals only. Keep individual pilot data out of performance reviews and discipline. There is no named leaderboard or team prize that coworkers can lose because someone uses the fallback.

**Run four reward weeks per team, then check what lasts.** Give everyone the fixes, paid practice, and feedback first. Put the checklist first on the job screen for all crews, with job details prefilled and safety answers blank. Measure two weeks with that setup, then randomly assign comparable teams to start rewards immediately or two weeks later. Compare adoption during those two weeks to see what the reward adds on top of the fixes. Each team’s reward ends after four weeks on the date announced at launch. Keep the checklist prominent and check adoption four weeks after each team’s rewards end.

Use one decision metric: the share of jobs requiring a check with a verified digital check completed before work starts. Include jobs handled through the fallback in this measure and compare it with the roughly 35% starting point using the same counting rule. At **80% or higher** four weeks after rewards end, keep the rollout as designed. At **60–79%**, fix the barriers crews report and measure again. Below **60%**, drop this incentive design and rework the digital rollout. The existing safety procedure applies throughout.

**Watch for rushed checks, bad counts, and pressure to work unsafely.** Each week the safety lead reviews 10 sampled checks against existing job and safety records for material omissions. Pause rewards if two or more sampled checks have a material omission, or immediately if a confirmed critical hazard was missed or someone started unsafe work under reward pressure. Honor awards already earned on valid checks. The safety lead and union representative agree on the fix before rewards resume. Use an automated weekly tally and the existing safety review so one pilot owner can run routine administration in under an hour a week.
