## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward a resolved ticket with a useful knowledge-base article.** The rule agents see is “Each eligible ticket counts once when you resolve it with an approved article that explains the fix.” Linking an existing article and writing a new one earn the same credit. If there’s a gap, agents can draft an article during paid work time. The ticket can close as soon as the customer’s issue is resolved, and credit is added when the article is approved and linked.

**Fix the workflow before launch.** In weeks 1–2, give agents from different shifts 30 minutes of protected time to walk through recent tickets. Fix the biggest obstacles they identify, such as poor search, missing articles for common issues, or slow approvals. In the ticket close flow, surface suggested articles and make it easy to link one or open a prefilled draft. Keep that workflow in place after the pilot. Tell agents which fixes came from their feedback. If linking routinely delays resolution or approvals pile up, fix that before starting rewards.

**Use existing ticket and article records.** The denominator is unique resolved tickets, with exclusions defined upfront using existing dispositions for spam, merged duplicates, and tickets with no support issue. The numerator is those tickets with a linked, approved article. A ticket resolved the old way stays in the denominator. Multiple links earn one credit, merged tickets count under the surviving ticket, and reopened tickets leave the count until resolved again. Before launch, test those cases plus a draft approved after closure. Recalculate the reported 12% baseline using the same definition.

**Show progress where agents already work.** When a ticket qualifies, show private confirmation in the ticket workflow. Each agent sees their own progress toward linking an article on half of their eligible resolutions during their four-week reward window. The goal scales with actual ticket volume and has no weekly reset. Post team totals in the existing support channel. Managers get team aggregates only, and programme counts stay out of performance reviews and CSAT bonus calculations.

**Give each team four weeks to earn a paid hour off.** Confirm with agents during setup that they value the reward and arrange shift coverage before announcing it. At launch, announce that everyone on a team earns one paid hour off if the team reaches 50% across its four-week window and clears the quality check. The support lead thanks the team in its huddle and schedules the hour with each agent. Randomly assign existing teams to start in week 3 or week 5. Everyone gets the workflow fixes and feedback in week 1–2, so weeks 3–4 give an early read on what the reward adds.

**Check for rushed or irrelevant links.** The knowledge-base owner reviews eight randomly sampled credited tickets per week across the teams. An article passes if it matches the issue and accurately explains the resolution. If two or more sampled tickets from a team fail in a week, the support lead pauses that team’s payout and resumes it after a clean follow-up sample. The knowledge-base owner corrects invalid links and disputed counts. Watch team reopen rates and CSAT alongside the sample. If either worsens, check whether agents are closing early, delaying replies, or avoiding difficult tickets. Stop rewards on any team where that pattern is confirmed. Use an existing report and cap routine administration at one hour a week.

**End rewards on schedule and measure what sticks.** Reward windows close in weeks 6 and 8. The decision metric is the share of eligible resolutions with an approved article, measured in the third and fourth weeks after each team’s reward ends. Set the decision bands before launch. At 40% or higher, keep the close-flow default and feedback. At 25–39%, fix the most common remaining workflow obstacle. Below 25%, retire the incentive and revisit why agents skip the knowledge base. Use the staggered start to judge whether the prize added anything before funding another round.
