## One working day for a useful first review

**Give every ready pull request a useful first review within eight working hours.** The current median is 2.6 days. Before launch, use the last four weeks of pull requests to calculate the percentage that already meet the eight-hour target.

### Fix the queue first

Ask five engineers across the team what delays reviews. Use their answers to fix routing and protect time for reviews. Give each squad a rotating reviewer and backup, with two 30-minute review blocks on their duty day. Put the oldest ready pull requests first in the queue engineers already use, and route each one automatically when it becomes ready. Run this setup for two weeks before introducing the reward. At launch, tell engineers what they reported and what changed.

### What earns the reward

If at least **85% of eligible pull requests** get a useful first review within eight working hours during the four-week reward period, everyone on the team gets a paid half-day off, scheduled around coverage. Announce the half-day at launch and have the engineering lead confirm it in person when earned. Each new pull request is another chance to help the team reach the target.

Use the events already recorded by the code host. Put the rule in the launch message:

> Every pull request counts once when someone other than its author submits an approval or a review with a concrete issue or next step within eight working hours of the pull request first becoming ready.

Use each squad’s published coverage hours, excluding weekends and team holidays. The clock starts when the pull request becomes ready, including when routing fails. Drafts enter the measure when marked ready. Include pull requests that miss the deadline even if they are still waiting. Reassignments, duplicate reviews, reopened pull requests, and moves back to draft give no extra credit or fresh clock. A dismissed review loses credit unless another qualifying review met the original deadline. Reviews given through the old workflow count if the qualifying response is recorded on the pull request in time.

### What engineers see

After a qualifying review, send the reviewer a private confirmation in the existing review notification channel. Engineers can see their own on-time reviews against the requests routed to them while on duty. Their personal goal is to answer the requests they can handle in their protected blocks and hand the rest to the backup before the deadline.

Post the team’s weekly percentage and eligible volume in its existing engineering channel. Managers see those team totals and have no individual drill-down. Keep programme data out of performance reviews. The engineering lead owns the rules. The queue owner fixes disputed counts and records the reason for each correction.

### Timing, ending, and failure modes

After the two-week run-in, start the four-week reward window two weeks apart across existing squads. Each squad knows its start date. Pool results from each squad’s four active weeks for the team reward. Compare squads during the stagger to see what changed when the reward started. Before prizes end, make automatic routing, oldest-first ordering, and protected review duty the default.

- **Rubber stamps.** Have a rotating senior reviewer spot-check five credited reviews a week against the diffs, including approvals. Pause reward credit if two or more lack evidence of a meaningful review. The engineering lead decides when credit resumes. The queue owner corrects invalid counts.
- **Easy changes crowd out hard ones.** Keep large pull requests in the measure. A reviewer can flag a specific risk or request a concrete change before completing a deeper review. Check the oldest unanswered items weekly for stalled handoffs or repeated moves back to draft.
- **Review duty overloads the same people.** Rotate duty, use the backup for absences and spikes, and treat a backlog that exceeds the protected blocks as a capacity problem. Keep weekly reporting and sampling under an hour.

Four weeks after the last reward window closes, use the **same on-time review rate** to decide the outcome. At 85% or higher, keep the workflow and retire the prize. If the rate is above the two-week run-in but below 85%, fix the remaining queue bottleneck and recheck after four weeks. If it falls to the run-in rate or lower, drop the reward and keep the workflow improvements.
