## Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets

Each agent earns **90 minutes of paid time off** by attaching a useful knowledge-base article to at least **40% of their eligible resolved tickets** during a four-week reward window. The goal scales to actual ticket volume. An agent who resolves 25 eligible tickets needs 10 qualifying tickets. A missed week leaves the agent free to earn the reward on their next case. Announce the reward and counting rule at launch, and have the support lead hand over the reward in person with specific thanks. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place.

## What counts

**Launch rule.** A resolved ticket earns one credit when it contains a published article that explains the fix or next steps, whether the agent linked an existing article or wrote a new one.

Use the existing ticket resolution status, ticket ID, resolver and article link, plus the article’s publication status. Count each ticket once, even if it has several links. A reopened ticket loses its credit until it is resolved again. A draft earns credit once published if the ticket is still resolved. A ticket closed through the old workflow still enters the denominator and earns credit if it has a qualifying link. Exclude spam, merged duplicates and approved privacy exceptions using existing ticket dispositions. A gap in the knowledge base is eligible work. Give agents paid time to write the missing article without copying customer data into it.

Before launch, the support lead tests the rule on a duplicate, a reopened ticket, a draft and a ticket closed through the old workflow. Agents can flag a wrong count privately through the existing support-ops channel. The support lead decides disputed cases, and the support-ops owner corrects the tally.

## What people see

After resolution, give the agent private confirmation that the ticket counted, or show what is pending. Show each agent their own qualifying count and eligible total in the ticketing tool. Put the team’s aggregate coverage in its existing weekly channel. Managers see team totals only. Support Ops handles individual reward fulfillment privately, and individual counts stay out of performance reviews. There is no named leaderboard.

## Rollout and ending

Spend two weeks with a handful of agents across queues and shifts finding where lookup, linking or publishing slows them down. Give everyone paid practice time, publish the fixes agents asked for, and put article search and linking directly in the resolution flow for every team. Turn on private feedback for everyone at the same time. Recalculate the reported **12%** baseline using the programme’s eligibility rules so the comparison uses the same definition.

Start the four-week reward window for half the teams in week 3 and the rest in week 5. Compare the change in coverage during weeks 3 and 4, when all teams have the workflow fixes and only the first group can earn rewards. Ramp the reward down by ending it on the announced dates and leaving article search, linking and private feedback in the normal workflow.

The decision metric is **the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying link**. Measure it in the fourth week after the last reward window closes. At **30% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback. From **20% to under 30%**, fix the most common remaining obstacle and recheck. Below **20%**, retire the campaign and revisit where article linking helps agents resolve cases. These are pilot thresholds.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles.** Support Ops samples 12 credited tickets across queues each week, checking whether the article fits the case and adds useful guidance. If **3 or more fail**, the support lead pauses reward accrual, corrects the affected credits and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Earned rewards are honored.
- **Slower replies or premature closure.** Review existing team CSAT and reopen rates alongside the sample. If either worsens for two consecutive weeks, the support lead checks whether article work is delaying replies or driving early closes and fixes the workflow before continuing rewards.
- **Unequal opportunity.** Use each agent’s own eligible volume, provide paid writing time across shifts, and make sure every queue has access to an article reviewer before its reward window starts.

Support Ops owns the weekly sample, tally and corrections, with a target of **under one hour per week** using existing reports.
