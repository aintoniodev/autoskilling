## Six-week safety checklist pilot

**Reward.** Each crew earns one hour of paid personal time per technician when at least 70% of its eligible jobs have a valid pre-job checklist during its six-week reward window. Everyone on the crew earns it, including apprentices, technicians on leave, and people with few assignments. Announce the reward at launch. At the next toolbox talk after a crew qualifies, the field-service lead thanks the crew and confirms how to book the hour. Agree on booking, coverage, and counting rules with union reps before launch.

**Make it workable first.** Spend two weeks with technicians and stewards from different shifts and routes. Give technicians paid time to try the checklist on their work phones, including at sites with weak signal. Fix access and offline problems, open the checklist directly from the assigned job, and prefill details the dispatch system already knows. Keep time for the safety check in the job schedule. At the toolbox talk, show technicians what changed in response to their feedback. Begin rewards once crews can reliably complete the check in the field.

**Counting rule.** “Each eligible job counts once when the required safety checklist is completed before work starts or records a safety stop before work starts.” Match the app’s existing completion record to the dispatch job ID and existing start or safety-stop record. Count a shared job once for the assigned crew. Exclude jobs cancelled before anyone attends. Keep jobs completed using the approved paper fallback in the denominator and give them reward credit during a verified outage. Track digital completion separately for the pilot decision, with paper jobs still in that denominator.

Before launch, the safety coordinator and a union steward test duplicate submissions, a voided checklist, a late submission, a safety stop, a paper fallback, and an offline checklist synced later. Accept a later sync if the existing record establishes that the check happened on time. If the systems cannot establish that order reliably, resolve it before starting rewards. Technicians can dispute a count privately through the steward or coordinator. The coordinator owns corrections and resolves them before rewards are issued.

**What people see.** After submission, the app confirms the job and shows whether the checklist counted or is awaiting sync. Each technician can see their own progress privately, with a goal scaled to their eligible jobs. For example, the target is three of four jobs or seven of ten. A missed week leaves progress intact. The existing toolbox board shows each crew its own aggregate progress toward 70%. Managers get crew totals only. The union agreement bars use of incentive records in performance reviews or discipline.

**What could go wrong.** A reward can encourage rushed checks, premature start records, or pressure to proceed when a job is unsafe. The safety coordinator uses the existing safety review process to spot-check ten randomly selected credited jobs per week for timing, missing items, and hazards that were recorded but left unresolved. Two material failures in the latest 20 checks, or one instance of work proceeding despite a recorded stop, pause reward credit for the affected crew while the coordinator and steward investigate. Preserve credit already earned on valid jobs. Use existing records and keep the weekly tally, sample, and corrections within one hour of coordinator time.

**Timing and ending.** Give every crew the app fixes and private feedback first. Then start half the crews on rewards and the rest three weeks later, grouping crews with similar job volumes and baseline completion before assigning start dates. Compare the groups during those three weeks to see what the reward adds. Each crew gets six weeks and a published end date. Before its reward ends, make the prefilled checklist the first step when opening a job and keep the completion confirmation in place.

Use one decision metric four weeks after each crew’s rewards end: the share of eligible jobs with a valid digital checklist completed before work starts or at a safety stop. Verify the reported 35% baseline using the same rule. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback with rewards ended. At **50–69%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **50%**, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The safety check remains required throughout.
