## Reward audited hand hygiene

Each ward can earn a hospital-funded meal offered across shifts after its two monthly incentive audits. Nurses choose the reward before launch. Announce it in the launch message and have the ward leader deliver it in person, with specific thanks for a workflow improvement staff made.

**Launch rule:** “Your ward earns a meal across shifts if it meets the hospital’s hand-hygiene target or improves by at least five percentage points from baseline across its two incentive audits, with no drop in either audit below the ward’s baseline.”

Everyone on the ward shares in the reward, including staff who missed shifts. Kiosk entries carry no points and there are no individual targets. Staff can pick up logging on their next eligible opportunity after an absence.

## Make logging easy before the reward starts

Give a few nurses from each ward, including night-shift and float staff, protected time to try the kiosk during real work. Ask where logging interrupts care, creates a queue, or duplicates another step. Have infection prevention approve where the kiosk sits and when it can be used. Fix the problems staff raise, show them what changed, and practise during paid time. Make the logging screen the kiosk’s default, with the ward prefilled. If care takes priority or the kiosk is unavailable, nurses move on and leave the entry missing.

After a save, the kiosk confirms **“Moment recorded.”** Staff see their own confirmation. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s latest audited compliance, sample size, and progress toward the meal. Managers see ward aggregates. Programme data stays out of performance reviews.

## Measure compliance and kiosk uptake

Use the existing monthly observer audits. Compliance is observed compliant opportunities divided by all observed eligible opportunities. Pool the counts from the two audits before the incentive starts for the ward’s baseline and from its two incentive audits for the reward decision. Infection prevention sets the minimum usable sample before launch and keeps observer scheduling independent of the contest. If a sample is too small, defer the decision until the next usable audit. If an incentive audit falls below baseline, pause reward decisions on that ward while infection prevention reviews the result. Handle clinical findings through the hospital’s existing process.

Use existing ward-level kiosk totals and outage records to see whether nurses are using the kiosk and where they need help. Those totals measure submissions. Before launch, test a double tap, a corrected entry, a kiosk outage, and a moment completed without logging. A retry should save once, a correction should count once, and unlogged hygiene still counts in the audit when observed. The kiosk owner fixes submission errors. The infection prevention lead resolves audit disputes and decides whether to resume rewards. The programme owner updates the huddle boards and reviews exceptions in under an hour a week using the existing reports.

## Run two audit cycles per ward, then stop

Start the kiosk fixes and feedback on all three wards together. After two monthly audits under the improved workflow, draw the ward start order and introduce the reward one audit cycle apart. Each ward gets two monthly incentive audits. Compare changes on wards already eligible for the reward with changes on wards still waiting. With three wards and monthly samples, use the comparison to guide a decision and expect some uncertainty.

After each ward’s second incentive audit, end the prize and leave the kiosk default, paid onboarding, and ward feedback in place. Check **audited compliance percentage** again at the first and second monthly audits after the prize ends. Keep the routine if the ward stays at or above its baseline. If compliance falls, investigate and change the workflow. Drop the incentive from future rollouts if eligible wards show no clearer improvement than waiting wards, or if their gains disappear after prizes stop.

The main failure modes are logging more while hygiene stays flat, better behaviour only when observers are present, and queues or pressure to log during care. Watch for rising submissions alongside flat audits, keep the existing audit schedule independent, and use huddles to surface workflow problems. If kiosk use interferes with care, suspend logging on that ward until the workflow is fixed.
