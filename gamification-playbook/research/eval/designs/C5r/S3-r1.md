# Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for verified hand hygiene.** Announce at launch that each ward can earn a catered meal on every shift, with an in-person thank-you from the chief nursing officer, by reaching its own target across two monthly audits. Set the target from the ward’s two most recent comparable audits before launch. Aim for a five-percentage-point improvement, capped at the hospital’s existing target. Wards already at or above that target earn the meal by meeting it. Every ward can qualify, and the whole team shares the reward, including staff who were on leave.

**Make the kiosk useful before launch.** Give day- and night-shift nurses from each ward 15 minutes of protected time to try logging during a routine round and flag where it interrupts care. Fix the bottlenecks first. Put the kiosk on an existing route, prefill the ward, open directly to the moment selector and show a clear save confirmation with an undo option. Clean hands at the required moment and log once when care permits. If urgent care or a kiosk outage prevents logging, resume at the next safe opportunity. Use anonymous ward-level logging patterns and staff feedback to identify where the workflow needs fixing, and post the fixes in the existing shift huddle.

**Use the monthly observer audit to determine eligibility.** Divide correctly performed hand-hygiene actions by observed opportunities for nurses, pooling the two reward months by adding their numerators and denominators. Each observed opportunity counts once. A missed action stays in the denominator, an observed action counts even if the kiosk is down, and duplicate, undone or fabricated kiosk entries have zero effect on the reward. Kiosk totals show recorded use. The monthly audit samples a different set of events, so dividing kiosk entries by audited opportunities would yield a misleading rate. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**Show progress in the shift huddle.** After each audit, post the ward’s result, sample size and target on its existing huddle board. Managers see ward totals only. Collect no staff or patient identifiers for the programme and exclude the data from performance reviews. At the kiosk, the nurse sees an immediate confirmation such as “After patient contact saved.” There are no individual quotas, rankings or streaks.

**Stagger the reward and close it on schedule.** Give all three wards the workflow fixes and feedback during a shared one-month run-in. Draw the start order. Rewards cover months 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4 respectively. Before each ward’s final reward month ends, make quick logging the kiosk’s default screen and keep the huddle feedback in place. Recheck audited compliance one month after each ward’s reward ends. Use that single measure to decide whether to repeat the incentive elsewhere. Repeat only if compliance rose when rewards started and held after they ended. Revise if the gain faded and drop the reward if there was no discernible lift. Three wards provide a directional comparison. If the audit sample is inconclusive, use another routine audit before deciding.

**Pause for unsafe workflow or unreliable counts.** Nurses may clean their hands more reliably when an observer is present, and shifts with different workloads can yield different audit results. Keep the audit schedule independent of ward management and use comparable shift coverage across periods. Before launch, Infection Prevention sets a minimum sample size and required shift coverage using the existing audit schedule. It spot-checks 10 audit entries before each payout and holds the decision if coverage falls short or two or more entries are miscounted. One confirmed care delay caused by logging pauses the kiosk pilot on that ward until the issue is fixed. Infection Prevention owns pause and restart decisions, corrects audit totals, and routes kiosk faults to the kiosk owner. Outside the routine audits, one coordinator can review issues and update the huddle boards in under an hour a week.
