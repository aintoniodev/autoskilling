# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Give each support team a four-week window to earn **one hour of paid time off for everyone on the team** by recording a useful knowledge-base article on **at least 35% of resolved tickets**. Agents can take the hour in staggered slots with queue coverage. Name the reward, target, and dates at launch. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility in place, and give agents permission to resolve the customer’s issue first.

### Before rewards start

Ask six agents across shifts to walk through recent tickets where they skipped the knowledge base. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice time. Fix the biggest friction points before starting the reward clock. For example, if finding the right article means leaving the ticket, put article search and suggestions in the resolution screen. Show the team what changed based on their feedback. If useful linking still adds substantial handling time or creates customer risk, delay the reward window and fix the workflow.

### What earns credit

**One resolved ticket earns one credit when its existing ticket record links to a published article that directly helps resolve that issue.** An existing article and a newly written article earn the same credit. A new article counts once it is published and linked within seven days of resolution. Agents can close the ticket as soon as the customer’s issue is resolved. If no useful article exists, they can close without a link.

Use existing ticket, link, and publication records. The denominator is all resolved tickets in the team’s reward window. Exclude test tickets and duplicate records using existing ticket statuses. Multiple links earn one credit. A reopened ticket counts once, at its final resolution, with any earlier provisional credit reversed. A genuine resolution completed through the old workflow stays in the denominator and earns credit if a qualifying link is recorded within seven days. Test those cases and a late publication before launch. The support operations lead owns the count, handles private corrections, and settles the team result after the seven-day window.

### What people see

Show agents prompt confirmation when a link is recorded, with a pending status for a new article awaiting publication. Each agent privately sees their count and a personal goal of roughly one qualifying ticket per three resolutions, scaled to their actual volume. Put the team’s aggregate rate and reward progress in the channel or dashboard it already uses. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. Progress accumulates across the four weeks, so a missed shift or week leaves the next ticket as the next chance to contribute.

### Timing and decision

Give all teams the workflow fixes and feedback for two weeks, then randomly assign existing teams to start rewards in week 3 or week 5. Each team gets four weeks. Compare the teams during the two weeks when only the first group has rewards to estimate what the reward adds. Make article suggestions the default in the resolution screen before the first group’s rewards end. Keep the workflow and private confirmation after the prizes stop.

Use the **share of resolved tickets with a qualifying article link** as the decision metric. Recheck it in weeks 3 and 4 after each team’s reward ends. Keep the workflow as standard at **30% or higher**. At **20% to under 30%**, fix the remaining friction and remeasure. Below **20%**, drop the incentive design and revisit why agents skip the tool. Compare with the reported 12% starting point after recalculating it under the same counting rule.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles inflate the rate.** The support QA lead reviews 10 credited tickets across active teams each week through the existing QA process. If two or more fail the relevance check, or any article exposes customer data, the QA lead pauses reward credit for the affected team while support operations fixes the issue and corrects the count.
- **Agents delay resolution or feel torn between the pilot and their CSAT bonus.** Review existing team CSAT and time-to-resolution alongside the link rate. If either worsens, the support operations lead checks ticket mix and sample size and pauses the affected team’s reward window if QA finds that chasing credit delayed or harmed customer resolutions.
- **Different queues have different opportunities to link.** Check the 35% milestone against each team’s ticket mix before launch. If it is unreachable for a team because of its case mix, set and announce a reachable milestone for that team before rewards begin. Keep the same measurement rule across teams.

Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, checking team totals, and correcting counts.
