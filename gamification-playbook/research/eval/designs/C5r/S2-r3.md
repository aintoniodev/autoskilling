# One-working-day code reviews

**Give engineers a faster, useful first review.** The current median is 2.6 days. Aim for 80% of review-ready PRs to receive a substantive first review within one working day. Before launch, spend 30 minutes with engineers from both sides of the queue. Look at recent slow PRs, agree on what a useful first review looks like, and fix the biggest routing problem. Assign a rotating primary reviewer and backup for each area, with 30 minutes of protected review time each working day.

**Reward the team for reviews that move real work forward.** Each pilot group earns one paid hour off per engineer if at least 80% of its eligible PRs receive a substantive review within one working day during its four-week reward window and it clears the quality check. Name the reward at launch. The engineering lead announces it in the regular team meeting and thanks the group for a specific improvement. Every engineer in an earning group gets the hour, including people who were away during the pilot.

**Measure it from the existing PR record.** Count each non-draft PR once, starting when it is ready for review and a review is requested. Use eight working hours on a published team calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. Route requests when PRs become ready and audit ready PRs with no request so routing delays stay visible. Count the first submitted human review from someone other than the author that explains an approval, identifies a concrete issue, or asks a specific question the author can act on. Use existing review events to calculate a provisional rate and sample the content for quality.

Before launch, test the rule on a few real PRs. A duplicate review earns no extra credit. A PR sent back to draft or reopened keeps its original clock. A chat-only answer earns credit when the reviewer records it on the PR, using the PR timestamp. An eligible PR with no qualifying review by the deadline is a miss, even if it merges. The rotation owner checks the underlying PR record and corrects disputed counts.

**Show progress in the tools engineers already use.** On a submitted review, privately confirm whether it met the timing target. Give each reviewer a private goal based on the work assigned to them, such as answering the next three assigned requests on time. A missed review leaves the next one available. Post the weekly group rate and denominator in the existing engineering channel. Managers receive group totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews, and there is no named leaderboard.

**Run for eight weeks, then check whether the habit holds.** In weeks 1–2, give everyone the routing fixes, protected time, and feedback. Calculate the baseline using the same rule as the pilot. Split the team along existing review areas with roughly comparable PR volume and randomly choose which group starts first. Group A can earn the reward in weeks 3–6 and Group B in weeks 5–8. Compare their changes from baseline in weeks 3–4, when only A has the reward. Treat that comparison as directional if volume is low or reviewers work across both groups.

Before week 8 ends, make the current primary and backup the default review route and put the oldest unanswered requests first in the shared queue. End prizes in week 8. Use the same on-time rate across weeks 9–12 to decide what stays. At 80% or higher, keep the workflow and retire the prize. Above baseline but below 80%, adjust coverage where requests wait longest. At or below baseline, drop the incentive and diagnose the remaining bottleneck.

**Watch for rushed approvals, cherry-picking, and overload.** The rotation owner samples five PRs per group each week, including complex or late cases. If two of five credited reviews in a group are superficial, the engineering lead pauses that group’s reward and decides when it resumes after the owner corrects the affected counts and addresses the cause. Oldest-first routing and a backup keep difficult reviews visible when the primary is busy or out. Budget 45 minutes a week for one rotation owner to sample reviews, resolve disputes, and post the totals.
