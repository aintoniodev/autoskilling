# One-working-day code reviews

Give every review-ready pull request a useful first review within one working day. The current median is 2.6 days. Run the pilot through the existing review queue, with a team reward and private feedback for reviewers.

### Fix the queue first

In weeks 1–2, give a few engineers paid time to walk through recent slow reviews, including a large PR and one that sat through an absence. Fix the main sources of delay, then tell the team what changed based on their feedback. Give each workday a primary reviewer and a backup, protect a review slot in their calendars, and put the oldest waiting PRs first in the queue engineers already use. Start the reward once the queue and coverage work.

### What earns credit

**A PR counts once when someone other than its author submits a substantive approval or change request within one working day of the PR becoming ready for review.**

An approval means the reviewer checked the change. A change request identifies a concrete issue and gives the author a next step. Acknowledgements and assignment changes earn zero credit. The first qualifying review counts. Review edits and routine reopens create zero additional credit. An approval withdrawn because the review missed a material issue loses credit.

Use existing pull request readiness and submitted-review events. One working day is eight hours on the owning team’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Draft PRs enter the queue when marked ready. Reassignment and a return to draft leave the original deadline in place. Count every eligible PR whose deadline has passed, including unanswered PRs. Exclude verified duplicates and PRs genuinely withdrawn before the deadline. The pilot owner records exclusion reasons and handles private correction requests.

### What engineers see and earn

After a qualifying review, the reviewer gets a short private confirmation in the existing review notification. Engineers can see their own on-time count and use a private goal of four out of every five eligible reviews they cover. The goal carries across weeks, so low volume and time away create zero catch-up debt. Show team totals in the existing engineering channel once a week. Managers get the same aggregate view, with individual counts kept out of performance reviews.

Announce the reward at launch. If at least 80% of eligible PRs receive an on-time review during the four-week reward window and the quality check is clear, the engineering lead hosts a team lunch during paid hours, with a meal allowance for remote staff. The reward covers the whole team.

### Timing and ending

Everyone gets queue fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. If the team already has two review queues with mostly separate reviewer pools, start their four-week reward windows in weeks 3 and 5. Each queue can earn the lunch on the same terms. Compare their on-time rates during the two weeks when only the first queue has a reward. If reviewers share one queue, run one four-week window from week 3 and compare it with the fixes-and-feedback period. Treat that comparison as directional.

Before the reward ends, make oldest-first ordering and backup coverage the defaults. Four weeks after the last reward window closes, use the **share of eligible PRs receiving a substantive first review within one working day** to decide what to do. At 80% or higher, keep the workflow. At 65–79%, fix the biggest remaining delay and recheck in four weeks. Below 65%, end the incentive experiment and revisit review capacity and routing.

### What could go wrong

- **Rushed approvals or token change requests.** Before launch, apply the rule to a duplicate, a withdrawn approval, a complex PR, and a review completed in chat. The pilot owner samples five credited reviews per week. Pause the reward if two of the latest ten sampled reviews are superficial or miss a material issue visible at the time. The engineering lead decides when to resume, and the pilot owner corrects affected counts.
- **Easy PRs get picked first.** Keep complex PRs in the denominator and route stalled ones to the backup or a subject expert. Check whether late reviews cluster around one area, oversized changes, or missing coverage.
- **The team sees it as surveillance or extra work.** Use existing PR events, keep individual feedback private, and preserve paid review time. One pilot owner can run the weekly report, sample, and corrections in under an hour.
