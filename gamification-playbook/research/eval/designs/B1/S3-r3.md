# Clean Hands, On Record: an eight-week pilot across three wards

**Reward verified hand hygiene, not kiosk traffic.** Award one provisional **ward credit** when an independent observer sees hand hygiene performed correctly at an eligible moment and the action matches one valid kiosk record. One action earns at most one credit, even if several indications apply. Unobserved records still count as documentation, but earn no credit. No points for clicks, logins, or self-reported volume. Nurses clean their hands when indicated and log when care safely allows, by the end of the shift. They can record a missed moment without losing a streak; it earns no credit.

## Measure two things separately

- **Clinical compliance:** observed hand-hygiene actions performed correctly ÷ all observed opportunities, including misses. Infection Prevention owns this measure; kiosk data never changes the denominator. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)
- **Kiosk adoption:** correctly performed actions with a valid, on-time kiosk match ÷ correctly performed actions in the same audit sample. Unmatched actions stay in the denominator. The finish line is **80%**; wards starting below 60% get an interim milestone of **10 percentage points above baseline**, not a lower clinical standard.

Use a prelaunch audit with the kiosk available for baseline, then the existing monthly audits around weeks 4 and 8. Sample across day, night, and weekend shifts without announcing the observation windows in advance. Observers submit their records before seeing kiosk data. An authorized reviewer matches on ward, pseudonymous nurse ID, moment type, and recorded event time within five minutes. Ambiguous matches get no credit. Collect no patient identifiers. If confidential matching cannot be approved, run a feedback-only pilot without credits. Show sample sizes; fewer than 20 observed, correctly performed actions per ward per round means **insufficient evidence** for an adoption award, not failure.

## What people see

Nurses get a private “record saved” acknowledgment and a weekly streak for closing at least **80% of their eligible patient-care shifts** on time. Closing means reviewing the shift’s entries and confirming they reflect what happened, including any known misses. This is a documentation habit, not proof of compliance. Use the roster as the denominator, not a fixed event quota. Freeze weeks with no eligible shifts. A blocker reported before using the fallback excuses the affected shift; allow retrospective reporting when urgent care prevents it. Publish what was fixed each week.

The ward board shows progress toward its adoption target, the latest audit result and sample size, and new provisional credits as they clear matching. No individual leaderboard: the sponsor signs off on that at launch. Nurses can earn private badges for first use, repeat use, on-time logging, reporting a blocker that gets fixed, and coaching a peer. At launch, tell staff that ward managers can see everyone’s logging activity in their own ward for pilot support, not performance appraisal. No names or shift-level breakdowns on the ward board.

## Finish and safeguards

Put dates, eligibility, matching rules, thresholds, exceptions, visibility, recognition, and retention in **one signed pilot policy**. Recompute nightly from the kiosk event log, roster, and locked audit records. Stop earning at week 8. Over the next 10 business days, Infection Prevention reviews duplicates, false entries, and disputed matches, reverses invalid credits with a private reason, and gives staff a correction window. Recognize qualifying wards at a huddle, with no cash or volume prize. Nurses with **six qualifying weeks** graduate privately; then retire the credits, badges, and streaks. Keep the kiosk only if adoption is useful and the safety review supports it. Monthly clinical audits continue either way.

**Pause recognition** if kiosk activity rises while audited compliance falls five percentage points from baseline, or if duplicate and correction rates exceed 10% in a week. Investigate any reported care delay immediately. During an outage or urgent care, hygiene and patient care come first; use the approved fallback without penalty. A small or uneven audit sample is a reason to gather better evidence, not to blame a ward or declare success.

## Key Learnings:

1. A kiosk record shows that someone logged an action. Only the observer audit measures clinical compliance.
2. Rewarding matched actions at ward level and tracking documentation privately avoids turning unequal workloads and sparse audits into individual rankings.
