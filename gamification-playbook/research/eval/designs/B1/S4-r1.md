# Eight-week knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward a useful answer, not a link.** Award one provisional credit per resolved customer ticket when the closing reply uses a relevant, approved knowledge-base article. Agents can reuse an existing article or write or improve one, get it approved, and use it in the reply. The article must support the actual fix or next step, and any link shared must be accessible to the customer. Drafts, generic links, logins, and article views earn nothing. Reopening, transferring, or merging a ticket never creates another credit. Leave the existing CSAT bonus unchanged. The pilot offers recognition and a team learning session, not cash or a prize for first place.

**Put the rules in one launch policy.** Support Ops owns a single versioned policy with the launch and end dates, timezone, eligibility, attribution, targets, workload brackets, visibility, rewards, quality triggers, and appeal deadlines. Before launch, remeasure the reported 12% using the same rules as the pilot. The denominator is all genuine customer tickets resolved by the agent; exclude only predefined categories such as spam, duplicates, and automated closures. A missing article is a coverage gap, not an exclusion. Attribute each ticket to the agent who sent the qualifying resolution. Recompute nightly from ticket events, the sent reply, article approval history, and QA decisions.

**Measure consistency relative to workload.** The end-state expectation is a useful article on every genuine resolution. For the pilot, an on-time week means qualifying on at least 40% of your resolved tickets in weeks 1–2, 55% in weeks 3–4, and 70% in weeks 5–8, with the required count rounded up. Zero-ticket weeks freeze the streak. Reporting a genuine blocker before sending a fallback answer excuses an otherwise missed week if Support Ops confirms it prevented the agent from hitting the target. The ticket stays in the team denominator. Agents should never delay a customer’s resolution for a credit. Publish a weekly list of what got fixed, with owners and dates for unresolved gaps.

**Show progress without turning it into a public performance review.** Each provisional credit gets a visible confirmation in the ticket and on the agent’s private dashboard. Agents see their credited tickets, next milestone, streak, and any reversals with reasons. Recognize first use, repeat use, regular use, on-time weeks, use across issue types, fixing a confirmed blocker, and coaching a peer whose next ticket qualifies. At launch, say explicitly that managers can see everyone’s activity, including agents who hide their public card. The sponsor can leave rankings off; if enabled, show only the top five opted-in agents by qualifying rate within baseline workload brackets (1–10, 11–25, and 26+ resolved tickets per working week). Hide brackets with fewer than 10 opt-ins and let anyone hide their card at any time.

**Give the team a finish line.** Aim for 65% qualifying resolutions org-wide across weeks 7–8, with team targets of 50% for teams starting below 15% and 70% for the rest. If the org meets its target and clears the quality check, give everyone a protected hour to share fixes and improve articles. Give agents a separate protected writing slot during the pilot so documentation doesn’t compete with customer response time.

**Watch for worse support with better scores.** QA samples 30 credited tickets weekly across queues and agents, checking relevance, accuracy, customer access, and whether the reply still answers the question. Also track seven-day reopens, corrections or rework, CSAT and survey response volume, and changes in closure or exclusion rates by queue. If credits rise while reopens or rework increase by at least 3 percentage points over the prelaunch baseline for two consecutive weeks, pause public rankings and team reward confirmation while QA investigates. Reverse invalid credits with a reason. Watch for irrelevant links, premature closes, cherry-picking, duplicate articles, and sensitive details copied into articles; route coverage gaps to the KB owner rather than pressuring agents to attach something.

**Retire the programme.** Stop earning credits at the end of week 8. Allow seven days for late reopens and QA checks, then give agents three business days to challenge reversals before finalizing scores. Graduate agents who finish with a streak of six qualifying weeks, excluding frozen or excused weeks from the count. Publish the final adoption rate, quality results, and remaining coverage gaps. Retire rankings, streaks, and prizes; keep article use and gap reporting in the normal support workflow.
