# Safe Starts: eight-week checklist adoption pilot

**Goal:** Raise verified pre-job digital checklist use from roughly 35% to 75% in weeks 7–8 without weakening safety checks or discouraging stop-work. The 75% target measures app adoption, not acceptable safety coverage: every eligible job still needs the approved pre-job check.

### What earns credit

One provisional credit per work order, awarded to the designated checklist owner when the crew completes a valid digital check **before work starts** and records the decision in the shared job record: proceed, hold, or stop and escalate. A correctly documented hold or stop earns the same credit even if the job never starts. No extra credit for retries, multiple crew members, fast completion, or a hazard-free result. Provide paid time for training and checks; never make the app or the incentive a reason to proceed unsafely.

### How it’s measured

Nightly, rebuild scores by joining dispatch work orders, app submissions, and independent job-start or hold records by work-order ID. Validate the checklist version, required responses, and event order. Count verifiable offline submissions by capture time, not sync time; send ambiguous timestamps to review rather than granting automatic credit. Dispatch defines the denominator: eligible jobs that reached the pre-job decision point, including safety stops. Exclude cancellations before that point and report exclusions separately.

Report two rates against that denominator: **verified on-time digital checks** and **verified on-time checks by any approved method**. If the app fails, use the approved fallback. Log the blocker before falling back when safe; otherwise report it as soon as practicable. A verified fallback counts toward safety coverage, not digital adoption.

### What technicians see

- **Private progress:** A quiet “Safe check recorded · provisional” confirmation for every credit, plus weekly progress as a share of jobs due, not a raw count. An on-time week means every due job had a valid digital check or an approved, documented exception with the required fallback check. Weeks with no jobs due, or only excused jobs, freeze the streak; they don’t extend it. Private badges recognise first use, repeat use, regular use, on-time weeks, use across job types, a blocker report that led to a fix, and peer coaching. Badges carry no extra points. Publish a weekly anonymised “you reported / we fixed” update.
- **Team finish line:** Show the team’s digital rate against 75% for weeks 7–8. Teams starting below 30% get a 15-percentage-point improvement milestone along the way. Show denominators and roll up small groups. Hitting the line earns recognition at a paid team huddle, not a cash or volume prize.
- **Visibility:** Launch without an individual leaderboard. If the sponsor and union later approve one, make it opt-in and hideable, show only the top five by rate within comparable workload brackets, and suppress brackets with fewer than 10 opted-in technicians. Tell everyone at launch that authorised managers can see every technician’s pilot activity, what they may use it for, and when it will be deleted. Pilot scores are for coaching and fixing barriers, not discipline, pay, or job allocation; existing safety processes remain separate.

### Launch, finish and failure controls

Before launch, management and union reps sign off on **one versioned policy** containing dates, eligibility, thresholds, workload brackets, exceptions, recognition, data access and retention, audit rules, and appeals. Validate the 35% baseline against the same job definitions. Run eight weeks, then freeze scoring. A six-scoring-week streak earns private graduation recognition after audit; people with too few eligible weeks are marked “not enough opportunities,” not failed.

Each week, Safety and a union representative review a random sample plus flags for duplicates, copied answers, backdating, and unexplained exclusions. If digital adoption rises while safety-related recalls or rework per 100 jobs also rise for two consecutive weeks, pause recognition and investigate. Review credible reports of rushed checks, reporting pressure, or unsafe starts immediately. Never reward low hazard or near-miss counts.

After week 8, allow five working days to audit and five more for appeals. Notify technicians privately of any reversed credit, with the reason and evidence. Retire points, streaks, and rankings after appeals; keep the checklist and fallback in normal operations. Recheck adoption and quality 30 days later to see whether the habit held without incentives.
