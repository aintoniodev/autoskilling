# Hand Hygiene Kiosk Pilot | Three wards, eight weeks

**Reward a usable shift record, not more entries.** Award one provisional credit per eligible patient-care shift when the nurse submits a usable record of actual hand-hygiene moments to Infection Prevention by shift end. Multiple indications at the same opportunity count as one moment. An honestly reported missed action does not disqualify the record. No credit for logins, practice entries, duplicates, or extra volume. Nurses perform hand hygiene when indicated and log at the next safe break in care; no one leaves the bedside to protect a streak.

**Measure adoption and safety separately.** Each night, recompute credits from the kiosk event log, worked roster and approved exceptions. Ward adoption is credited shifts divided by eligible worked shifts. Show excused shifts separately so outages cannot quietly improve the rate. A usable record must match the nurse’s worked ward and shift, distinguish action taken from action missed, and contain no patient identifiers. It is a self-report, not proof of compliance or of complete capture. Independent observers continue monthly audits: observed actions performed divided by observed opportunities, with sample counts and coverage across day, night and weekend shifts. Never use kiosk totals as the denominator or infer individual compliance from ward audits. [WHO observation method](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241598606)

**Adjust for workload.** A week counts as on time when at least 80% of a nurse’s non-excused eligible shifts earn a credit, rounded up. Weeks with no eligible shifts freeze the streak. A kiosk outage or care emergency can excuse a shift: notify the charge nurse before using the downtime form if safe, or as soon as care permits. If every shift is excused, freeze the week. The downtime form preserves the record but earns no kiosk credit. Publish a weekly “you reported / we fixed” update without naming reporters.

**Show progress without ranking nurses.** Each credited shift gets a private “Record accepted · provisional” confirmation. Nurses see their own credits, streak and badges for first use, repeat use, regular on-time reporting, helping resolve a blocker and coaching a peer. Skip badges for logging every type of moment; that would encourage nurses to hunt for entries. Ward huddles show a team finish line, not a league table. Using a one-week, no-reward baseline, set each ward’s final two-week adoption target at 85%, or 10 percentage points above baseline for wards starting below 70%. A softer adoption target never lowers the clinical standard. The sponsor keeps individual leaderboards off. At launch, tell nurses that their ward managers can see individual pilot activity and exceptions, and that pilot scores are not used for performance appraisal.

**Put the rules in one policy.** Nursing and Infection Prevention sign off before launch on one versioned policy containing dates, eligibility, deadlines, exceptions, thresholds, visibility, audit coverage, hold criteria, appeals and recognition. Recognition is a private graduation note for a six-week on-time streak and a huddle thank-you for wards that meet their target and clear the safety review. No cash, shift preference or prizes tied to volume.

**Watch for harm and close the pilot.** Flag duplicate or implausibly batched entries for review, not automatic penalties: delayed entry can be legitimate. If adoption rises while corrections or observed misses rise, Infection Prevention holds recognition and checks for fabrication, skipped hygiene, kiosk queues or unequal access by shift. A patient-care delay linked to the kiosk triggers an immediate pause in scoring. Sparse or uneven audits are inconclusive, not a pass.

Stop scoring after week eight. Within two weeks, review flagged records and the final monthly audit, reverse confirmed invalid credits with a private reason and an appeal route, and recognize graduates after reconciliation. If the audit is insufficient, withhold ward recognition rather than treating better logging as a safety win. Retire badges and streaks; keep routine reporting and independent audits.
