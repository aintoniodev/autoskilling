# Safe Start: an eight-week field pilot

**Goal:** Raise verified pre-job digital checklist use from about 35% to 80% in weeks 7–8. That’s an adoption target, not a safety standard: every eligible job still requires a safety check.

### What earns credit

Award **one provisional credit per dispatched work order** when the designated lead technician completes the job-specific digital checklist before work starts and records a valid decision: proceed with required controls in place, or stop and notify dispatch because the job cannot safely proceed. A documented safety hold earns the same credit as a safe start, even if the job never goes ahead. No credit for opening the app, late forms, retries, or duplicate crew submissions. No bonus for speed, job volume, or an injury-free record.

Before launch, the union and sponsor sign off on one published, versioned policy containing the dates, eligibility, scoring, exceptions, targets, recognition, data access, retention, and appeals. Give technicians paid training and checklist time in dispatch schedules, company-provided access, and an approved offline fallback. The incentive is optional; the safety procedure is not.

### How it’s measured

Each night, rebuild scores from the dispatch work-order register and checklist audit log. Match job ID, assigned lead, checklist completion time, and recorded start or hold. Use verified offline completion time, not upload time. Send uncertain timestamps and missing job links for review; don’t silently count them as failures or successes.

**Digital adoption = verified on-time digital decisions ÷ all eligible work orders.** Include approved paper fallbacks and missed checks in the denominator. Include safety holds; exclude duplicates and jobs cancelled before a check was due. Publish fallback and missing-data rates alongside adoption so an outage can’t be mistaken for noncompliance. Validate the 35% baseline against this definition using the four weeks before launch.

A technician advances their streak when **every eligible job that week** has an on-time digital decision. That’s 2 of 2 jobs for one technician and 20 of 20 for another. Weeks with no eligible work freeze the streak. If a technician reports an app blocker before using the approved fallback, excuse the week rather than breaking or advancing the streak, provided the other jobs were handled on time. If the app is down, use the dispatch phone channel. Publish a weekly “reported / fixed / still open” list without names.

### What people see

- **Technicians:** A private “Safe decision recorded” receipt for each provisional credit, plus job-level status, weekly progress, and streak. Private badges recognise first use, repeat use, regular and on-time use, use across job types, a blocker report that led to a fix, and peer coaching. Badges add no credits and don’t affect pay.
- **Teams:** An aggregate progress bar, not a ranking. Teams starting below 40% get a 60% finish line for this pilot; other teams get 80%. Keep the company-wide 80% target visible. Combine groups with fewer than 10 technicians.
- **Managers:** At launch, explicitly tell technicians that authorised managers can see every technician’s job-level checklist activity and exceptions. The pilot dashboard is for coaching and fixing blockers, not discipline, pay, or job allocation. Recommend **no individual leaderboard**. If the sponsor and union approve one, it’s opt-in, top five within comparable workload brackets only, hidden below 10 opted-in technicians, and anyone can hide their card at any time.

### What could go wrong

**Box-ticking, backdating, or hiding missed jobs.** Safety and a union representative review a random sample of 20 work orders weekly, including stops, fallbacks, and missed checks. Spot-check whether recorded controls were actually used. Flag suspicious timestamps and unexplained cancellations for review, not automatic penalties. If adoption rises alongside safety-related callbacks or rework per 100 started jobs, investigate by job type. Pause recognition if the audit finds unsafe shortcuts; keep the safety process running.

**Pressure to start work or stay quiet.** Make clear that stopping safely never costs a credit, and reporting an injury, hazard, or near miss never costs anyone recognition. Provide a confidential reporting route outside line management. Don’t use a rise in reports as evidence of failure. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation)

### How it ends

Stop earning credits after week 8. Within five working days, Safety and the union representative reconcile exceptions and reverse invalid credits with a written reason. Give technicians five working days to appeal before finalising. Privately recognise anyone with six qualifying weeks in an unbroken streak; frozen weeks don’t count toward six. Publish aggregate adoption, fallback use, audit findings, and unresolved blockers whether or not the target was met. Retire badges, scores, and rankings; delete pilot-only personal data after 90 days under the agreed policy. Keep the pre-job workflow and check adoption again 30 days later without incentives.
