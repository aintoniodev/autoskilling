# Clean hands first: an eight-week kiosk pilot

**Reward a usable shift record, not a high hand-hygiene count.** Across the three wards, a nurse earns one provisional credit per eligible patient-care shift for recording actual hand-hygiene moments and closing the record by handover. The record includes ward, shift, moment category and whether hygiene was performed, missed or uncertain. No patient identifiers. A record with an honestly reported miss earns the same credit as one with no misses. Extra entries, logins and claimed perfect compliance earn nothing. Never interrupt care or delay hand hygiene to reach a kiosk. If an entry cannot be made safely, mark the gap rather than reconstructing it from memory.

### Measurement and milestones

- **Kiosk adoption:** Count accepted, on-time shift records divided by eligible shifts worked, using the kiosk event log and staffing roster. Recompute nightly. Exclude leave, nonclinical assignments and approved exceptions; show the number of exceptions alongside the rate so exclusions cannot quietly inflate it. No minimum number of moments per shift.
- **Individual habit:** An on-time week means closing at least 80% of eligible shifts on time, rounded up. The target scales with shifts worked. A week with no shifts due freezes the streak. A kiosk outage or care emergency reported before switching to the fallback, or as soon as safe in an emergency, excuses the affected shift without awarding a credit. Post a weekly “you reported / we fixed” summary without names.
- **Clinical compliance:** Infection Prevention keeps the monthly observer audit separate from the game: observed hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities, not by kiosk entries. Use the last comparable pre-pilot audit as baseline and sample days, nights and weekends consistently. Report numerators and denominators. A thin or unrepresentative sample is inconclusive, not a pass. Never infer individual compliance from a ward audit. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring method](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/infection-prevention-control/hand-hygiene/monitoring-tools)

### What people see

Nurses get a quiet, private **“Shift record accepted · 1 provisional credit”** confirmation, their weekly target and their streak. Private badges mark first use, repeat use, a four-week streak, a confirmed blocker fix or peer coaching. No prizes for logging more categories.

The sponsor keeps individual leaderboards **off**. Each ward sees its own aggregate finish line: **80% on-time shift records over the final two weeks**. Wards below 50% in a pre-launch dry run get an interim week-four target of baseline plus 10 percentage points, not a lower clinical standard. At launch, tell nurses exactly what their ward manager can see: named shift-record status, credits and exceptions for staff they supervise. No public names or use of pilot scores in performance ratings.

### Safety checks and exit

Nursing and Infection Prevention sign one policy before launch covering dates, eligibility, deadlines, exceptions, thresholds, visibility, recognition and appeals. Duplicate submissions cannot earn a second shift credit. Flag bulk backfills, shared logins and repeated corrections for human review, not automatic penalties. Check for kiosk queues, missed breaks and unequal access on nights. If logging interferes with care, pause scoring and fix access or workflow before resuming.

**Pause ward recognition and investigate** if kiosk adoption rises while observed compliance drops by five percentage points or more against a comparable baseline, or if record corrections rise for two consecutive weeks. Treat these as review triggers, not proof of gaming. If the audit sample is inadequate, hold ward recognition until Infection Prevention can assess it. Check shift mix, observer effects and recording burden before drawing conclusions.

Stop earning credits at the end of **week eight**. Within two weeks, reconcile duplicate and disputed records, reverse invalid credits with a private reason and an appeal route, and recognize nurses who reached six consecutive on-time eligible weeks. Recognize wards that met the adoption target and cleared the clinical safety review at a staff huddle; no cash or rank-based prizes. Then retire credits, streaks and displays. Keep the kiosk only if it is useful without rewards and does not disrupt care. Continue monthly observer audits and check adoption again a month after incentives end.
