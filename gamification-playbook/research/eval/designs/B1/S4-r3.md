# Knowledge at Close: eight-week pilot

**Goal:** Make using the knowledge base part of resolving a customer ticket. Move from 12% recorded adoption to 70% verified coverage in weeks 7–8, without worsening support quality. The standing expectation is to document every resolution; 70% is the pilot finish line, not the new standard. **The existing CSAT bonus stays unchanged.** Credits, badges and rankings do not affect pay or performance ratings.

### What earns credit

Award **one provisional credit per unique ticket** when the resolving agent sends the customer a substantive resolution and records a relevant, accurate knowledge-base article on the ticket by the time they send it. Share the link with the customer only if it is customer-safe and useful; an internal article can support a customer-facing answer without being exposed.

Reuse an existing article when it fits. If knowledge is missing, an agent can submit a reusable, de-identified draft linked to the ticket before sending the resolution. The credit appears only if a knowledge editor approves it within two business days. Never delay a customer reply for approval. No extra credit for writing instead of linking, adding multiple links, reopening and reclosing, or splitting a ticket.

### How it is measured

Support Ops publishes one versioned policy before launch containing dates, eligibility, deadlines, targets, workload brackets, visibility, QA triggers, rewards and appeal rules. Each night, recompute from helpdesk resolution events and knowledge-base approval records, keyed by canonical ticket ID and attributed to the agent who sent the resolution.

Coverage is qualifying tickets divided by all genuine customer resolutions. Exclude spam, tests and merged duplicates, not hard cases or knowledge gaps. Recalculate the four-week baseline under the same rules and show it alongside the original 12% if they differ.

An on-time week requires valid documentation on **70% of that agent’s eligible resolutions**, rounded up. The ticket count needed scales with workload. Weeks with no eligible resolutions freeze the streak. If an agent logs a blocker before sending an undocumented fallback and a lead verifies it would have changed the weekly result, freeze rather than break the streak. The ticket still counts in team coverage and earns no credit. Publish a weekly “you reported / we fixed” list.

### What people see and earn

- **Agents:** Each credit gets a visible “+1 provisional” on their private dashboard, alongside ticket-level reasons, weekly progress and streak. Badges mark first credit, a second distinct ticket, three qualifying weeks, a four-week on-time streak, coverage across three issue categories, a verified blocker fix, and peer coaching confirmed by the recipient. Badges add no points.
- **Teams:** Show credited tickets out of eligible resolutions and progress toward the 70% org finish line. Queues starting below 15% also get a 50% local finish line for weeks 7–8. If the org hits its target and clears QA, celebrate with a team-selected lunch.
- **Leaderboard:** Off unless the sponsor enables it. If enabled, opt-in only, top five by coverage within comparable pre-pilot workload brackets, with at least 10 visible participants per bracket. Agents can hide their card at any time. Say at launch that managers can see everyone’s activity regardless of leaderboard settings, for coaching and fixing workflow problems.

### What could go wrong

QA reviews 30 random credited tickets each week across queues and separately checks repeated generic links, duplicate drafts and unusual closure patterns. Reviewers check whether the article actually supports the answer and whether the answer resolved the issue. Track seven-day reopens or repeat contacts, corrections, time to resolution and CSAT by queue and issue type, with response counts. If coverage rises while reopens or corrections rise by at least 2 percentage points against baseline for two consecutive weeks, pause public recognition and rewards while QA investigates. Also investigate slower resolutions or falling CSAT rather than treating higher coverage as success. Wrong links and manufactured tickets lose credit with a reason; suspected privacy exposure goes straight to incident handling.

### How it ends

Stop earning after week 8. Allow seven days for late reopens, then complete the audit and give agents three business days to appeal reversals before finalizing. Graduate agents with a six-week on-time streak; frozen weeks neither advance nor break it. Announce results, award any earned recognition, and retire credits, badges and rankings. Keep the close-time workflow and blocker reporting. Four weeks later, check whether coverage held without rewards and whether customer outcomes stayed stable.
