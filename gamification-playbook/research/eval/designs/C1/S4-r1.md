# Knowledge base adoption programme

**Goal.** Raise the share of resolved tickets with a useful knowledge-base article from 12% to 60% during a six-week pilot. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules unchanged.

**Fix the closing workflow first.** Spend one week watching agents close common ticket types. Fix the main barriers before starting the reward clock. Put article search inside the resolution screen, suggest relevant articles, and prefill a short article template from the resolution. Give agents a clear way to write an internal article when the answer should stay private. Show the team what changed in its usual support channel. Start the pilot once agents can complete the common cases without leaving the ticket.

**What earns credit.** “A resolved ticket counts once when you link an article that explains the fix or write a reusable article that does.” A new article can start as an internal draft if publication requires review. It must already contain enough information for another agent to use. Reopening and closing a ticket again earns no extra credit. Agents can record a reason to skip when the resolution cannot safely be documented. Skips stay in the denominator and the owner reviews recurring reasons.

**How it’s measured.** Pull unique resolved ticket IDs and their linked article IDs from the support tool. Use all resolved tickets in the denominator. Each week, spot-check eight credited tickets across queues, including both links and new articles. Verify that the article matches the fix, is usable, and contains no customer-sensitive details. Remove credit for invalid entries, tell the agent privately what to fix, and restore credit when the entry is corrected. Repeated misses trigger a focused check of the affected queue or article before the team reward is confirmed.

**What people see and earn.** After each qualifying closure, the ticket shows “Article recorded” with a link to the article. Each agent sees their own cumulative count and a goal of covering half their resolved tickets, so the target scales with workload. Progress carries through the full pilot, and a missed week costs no accumulated credit. The weekly support-channel update shows the team’s coverage, progress toward the milestone, and one example of an article a colleague could reuse. Managers receive team totals and use individual records only to coach or correct a specific issue. When the team reaches 60% coverage across any two consecutive pilot weeks, it earns one hour of paid personal time per agent, scheduled around queue coverage. The reward is earned once.

**How it ends.** By week five, make suggested articles the first option on the resolution screen, with “Write an article” and “Skip with reason” available in the same panel. The pilot and reward eligibility end after week six. For the next four weeks, track weekly coverage, review the same quality sample, and monitor CSAT, time to resolution, and reopen rate against the pre-pilot baseline. Keep the workflow if coverage holds near 60% and service quality holds. If adoption falls, inspect skipped tickets and fix the biggest remaining workflow barrier. If adoption stays low after that fix, retire the pilot reporting. Use a saved report and reserve 50 minutes a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.

**What could go wrong.** Agents may attach generic articles, create duplicate drafts, or rush closures to lift coverage. Use the sample to catch weak links and duplicates, and check reopen rates and resolution times for shifts. Article writing can also crowd out the care that earns the CSAT bonus. Review any sustained CSAT decline alongside response counts and affected ticket types. If the workflow is driving poorer service or exposing customer information, pause reward accrual and fix the cause before resuming.
