## One-working-day code review pilot

Run a six-week pilot for the 25-engineer team. Call it the **review turnaround pilot**. The goal is for authors to get a useful first review within one working day, so they know whether to merge, revise, or discuss a decision.

### Fix the workflow first

In week 1, use the team’s existing review queue to identify what causes requests to sit. Give every ready PR one primary reviewer through the existing ownership rules, with a rotating backup for absences and overload. Put the oldest requests first and protect a short review block in the workday. Post the fixes in the team channel in plain language, such as “You told us review requests sat when the owner was away. The backup now picks those up.”

Start the reward in week 2 once requests have a clear owner and reviewers have time to answer them. If those conditions take longer to establish, shorten the reward window and keep the week 6 end date.

### What earns the reward

Tell the team the rule in one sentence. **A ready PR counts once when a human gives it a useful first review within one working day.**

An approval after checking the change, a concrete request for changes, or a specific blocking question with a next step qualifies. An emoji, a bot comment, or “I’ll look later” does not. Any qualified reviewer can answer, so a backup can save a request without an author having to chase it.

If at least **85% of eligible PRs receive a qualifying review on time in each of weeks 5 and 6**, everyone gets **one hour of paid time back**, scheduled within the following two weeks. Put that hour on the calendar when the team hits the target. The team earns it together, including people who had few review opportunities or were on leave.

### Measurement and feedback

- **Clock.** Start when a PR is ready and review is requested, using the team’s published work hours and holiday calendar. One working day means eight hours on that clock. Drafts wait until they are ready, and reassignments and new commits do not reset the clock.
- **Score.** Group PRs by the week their review deadline falls in. Divide those reviewed on time by all eligible PRs, including unanswered requests. Exclude bot-authored PRs and PRs genuinely withdrawn before the deadline, and report the withdrawal count separately. Recalculate the baseline using the same rules so the new median is comparable with the reported 2.6 days.
- **What people see.** After a qualifying review, send a private confirmation with the elapsed working time. Give each reviewer a private view of their assigned requests and a personal goal of answering or arranging backup before the deadline for each request they have capacity to own. Share the team’s weekly on-time percentage, PR count, and median in the existing team channel. Managers see those totals. A missed request stays visible in the queue and the next request is a fresh chance.
- **Quality.** Each week, the pilot owner samples five first reviews, including approvals, to check whether the feedback gave the author a usable answer. Correct any miscount before posting the weekly score. Automate the counts from existing review events and budget 45 minutes per week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.

### Ending and risks

Keep ownership, backup coverage, and oldest-first ordering as defaults before the reward ends in week 6. Stop reward confirmations after the pilot and track the same measures for four more weeks. If turnaround holds and sampled quality is sound, keep the workflow. If it slips, inspect where requests are waiting and adjust coverage or review time.

The main risk is rushed approval. If the sample shows rubber-stamping, tighten what qualifies and inspect the review load. If people cherry-pick small PRs, use the oldest-first queue to spot and assign aging work. If reviews spill into evenings or repeatedly land on the same people, rebalance the rotation and reduce competing work. If scepticism persists, use the team update to show which delays were removed and ask authors whether the earlier replies actually helped them move forward.
