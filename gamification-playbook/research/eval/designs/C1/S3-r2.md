## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Spend two weeks testing the kiosk with nurses on days, nights, and weekends. Put it on an existing staff route, use badge tap, prefill ward and shift, and let nurses select the moment and save in a few seconds. Keep patient identifiers out. Nurses log at the next safe pause in care. Fix access, queues, and usability problems before starting rewards, and report back in the shift huddle with “You reported it, we fixed it.” Use these two weeks to establish kiosk uptake and the latest routine observer audit as the baseline for hand hygiene.

**Reward one ward milestone.** Run the incentive for eight weeks. Each ward earns a hospital-funded meal, with equivalent options for every shift, when 80% of its eligible nursing shifts have at least one valid entry over the pilot. All three wards can earn it. Tell nurses the rule in one sentence: “Log each hand-hygiene action you performed once, when care allows, and your shift counts toward the ward meal as soon as you save your first valid entry.” Additional entries serve the hospital’s documentation needs. A missed shift leaves the next shift available to contribute, and part-time nurses contribute on the shifts they actually work.

**Measure logging and hand hygiene separately.** Use worked direct-care nursing shifts as the denominator for kiosk adoption. Exclude shifts affected by documented kiosk outages or access failures. Count each nurse-shift once toward the milestone, and let nurses correct mistaken entries. Each week, the ward quality lead checks a small sample for duplicate entries, implausible timestamps, or repeated batches and resolves concerns privately. During existing monthly audits, infection prevention can use clearly matchable examples to check whether some observed actions were logged and treat ambiguous matches as inconclusive. Keep the clinical measure on its existing basis: observed hand-hygiene actions performed divided by observed opportunities. Review the counts alongside the percentages and cover different shifts where feasible. Audit scores carry no prize. **Show people what they need.** After saving, show a brief confirmation such as “Moment saved. This shift has counted toward your ward’s goal.” Nurses can see and correct their own entries after badge-in. Post the ward’s aggregate progress in its existing weekly huddle or staff bulletin. Managers receive ward totals, with individual records accessed only for a specific correction or coaching need. The weekly update and spot check should take the pilot owner under an hour across the three wards.

**End the reward on schedule.** Before week eight ends, make the prefilled kiosk form the default after badge tap and keep the save confirmation. Retire the prize and its progress display at week eight. Track eligible-shift coverage for four more weeks and review the next monthly audit. Keep the workflow if logging holds and the audit gives no reason for concern. If logging slips, ask nurses on the affected shifts what got in their way and fix that friction. If routine data needs require more complete capture, use the sampled audit checks to assess that separately from shift coverage.

**Watch for failure modes.** One token entry per shift can win the meal while leaving most moments undocumented, so report coverage as uptake and inspect sampled capture separately. If queues delay care, pause the incentive on the affected ward until the workflow is fixed. If kiosk use rises while observed compliance falls, have infection prevention review audit coverage and investigate the care workflow promptly. Check whether night and weekend shifts have equal access to the kiosk and the reward.
