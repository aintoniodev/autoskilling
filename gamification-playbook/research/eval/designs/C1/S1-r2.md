# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Before launch.** Spend two weeks with union stewards, dispatch and technicians from different shifts testing the checklist on actual jobs. Make it available offline, prefill job details and give technicians paid time to complete it. Test the approved paper fallback for app or device failures. Use the existing crew briefing to show what technicians reported and what changed. Start the incentive once crews can complete the checklist on a typical job without delaying dispatch or working off the clock.

**What earns credit.** “Complete the safety checklist in the app before work starts and get one credit per job, including when the checklist leads you to stop or escalate.” A checklist counts when all required items are answered and any identified hazard has a recorded disposition, including a stop or escalation. A job stopped for safety earns the same credit as a job that proceeds.

**How it’s measured.** Match checklist records to dispatched job IDs and compare the recorded completion time with the existing job-start record. An offline checklist counts when it syncs if it was completed before work started. For jobs stopped before work begins, use the recorded stop or escalation instead. Count each job once. The denominator is all dispatched jobs where a pre-job checklist was required, including jobs handled on paper and jobs stopped for safety. Exclude jobs cancelled before technicians arrive and any exemptions already defined in the safety procedure. Report paper completions and app failures alongside the digital rate so crews can see what prevented digital completion. Give technicians a private way to correct a mismatched job or timestamp before the weekly tally closes.

**What people see and earn.** After submission, the app confirms the specific job and shows whether it has counted or is waiting to sync. Each technician privately sees their completed checklists against their eligible jobs and a suggested goal of three in four, rounded down for low volumes. A missed week carries no penalty. Put aggregate progress in the existing weekly crew briefing, pooling small crews where individuals could be identified. Managers see aggregate results and access individual records only for a specific safety, coaching or correction need under rules agreed with union stewards. If the company reaches 75% digital completion across weeks five and six combined, every participating technician earns 30 minutes of paid release time, scheduled on their regular shift. Participation includes technicians who had no eligible jobs during the milestone window.

**How it ends.** Run the incentive for six weeks. In week five, make the prefilled checklist the first screen when a technician opens an eligible job. Announce the result and end the incentive after week six. Keep the app default and paper fallback. For four more weeks, track digital completion, paper fallback use and checklist quality against the comparable pre-pilot baseline, currently estimated at 35%. If the rate drops, use the fallback records and crew feedback to identify what made the app harder to use and fix that first.

**What could go wrong.** Rushing or ticking every box can make the rate look better while making the safety check worse. Each week, the safety lead reviews five records, including a stop or escalation when available, for job-specific hazards and follow-through. If the sample suggests rushed checks or crews report pressure to proceed, pause the incentive and review with union stewards. During an outage, crews follow the approved fallback and dispatch records the outage. If a widespread outage compromises the milestone window, apply a rule agreed before launch to extend it by the affected shifts. Automated counts, the five-record sample and one briefing update should keep routine administration under an hour a week.
