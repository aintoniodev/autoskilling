## Knowledge base adoption programme

**Run a two-week setup, a six-week incentive, and a four-week follow-up.** Today, 12% of resolved tickets have an article linked or written. The goal is to make adding useful knowledge a routine part of closing a ticket while protecting customer satisfaction.

### Setup

Watch agents close tickets across the main queues. Fix the biggest sources of friction before launching. Put article search in the resolution panel, suggest articles from the ticket topic, and let agents create a draft from a short template in the same panel. Give agents a way to flag sensitive cases or cases with no reusable guidance. Share the fixes in the team’s usual channel with a specific “you reported it, we fixed it” update. Use these two weeks to confirm baseline coverage, CSAT, reopen rate, and time spent closing tickets.

### What earns the reward

**The rule agents see**  
A resolved ticket earns one credit when you link an article that supports the resolution or submit a reusable draft when suitable guidance is missing.

Give agents **30 minutes of paid time away from the queue** when they earn credit on at least **50% of their eligible resolved tickets** across the six weeks. Schedule it during a staffed period. The target scales to each agent’s actual volume, so 8 eligible resolutions require 4 credits and 40 require 20. Progress is cumulative. A missed week or time off carries no penalty. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its calculation unchanged.

### How it is measured and what people see

Use ticket and knowledge-base records to count one credit per ticket ID. Reopened tickets count once, after they’re resolved again. A draft must contain reusable steps and be submitted for review. Give credit at submission so approval delays don’t cost agents their reward. Remove credits for sampled links or drafts that fail the quality check, and let agents correct them before the final tally.

Track coverage against **all resolved tickets** alongside eligible-ticket coverage and the exception rate. Exceptions earn no credit and leave the personal denominator only when they use a defined reason, such as a privacy restriction or a case with no reusable guidance.

Show agents immediate confirmation in the resolution panel and their own running tally. Post weekly org-wide coverage and progress toward the 50% eligible-ticket target in the channel the team already uses. Managers see aggregate results. Individual records are available for specific coaching or corrections. There’s no leaderboard.

### How it ends

Before week six ends, make article suggestions and draft creation part of the standard resolution panel, with a one-click exception path so agents can close urgent cases promptly. Issue earned rewards and end the incentive on schedule. Keep the workflow and track coverage, exceptions, CSAT, reopens, and closing time for four more weeks. Compare the final two weeks with the setup baseline and the final two incentive weeks. Keep the defaults if coverage holds above baseline and service quality stays steady. If coverage falls or service quality slips, inspect affected queues and fix the workflow before considering another incentive.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, duplicate drafts, or easy exceptions.** One owner samples eight tickets a week across links, drafts, and exceptions. Check that links match the resolution, drafts add reusable guidance without customer details, and exceptions fit the stated reasons.
- **Pressure to close early or spend longer on tickets.** Review CSAT, reopens, and closing time weekly. If a metric worsens for two consecutive weeks, inspect the affected queue and pause the incentive there if the added step is contributing.
- **A draft backlog.** Route drafts to the existing knowledge-base owner. If the queue grows, prioritize articles for recurring issues and improve suggestions before asking agents to write more.

Use an automated weekly rollup. Budget 30 minutes for sampling and 20 minutes for reviewing trends, posting progress, and handling corrections.
