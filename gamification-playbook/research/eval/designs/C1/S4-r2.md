# Six-week knowledge-base incentive

**Fix the workflow before launch.** Sit with agents from two queues and watch them close tickets using the knowledge base. Fix the biggest sources of delay first, such as poor search results, missing permissions, or having to enter the same information twice. Give agents a short template for drafting a missing article from a resolved ticket and name a reviewer who can clear drafts each workday. Share what agents reported and what changed. Start the six-week clock once agents can complete both paths from the ticket.

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** Tell agents the rule in one sentence. “A resolved ticket earns one credit when you attach an article that supports the resolution or write one that gets approved for reuse.” A relevant article can count on multiple tickets. Each ticket counts once, even if it reopens, gets reassigned, or has several articles attached. Credit goes to the agent who resolved it. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place and give agents a separate reward of 30 minutes of paid time away from the queue, scheduled with normal coverage.

**Measure finished work.** Use ticket and knowledge-base records to count qualifying resolutions automatically. Show draft credit as pending and assign it to the original ticket once approved. Allow a five-business-day review window after week six so a late approval can still count. Give agents a one-click exception for tickets where a reusable article would be inappropriate, such as a privacy-sensitive case. Track the percentage of **all** resolved tickets with a qualifying article, using the current 12% as the starting reference. Recalculate that baseline under the new counting rule if historical records allow. Calculate individual goals on eligible tickets and show the team’s exception rate alongside overall coverage so exceptions cannot quietly make progress look better.

**Give agents a reachable goal and immediate feedback.** Each agent earns the reward by covering at least 30% of their eligible resolutions across the six weeks. Show their personal count, rate, and remaining credits based on their eligible volume to date. Show immediate confirmation when a ticket earns credit or a draft enters review. Leave and a missed week carry no penalty. Put aggregate coverage, exception rate, and the team’s 30% overall coverage target in the existing support huddle or dashboard. Managers see team totals and review individual tickets only to coach or resolve a specific issue.

**End the reward and keep the easy path.** By week five, make relevant article suggestions the first option in the resolution form and prefill the article template from the ticket. Keep a visible exception option. End earning after week six and honor any pending credits approved in the review window. Keep the workflow and check aggregate coverage weekly for four more weeks. Keep the defaults if coverage stays at or above 25% in at least three of those weeks and service quality holds. If coverage falls, look at where agents skipped the step and fix that friction before considering another incentive. Change or remove any default that persistently slows resolution or encourages irrelevant links.

**Catch the failure modes early.** Each week, the support lead samples six credited tickets across queues and two exceptions. Check that links fit the resolution, new articles add reusable guidance without exposing customer details, and exceptions are justified. Correct individual credits privately and look for patterns such as one generic article appearing across unrelated cases or a queue claiming unusually many exceptions. Track CSAT, reopen rate, and median resolution time by queue against the four weeks before launch. If any worsens for two consecutive weeks, inspect affected tickets and pause earning in the affected queue if the article step is contributing. Automate the counts so the lead can handle the sample, metric check, and any corrections within an hour a week.
