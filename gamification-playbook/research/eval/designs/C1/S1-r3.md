# Pre-job safety checklist incentive

**Reward a real pre-job safety check.** Give each technician one 30-minute paid-time credit after they submit valid digital checklists for three distinct jobs before work starts. Technicians assigned only one or two eligible jobs during the six-week programme earn the credit by checking those jobs. Agree with union representatives how the credit can be used and confirm that using it won’t increase the technician’s workload. A check that identifies a hazard and leads to a stop or escalation earns the same credit as one that clears the job. Missed jobs and absences never erase progress.

**Fix the workflow before offering the credit.** Spend two weeks with union representatives, dispatch and technicians from different shifts testing the checklist on real jobs. Make it available offline, prefill job details, and schedule paid time to complete it. Agree on who owns the check for a multi-technician job and how the crew reviews it before starting. Keep an approved fallback available for device failures. Tell crews what they reported and what changed. Start the incentive once technicians can complete the check reliably in the field.

**Measure coverage from dispatch records.** Count one digital check per eligible job, including jobs stopped because of a hazard. Use dispatched jobs that reached the pre-work assessment as the denominator. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point and log exclusions separately. Use the original submission time for offline entries and compare it with the existing work-start record. Late entries and duplicate submissions earn no credit. Report approved fallback checks separately so an app outage stays visible without making a completed safety check look like a missed one. Verify the reported 35% baseline against the same rules and aim for 85% digital coverage by week six.

**Give technicians immediate, private feedback.** After submission, show whether the check counted and how many qualifying jobs they have toward the credit. Show offline entries as saved and awaiting sync. Give technicians a weekly goal of completing the check on every eligible job they actually work. Post team coverage in the existing weekly safety briefing, alongside any app or scheduling fixes. Managers see team totals. Restrict individual records to authorized staff handling credit corrections, safety follow-up or specific coaching under the agreed rules. Keep the programme free of named rankings and individual adoption scores in performance reviews.

**Run for six weeks, then measure for four.** Publish the end date at launch. Before week six ends, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job, with the stop-work and fallback paths easy to reach. Issue earned credits, end the incentive and keep the workflow in place. Track weekly digital and fallback coverage for four more weeks. If digital coverage holds at or above 85% and sampled checks are sound, keep the workflow as standard. If coverage falls, use the missed-job and fallback records to fix the cause before considering another incentive.

**Watch for rushed checks and pressure to start.** Each week, the safety lead reviews a small sample of five checks for site-specific answers, credible timing and appropriate handling of hazards. Review unusual patterns privately and verify the circumstances before correcting credit. If dispatch pressure is driving rushed or late checks, fix the scheduling. If the app is failing, use the approved fallback and resolve affected credits through the agreed correction process. Reward eligibility never depends on incident rates or finding zero hazards. Automate the counts and credit tracking so one person can run the weekly review and update in under an hour.
