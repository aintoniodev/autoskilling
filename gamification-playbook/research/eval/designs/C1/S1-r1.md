## Before Every Job

Run a six-week pilot for all 300 technicians, with the union and field safety leads agreeing on the rules before launch. Aim to raise verified pre-job checklist completion from about 35% to 80% by week six and sustain it for four weeks after rewards end.

### Make the checklist easy to use

Before the pilot, have technicians from different shifts try the app on real jobs, including sites with poor reception. Fix the main blockers before launching rewards. Open the checklist directly from the job card, prefill job and equipment details, and let technicians complete it offline. Leave safety answers blank for the technician to assess. Build checklist time into paid job time and tell crews what changed in response to their feedback.

### Reward a completed safety check

**Complete the digital safety checklist before starting work, or use it to record why work must pause, and it counts once per job.**

Each technician can earn 30 minutes of paid release time once during the pilot. Set a private milestone at launch using expected job volume. For example, a technician expecting at least 10 jobs earns it after eight qualifying checks, while someone expecting four jobs earns it after three. Give technicians with one eligible job a one-check milestone. Adjust the milestone if leave or dispatch changes reduce someone’s actual opportunities. Checks accumulate throughout the pilot, so a missed job or week leaves progress intact. Schedule earned time during a paid shift, with coverage arranged by dispatch.

### Measure and show progress

Match each submission to its dispatch job ID. Use the app’s recorded completion time, including for offline submissions, to verify it came before the recorded work start. For a job paused before work starts, count a completed checklist with a recorded safety hold. Count each job once. Report the percentage of eligible jobs with a qualifying check, including jobs paused for safety in the denominator. Exclude canceled jobs where no technician attended. Track approved fallback checks during app outages separately and protect technicians’ reward eligibility when they use them.

After submission, show a specific confirmation such as **“Checklist saved before work started. 5 of 8 toward your paid time.”** For offline jobs, show **“Saved on this device. Progress updates when you reconnect.”** Put the company or crew completion rate in the existing weekly dispatch update. Technicians see their own job history and progress. Managers see aggregate rates, with individual records accessed for a specific safety review, coaching need, or correction under the agreed rules.

### End the rewards and check what sticks

Keep the checklist as the first step on the job card and keep paid time for it in the schedule. Confirm both are in place before week six ends. Stop awarding paid release time at the end of week six and honor all earned time. For the next four weeks, compare weekly completion and checklist quality with the baseline and the final two pilot weeks. If completion drops, review missed jobs with technicians and fix any recurring dispatch or app friction before considering another incentive.

### Watch for failure modes

- **Box-ticking or backdating.** Each week, a safety lead reviews five jobs for timing and job-specific answers. Include a safety hold when available. Use the review to catch copied answers, rushed checks, and confusing fields.
- **Pressure to start an unsafe job.** A documented safety hold earns the same credit as a completed check on a job that proceeds. Use the existing stop-work and escalation process, with no reward tied to job speed or clearance.
- **Unequal access or distrust.** Track outages and fallback use by shift or region. Give technicians a private way to correct missing credit. Agree with the union up front on who can access individual records and how long pilot data is retained.

Use one automated weekly report and a five-job sample so one coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
