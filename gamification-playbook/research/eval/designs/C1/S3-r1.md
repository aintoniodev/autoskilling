## Eight-week kiosk pilot for three wards

**Reward accurate logging of real hand-hygiene moments.** Each ward can earn one modest team meal, with the same allowance for day and night staff, including float staff who worked on the ward. Every ward can qualify. Give nurses one rule: “Clean your hands as usual, then record the completed moment once when it’s safe to do so.” Missed entries never reset progress.

### Before launch

Have nurses on each shift try the kiosk during a normal shift. Fix placement, sign-in delays and entry friction before starting the reward. Set the kiosk to open directly to the logging screen with the ward prefilled, and provide an easy way to correct an accidental entry. Post the fixes where staff already look. If getting to the kiosk interrupts patient care or hand hygiene, resolve that before launch.

### Measurement and reward

Use the two regular monthly observer audits during the pilot. Keep the existing compliance measure: observed hand hygiene performed divided by observed hand-hygiene opportunities. Use the latest pre-pilot audit as a reference and continue reporting compliance separately from kiosk use.

During existing audit rounds, sample short windows across shifts where an observer can cover a defined care area. Reconcile the completed moments they saw with kiosk entries using the care area, moment type and a timestamp tolerance agreed during the trial. Match each entry and observed moment at most once. Use no patient identifiers. Exclude ambiguous matches and report how many were excluded.

A ward earns the meal once it has at least 20 assessable observed completions across two or more shifts, at least 80% of those completions have a matching kiosk entry, and at least 90% of assessable kiosk entries in the sampled windows match an observed completion. The first check catches missing entries and the second catches duplicates and unsupported entries. Use the second scheduled round to fill any gaps in shift coverage. If coverage remains too thin, report insufficient evidence and treat it as a measurement problem.

### What staff see

The kiosk gives immediate, private confirmation of what was saved, with a correction option. Nurses can see their own recent entries. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s last audit result, sample size and progress toward the meal. Managers see ward aggregates. A designated audit lead handles individual corrections or coaching privately. Raw entry counts carry no reward, and staff have no individual quota.

### Ending and follow-up

Close reward eligibility at week eight and recognize qualifying wards promptly, naming the work they did well. Keep the direct-to-log screen, prefilled ward and correction option. At the next monthly audit, repeat the matching check and review it alongside observed compliance. Keep the workflow if accurate logging holds and the kiosk fits the work. If logging drops, ask which shifts or care situations lost coverage and fix the friction before considering another incentive. Use an automated ward summary and the existing huddle so routine administration fits within an hour a week.

### What could go wrong

- **Recording crowds out care.** Tell staff to defer the entry whenever care takes priority. Pause the reward on any ward where the kiosk creates queues or disrupts hand hygiene, and fix the workflow.
- **Staff log extra entries or pressure colleagues to make the numbers.** Award the meal on audited agreement, give no credit for duplicate entries, and review discrepancies privately.
- **The audit overrepresents a shift or changes behaviour while observers are present.** Vary sampled times within the existing audit rounds, include nights, and show sample sizes. Treat kiosk logs as a record of entries and the observer audit as the measure of compliance.
