## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene verified by its existing monthly audit.** Each ward can earn one staff-selected reward, such as a meal provided across shifts, when its two pilot audits meet a target set with infection prevention before launch. Set the target five percentage points above the ward’s recent baseline, capped at the hospital’s existing goal. Wards already at or above the goal qualify by maintaining it. Every ward can earn the reward. Kiosk entries carry no points or prizes.

**Make logging easy before offering the reward.** Walk through the workflow with day- and night-shift nurses on each ward. Give them ten minutes of paid practice and fix what they identify, such as a kiosk queue, repeated sign-in, or a location that takes them away from care. Share what changed at handover. The instruction is simple: “Clean your hands when required, then log the completed action once when patient care allows.” Set the kiosk to open directly to the short entry form, with the ward prefilled where appropriate. Keep the hospital’s fallback for outages. After an absence or a missed entry, resume at the next opportunity.

**Measure adoption and clinical practice separately.** During each routine monthly audit, sample up to 20 observed, completed hand-hygiene actions per ward across the shifts the audit covers. After care, ask the nurse to confirm whether the action was saved in the kiosk using the ordinary confirmation or entry screen. Record only ward-level totals for confirmed saves, unsaved actions, and cases that could not be verified. Use confirmed saves divided by all sampled actions as a conservative adoption measure and show the sample size. Use the existing audit for clinical compliance: performed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Keep observer scheduling and sampling consistent across phases. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

**Give nurses immediate feedback and wards a shared view.** The kiosk shows “Saved” with an undo option. The existing ward huddle board shows aggregate kiosk adoption, the monthly audit result, progress toward the ward reward, and any workflow fixes. Nurses can see their own confirmation. Managers receive ward totals only. Keep names, rankings, and individual histories out of programme reporting and performance reviews.

**Stagger the reward and set an end date.** Give all three wards the fixes, paid practice, and feedback for four weeks. Then run the reward for two monthly audit cycles per ward, starting wards four weeks apart in a randomly chosen order. Compare each ward with its own run-in and look for changes when rewards start. Three wards give a directional comparison. End each ward’s reward on schedule and keep the simpler kiosk workflow. At the first monthly audit four to six weeks later, use confirmed kiosk adoption to decide what stays: keep the workflow at **80% or higher**, fix and retest at **60–79%**, and drop the current logging workflow for redesign below **60%**. Require at least 15 sampled actions to make that decision. Otherwise use the next routine audit.

**Agree on the failure cases before launch.** Test a double tap, an undone entry, a missed action, and an outage. A double tap creates one saved entry, an undone entry counts zero, and a completed action handled through the fallback counts in the clinical audit and is recorded as unsaved in the kiosk sample. Infection prevention pauses the reward if audited compliance falls five percentage points below baseline or staff report that logging delayed care. The ward lead collects corrections privately, the kiosk owner fixes entry or counting errors, and infection prevention resolves audit disputes. One pilot coordinator reviews flags and updates the ward totals in under an hour a week, using the existing monthly audit.
