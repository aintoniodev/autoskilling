# Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** A ticket earns one credit when it is resolved with a relevant, accurate knowledge-base article attached in the existing ticket field. Reusing an article and writing a new one earn the same credit. A new article counts once it clears the normal publishing review and is linked to the ticket. Agents keep their existing CSAT bonus on its current terms.

**Make it easy during the shift.** Give a few agents across shifts paid time to walk through recent tickets and show where finding, linking, or writing an article slows them down. Fix the biggest barriers before prizes start. Put article search in the resolution form, make linking a one-click action, and give agents protected time to draft or improve missing articles. Tell the team which reported problems were fixed. Give every team the improved workflow and feedback at the same time.

**Count from existing ticket records.** Divide distinct customer tickets resolved with a qualifying link by all distinct customer tickets resolved. Use the existing ticket ID, status, article link, and publication record. Before launch, confirm those records can support the count and reproduce the reported 12% baseline. Test the rule on a merged duplicate, a reopened ticket, a pending draft, and a ticket closed through the old workflow. A merged duplicate counts once under the surviving ticket. A reopen reverses provisional credit until the ticket is closed again. A ticket closed without a link stays in the denominator. Allow a documented exception for cases where a reusable article would be inappropriate, and keep those tickets in the overall denominator so the rate stays comparable.

**Show progress where agents already work.** At close, show the agent “Article linked,” “Credit pending publication,” or “No article linked.” Each agent privately sees their own progress toward linking articles on one in three tickets over their four reward weeks. The goal scales with ticket volume, and progress carries across weeks. In the existing team huddle or channel, show the team’s rate and progress toward a 35% milestone over the same four weeks. A team that reaches it and clears the quality check earns a modest team meal with equivalent access across shifts. Managers get team aggregates only. Keep individual programme counts out of performance reviews.

**Run four reward weeks per team, then stop.** Give everyone two weeks with the fixes and feedback to establish a baseline. Stagger the reward start by two weeks across existing teams and compare teams with rewards against teams still using the improved workflow alone. Keep article search and linking in the default resolution form after the final reward. Four weeks after each team’s prizes end, use its qualifying-link rate over the preceding two weeks to decide what to do with the programme. At **30% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback as the standard. At **20–29%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **20%**, retire the campaign and investigate why the workflow failed to stick. Prizes end on schedule in every case.

**Watch for weak links, rushed work, and review backlogs.** The support operations lead samples 10 credited tickets across teams each week, checking whether the article actually helps resolve the issue and handles customer information appropriately. Two failed checks in a weekly sample pause rewards pending a fix. Any exposure of customer information triggers an immediate pause. If team CSAT drops five percentage points from its pre-reward baseline for two consecutive weeks, pause that team’s rewards and review the response volume and affected tickets. The support operations lead owns pause and restart decisions and corrects disputed counts through the existing support channel. Use a saved report and the weekly sample to keep administration under an hour.
