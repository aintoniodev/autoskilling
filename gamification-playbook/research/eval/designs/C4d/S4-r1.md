## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** Give agents one credit when they resolve a ticket with a relevant, accurate knowledge-base article attached. An existing article and a newly published article earn the same credit. The same article can help on multiple distinct cases. Each participating team earns a team lunch when at least 40% of its resolved tickets qualify over its four reward weeks, subject to the quality check below. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula in place.

**Fix the workflow before starting the reward.** Ask six agents across shifts to walk through recent tickets and identify where finding, linking, or publishing an article slows them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and reserve time during shifts to draft articles. Put article search in the resolution screen, show agents which problems their feedback helped fix, and start the reward once agents can complete the workflow in their normal shift. When a case has no safe or useful article, agents can close it normally and flag a knowledge gap through the existing workflow.

**Count using existing records.** Use ticket resolution, article attachment, publication, merge, and reopen records. The denominator is all distinct tickets resolved by the team in the reporting window. A merged duplicate counts under the surviving ticket. Count a qualifying ticket once, even if several articles were attached or it was resolved twice. If it reopens within seven days, reverse the provisional credit and reassess it after the final resolution. A ticket resolved through the old workflow without an article stays in the denominator. Confirm before launch that the existing records support these rules. If the tool only records article publication, reward resolved tickets with a verifiable link through the existing integration and delay the reward if that link cannot be verified.

**Show progress where people work.** At resolution, confirm privately that the ticket earned provisional credit. Give each agent a private weekly tally and a suggested personal goal of two qualifying tickets per five they typically resolve, adjusted for their actual volume. A missed week has no carryover requirement. Show team progress toward 40% in the existing support channel, rolling small groups into a larger total where individual results would be inferable. Managers get team totals only. Pilot data stays out of performance reviews. Agents can flag a wrong count to the KB lead, who checks the ticket record and corrects the tally.

**Run two staggered starts.** Give all 60 agents the workflow fixes, practice, and feedback for two weeks. Then randomly assign intact teams to start the four-week reward in week 3 or week 5. Use weeks 3 and 4 to compare the change in article coverage between teams with and without the reward. Before each team’s reward ends, make article search the first step offered in the closure workflow, with a clear skip option. Leave search, private feedback, and protected drafting time in place after the lunch is earned or the reward window closes.

**Check quality and decide what stays.** Each week, the KB lead samples eight credited tickets across participating teams, including new articles when available. Check that the article fits the issue, supports the resolution, and contains no customer-sensitive information. The Support Ops lead pauses rewards if two of eight fail, or immediately if any article exposes customer-sensitive information. The KB lead corrects the issue and Support Ops resumes rewards after a passing sample. Use the existing team CSAT and reopen reports to spot whether agents are closing too quickly or spending less time on customers, and check for redundant articles and pressure to attach something to every ticket. Review those signals alongside ticket mix and response volume before attributing changes to the pilot. Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, the team update, and count corrections.

Four weeks after the final reward ends, use **quality-adjusted article coverage** to decide whether to keep the workflow as configured. Multiply the share of resolved tickets with a qualifying attachment by the audit pass rate for that period. Keep it at **30% or higher**, revise it at **20–29%**, and drop the pilot-specific workflow changes below **20%**. Compare with the 12% starting attachment rate for context and audit the pre-pilot tickets using the same quality criteria for a like-for-like baseline.
