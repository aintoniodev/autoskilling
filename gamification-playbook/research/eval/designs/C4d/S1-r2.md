## Pre-job safety checklist programme

**Start with two paid weeks to fix the workflow.** Have a union steward and technicians from different shifts try the app on real jobs, including low-signal sites and shared devices. Fix access, offline completion and any duplicate entry before rewards start. Give every technician paid practice time and publish a short “you reported it, we fixed it” update. Agree the counting, privacy and dispute rules with the union before launch. Keep the existing safety procedure available whenever the app is unusable.

**Reward one completed digital check per eligible job visit.** Tell technicians: “Your crew gets one credit when you complete the app checklist before work starts, or when the completed check puts the job on a safety hold.” A reported hazard earns the same credit as a clear check. Tie the credit to the existing visit ID and compare the checklist completion time with the existing job-start or safety-hold record. For offline jobs, use the completion time saved by the app when it syncs. If those records cannot establish the order reliably, resolve that before offering rewards.

Measure credits as a share of all eligible visits that reached the pre-job safety step. Paper checks and missing checks stay in the denominator. Exclude visits cancelled before that step. A duplicate adds nothing, a voided check loses its credit unless a valid replacement was completed in time, and a checklist submitted after work starts earns no credit. Test those cases, plus an offline sync and a hazard hold, before launch. Use the same rule to verify the reported 35% baseline.

**Make progress visible and the reward reachable.** The app confirms credit privately after submission and shows “pending sync” when offline. Technicians see their own count and a personal goal of four out of every five eligible visits they actually handle. A missed week costs no progress. Post the crew’s combined rate in its usual shift briefing, combining small groups where a total could reveal an individual’s activity. If the crew reaches 65% across its four reward weeks, everyone in that crew gets 30 minutes of paid time to use as they choose, scheduled across shifts. Managers see crew totals only. Programme data stays out of performance reviews and there is no named leaderboard.

**Run four reward weeks per crew, with starts three weeks apart.** Give everyone the app fixes and private feedback first. Pair crews with similar baseline rates and job mixes, then randomly assign one from each pair to start first. Compare the groups during the three weeks when only the first group has rewards. Before its prizes end, make the checklist the first screen when anyone opens an assigned job and prefill the job details. Leave the safety answers blank. After prizes end, keep the default and private confirmation. Use the **digital completion rate across the four weeks after the final crew’s rewards end** to decide what to do: at 65% or above, keep the workflow and retire prizes as planned. At 50–64%, fix the remaining friction and measure again. Below 50%, drop the incentive design and revisit the app workflow with technicians.

**Watch for rushed checks, inflated counts and unequal access.** Each week, the safety lead samples 10 credited visits against existing job and hazard records. One confirmed missed critical safety item or two materially defective checks pauses new credits while the lead investigates with the union steward. Technicians can raise wrong counts privately with the steward, and the programme coordinator corrects them against the existing records. Review recurring sync or access failures by crew so technical problems can be fixed before they cost a crew its reward. Use the existing app and dispatch records for the tally and budget under an hour a week for the routine sample, update and corrections.
