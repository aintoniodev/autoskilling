# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Start with two weeks of paid setup.** Have a union steward, technicians from different shifts and routes, dispatch, and the safety lead walk through real jobs, including poor-signal sites and urgent callouts. Fix the biggest barriers first. Put the checklist first when a technician opens a job, prefill job details, and make offline completion work. Give technicians paid time to practice and budget checklist time into each job. Share what technicians reported and what was fixed. Agree with the union on the counting rule, reward, data access, and correction process before launch.

**Reward one completed checklist per job visit.** “Your crew gets credit when the assigned technician completes the digital safety checklist before work starts.” Use the app’s existing checklist completion time and the existing job-start record, matched by visit ID. Confirm during setup that the job-start record reflects when work actually begins. A checklist that identifies a hazard earns credit on the same terms as a clear checklist. If a hazard stops the job before work starts, use the existing stop-work record to verify that the checklist was completed before that decision. Safety and emergency procedures always take priority.

- Duplicate submissions count once. A voided checklist loses credit unless a valid replacement was completed before work began. A return visit counts separately when it has its own visit ID.
- An offline submission counts when it syncs if the app preserved its original completion time. A checklist first completed after work starts earns no credit. A paper fallback protects the safety process and is recorded separately from digital completion.
- Measure qualifying digital checklists as a share of visits that started or were stopped for safety after arrival. Exclude cancellations before arrival. Keep app outages and paper fallbacks visible in the denominator so they show up as barriers to adoption. Verify the reported 35% baseline using the same rule before comparing results.

**Give private feedback and a reachable team reward.** After submission, show “Counted for this visit” or “Saved offline, awaiting sync.” Technicians can see their own credited visits and a private goal of completing the checklist on their next four of five eligible visits, carried forward across shifts and absences. Show crew-level progress weekly in the existing dispatch bulletin, combining small crews where a total could identify someone. A crew that reaches **70% across its four reward weeks** earns a company-paid meal during paid time, with an equivalent option for technicians who cannot attend. Managers see aggregate results only. Pilot counts stay out of performance reviews and discipline. A technician can raise a disputed visit privately with the pilot coordinator or through the union steward.

**Run rewards for four weeks per crew, then end them.** Give everyone the app fixes and private feedback at the same time. Group comparable crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly assign which start rewards in weeks 1–4 and which start in weeks 3–6. Compare the groups in weeks 1–2 to estimate what the reward adds. Announce the end date upfront, deliver earned meals promptly, and keep the checklist as the first step in the app with paid time and offline support. Use the **digital pre-start completion rate in weeks 9–10** to decide what to do next. At **75% or higher**, keep the workflow and close the incentive. At **60–74%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **60%**, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians. Safety checklist requirements apply throughout.

**Watch for rushed checks, distorted timestamps, and peer pressure.** Each week, the safety lead uses existing records to spot-check ten credited visits for timing and required entries, including offline and stop-work cases when available. If **two or more of ten** have a material defect, the safety lead pauses reward scoring for the affected crew and reviews the cause with the union steward. A credible report of pressure to skip a check, hide a hazard, or delay a job-start entry also triggers a pause. The pilot coordinator fixes verified counting errors, restores valid credit, and reports recurring app or dispatch issues through the existing safety huddle. Use the same weekly review to update totals and handle disputes, with a target of under an hour of routine administration.
