### Reward

Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, with equivalent provision for every shift. The ward earns it when the share of audited, completed hand-hygiene actions correctly recorded in the kiosk reaches its target and observed hand-hygiene compliance stays within the safety limits below. Set each ward’s target at 10 percentage points above its baseline, capped at 90%. Wards starting at 90% or higher aim to maintain their baseline. Each ward has two monthly audits to reach its target.

Tell nurses the rule in one sentence. “Clean your hands when required, then record the completed action once at the kiosk when patient care allows.”

### Measurement and feedback

Before launch, ask nurses from day and night shifts on each ward to walk through the logging process on paid time. Fix kiosk placement, access, and any steps that interrupt care. Give all three wards the same fixes, practice time, and feedback before measuring baseline.

During the existing monthly audit, observers keep the standard compliance measure: observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. For a sample selected in advance, the observer also tallies whether each completed action has one correct kiosk entry by the next safe logging point within the audit session. Verify the saved entry with the nurse when care allows, and retain only ward-level counts. Divide verified entries by all sampled completed actions. A duplicate gives no extra credit, a voided entry counts only if correctly replaced, and an action documented through the fallback still counts toward hygiene compliance. Report unverified cases alongside the rate. If reliable verification needs staff identifiers, patient details, or extra observation rounds, fix the measurement before starting rewards. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

The kiosk confirms each save and offers a correction option. In their existing huddle, nurses see their ward’s result, sample size, target, and one issue the team has fixed. Managers see the same ward totals. Keep individual scores, ward rankings, and audit data out of performance reviews.

### Schedule and ending

Use month 1 for baseline after the fixes. Randomly assign wards to start rewards in months 2, 3, and 4. Each ward’s reward window lasts two months. The stagger shows whether logging improves when the reward starts or was already improving with the fixes and feedback. With three wards, treat the comparison as directional.

Make the kiosk the default for routine documentation before rewards start, with a fallback for outages and urgent care. At the first monthly audit after each ward’s reward window closes, use the **verified documentation rate** to decide what stays. Keep the workflow if the ward still meets its target and clears the safety limits. If the rate is above baseline but below target, fix the biggest remaining obstacle and recheck once. If it returns to baseline or worse, end the rollout on that ward and review whether the kiosk is worth the documentation burden.

### What could go wrong

- **Logging competes with care or hygiene slips.** The infection-prevention lead pauses rewards after any reported care delay attributable to logging, if audited compliance drops more than five percentage points below baseline, or if it falls below the hospital’s existing minimum. The ward lead fixes workflow problems and resolves disputed counts with the audit lead before rewards resume.
- **Staff log extra entries or change behaviour around observers.** Kiosk volume earns no credit. Use the same sampling method before, during, and after rewards. Require at least 30 sampled completed actions across day and night shifts for an award decision. If a round falls short, carry eligibility forward to the next routine audit. Direct observation captures a sample and can itself change behaviour. [Hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/11996/cdc_11996_DS1.pdf)
