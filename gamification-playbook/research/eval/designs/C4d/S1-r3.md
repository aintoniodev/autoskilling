**Reward a completed safety check before each job.** A job earns one credit when the technician completes the required digital checklist before work starts, or completes it and records a safety hold before any work begins. Finding a hazard earns the same credit as clearing a job. Let technicians use the approved paper checklist during outages or when the app is inaccessible. Give those checks equal reward credit through the existing exception process and track them separately when measuring digital adoption.

**Fix the workflow first.** In weeks 1–2, meet with union stewards and technicians from different shifts and routes. Use paid time to try the checklist on real jobs, including a low-signal site. Fix the main barriers they identify, show crews what changed, and give everyone paid practice. Put the checklist first when a technician opens a job and prefill job details. Keep safety answers blank. Agree with the union on the reward, eligibility, and data rules before launch.

**Count from existing job records.** Match the checklist to the work order and compare its original completion time with the recorded work start or safety hold. Count a valid offline submission after it syncs if the app preserves the original timestamp. A duplicate earns one credit, a voided checklist loses its credit, and a checklist completed after work starts earns zero. Exclude jobs cancelled before attendance. Before launch, walk through those cases with technicians and test a paper fallback and a valid safety hold. If existing records cannot reliably establish the order of events, fix the records before tying them to a reward.

**Give crews private feedback and a modest shared reward.** After each check, show the technician “Counted” or “Pending sync.” Show their own progress toward a personal goal of 80% of eligible jobs. A tech with five jobs has a four-job goal, and one with 20 has a 16-job goal. Show crew progress in the usual weekly briefing, combining small crews where a total could reveal an individual’s result. Each crew that reaches 80% across its four reward weeks earns 30 minutes of paid release time for every member, with flexible scheduling. Crews qualify independently. A missed week leaves the next job available. Managers see crew totals only, and incentive data stays out of performance reviews and discipline. The programme coordinator corrects disputed counts privately, with a union steward available to help.

**Run two four-week waves and end the reward on schedule.** Give every crew the workflow fixes and private feedback before rewards begin. Randomly assign comparable crews to start in week 3 or week 5. Compare the waves in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds. End rewards after weeks 6 and 8 respectively. Keep the checklist first in the job flow. Four weeks after each wave ends, measure the share of eligible jobs with a verified digital checklist completed before work or a safety hold. Use the same definition to verify the reported 35% baseline. At 75% or above, keep the workflow and feedback. At 60–74%, fix the most common remaining barrier and recheck. Below 60%, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with the union.

**Watch for rushed checks, hidden hazards, and unfair counts.** Use the existing safety review to sample 10 credited jobs across crews each week for critical omissions and incorrect hazard dispositions. If two sampled checks have a critical defect, or an incident review confirms any bypass of a required safety hold, the safety lead pauses rewards, preserves credit already earned, and agrees on a fix with the union steward before restarting. Review paper fallbacks and disputed counts for recurring connectivity or access problems. Use one weekly report and the existing safety review so the coordinator can run the programme in under an hour a week.
