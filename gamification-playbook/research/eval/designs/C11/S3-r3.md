## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

Run a four-week baseline, a 12-week incentive pilot, and one follow-up audit four weeks after rewards end. Each ward can earn the same team reward independently. Patient care and hand hygiene always take priority over kiosk entry.

**Before launch.** Walk through the workflow with nurses from day, night, and weekend shifts on each ward. Give nurses 15 minutes of protected paid practice. Fix barriers they identify, such as a kiosk that requires a detour or a slow sign-in, and report back on what changed. During the baseline month, measure both observed hand-hygiene compliance and the share of completed actions logged correctly. If observers cannot reliably verify logging during their existing rounds without collecting identities or following staff, fix the workflow before attaching a reward.

**What earns the reward.** Announce at launch that each ward earns one hospital-funded team meal, served at handovers across all shifts, when at least 80% of sampled completed hand-hygiene actions are correctly logged in two of the three monthly audits and the ward clears the safety check in both. The nursing director thanks each qualifying ward in person and names a workflow improvement the team made. Every shift shares in the reward. A missed week has no carryover penalty.

**How it’s measured.** During existing monthly rounds, trained observers keep two separate ward-level tallies.

- **Hand-hygiene compliance** is completed actions divided by observed opportunities. Use the hospital’s existing audit method and denominator. [WHO technical reference manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)
- **Logging adoption** is completed actions with one correct, visibly confirmed kiosk entry divided by completed actions for which the observer could verify whether an entry was made. Count each completed action once, even when it covers several indications. A missing, wrong, or reversed entry earns zero credit. Extra entries earn zero additional credit, and an entry without an observed action earns zero credit. Keep a separate tally of cases where logging could not be verified so a narrow sample cannot pass unnoticed.

Before launch, test a duplicate, an undo, a paper-only record, an urgent interruption, and a kiosk outage. Infection Prevention sets the minimum sample and shift coverage using the baseline rounds. A month with insufficient coverage or a material outage is ineligible for reward decisions. If observed compliance falls five percentage points or more below the ward’s baseline, or below an existing hospital safety floor, Infection Prevention pauses the reward and investigates. The kiosk owner fixes workflow and counting errors. Infection Prevention corrects the published tally and decides when rewards can resume.

**What staff see.** The kiosk immediately confirms the moment recorded and offers a quick correction. The usual ward huddle board shows the monthly logging percentage, the sample size, and progress toward two qualifying months. Nurses and managers see the same ward totals. Programme reporting contains no staff or patient identifiers, individual scores, rankings, or data for performance reviews. The pilot lead uses the existing audit summary and spends up to one hour a week updating progress and routing issues.

**What could go wrong.** Nurses could log extra moments, focus on logging when observers are present, or delay care to reach the kiosk. Use the independently observed sample for reward decisions and vary audit times across shifts while keeping sampling comparable to baseline. Tell staff to skip the entry when care takes priority. Review outage and unverifiable-case counts with nurses so poor kiosk placement or uneven shift coverage surfaces before any ward is judged.

**How it ends.** Before week 12, make the kiosk’s logging screen the default, preselect the ward where possible, and keep correction easy. End rewards on schedule. At the next monthly audit, use **verified logging adoption** to decide the outcome for each ward. Keep the workflow at 80% or higher, fix the main barrier and recheck at 60–79%, and drop the current kiosk workflow below 60%. The safety pause still applies, and a sample that misses the agreed coverage requirement defers the decision.
