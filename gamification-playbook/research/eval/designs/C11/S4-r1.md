## Six-week knowledge-base rally

**Reward one useful article on each resolved ticket.** The launch message reads: “Close a ticket with one approved article that helped resolve it or documents the fix. If we reach 50% of resolved tickets in weeks five and six and pass the quality check, everyone on the support team earns one paid hour off, scheduled around queue coverage.” Ask agents across shifts whether they value that reward before announcing it. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its calculation unchanged.

**Make the workflow fit the queue.** Before launch, watch six agents across shifts resolve tickets and ask where finding, linking, or publishing an article costs them time. Fix the biggest obstacle and show the team what changed. Give every agent 30 minutes of protected time to practise and give each shift access to a knowledge-base reviewer. If writing an article would delay a customer resolution, agents can close the ticket and finish the article during protected time. An approved article attached within three business days can still earn credit.

**Count tickets from existing records.** Use the ticket system’s resolution and article-link records and the knowledge base’s approval status. The denominator is all resolved customer tickets, including tickets handled the old way. The numerator is distinct resolved tickets with at least one qualifying article. Multiple links count once. A reopened ticket loses credit until it is resolved again and can count only once overall. An unapproved draft earns credit once approved and linked within the three-day window. Use approved internal articles for sensitive fixes. Cases with no safe or useful article stay in the denominator, and agents can flag the gap in the existing knowledge-base backlog.

**Show agents their own progress and everyone the team’s progress.** Confirm credit in the ticket workflow as soon as the records qualify. Each agent privately sees their credited tickets, total resolved tickets, and a personal target equal to half their own resolved volume for the two-week milestone. The personal target is a guide. The reward depends on the team result, so an absence or a low-volume shift cannot disqualify anyone. Post the team percentage weekly in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews.

**Check quality before awarding the reward.** The support operations lead samples 10 credited tickets per week and checks that the article matches the fix, is usable, and has the right visibility. If fewer than 9 pass, or if any link exposes restricted information, the lead pauses reward accrual and fixes the workflow with the knowledge-base owner before resuming. The lead also corrects duplicate, reopened, or missing credits raised through the existing support channel. Watch team CSAT and reopen rates against the pre-launch baseline for signs that agents are rushing resolutions. Pay particular attention to generic links attached just before closure and bursts of near-duplicate articles. Automate the counts from existing records and budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.

**End the reward after week six.** Before launch, record the prior four weeks’ coverage using the same counting rule and verify the reported 12% baseline. By week five, put relevant article suggestions and a one-click link in the resolution screen, with an option to close without an article. In week six, the support lead announces the result at the team huddle and hands over the paid-time reward if earned. Four weeks after rewards end, use coverage over those four weeks to decide what stays. Keep the default at **40% or higher**, revise the workflow at **25–39%**, and drop the rally format below **25%** while investigating where the workflow still costs agents time. Apply the same quality check before keeping the default.
