## Six-week knowledge-base rally

**Start with the work.** In the week before launch, spend 30 paid minutes with six agents across shifts. Watch them resolve tickets with and without an existing article. Fix the biggest blocker they identify, such as poor search results or a slow publishing approval, and show agents what changed. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected practice time and make article search and drafting available from the ticket. Confirm the reported 12% baseline against the counting rule below using the previous four weeks.

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** The launch rule is simple. *A resolved customer ticket counts once when it links to a published article that would help someone solve the same issue.* Agents can use an existing article or write one. If an article needs review, agents resolve the customer’s issue on time and have seven days to attach the published article. There are no extra points for writing an article. The reward is **one hour of paid time off for every member of a team that reaches 50% coverage in two consecutive weeks**. Announce it at launch and have the support lead award it at a team huddle. Offer scheduling across shifts so everyone can use it.

**Measure with existing ticket records.** Each week, divide qualifying tickets by all customer tickets resolved that week. Exclude spam, tests, and duplicate records from merges. Attribute each ticket to its resolving team and freeze the count seven days after resolution. Multiple links earn one credit. A link removed before the freeze earns zero. A ticket reopened before the freeze moves to the week it is resolved again. A ticket resolved without an article stays in the denominator and earns zero. An article still in draft at the freeze earns zero. The support operations lead owns the count and corrects errors reported privately by agents.

**Show progress where agents already work.** Use the ticket’s existing confirmation or status field to acknowledge an eligible link immediately and show when a draft is pending. Give agents a private weekly count and a target based on their own volume, such as linking a useful article on five of their next ten resolutions. An absence simply means they pick up with their next ticket. Post team coverage and progress toward the milestone in the existing team channel. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews, and eligibility for the existing CSAT bonus is independent of the rally.

**Watch for irrelevant links and slower support.** Each week, the support operations lead samples ten credited tickets across teams. A pass requires a relevant, accurate article with no customer-sensitive information. If fewer than nine pass, the lead pauses reward qualification, corrects affected counts, and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Agents should flag approval delays and knowledge gaps in the existing support channel so the lead can fix recurring blockers. Review team CSAT alongside response volume and time to resolution. If either service measure worsens for two consecutive weeks, inspect those tickets before continuing the rally. Budget 45 minutes a week for the count, sample, and team update.

**End the reward and keep the easier workflow.** By week five, make article search the first option in the resolution panel, prefilled from the ticket’s issue category, with drafting one click away. End prize eligibility after week six and honour awards once the final counts settle. Four weeks after prizes end, compare coverage across those four weeks with the baseline. Use that single number to decide: **keep** the workflow at 50% or higher, **change** it if coverage is above baseline but below 50%, and **drop** the resolution prompt if coverage is at or below baseline.
