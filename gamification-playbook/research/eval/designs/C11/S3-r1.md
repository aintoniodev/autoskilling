## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward.** Each ward can earn a catered meal for all shifts, including night and weekend staff, at the end of an eight-week pilot. Announce the meal and the ward’s target at launch. The rule staff hear is “Your ward earns a meal if it meets its hand-hygiene target in the second monthly observer audit and clears the pilot’s safety checks.” The ward leader hosts the meal across shifts and thanks staff for a specific improvement they made. Kiosk entries carry zero reward value.

**Before launch.** The infection-prevention lead walks through the kiosk workflow with nurses from day and night shifts on each ward, including a busy shift and an urgent-care scenario. Give staff paid, protected time to try it. Fix placement, access, and workflow problems before starting, then tell staff what changed because of their feedback. Agree where logging fits safely into care. If it delays care or introduces a contamination risk, pause kiosk use and follow the existing hand-hygiene procedure.

**Measurement.** Use the latest comparable monthly observer audit as each ward’s baseline. The score is the percentage of observed hand-hygiene opportunities completed correctly under the hospital’s existing audit protocol. Set the reward target five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to reach at least 95% again. Keep the audit method and sampling rules consistent across the baseline, pilot, and follow-up. If a ward’s sample falls short of the existing audit minimum, defer its reward decision until a valid audit is available. Each ward can qualify independently.

Track kiosk adoption separately. During a small sample of existing audit windows, observers tally at ward level how many observed hygiene moments eligible for kiosk logging were logged at the approved point in the workflow. Record aggregate counts only. If observers cannot see both steps reliably, mark that window unmeasurable. The infection-prevention lead checks a small sample of anonymous kiosk records weekly for duplicate submissions, corrections, and anomalies. Count a retried submission once and exclude voided entries. A correctly performed hygiene moment without a kiosk entry counts in the hygiene audit and as a missed log when logging was feasible. Test these cases and kiosk downtime before launch.

**What staff see.** The kiosk confirms a saved entry immediately. A short update in the existing ward huddle shows the latest observer score, the ward’s target, and any workflow fix. Staff can see their own confirmation. Managers receive ward totals only, and pilot data stays out of performance reviews. Each eligible moment is a fresh opportunity to use the kiosk, including after an absence.

**Safety and ownership.** The infection-prevention lead pauses the incentive on a ward if its observer score drops five percentage points or more below baseline, or if staff report a credible kiosk-related care delay or contamination risk. The ward lead fixes workflow issues and the kiosk owner corrects data errors. Publish any corrected ward total in the next huddle. Watch for backfilled entries, pressure to log during urgent care, and better performance only when observers are present. Review those signals alongside staff feedback. Use the existing audit team and cap routine pilot administration at one hour a week.

**Ending.** Before week eight ends, make logging the first action on the kiosk’s ward screen and remove any unnecessary fields. End the reward after the second monthly audit. Four weeks later, use the next monthly observer audit to decide ward by ward. **Keep** the workflow if the score meets the original target. **Change** it if the score misses the target but remains less than five points below baseline. **Drop** the kiosk workflow if the score falls five points or more below baseline. Any unresolved safety issue keeps kiosk use paused. Review the sampled logging rate to see whether adoption persisted and where the workflow still needs work.
