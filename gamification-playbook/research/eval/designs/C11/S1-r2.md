## Pre-job checklist pilot

**Give technicians paid time to do the check.** Before launch, run a paid 30-minute session with union stewards and technicians from different shifts, including crews that work without reliable signal. Walk through a real dispatch and agree on the reward, counting rules, data access and appeals. Put the checklist first on the job screen, prefill job details, leave safety answers blank and make offline completion reliable. Have dispatch build the check into paid job time. Show what changed at the next toolbox talk, for example, “You told us checks disappeared when you lost signal. The app now saves them on your phone.” Verify the fixes in the field before launching the reward.

**Reward one completed pre-job check per job.** The launch message is simple: “Complete the app checklist before work starts, or use it to document why the job must stop. Each job counts once. If we reach 65% over six weeks, everyone in the pilot group gets one hour of paid time off.” Confirm the reward with the union and arrange shift coverage before announcing it. Award the hour at a toolbox talk after verification. Everyone in the group earns it together, including people on leave or with few jobs.

**Use records the company already has.** Divide qualifying jobs by all dispatched jobs that reached the point where a pre-job check was required. Include jobs held for safety and jobs checked on paper. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. Match the checklist and dispatch records by job ID. Use the recorded completion time, including a saved offline completion time, against the existing work-start record or safety-hold record. Verify those records can establish the sequence before launching. Keep the approved paper fallback available during outages and include those jobs in the denominator so outages remain visible.

Test the rule before launch. A duplicate earns one credit. A voided checklist earns credit only if a valid replacement was completed before work began or the safety hold was recorded. A checklist entered after work starts earns zero. An offline checklist completed on time earns credit when it syncs. A checklist that documents a hazard and the resulting stop earns the same credit as one that clears the job.

**Give technicians immediate feedback and keep scores private.** Show “Pre-job check saved for job 1842” immediately and “Pending sync” when offline. In the app, each technician sees their own count against a goal scaled to their actual jobs. For example, someone with 10 jobs sees a goal of 7. The next job is a fresh chance after an absence or miss. Share the group’s percentage against 65% at the weekly toolbox talk. Managers see aggregate totals with small groups combined. The union agreement bars individual score access for managers and use of programme data in performance reviews or discipline. A technician can raise a disputed count privately through a steward. The programme coordinator checks the source record, corrects the tally and confirms the outcome.

**Watch for rushed checks and pressure to clear jobs.** Each week, the coordinator samples five credited jobs against existing job and safety records, checking timing, applicable items and whether flagged hazards received the required response. Repetitive answers can prompt a closer look. If two of five checks have material defects, or anyone credibly reports pressure to skip a check, clear a hazard or stay quiet, the safety lead and union steward pause reward verification and fix the cause before resuming. Hazard, near-miss and injury reporting stay open throughout, and reporting never costs the group its reward. Automate the tally and budget 45 minutes a week for routine review. [Worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safeandsound/safety-and-health-programs/worker-participation)

**End the reward on schedule.** Recalculate the reported 35% baseline using four weeks of records and the same counting rule. Give every crew the improved workflow at the same time. If dispatch already has comparable crew groups, start one group’s six-week reward two weeks later and compare their adoption during that window. End rewards after six weeks per group and keep the checklist first in the job flow. Use the same digital completion rate for the four weeks after each group’s reward ends. Set the decision thresholds before launch. At **65% or higher**, keep the workflow and retire incentives. Below 65% but **at least 10 percentage points above baseline**, fix the remaining workflow barriers and remeasure. Below that improvement, drop the incentive design and revisit what makes the app harder to use in the field.
