**Reward audited hand hygiene.** Run an eight-week pilot across the three wards. Each ward can earn a hospital-funded team meal, offered on every shift, when its routine monthly audit meets its target. Set the target before launch at five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Announce the meal and the rule at launch. When a ward earns it, the ward lead thanks the team in person at each shift huddle and names a specific improvement nurses made. Each ward can earn the meal once, and a ward that misses the first audit can still earn it at the second.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Before launch, ask nurses from day and night shifts on each ward to walk through a real case on paid time. Fix problems they identify, such as a kiosk that is too far from the work or a screen that takes too many steps. Report the fixes at the next huddle. If routine logging still delays care, defer the incentive and fix the workflow. The personal goal is to record each completed hand-hygiene moment once when care permits. There is no quota or streak to recover after an absence.

**Use the two records for different decisions.** The reward follows the existing observer audit. For each ward, divide observed opportunities where hand hygiene was performed by all eligible opportunities observed, using the hospital’s current definitions and sampling procedure. Infection Prevention reviews the numerator, denominator and shift coverage before approving the result. Agree on minimum sample size and shift coverage before launch. If either falls short, hold the award decision until the next comparable audit. Pause awards if any ward’s audited rate falls five percentage points or more below baseline while its kiosk entries rise. Infection Prevention decides when to resume, and the kiosk owner fixes any counting or workflow issue.

Use ward-level kiosk totals to see whether the system is being used and investigate friction. A rising total can reflect heavier workload, duplicate entries or better adoption, so it earns no reward and never becomes a compliance percentage. Test three cases before launch. A double-tap saves once, an erroneous entry can be undone, and an observed hand-hygiene moment that goes unlogged still counts in the audit.

**Show nurses what happened.** The kiosk confirms “Saved” immediately and offers Undo. At the usual ward huddle, share the ward’s audited rate, sample size, target and meal status. Report that an audit is pending between monthly observations. Managers see ward aggregates only. The programme adds no individual tracking or leaderboard, and its data stays out of performance reviews.

**End on schedule.** Use the last comparable pre-launch monthly audit as each ward’s baseline. Before week eight ends, make the quick-entry screen the kiosk default and keep kiosks where nurses found them easiest to use. End the meal incentive after the pilot and repeat the routine audit four to six weeks later. Use the audited hand-hygiene rate to decide whether to keep the rollout in each ward. Keep it if the ward still meets its target, revise the workflow if it falls short but remains at or above baseline, and suspend the rollout for review if it falls below baseline. Defer the decision if the follow-up sample is insufficient.

**Watch for selective improvement.** Staff may change behaviour when observers are present, and a day-shift-heavy sample can hide problems at night. Use the existing audit schedule and check comparable shift coverage before interpreting a change. A higher audit rate alongside sparse kiosk use is a reason to revisit logging with nurses. Assign one coordinator to review ward totals, handle count corrections and update huddle material in under an hour a week, using the audits already in place.
