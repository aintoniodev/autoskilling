## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Before launch.** Have the safety lead and union stewards run a paid field walkthrough with technicians from different shifts, including crews with poor connectivity and shared devices. Check whether technicians can open the right checklist, complete it offline, and raise a hazard before starting work. Fix the barriers they find and show crews what changed. Give everyone paid practice time. Launch once the app works offline and existing records can reliably establish whether a checklist was completed before work started.

**What earns the reward.** Each crew earns a 30-minute paid meal break with food provided if at least 80% of its eligible jobs in weeks 5 and 6 have a valid digital checklist completed before work starts. Announce the meal and the rule at launch. Deliver it in person across shifts, with an equivalent paid option for anyone who cannot attend. Everyone on a qualifying crew shares the reward, including people who were on leave or had few jobs. The safety expectation is a pre-job check on every job. A checklist that identifies a hazard counts, including when the crew stops or postpones work.

**How it’s measured.** Use the existing work-order ID, checklist completion time, and recorded work-start time. Count each eligible work order once, even if several technicians submit or the app syncs twice. Count offline submissions using the app’s saved completion time. Use started jobs that require a checklist as the denominator, plus jobs with a recorded pre-start safety hold. Exclude ordinary cancellations before work begins. Late submissions and jobs with missing timing stay in the denominator without digital credit until the records can be verified. A valid paper fallback satisfies the safety procedure and is recorded separately from digital completion. Before launch, test a duplicate, a voided checklist, a late offline sync, a paper fallback, and a job stopped over a hazard. Publish the counting rule and the correction route. Dispatch corrects work-order records, and the safety lead and union steward resolve disputed eligibility before rewards are finalised.

**What people see.** After submission, the app confirms that the checklist was saved and shows whether sync is pending. Technicians can see their own eligible jobs and progress toward a private goal based on their workload. A missed job or week carries no penalty into the next one. Post crew-level progress at the usual safety huddle or in the dispatch channel. Combine small crews for reporting where totals could identify someone. Managers see crew totals only. Programme data stays out of individual performance reviews.

**What could go wrong.** People may rush through checks, enter them late, or feel pressure to hide a hazard. The safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected checklist records each week using existing safety records, looking for material omissions or conflicts. Two material failures in a sample pause reward decisions for the affected crew. Credible evidence of pressure to start unsafe work or falsify a record triggers an immediate pause. The safety lead and union steward review the issue together, assign the fix, and agree when to resume. Track paper fallback and missing timestamps in the weekly tally so an app outage or dispatch problem is visible.

**How it ends.** Run the pilot for six weeks. Before the reward ends, make the checklist the first action on opening an eligible job, with job details prefilled and the paper fallback available. Verify the baseline using the same counting rule before launch. The starting estimate is 35%. Use one decision metric four weeks after the reward ends: the share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-start digital checklist. At 75% or above, keep the app workflow as the default. At 60–74%, fix the main barrier and recheck in four weeks. Below 60%, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. Use one weekly tally and the 10-record sample to keep routine administration under an hour.
