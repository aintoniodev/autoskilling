## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Run an eight-week pilot to raise digital checklist completion from about 35% to 75% of eligible jobs. Before launch, meet with union stewards and technicians from different shifts and routes. Give technicians paid time to try the app on a real job and identify what slows them down, including poor signal, shared devices, and duplicate paperwork. Fix rollout blockers first and show crews what changed. Agree with the union on eligibility, paid release time, data access, and how to resolve disputed counts.

### What earns the reward

**Launch message:** “Complete the app’s safety checklist before work starts on at least 80% of your eligible jobs during your four-week reward window and earn 30 minutes of paid time off. A checklist that leads you to stop an unsafe job counts.”

Set the personal goal as a percentage of assigned jobs so part-time technicians and crews with different workloads have a reachable target. A missed week leaves the technician free to earn credit on their next job. Let technicians with too few opportunities because of leave or low job volume carry their tally into a short makeup window agreed with the union. Announce the reward at launch and have a supervisor thank each recipient privately when confirming their paid time off.

### How it is measured

Use the existing dispatch job ID, checklist submission record, and work-start event to count one completed checklist per job, attributed to the technician already responsible for the check. A valid submission precedes work start, or precedes the recorded safety hold if work never starts. Before launch, verify that those records establish event order, including offline submissions. If they cannot, fix the workflow before offering rewards.

Test the rule on duplicate submissions, a voided checklist, a late submission, a safety hold, and a paper fallback. Duplicates count once, voided and late submissions earn no credit, and a documented safety hold counts. Paper remains available when needed and earns no digital credit. Documented outages remove affected jobs from the personal reward calculation. Keep paper and outage jobs in the company adoption denominator so the result reflects how often the digital workflow actually worked.

The safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected credited checklists each week against existing job records. Pause reward processing if fewer than 9 of 10 pass, or if any sampled checklist misses a critical hazard or records a required stop that was ignored. The safety lead decides when processing resumes after the issue is addressed. The pilot coordinator corrects disputed counts and confirms the result privately with the technician.

### What people see

After submission, the app confirms whether the checklist counted and shows the technician’s own tally and goal. Post the team’s weekly completion rate in its existing crew channel. Managers receive aggregate results only, with no individual drill-down or named leaderboard. Restrict access to individual records to the technician and the people who need them for corrections, reward administration, or safety review. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews. Automate tallies and updates so one coordinator can run the weekly routine in under an hour, with the safety lead handling the sample.

### How it ends

Give one group of crews its four-week reward window in weeks 1–4 and a comparable group its window in weeks 5–8. Give both groups the app fixes and paid training from day one. Compare their first four weeks to estimate whether the reward adds lift beyond those changes. Before the first window closes, make the checklist the first action on the job screen, with job details prefilled and the safety hold available from the same screen.

Record the baseline using the same counting rule and check the **share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-job digital checklist** again four weeks after the final reward window. Keep the workflow default at 75% or higher. At 50–74%, fix the main remaining barrier and recheck. Below 50%, end the incentive and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The underlying safety check remains required throughout.

### What could go wrong

Technicians could rush through the checklist, submit it before inspecting the site, or feel pressure to proceed through a hazard. The quality sample and full credit for safety holds address those risks. Poor signal, device access, or unequal job volume could make rewards feel arbitrary. Check complaints with stewards weekly, correct affected counts, and fix recurring access problems. If completion falls after rewards end, use technician feedback and job records to find where the digital workflow still costs time.
