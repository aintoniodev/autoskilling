## Digital safety checklist pilot

**Offer.** Technicians earn one hour of paid personal time when their dispatch group completes a valid digital pre-job checklist on at least 70% of eligible jobs during its four-week reward window. Everyone in the group earns the hour, including technicians who were on leave or had few assignments. Agree the reward and how crews can take it with union stewards before launch. Name it in the launch message and have the field-service lead acknowledge the crews in their regular safety huddles.

**Make the app usable first.** Give technicians from different shifts and routes paid time to try the checklist at real job sites. Ask where it adds time or creates risk, especially on shared jobs and routes with poor signal. Fix the blockers, show crews what changed, and give everyone paid practice time. Keep the approved paper fallback available. Technicians can stop work or use the fallback whenever safety or access requires it, without individual penalty.

**Counting rule.** “Each job counts once when the assigned technician submits the required digital checklist before work starts, or completes it and records a safety hold instead of starting.” Use existing dispatch job IDs, checklist records, and job-start or safety-hold records. The denominator is all jobs that reach the pre-job safety decision, including jobs using paper and jobs stopped for safety. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. Assign one checklist owner on shared jobs, following the existing safety procedure.

Before launch, test duplicate submissions, a voided checklist, paper use, and a safety hold. A duplicate earns one credit. A voided submission loses credit unless a valid replacement was completed before the decision. A paper check stays in the denominator. A valid digital checklist that leads to a hold earns credit. An offline submission earns credit if existing records can establish its completion time once it syncs. Verify those records before launching the reward.

**What people see.** After submission, show the technician the job and whether its checklist counted or is awaiting sync. Privately show their own completed and eligible jobs, with a weekly target based on their assignment volume. Each eligible job is a fresh chance to contribute. Put group progress in the existing safety huddle or dispatch briefing. Managers see totals for groups large enough to protect privacy. Keep individual counts out of manager dashboards and performance reviews. The pilot coordinator handles disputed counts privately, with a union steward available at the technician’s request.

**Timing and ending.** Give all groups the fixes and feedback for two weeks and establish a baseline using the pilot’s counting rule. Pair comparable dispatch groups by baseline and job mix, then randomly assign one in each pair to start rewards in week 3 and the other in week 6. Each group gets four reward weeks. Compare their rates during weeks 3–5 to see what the reward adds while both groups have the same app and feedback. Before the first reward window closes, make the checklist the first action on every assigned job and prefill the job details. Leave safety answers for the technician to complete.

Four weeks after the last reward window closes, use the company-wide rate of valid digital checks over those four reward-free weeks to decide what to do. At 70% or above, keep the default and end the incentive. At 50–69%, fix the main remaining barrier and remeasure. Below 50%, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The pre-job safety requirement stays in force throughout.

**What could go wrong.** Rushed answers, backdating, pressure to skip paper, and uneven app access can all inflate the apparent lift or undermine safety. Use the existing safety QA review to spot-check five jobs a week across crews, including holds and fallbacks when available. Pause reward accrual if two of the last ten sampled digital checks have a material safety defect, or immediately if there is credible evidence of falsification or pressure to proceed unsafely. The safety lead owns the pause and restart, with the union steward involved in reviewing the fix. The pilot coordinator checks totals and resolves exceptions using the existing records, with a budget of one hour per week.
