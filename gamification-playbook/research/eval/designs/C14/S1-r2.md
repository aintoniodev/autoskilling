## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward.** Each crew that completes valid digital safety checklists before at least **65% of its started jobs** over a four-week reward window earns **one hour of paid time off for every crew member**. Announce the reward and the dates at launch. A supervisor and union steward recognize qualifying crews at an existing huddle, and technicians book the hour through normal scheduling without losing pay. The reward is collective, and an absence never resets progress.

**Fix the workflow first.** Spend two weeks with technicians from different shifts and routes and a union steward. Give them paid time to try the checklist on real jobs and identify what gets in the way, including poor reception, shared devices and duplicate paperwork. Fix the blockers and report back at the huddle with what changed. Agree with union representatives on the counting rule, privacy, reward eligibility and dispute process before launch. If crews still lack a reliable way to complete the checklist in the field, fix that before starting their reward window.

**Counting rule.** “One job counts when its completed digital safety checklist is recorded before work starts.” Use the existing job ID, checklist completion timestamp and job-start record. Validate with technicians that the start record reflects when work actually begins. Count each started job once, exclude cancelled and never-started jobs, and accept offline entries when the app already preserves a reliable completion timestamp. A checklist voided for error counts only if a valid replacement was completed before work began. Paper fallback remains available under the existing safety procedure. A started job using paper stays in the denominator and is logged as a fallback using existing records. A job stopped before work begins stays out of the denominator. If existing records cannot establish the order reliably, hold the reward launch until there is a countable rule using records the company already keeps.

**What people see.** The app confirms when a checklist is saved and says whether it is pending sync. Technicians can see their own credited jobs and a private goal sized to their scheduled volume. Crews and managers see weekly crew totals at the existing huddle, with very small groups rolled up for privacy. Managers have no access to individual incentive totals, and incentive data stays out of performance reviews. Technicians can privately raise a missing or wrong count with the pilot coordinator, who checks the existing records and corrects the tally.

**Quality and failure modes.** Before launch, test a duplicate submission, a void and replacement, a paper fallback, a delayed sync and a job stopped for a hazard. During the pilot, use the existing safety review to spot-check five credited jobs per crew per week where volume allows. If two of the five lack a required check or were completed after work began, the safety lead pauses that crew’s reward decision, investigates with the union steward and has the coordinator correct affected counts before releasing the decision. Any work begun with an unresolved critical hazard goes straight through the existing safety escalation process. Tell crews explicitly that reporting a hazard, stopping work and using the approved fallback are expected when needed. At huddles, ask about pressure to rush checks or delay marking jobs started. Use a saved report and the existing review so one coordinator can administer the pilot in under an hour a week.

**Run and end.** Give all crews the fixes, private feedback and crew totals first. Then start half the crews in week 3 and half in week 5, using a random draw among comparable crews where practical. Each crew gets four reward weeks. Compare the groups during the two weeks when only the first group can earn the reward to estimate what it adds. Before each window closes, make the checklist the first action on opening a job in the app, with the job details prefilled and the approved fallback accessible. Four weeks after the last rewards end, measure the same pre-start digital completion rate across all crews. **Keep** the workflow at 65% or above, **change** it at 50–64%, and **drop** the reward programme at under 50% and revisit field barriers. The safety checklist remains required throughout.
