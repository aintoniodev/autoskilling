## Reward a resolution that leaves useful knowledge behind

Run a nine-week pilot for the 60-person support team. Give each team that reaches **60% of resolved tickets with a qualifying article over its four reward weeks** a 45-minute paid break for every team member, scheduled across shifts to preserve coverage. Ask agents during setup whether they value that reward and confirm it in the launch message. Have the support lead thank the team in person and name a specific example of how its work helped customers. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules in place.

## Count each ticket once

Use the ticket system’s existing resolution and article records. Tell agents the rule at launch: **“A resolved ticket counts once when it links to a relevant, published article or to a new article approved through our usual review.”** The article must help someone solve the issue again and use the appropriate audience permissions. An existing article earns the same credit as a new one.

Divide qualifying resolutions by all unique tickets resolved in the period. A ticket closed the old way stays in the denominator. Multiple links earn one credit. A reopened ticket loses its credit while open and can count once after re-resolution. A pending draft earns credit once approved and linked. Use existing merge records to count duplicate tickets under the surviving ticket. Calculate a fresh baseline using these exact rules alongside the reported 12%.

## Give agents feedback where they already work

Show a confirmation on the ticket when an article qualifies, or show that approval is pending. Agents see their own weekly count and a private goal of roughly six in ten resolutions, scaled to their actual ticket volume. Every new ticket is a fresh chance to contribute after a missed week or time off. Post only team totals and milestone progress in the existing support channel. Managers get the same totals without individual drill-down, and incentive data stays out of performance reviews. Agents can flag an incorrect count through the existing support channel for tooling issues. The pilot owner checks the record and corrects the count before the reward is awarded.

## Fix the workflow, then test the reward

- **Weeks 1–3.** Sit with agents from different shifts and queues and give them 30 minutes of paid practice. Fix the barriers they identify, such as poor search, slow approvals, or unclear article permissions. Post what was fixed. Give every team the same workflow and feedback, and test the count on a duplicate, a reopen, a pending draft, and an unlinked resolution.
- **Weeks 4–9.** Assign existing teams to two waves, balancing baseline adoption and ticket mix as far as practical. Wave one earns rewards in weeks 4–7 and wave two in weeks 6–9. Compare the change in qualifying resolutions during weeks 4–5 while both waves have the same workflow and only wave one has an active reward. In week 6, put article search and linking directly in the resolution screen and prefill new drafts from approved, non-sensitive fields. Keep those defaults after the rewards end.
- **Four weeks after the last reward ends.** Use the qualifying resolution rate over those four weeks to decide what stays. At **60% or higher**, keep the workflow without prizes. **Above baseline but below 60%**, fix the main remaining barrier and remeasure. **At or below baseline**, retire the incentive and revisit whether linking on every resolution is useful and practical.

## Catch problems in existing QA

Have the QA lead include ten credited tickets across queues in the existing weekly review. Check relevance, accuracy, duplication, and permissions. If **two or more of ten fail**, pause reward certification for the affected team. The QA lead decides when a fresh sample passes, and the pilot owner corrects any affected counts. Remove any exposed customer information through the existing incident process.

Watch CSAT in the existing weekly review. If it falls **five percentage points below the four-week baseline over a rolling two-week period with at least 30 responses**, pause reward certification while the support lead checks whether agents are rushing replies, delaying closure, or diverting time from customers. Schedule writing and review during paid work. Use the existing QA meeting and automated counts to keep pilot administration under an hour a week.
