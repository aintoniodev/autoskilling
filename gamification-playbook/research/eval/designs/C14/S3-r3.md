# Three-ward hand hygiene pilot

**Start with the workflow.** Give day, night, and weekend nurses 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk and identify what slows them down. Fix placement, sign-in, and cleaning before prizes start. Put the kiosk on the normal work route and make “Log hand hygiene” its first screen, with the ward prefilled. Log a completed action at the next safe opportunity. During urgent care or an outage, hand hygiene and patient care come first. Use the existing ward huddle to show what changed in response to staff feedback.

**Reward the ward for observed hand hygiene.** At launch, announce a catered meal for each ward that meets its own target, with equivalent service for every shift and specific thanks from the nursing director at handover. The pilot target is five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. These are reward thresholds. The hospital’s clinical standard still applies to every opportunity.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** Infection Prevention calculates observed nursing compliance as completed hand hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Pool the counts from the two baseline audits and, separately, from the two reward-period audits. Use comparable shift coverage and the existing observation protocol. Set a minimum sample and shift coverage before launch based on the hospital’s audit practice. If either falls short, hold the reward decision until the next comparable audit. The Infection Prevention lead validates the result, resolves disputed audit counts, and pauses rewards for review if a comparable monthly audit falls five percentage points or more below baseline. [WHO hand hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**Give nurses immediate feedback and keep reporting at ward level.** The kiosk confirms “Entry saved” and lets the nurse undo an accidental entry. Staff and managers see the same ward audit result, sample size, and progress toward the meal in the existing huddle. The programme uses no named leaderboard, individual reports, or performance-review data. The ward champion uses aggregate kiosk activity and outage reports to spot workflow problems. A kiosk entry carries no reward credit. Before launch, test a double tap, an undo, and an observed hand hygiene action with no kiosk entry. The last case still counts in the audit. Route kiosk count corrections to the kiosk owner through the existing support channel.

**Stagger the reward and remove it on schedule.** Give all three wards the fixes and audit feedback for two monthly cycles. Draw the start order and begin the eight-week reward window for each ward one monthly audit cycle apart. Use the waiting wards as a rough comparison. Keep the kiosk defaults and routine feedback after the meal. Check the same observed compliance measure four and eight weeks after each ward’s reward window. Precommit to this decision rule using the pooled follow-up audits: keep the routine if the ward holds its target, fix the workflow and retest if it falls below target without falling below baseline, and retire the incentive if it falls below baseline. The ward champion runs the programme in the existing huddle and audit review, within an hour a week.

**Watch for gaming and disruption.** More kiosk entries can reflect duplicate taps, backfilling, or easier access. Review a small sample of aggregate counts and outage reports during the existing audit review. Nurses may also clean their hands more often when an observer is present. Use the same sampling approach throughout, cover the usual shift mix, and interpret the result as observed compliance. If logging delays care or creates a queue, simplify or relocate the kiosk before asking staff to log more. A missed shift or week resets nothing. [SHEA guidance on observation bias](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)
