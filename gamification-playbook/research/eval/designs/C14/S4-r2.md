## Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets

Run a 10-week pilot for the 60-person support team. The behavior is to link a relevant knowledge-base article or write one that someone else can reuse when resolving a ticket. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility unchanged.

In weeks 1–2, give agents 30 minutes of protected, paid time to try the workflow. Ask agents across shifts to show where search, permissions, or publishing slow them down. Fix what blocks them from doing this during paid ticket time, and post what changed in the support channel. Give every team the fixes and feedback before rewards begin.

## What counts and what people earn

Tell agents the rule at launch. “A resolved ticket counts once when its record includes an article that would help someone solve the same issue again.”

Use existing ticket-resolution and article-link records. A new article counts once it passes the normal publishing review and is linked to the ticket. An existing article can count on multiple distinct tickets when it genuinely applies. Establish the denominator before launch using all distinct resolved tickets, with narrow exclusions for existing ticket categories where an article is prohibited, such as restricted cases. Missing articles and tickets handled through the old workflow stay in the denominator.

Before launch, replay four cases against the report. Two links on one ticket earn one credit. A reopened ticket loses its credit until it is resolved again with a valid link. An article link removed before the final tally loses its credit. An eligible ticket resolved the old way earns zero and remains in the denominator. The support operations lead owns the tally and corrects errors through the existing support operations channel.

Announce the reward at launch. Each pilot group that reaches **40% of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article** over its four reward weeks earns **one hour of paid time off for every agent in the group**, scheduled around coverage. The support director announces the result and thanks the group for a specific example of useful work at its regular huddle. Each agent gets a private goal of two qualifying tickets per five eligible resolutions, so the number of tickets needed scales with workload. Every eligible resolution during the reward window can help the group reach its goal, including the next case after an absence.

## What people see

Use the existing ticket confirmation to acknowledge a counted link and show agents their own progress privately. Post group totals and the percentage toward the milestone in the support channel each week. Managers see group totals only, with no individual drill-down or use of pilot data in performance reviews. Use existing records and QA reviews, with no new employee tracking.

## Timing and decision

Start one group of existing teams in weeks 3–6 and the other in weeks 5–8. Assign teams to the two start dates by lot, balancing ticket volume where practical. In weeks 3–4, compare how each group’s article coverage changes from its own baseline while both have the workflow fixes and feedback. Use that comparison to estimate what the reward added.

Before the first reward window closes, make article search the first option in the resolution workflow and prefill the search from the ticket category where the tool supports it. Keep a way to close promptly when no article fits. Pay out at the end of each group’s four weeks and retain the workflow and feedback.

Use **qualifying article coverage in the two weeks after each group’s reward ends** as the decision metric. Keep the workflow at **35% or higher**, revise it at **20–34%**, and drop the campaign below **20%**. Apply the same counting rule to the reported 12% baseline before comparing results.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles inflate coverage.** In the existing QA pass, sample 10 credited tickets per week across the active teams and check relevance, accuracy and reusability. If fewer than 8 pass, the support lead pauses reward accrual for the affected group, explains the issue at the next huddle and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Preserve previously earned valid credit.
- **Agents delay resolution or feel pressure to trade CSAT for credit.** Review existing CSAT response counts, scores, and resolution times alongside coverage. If service deteriorates, the support lead checks ticket mix and reviews examples in the regular huddle. Pause accrual where the article step is causing delays, and fix the workflow before restarting. Agents can close a ticket promptly and receive credit if an appropriate article is linked before the reporting cutoff.
- **The pilot becomes another reporting job.** The support operations lead refreshes the existing report, checks flagged counts and posts progress. Use the regular QA pass and huddle for the reviews, with under an hour of additional administration each week.
