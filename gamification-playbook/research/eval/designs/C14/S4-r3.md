# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward resolved tickets with a useful article.** Each team earns two hours of paid time off per person when 35% of its eligible tickets carry a qualifying article during its four-week reward window. Announce the reward and milestone at launch. The support director hands over the recognition at an existing team huddle, and workforce planning schedules the time off with coverage. The current article rate is 12%. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place.

**Fix the workflow before starting the reward.** In weeks 1–2, ask agents from each shift to walk through tickets where finding, linking, or writing an article is slow or risky. Give agents 30 minutes of protected time to practice and paid queue coverage for article writing. Fix the biggest friction points, such as poor search results or a link field buried in the resolution form. Show agents what changed in the team huddle. Start the reward once agents can complete the flow during a normal shift.

**Count one ticket once.** Use existing ticket resolution and knowledge-base link records. The rule agents hear is “A resolved ticket earns one credit when it has a relevant, approved article linked and stays closed for seven days.” An existing article and a newly written article earn the same credit. Additional links earn zero extra credit. Reopening a ticket during the seven days cancels the pending credit. A later resolution can qualify once. An eligible ticket resolved without an article stays in the denominator. Agree before launch which existing ticket categories require an exception, such as cases where documenting the answer would expose sensitive information. Use the same eligibility rule for baseline, pilot, and follow-up.

**Give agents immediate feedback and a reachable goal.** After resolution, show a private confirmation in the ticket tool, such as “Password reset article linked. Credit pending until the ticket stays closed for seven days.” Agents see their own confirmed count and a cumulative goal sized to their usual eligible ticket volume. Someone who resolves 20 eligible tickets in four weeks gets a goal of seven. A missed week leaves the next ticket available for credit, and the team milestone determines the reward. Managers see only team totals in the existing support dashboard, with no individual drill-down. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews.

**Use existing QA to catch bad links.** Review 10 qualifying tickets across teams each week in the existing QA pass. Check whether the article addresses the actual issue, is accurate, and is appropriate for the audience. Before launch, test a duplicate link, a reopened ticket, and a ticket resolved the old way. Pause reward accrual if three or more of the latest 20 sampled links fail. The support lead makes the pause and restart decision. The knowledge-base owner fixes article issues, and the reporting owner corrects miscounts raised through the existing support operations channel. Budget under an hour a week for sampling, reviewing totals, and handling corrections.

**Stagger the reward and make linking the default.** Give all 60 agents the fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Randomly assign existing teams to start their four-week reward windows in weeks 3, 5, or 7. Compare the early teams’ change from baseline with teams still waiting for the reward. In week 6, before the first window ends, move article suggestions and the link field into the standard resolution screen for everyone. Keep a quick skip when no article fits. Close the last window at the end of week 10 and allow seven days for pending credits to settle.

**Decide whether to keep the workflow in weeks 13–14.** Use the percentage of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article as the decision metric. Set the thresholds at launch. Keep the workflow at 30% or higher, revise it at 20–29%, and drop the pilot approach below 20%. Review CSAT and reopen rates through the existing team review. If either breaches its prelaunch alert threshold, pause rewards and check whether agents are rushing resolutions or losing queue time. Use protected writing time to address workload, and use QA findings to address generic links or unnecessary articles.
