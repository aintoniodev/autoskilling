## Clean hands, quick record

Run an eight-week pilot on each of the three wards. Each ward can earn a hospital-funded team meal, with equivalent options for night and weekend staff. At the launch huddle, name the reward and explain how to earn it in one sentence: “Your ward earns a team meal if it meets its hand-hygiene target across two routine audits.” The nursing director delivers the meal and thanks the ward in person at shift huddles.

**Make logging easy before launch.** Ask nurses from day, night and weekend shifts to try the kiosk during paid time. Fix the issues they find, such as a slow sign-in or a kiosk that requires a detour, and report the fixes at the next huddle. Set the kiosk to open directly to the ward’s one-tap logging screen. Nurses clean their hands at the clinically indicated moment and record the action when care allows. During urgent care or kiosk downtime, they can skip the entry and resume on the next opportunity. [CDC hand-hygiene guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/)

**Reward completed hand hygiene.** Use the existing monthly observer audits. The score is the number of observed opportunities where nurses performed hand hygiene correctly divided by all observed opportunities for nurses. Count each opportunity once, including correctly completed hygiene during kiosk downtime or when a nurse uses the old process. Pool the counts from the two reward-period audits. Before announcing targets, use recent comparable audits to set each ward’s target at five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Every ward can earn the meal. [WHO guidance on measuring hand-hygiene compliance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Measure kiosk adoption and show progress.** In the same monthly audit, add one anonymous ward-level tally for the subset of correctly completed actions where the observer can naturally see whether the action was logged. Report the share logged and the sample size. If the observer cannot tell, leave the action out of that subset. Keep the observer’s normal route and review the tally during the existing safety huddle. The kiosk immediately shows “Recorded” and allows an accidental tap to be undone. The huddle board shows the ward’s audit score, target and logging share. Staff and managers see ward totals only. Collect no staff or patient identifiers in the pilot data and keep it out of performance reviews.

**Stagger the start and set the end date.** Give all three wards the improved kiosk and feedback in weeks 1–4. Use that month’s audit as the logging baseline. Start rewards in weeks 5, 9 and 13, with two monthly audits per ward during its eight-week reward window. Leave the one-tap screen in place when prizes stop. Four weeks after each ward’s reward window, use the next routine audit to decide whether to keep the setup. Where baseline logging was below 80%, keep it if at least 80% of eligible sampled actions are logged, revise it at 60–79%, and drop the incentive approach below 60%. For wards already at 80% or above, use their baseline as the keep threshold and 20 percentage points below it as the drop threshold. Agree on a minimum usable sample before launch. If an audit falls short, defer the decision to the next routine audit. Three wards give a practical comparison of rollout timing, with limited ability to isolate the effect of the reward.

**Catch problems early.** Before launch, walk through a double tap, an undo, an old-process entry and an urgent-care miss. A double tap adds no reward credit, an undo removes the mistaken entry, and correctly performed hygiene counts in all four cases. If a monthly audit falls five percentage points below the ward’s clinical baseline, or staff report that logging delayed care or hand hygiene, the infection prevention lead pauses rewards on that ward and reviews the cause in the existing huddle. That lead corrects audit counts with the observer. The kiosk owner fixes entry errors, queues or outages. Give one pilot coordinator 45 minutes a week to update the board and follow up on issues.
