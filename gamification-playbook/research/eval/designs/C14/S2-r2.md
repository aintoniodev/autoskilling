## One working day for a useful PR review

Give engineers a reliable answer on review-ready PRs and reward the team for making that routine. The current median is 2.6 days. The pilot target is **80% of eligible PRs receiving a substantive first review within one working day**.

### Fix the review queue first

Use an existing team meeting to ask a few frequent authors and reviewers where PRs wait. Give the on-duty reviewer a protected 30-minute block each working day and name a backup for absences and overload. Route ready PRs to the responsible reviewer through the existing code-host workflow and put the oldest unanswered PR first in the queue. Show the team what changed in response to their feedback.

Ask the team whether two hours of paid time back is a reward they would value and secure manager approval and coverage before launch. The launch message names the reward: if a review group reaches 80% across its four-week reward window and clears the quality check, everyone in that group earns two hours of paid time back, scheduled with coverage. The engineering lead announces the result and thanks the group at its regular meeting.

### Count one useful answer per PR

The rule engineers see: **“A ready PR counts once when a human reviewer approves it after reviewing the change or leaves specific feedback the author can act on, within eight hours on our published team working calendar.”**

Use existing PR ready and review timestamps. Start the clock when a human-authored PR first becomes ready for review, or when it opens if it opens ready. Use one agreed calendar for working hours and holidays. Every eligible PR enters the denominator once its deadline passes, including those still unanswered. Count a useful review documented on the PR even if the discussion happened in a call.

Before launch, walk through a duplicate review, a dismissed review, a return to draft and a PR handled over chat. Extra submissions earn no additional credit. Reverse credit if a review is dismissed before the weekly tally. Returning to draft or reassigning a reviewer leaves the original clock in place. The pilot owner corrects disputed counts from the PR record and updates the team total.

### What people see

Use the existing PR notification to tell authors when the answer arrives. Give reviewers a private view of their assigned queue and due times, with a personal goal sized to the work routed to them. A missed case or week has no carryover penalty. Post the group’s weekly percentage, the numerator and denominator, and progress toward 80% in the channel engineers already use. Managers see the same group totals. There are no individual score reports, and pilot data stays out of performance reviews.

### Run it, then remove the reward

Pull the previous four weeks of PR history to establish the baseline for the one-day measure. Give everyone the queue fixes and feedback for two weeks. If there are two existing review groups with separable queues, start their four-week reward windows two weeks apart and assign each PR to its owning group when it becomes ready. If the team shares one queue, run one four-week window and use the fixes-only period as the comparison. Keep routing, backup coverage and protected review time in place when rewards end.

Four weeks after each reward window closes, check the same measure over the four reward-free weeks. **Keep** the workflow at 80% or higher. **Change** it if it lands below 80% but above the fixes-only period. **Drop** the reward programme if it returns to the fixes-only rate or lower. If the fixes-only period already reaches 80%, end the pilot there and keep the workflow.

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamping.** In an existing weekly team meeting, sample five credited PRs across sizes and check whether the answer was substantive. Two or more inadequate reviews in a sample pauses rewards. The engineering lead decides when to resume after the pilot owner corrects the affected counts and a fresh sample clears.
- **Easy PRs jump the queue.** Keep the queue ordered by deadline and include large and late PRs in the weekly sample. The backup takes overflow when the protected block fills.
- **People delay marking PRs ready.** Compare draft-to-ready times with baseline using existing timestamps. Ask authors about a sustained shift and fix the source of the delay before interpreting a higher on-time rate as an improvement.

One pilot owner posts the tally, brings the sample to the existing meeting and handles corrections within 45 minutes a week.
