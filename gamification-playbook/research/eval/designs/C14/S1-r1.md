## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Start with two weeks of paid setup.** Pair the safety lead and a union steward with technicians from different shifts, routes and coverage areas. Walk through real jobs and fix what makes the app harder to use in the field, such as slow loading, duplicate entry or poor reception. Test the approved fallback and give crews paid practice time. Report the fixes in the existing safety huddle. Agree with the union on the reward, counting rules, access to data and correction process before announcing the pilot. Start rewards only once crews can complete the checklist reliably in the field and the existing records can verify it happened before work began.

**Reward one completed check per job.** Publish one sentence at launch: “A job counts once when its completed app checklist is saved before work starts, and our crew earns a paid hour off per member if at least 70% of eligible jobs count during our four reward weeks and we pass the safety quality check.” Give everyone on an earning crew the hour, including people who were on leave or had few jobs. Have the operations lead recognize the crew at its safety huddle and roster the paid time off.

**Use the job records already in place.** Divide qualifying jobs by all jobs that started and required a checklist under the existing safety procedure. Match the saved checklist to the job ID and compare its completion time with the existing job-start record. Use an existing on-device completion timestamp for offline submissions if it survives sync. Duplicates earn one credit, a voided submission loses credit, and a checklist saved after work starts earns none. Canceled jobs that never started stay out of the denominator. Approved paper checks count toward safety compliance and stay in the adoption denominator. Agree up front that a verified app outage that materially affects a crew triggers replacement of the affected reward week. Keep those jobs in the adoption report so outages remain visible. If the records cannot establish the order of events reliably, fix the workflow before attaching a reward.

**Show technicians their own progress.** After a qualifying check, show “Checklist saved for this job” and update the technician’s private progress once the job-start record confirms credit. Give each technician a private goal of seven out of their next ten eligible jobs, based on jobs they actually work. A missed job or week never resets progress. Post only the crew’s percentage and progress toward 70% in its usual huddle or team channel. Managers get crew totals without a per-person drill-down. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews. Technicians can raise a wrong count privately with the steward or safety lead, and the app administrator corrects verified errors.

**Check quality in the existing safety review.** Have the safety lead sample five credited jobs across the active crews each week using records already available in that review. Check that required items were completed and any flagged hazard was handled under the existing procedure. If any sampled check missed a required hazard response or was credited after work started, the safety lead pauses the affected crew’s award. The safety lead and steward review the cause, the app administrator fixes counting errors, and the safety lead resumes awards once the fix is verified. Tell crews at launch that stopping unsafe work and using the approved fallback take priority over earning credit. Watch for rushed checks, manipulated start times and pressure from coworkers. Use the existing safety reporting route to surface those issues.

**Stagger the reward and check what sticks.** After setup, give every crew two weeks with the fixes and private feedback. Then randomly assign comparable crews to start their four reward weeks either immediately or two weeks later. Compare crew adoption during the two weeks when only the first group has rewards. Before each crew’s reward ends, put the checklist first on the job screen and prefill the job details, leaving safety answers for technicians to complete. The paid-hour offer ends after four reward weeks. Keep the easier workflow and private feedback.

Use the **percentage of eligible jobs with a valid pre-start app checklist** in weeks three and four after each crew’s reward ends to decide what to do. At **65% or higher**, keep the workflow as the standard. From **50% to under 65%**, fix the most common remaining obstacle and recheck without prizes. Below **50%**, retire the incentive and revisit the workflow with technicians and the union. Apply the same counting rule to the baseline and follow-up so both can be compared with today’s roughly 35%.
