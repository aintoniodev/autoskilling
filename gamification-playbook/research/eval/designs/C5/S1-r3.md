# Safe Start: a pre-job checklist pilot

**Reward the safety decision, not the job start.** A completed digital checklist earns one credit per work order when it records a valid pre-job decision: either it’s safe to start, or work is held because of a hazard. Both earn exactly the same credit. No credit for opening the app, ticking boxes after starting, or completing more jobs. Reporting a hazard, near miss or injury never costs anyone a reward. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation)

### Before launch

Have the safety lead and union stewards walk through real jobs with technicians from different shifts and routes, including poor-signal sites. Agree in writing on paid checklist time, the reward, what data can be used, how to dispute a count, and who can stop the pilot. Give everyone 20 minutes of paid practice and make room in the dispatch schedule for the actual check.

Make the checklist the first screen on the work order. Prefill job details, never safety answers. Test offline saving and make the existing paper or dispatch fallback easy to find. Post fixes at the usual safety huddle: “You told us the checklist disappeared when you lost signal. It now saves on the device.” Don’t launch rewards until crews can reliably complete the check without starting early or working unpaid.

### What technicians earn and see

Announce at launch: **“Record a valid digital pre-job safety decision on at least 70% of your eligible jobs over four weeks and earn one hour of paid time off.”** It’s one award per person, not a race. The target scales with actual job volume. Leave creates no missed jobs, and a missed check doesn’t reset anything. Confirm the award privately at a shift handover and let technicians book the hour without losing pay or having to make up the work.

After each submission, show specific private feedback: “Safety hold recorded for job 4812. Credit pending sync.” Once verified, show the technician their own count and progress toward 70%. At the weekly safety huddle, show the team’s rate against its own baseline, not other teams. Roll small crews into a larger group. Managers get aggregates only, with no individual drill-down, location tracking or use in performance reviews, discipline or job allocation.

### How it’s counted and checked

Use existing app submission records and dispatch work-order records. The **digital adoption rate** is qualifying digital checks divided by dispatched jobs that reached a pre-job decision, including safety holds and fallback jobs. Exclude ordinary cancellations before that point. Verify that the check preceded the independently recorded start, or that it documented a hold before any work began. If existing records can’t establish the sequence reliably, don’t launch rewards.

Before launch, test a duplicate upload (one credit), a voided check with no valid replacement before work (zero), an offline check saved before work but synced later (one if timing is verifiable), and verified paper fallback during an outage (no digital credit, but excluded from the worker’s reward denominator). Keep fallback jobs in the adoption denominator so outages don’t make the rollout look better. Technicians can raise a wrong count privately through a steward; the dispatch coordinator checks the source record and corrects it.

**Watch for rubber-stamping and pressure to proceed.** Each week, the safety lead samples eight credited jobs and compares the checklist with existing field safety observations or dispatch hazard records. A timestamp alone doesn’t show that anyone checked the site. One confirmed missed critical check, or two material mismatches in the sample, pauses new reward accrual. Credible pressure to start unsafely or hide a report also triggers a pause. The safety lead and union steward agree on the fix before restarting; valid credits already earned stand. Budget 45 minutes a week for the review, count corrections and team update.

### Timeline and ending

Give everyone the fixes and feedback first. Run two reward-free weeks to establish the baseline under the same counting rule; treat today’s 35% as provisional. Then split comparable crews by lottery into two waves. Wave A earns in weeks 3–6 and Wave B in weeks 7–10. Compare the waves’ changes in weeks 3–6 to see what the reward added beyond the fixes.

Prizes end on schedule. Keep the checklist first on the work order, with paid time, private confirmation and the fallback intact. Use the **digital adoption rate in the first four prize-free weeks for each wave** to decide: **70% or more**, keep the workflow without prizes; **55–69%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure; **below 55%**, drop the incentive and redesign the digital workflow. An unresolved safety issue overrides any adoption result. The pre-job safety check remains required in every case.
