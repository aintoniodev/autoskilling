# Check Before You Start

**Goal:** Raise pre-job digital checklist completion from about 35% to at least 80% four weeks after rewards end, without rushing checks or discouraging anyone from stopping unsafe work.

### Before launch

The safety lead and union stewards run a paid ride-along and short workshop with technicians from different shifts, routes and job types. Fix what makes the app harder than paper: open the checklist directly from the dispatched job, prefill job details but never safety answers, and make offline completion and sync work. Dispatch builds check time into every job. Give everyone 20 minutes of paid practice, including an offline job and a hazard hold. At the next huddle, show technicians what changed because of their feedback.

Agree the reward, data access and appeals with the union before announcing anything. The checklist remains part of the safety procedure whether or not a technician opts into the reward.

### What earns credit

**Launch message:** “Complete the digital safety check before starting work on at least 80% of your eligible jobs during your four-week window and earn one paid hour off. If you find a hazard and hold the job, that check counts too.”

Use existing work-order IDs, checklist completion times and dispatch start or hold records. Count one valid check per job, credited to the technician assigned to do it. A safe start needs a completed check before work starts. A hazard hold needs a completed check linked to the documented hold, with no work started. Neither earns more than the other. Never reward speed, a low incident count or an all-clear answer. [OSHA guidance on avoiding incentives that suppress reporting](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation).

Before launch, replay five cases with technicians and dispatch: a duplicate sync counts once; a voided submission loses provisional credit unless a valid replacement was completed on time; a late check earns nothing; an offline check uses its recorded completion time, not its upload time; and a verified paper check during an outage is neutral for the reward. Use the approved fallback if the app fails; never skip a check to earn credit. If existing records cannot reliably establish the sequence, delay the reward rather than add worker tracking.

### What people see

After submitting, technicians see “Pre-job check recorded” or “Saved offline; credit pending sync.” Their private view shows their own count and goal, such as “6 of 8 eligible jobs checked; 7 needed.” The target scales with actual job volume, excludes verified fallback jobs, and has no weekly minimum or streak. Only one reward is available; a missed week does not wipe out progress.

The existing crew huddle shows the team’s completion rate against its own baseline, with small crews pooled. Managers get team totals only: no named leaderboard, individual drill-down, or use of programme data in reviews or discipline. The safety lead and steward hand over the paid-hour confirmation privately after verification. Technicians can dispute a count through their steward; the safety lead decides within five working days and the programme coordinator corrects the tally.

### Timing and decision

Once the fixes are ready, give all crews two weeks with the same app, training and feedback but no prizes. Pair similar crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly pick which starts rewards first. Group A gets four reward weeks; Group B starts two weeks later and gets the same four weeks. Compare their changes from the common baseline during the two weeks when only A has rewards. Before prizes end, make the checklist the first screen when opening a dispatched job for everyone; keep that default and the paid check time.

Track one decision metric: **valid pre-job digital checks ÷ all jobs that reached a start-or-hold decision**. Exclude jobs cancelled before that decision. Include paper fallback jobs in the denominator and report their share separately so outages don’t disappear. Measure the rate again over the four weeks after each group’s prizes end. Agree thresholds up front: **80% or more**, keep the workflow without prizes; **60–79%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck; **below 60%**, retire the incentive and redesign the digital workflow. The safety check stays in every case.

### What could go wrong

A timestamp does not prove a real check. The coordinator spends under an hour a week reviewing the automated tally and sampling eight credited jobs for missed required checks, copied answers or a mismatch with the dispatch record. **Two material failures in eight, or one verified unsafe start credited by the programme, pauses new awards.** The safety lead and steward review the cause, correct the rule or workflow, and jointly approve any restart. Reports of pressure to rush, hide a hazard or avoid the paper fallback go directly to the steward and safety lead for immediate review.
