# Three-ward pilot: Clean hands first, kiosk second

**Launch message:** “When our ward accurately logs at least 8 in 10 hand-hygiene actions sampled in the routine audit, and passes the safety and accuracy checks, the nursing director will thank the team at handover and bring a catered meal to every shift. Extra entries earn nothing. Patient care and hand hygiene always come before logging.”

### Fix the workflow before offering a prize

Walk the workflow with day, night and weekend nurses on all three wards. Give staff 15 minutes of protected paid time to try the kiosk. Fix queues, awkward placement and slow sign-in before scoring anything. Make the logging screen the kiosk’s home screen, prefill the ward, and let nurses correct an entry in one step. Never prompt during care or require a detour before hand hygiene. Post the fixes at handover: “You reported it; we fixed it.”

### Reward accurate recording, not more entries

Each ward can earn the meal once during its eight-week reward window. There are no individual prizes or ward rankings. Infection Prevention uses a small sample from the **existing monthly observer audit** to calculate three separate numbers:

- **Logging coverage:** correctly performed actions with one valid kiosk match ÷ all correctly performed actions observed in the sample. Milestone: **80%**, with at least 30 observed actions across representative shifts. A correctly performed action recorded only via the fallback counts toward clinical compliance, not kiosk coverage.
- **Logging accuracy:** uniquely corroborated entries ÷ all entries claiming actions in the sampled windows. Require **90%**. Outside those windows, entries are unverified, not failures.
- **Clinical compliance:** hand-hygiene actions performed ÷ all observed opportunities, including missed ones. This is a safety check, not the prize score. [WHO hand-hygiene technical reference manual](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/infection-prevention-and-control/hand-hygiene/monitoring-tools)

Before launch, test whether existing records can reliably match an action by ward, moment and timing, using an existing care-zone field if available. Set the time window from the dry run and freeze it. Test two similar actions close together, a double tap, an undone entry, a wrong moment and an old-route record. A confirmed duplicate gets no extra credit; an undone entry gets none; ambiguous matches earn none. If reliable matching would require staff or patient identifiers, new monitoring, or an expanded audit, **do not offer a logging prize**. Have the director recognise verified workflow fixes instead.

### Show progress without tracking people

The kiosk confirms each submission immediately: “Before patient contact saved. Undo.” It does not say “compliance verified.” At the existing staff huddle, show only the ward’s last verified result and sample size, such as “23 of 30 sampled actions logged; target 24.” Managers see the same aggregate, with small samples suppressed and no person or shift drill-down. No scores feed performance reviews. No streaks: after an absence or missed entry, resume at the next safe opportunity.

### Run it, pause it, end it

Give all wards the fixes and feedback for one baseline audit cycle. Randomise start order, then open eight-week reward windows four weeks apart. Compare each ward with its own baseline and with wards still on feedback alone. With just three wards, treat differences as directional. The quality lead owns the dashboard and corrections; budget 45 minutes a week outside routine audit work. Staff can flag a wrong count privately without naming a patient.

Infection Prevention holds rewards if a sample is too small, entry accuracy falls below 90%, or observed compliance drops five percentage points or more below that ward’s baseline. Pause immediately if logging delays care or hand hygiene. Investigate, fix the cause, and require a fresh passing sample before resuming. Keep a fallback during outages; don’t ask staff to reconstruct entries from memory.

At week eight, close each ward’s reward window. Keep the kiosk shortcut, paid onboarding and feedback. Four weeks later, use the same **verified logging coverage** measure: keep the workflow without prizes at **80% or higher**, fix and retest at **60–79%**, and drop the kiosk as the default below **60%**. Safety overrides the score. If adoption rose only during the prize period, don’t renew the prize.

## Key Learnings:

1. More entries can mean more duplicates or selective logging, not better hygiene. Keep the observer denominator independent of the kiosk.
2. Staff may behave differently while observed. Rotate audit windows across shifts, keep exact times out of the reward announcements, and don’t claim the sample represents every shift or proves fewer infections.
