# Safe Start: pre-job checklist pilot

**Reward a real safety decision, not a green checkmark.** A technician earns one credit for completing the digital pre-job checklist and recording the appropriate decision before work begins: proceed, or stop and escalate. A properly documented stop counts even if the job never starts. No rewards for jobs completed, all-clear answers, or fewer incidents or hazard reports.

### Before launch

Have the safety lead and union steward walk through real jobs with technicians from different shifts, including low-signal routes. Fix the barriers they find first: put the checklist at the top of the work order, prefill job details but never safety answers, and test offline save and sync. Give everyone 20 minutes of paid practice and allow time for the check in dispatch schedules. Post “You reported it, we fixed it” updates at existing crew huddles.

Agree the reward, scoring, appeals and data limits with the union in writing. The incentive is voluntary; the pre-job safety check is not. Keep the approved fallback available when the app or device fails. Never delay a stop or ask someone to use a phone in an unsafe spot.

### What counts and what people see

**Launch message:** “Do the digital safety check before work starts. A documented safety stop counts just like a safe start. Reach your private goal and earn 30 minutes of paid time off, once. No leaderboard.”

- **Score from existing job records.** Match the submitted checklist to the dispatch work order and its recorded job start or safety hold. Count only one valid pre-job decision per work order. Duplicate uploads do not add credit; a voided record loses credit unless another valid pre-job record exists. A paper check or late digital entry does not earn digital credit. An offline check can count after sync if the existing records establish that it happened in time. If those records cannot reliably establish the sequence, fix the workflow before launching rather than adding worker tracking.
- **Measure digital adoption against all eligible dispatched jobs that reach the pre-job check stage.** Include work stopped for safety and jobs checked on paper; exclude jobs cancelled before that stage. Show fallback use separately so app trouble is visible, not mistaken for skipped safety checks.
- **Make the milestone reachable.** The goal is 60% of a technician’s eligible opportunities over four weeks, rounded up and capped at six credits. Show an estimated target from scheduled work and reconcile it to actual opportunities so leave and lighter routes don’t count against anyone. No streaks or weekly resets. Award the paid time privately and let technicians schedule it without losing pay.
- **Keep progress private.** The app confirms “Pre-job check recorded” or “Saved offline; credit pending” and shows only the technician’s own progress. The weekly huddle shows a pooled crew adoption rate and the latest fixes, with small groups combined to prevent identification. Managers have no individual drill-down. Pilot data never feeds performance reviews or discipline. The steward takes count disputes; the pilot coordinator corrects the record or explains the result within five working days.

### Timing and decision

Give all crews two weeks with the fixes, paid practice and feedback. Then randomly assign comparable crews to four-week reward windows starting two weeks apart. Compare their change in adoption over the first two weeks, when only the early crews have rewards. Publish the end dates at launch. When each window closes, remove the reward counter but keep the checklist first in the work order, along with offline support and the fallback.

Use one decision metric: **the share of eligible jobs with a valid digital pre-job decision**. Compare it with the fixes-only baseline and check it again four weeks after each crew’s reward ends. Pre-agree the decision: retain the workflow without prizes if the follow-up rate is at least 80% and the quality gate is clear; at 60–79%, fix the remaining barriers and retest; below 60%, drop the incentive approach and redesign the digital workflow. The safety check stays in every case.

## Key Learnings:

1. **A timestamp is not proof that the check happened.** Use ten jobs a week from existing independent safety spot checks, including credited and uncredited jobs, to compare the recorded decision with observed checks or independently documented site conditions. The safety lead pauses new awards for any critical missed hazard, or if two of ten checks lack substantive evidence. The lead and steward review causes before restarting. Keep the routine weekly review under an hour by using the existing audit process; if it cannot support this check, don’t launch rewards.
2. **Never make reporting a hazard cost someone a reward.** A worker who stops the job keeps the same credit as someone who safely proceeds. A credible report of pressure to rush, backdate, or suppress hazards triggers an immediate pause and joint review, without suspending the safety process.
