# Close the Ticket, Keep the Answer

**Launch message:** “Close the ticket with an article that would help the next agent solve it. If your team does that on 35% of resolved tickets during its four-week pilot and passes the quality check, everyone on the team gets one hour of paid time off. Your CSAT bonus stays exactly as it is.”

### Make it easier before offering a prize

Give six agents across queues and shifts 30 minutes of paid time to walk through real tickets: one with a good article, one where search fails, and one that needs a new article. Fix the biggest blockers first. Put the article picker first in the resolve screen, prefill search from the ticket topic, and offer a draft template when nothing fits. Keep **Resolve without an article** available. Publish a short “You reported it, we fixed it” note and give everyone paid practice time.

Schedule protected writing time off the live queue. Resolve the customer’s issue first; never hold a ticket open waiting for an article to be written or approved. If there’s no reusable lesson, skip the article rather than inventing one.

### Reward one useful resolution, not one more link

**Counting rule:** A resolved support ticket earns one credit if it has a relevant, approved article that explains the actual fix, whether reused at close or newly written and approved within seven days.

Use existing ticket IDs, resolution records, article links and publication status. Count each ticket once. Before launch, test these cases with agents: two links still equal one credit; merged duplicates count only under the surviving ticket; a reopened ticket loses provisional credit until resolved again; a draft alone earns nothing; and a real ticket closed the old way stays in the denominator but earns no credit. Exclude spam and test tickets using existing dispositions. Don’t exclude hard cases or tickets with no suitable article. If the existing records can’t verify an event, don’t add employee tracking to count it.

### Give agents feedback, not a ranking

At close, show a private confirmation: “Article linked to ticket #1234; one provisional credit,” or “Draft pending approval.” Give each agent a private, cumulative practice goal of roughly one in three of **their own** resolved tickets, not a fixed quota. Missing a week costs nothing; pick up on the next case.

Post each team’s progress toward 35% weekly in its existing support channel. Managers see the same team totals, with small groups rolled up and no individual drill-down. No leaderboards, individual exports, or use in performance reviews or CSAT pay. Announce the paid hour at launch, have the support director thank qualifying teams in their huddle, and let agents book the time across shifts without losing coverage.

### Run it for eight weeks, then stop paying

- **Weeks 1–2:** Everyone gets the same fixes, defaults and private feedback. Recalculate the reported 12% baseline using the pilot’s counting rule and audit method.
- **Weeks 3–6:** Half the existing teams start earning the reward. **Weeks 5–8:** The other half get the same four-week opportunity. Pair teams with similar queues and baselines, then draw start order. Compare the change in weeks 3–4 for early versus waiting teams to see what the prize adds beyond the fixes. Treat the comparison as directional.
- **After each window:** Allow seven days for approvals and final checks, then award earned time. Keep the picker, private feedback and protected writing time. End prize messaging and team scoreboards.

### Catch bad incentives and decide what stays

The KB lead uses existing QA access to sample 12 credited tickets weekly, split across active teams and including both reused and new articles. Check that the article matches the actual resolution, is usable, and contains no customer data. Remove failed credits. The support ops lead pauses reward accrual for the affected team if more than 10% of its rolling four-week sample fails, and pauses immediately for exposed customer data. Also pause and investigate if team CSAT falls at least five percentage points or the seven-day reopen rate rises at least three points versus the pre-pilot baseline over two comparable weeks. Check response counts and queue mix before interpreting a CSAT swing. Never change the CSAT bonus. Agents can privately send a ticket ID to the KB lead, who fixes mistaken counts within two working days. Budget 45 minutes a week for the sample, scorecard and corrections.

**Four weeks after the last prize window**, use one decision number: **useful coverage** (credited tickets divided by all resolved support tickets, discounted by the sampled failure rate). Set the thresholds before launch: **30% or more**, keep the workflow without prizes; **20% to under 30%**, fix the most common blocker and retest without prizes; **below 20%**, drop the campaign mechanics and redesign the closeout flow. An unresolved quality or customer-service breach overrides any apparent win.
