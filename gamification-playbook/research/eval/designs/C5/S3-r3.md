## Clean hands first: a three-ward kiosk pilot

**Reward accurate logging, not more logging.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, offered on every shift, with an in-person thank-you from the chief nurse. Announce it at launch: “Our ward earns a meal if at least 6 in 10 hand-hygiene opportunities sampled by our usual observers were both correctly completed and accurately logged in the kiosk, without a drop that triggers the safety pause.” Every ward can earn it. No individual prizes, rankings, or penalties.

### Make the kiosk worth using

Before launch, walk the workflow with day, night, and float nurses from each ward. Give them 15 minutes of paid practice. Fix queues, unreliable access, and awkward kiosk placement before offering rewards. Make the logging screen the default, prefill the ward, and let nurses correct an entry without starting over. Tell staff what changed because of their feedback. Show wards what their reports helped fix, so logging has a purpose after the meal. **Clean hands and patient care always come first. Never leave care to log.**

### Count one real opportunity once

Use the existing monthly Infection Prevention audits. Observers record opportunities and whether hygiene was performed before seeing kiosk data. In a small sample of existing observation windows, reconcile the records using the kiosk’s existing location, timestamp, and moment type. Count a match only if the observer can confirm the same event was correctly performed and logged, without recording a nurse or patient identifier. If the existing workflow cannot establish that link reliably, run the kiosk improvements and feedback **without** the prize.

The ward score is **uniquely matched, correctly completed and logged opportunities ÷ all observed opportunities in those windows**. A duplicate adds nothing; an undone entry does not count; a corrected entry counts once if it still matches. Hygiene performed but not logged, including during downtime or on a fallback form, counts toward clinical compliance but not kiosk adoption. Report downtime separately. Trial those cases before launch.

Use at least 30 observed opportunities per ward across the two reward audits, covering days, nights, and weekends. If coverage is inadequate, defer the decision rather than call it a miss. Vary observation windows within the existing audit schedule and don’t publish them. Observation can itself change behavior, so report the sample size and don’t present it as a census. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

### Show progress without watching individuals

The kiosk confirms “Saved: [moment type]” immediately, not “compliant.” Staff and managers see only their ward’s verified progress and separate clinical audit rate in the usual huddle report. No individual scores, manager drill-down, or performance-review use. A missed shift or week resets nothing. Nurses can report a wrong count privately to the ward champion; Infection Prevention rules on eligibility, and the kiosk administrator fixes any record or counting error. The champion uses the existing huddle and spends under an hour a week on the pilot.

### Start together, stagger rewards, and stop on schedule

Give all three wards the fixes and feedback during a four-week run-in. Use that month’s audit as the baseline and confirm the milestone is attainable before launch. Randomly assign wards to start rewards four weeks apart. Each gets two monthly audits to earn the meal, then rewards end. Compare each ward with its own baseline and with wards still on fixes and feedback alone. With three wards, treat the comparison as directional, not proof.

Infection Prevention pauses rewards if a ward’s **observed clinical compliance falls 10 percentage points below baseline**, or immediately if logging is found to delay care. Investigate before restarting. More kiosk entries cannot override that pause.

At the next audit, about four weeks after rewards end, use the verified logging score to decide: **60% or more**, keep the kiosk defaults and feedback without prizes; **40–59%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck once; **below 40%** on an adequate sample, drop the kiosk rollout and review a lower-burden reporting option. Routine hand hygiene and monthly audits continue regardless.
