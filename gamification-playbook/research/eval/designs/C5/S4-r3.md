# Close the ticket. Leave an answer worth reusing.

**Fix the workflow before offering a prize.** Sit with six agents across queues and shifts and watch them close real tickets. Ask what makes linking or writing slower than the current process, and what makes it feel risky under the CSAT bonus. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and protect time for drafting and review during shifts. Put relevant article suggestions and a prefilled draft in the ticket closeout screen. Never auto-attach an article or block a resolution. Post the changes in the support channel: “You told us search took too long, so we put article suggestions in the closeout screen.” Don’t start rewards until agents can use both paths without making customers wait.

**Reward one useful resolution, not one article.** The launch rule: “A resolved ticket counts once if it has a relevant, accurate, published article linked to it, whether you reused one or wrote it.” Use existing ticket IDs, resolution and reopen history, article links, and publication status. Give immediate private feedback when a link is recorded or a draft is pending. Finalize credit seven days after resolution: a new article must be published and linked by then, and a reopened ticket must be re-resolved before it can qualify. A removed link loses provisional credit; multiple links or repeated closes never earn extra credit. A ticket closed the old way still counts in the denominator, not the numerator. Exclude only pre-defined system categories such as spam and merged duplicates. If nothing reusable or safe to document exists, close the ticket anyway. No filler article.

**Make the reward visible without ranking people.** Announce at launch: “If your team reaches 30% useful coverage over its four-week window and passes the quality check, everyone on the team earns two hours of paid time off.” Check that 30% is reachable across queue mixes during the run-in, then lock the threshold before the first wave. Every team can win. The support director announces the award at the team huddle, thanks the team for a specific useful article, and schedules the time off with coverage. Agents see only their own progress and a goal sized to their normal ticket volume, such as three useful links per ten closes. The existing support channel shows team totals and progress toward the milestone. Managers get no individual drill-down; pilot data stays out of performance reviews and pay calculations. No leaderboard, streak, or penalty for missing a week. Just start with the next case.

**Protect service quality and the existing CSAT bonus.** Leave the CSAT formula and payouts unchanged. Don’t hold a customer reply or resolution for article approval. Before launch, replay a duplicate link, an unlink, a reopen, a late publication, and an old-way close against the counting rule. Each week the KB lead checks ten randomly selected credited cases across teams for relevance, accuracy, and safe content. Estimate useful coverage as the credited-ticket rate multiplied by the sample pass rate. The support ops lead pauses awards if two or more of ten links fail, if any article exposes customer data, or if rolling two-week CSAT falls five percentage points below the pre-pilot baseline with at least 30 responses. Review reopen rates and backlog alongside CSAT to catch premature closes or drafting that crowds out service. The KB lead fixes or retires bad articles; support ops corrects counts through a private appeal with the ticket ID. Use the existing report and QA queue so one owner can review the pilot in under an hour a week.

**End the prize on schedule.** Give everyone two weeks of fixes and feedback first. Then randomly assign comparable teams to four-week reward windows starting two weeks apart. During the overlap when only the early teams have rewards, compare their change from the run-in with that of the teams still on feedback alone. Keep the article picker and draft shortcut as the default closeout path after awards stop. Four weeks after the last award window, use **audited useful coverage** to decide: at least 30%, keep the workflow without prizes; 20–29%, fix the biggest remaining obstacle and recheck; below 20%, drop the campaign and redesign the workflow with agents. Don’t renew the prize to prop up the number.
