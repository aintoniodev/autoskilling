# One working day for a useful code review

**Fix the queue before offering a reward.** In week 1, use a paid 30-minute session with six engineers, including frequent authors, reviewers and a code owner, to replay recent slow reviews. Fix the blockers they identify: route review-ready PRs to a rotating reviewer and backup, reserve 30 minutes a day for whoever is on rotation, and take equivalent work off their sprint plan. Put the oldest review-ready PR first in the existing review queue. Post “You reported it, we fixed it” in the team channel. If the team still lacks review capacity, fix that before starting the reward.

### What earns the reward

**Launch message:** “Get at least 80% of review-ready PRs a useful first review within one working day over your three-week pilot, without cutting review quality, and everyone in your group gets a two-hour paid project block of their choice.”

Agree the reward with engineers and get the time approved before announcing it. The engineering lead thanks each qualifying group in person and protects the time on the calendar, with coverage arranged. Both groups can earn it. No points, rankings, individual prizes or streaks.

### What counts

Use existing PR events. Count **one first useful review per PR**: a genuine approval or a submitted review that gives the author a specific change, question or next step. No credit for “looking soon,” bot messages, self-review or emoji. A review can request changes; the goal is an answer, not a merge.

- **Start:** When a human-authored PR is first marked ready, or opened as ready. **Deadline:** Eight working hours on the published team calendar, excluding nights, weekends and holidays. A Friday 4 p.m. PR is due Monday at 4 p.m. on a 9-to-5 calendar, unless Monday is a holiday.
- **Score:** Of all eligible PRs whose deadline fell in the measurement window, what share got a useful review by that deadline? Unanswered, late and merged-without-review PRs are misses. A genuinely withdrawn PR closed unmerged before the deadline is excluded and reported separately.
- **Dry-run before launch:** A duplicate review adds nothing. A handoff, reopen or draft toggle cannot reset the clock. A dismissed rubber-stamp doesn’t count; use the next qualifying review’s timestamp. A hallway or chat review counts only if the reviewer records its disposition on the PR by the deadline. Recalculate the preceding four weeks with the same rule; the stated 2.6-day median does not tell you the baseline one-day rate.

### What people see

Give the reviewer a private confirmation on submission: “PR #184 answered in 5 working hours; counted provisionally.” During their rotation, their private goal is to answer or hand off the work they actually receive in time for the deadline. No quota on quiet weeks, and leave is covered by the backup. Post the pooled numerator and denominator weekly in the existing team channel. Managers get aggregates only, no individual drill-down, and nothing goes into performance reviews.

### Timing and ending

- **Weeks 1–2:** Both existing work groups get the fixes, feedback and quality checks. If both already meet 80%, skip the prize.
- **Weeks 3–5:** Randomly pick one group to start its reward window. The other keeps the same workflow and feedback. Compare each group with its own baseline and use this overlap as a directional check on what the reward added, not proof.
- **Weeks 6–8:** The second group gets the same three-week opportunity. The first group’s reward window is over.
- **Weeks 9–12:** No more prizes. Keep the oldest-first queue, protected capacity and backups. Use the four-week one-day response rate to decide: **80% or more**, keep the workflow if quality held; **65–79%**, fix the biggest remaining bottleneck and retest; **below 65%**, retire the incentive reporting and redesign routing or capacity. Don’t restart prizes to rescue the number.

## Key Learnings:

1. **A fast rubber stamp is still a miss.** In every live phase, a peer pilot owner randomly checks five submitted reviews per group each week against the same standard: does the review address the actual change and give a defensible disposition? If two of a group’s latest ten sampled reviews fail, the engineering lead pauses payout and fixes the cause before resuming. The pilot owner corrects miscoded PRs privately and republishes the total. Budget 45 minutes a week for checks, corrections and reporting.
2. **Watch what gets pushed out of the queue.** Review the oldest overdue PRs and any spike in withdrawals or draft toggles at the weekly check. If hard PRs are piling up or reviewers are working after hours, assign a qualified backup or cut sprint scope. Don’t exclude hard PRs or ask people to work nights to hit the target.
