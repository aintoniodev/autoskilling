# S4: Resolve it once, make the answer reusable

**Fix the workflow before offering a reward.** Sit with six agents across shifts and queues as they close real tickets. Find out where search fails, which answers are missing, and when writing would delay a customer. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and protected writing time during shifts, with queue coverage. Put suggested articles and a prefilled draft template in the resolution screen. Tell agents what changed: “You told us search took too long; now you can find and attach an article without leaving the ticket.” Don’t start the reward while search or article approval is still a bottleneck.

**Reward useful resolutions, not documentation activity.** The rule agents see: **“A genuinely resolved ticket counts once when it has a relevant, published article that explains the fix, whether you reused it or wrote it.”** Link an existing article first. If none fits, resolve the customer’s issue without waiting, flag the gap, and draft during protected time. The KB editor checks accuracy, duplicates and customer data before publishing; the ticket can earn credit if the article is published and linked within seven days. Drafts, generic links and gap flags earn nothing.

Use existing ticket status, ticket ID, article-link and publication records. The denominator is all genuine customer tickets resolved in the period, including tickets closed the old way or with no suitable article; exclude spam, tests and merged duplicates. Attribute credit to the team that resolved the ticket. Before launch, test that two links earn one credit, removing the only valid link removes credit, and reopening within seven days reverses provisional credit until a valid re-close. A ticket never earns a second credit. If existing records can’t verify the event, fix the workflow before offering rewards; don’t add agent tracking.

**Give agents feedback and teams a reachable milestone.** At close, show the agent “Article linked to this resolution; credit pending” or explain what’s missing. Show private progress toward a personal goal sized to their own caseload, such as 3 of their next 10 genuine resolutions. A missed week changes nothing; the next case is the next opportunity. Post only team totals and progress to the milestone in the existing support channel. Managers get no individual drill-down, named rankings or exports for reviews.

Announce the reward up front: **“If your team covers 30% of genuine resolutions during its four-week window and passes the quality check, everyone on the team gets one paid hour off, rostered with queue cover.”** Confirm agents value the reward and can reach the milestone once gaps are fixed. If a team is already at 30% in the run-in, agree and announce a higher, reachable milestone before launch. The support lead thanks the team in person for a specific article that helped; no individual qualification and no changes to CSAT pay.

**Protect service quality.** Each week, the KB editor samples 12 credited tickets across queues and shifts, checking that the article is accurate, relevant, reusable and free of customer data. Pause rewards if fewer than 90% pass across two weeks, or immediately for any customer-data exposure. Support Ops also pauses the affected team if its two-week CSAT falls 3 percentage points below its run-in baseline with at least 30 responses, or its seven-day reopen rate rises 2 points. Check CSAT response rates and queue mix before interpreting a swing; don’t treat sparse feedback as a clean bill of health. Keep survey rules and bonus terms unchanged. If pressure leads to filler links, rushed closes or cherry-picking, fix the workflow before restarting. Agents can privately send a disputed ticket ID to Support Ops, who corrects the count; the support lead decides when rewards resume. Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling and reporting using the existing QA workflow.

**Test the reward, then end it.** Give every team the same fixes, paid time and feedback for a two-week run-in. Use the same counting rule to remeasure the reported 12%. Assign comparable teams to start rewards two weeks apart, with four reward weeks each. Compare each team’s change from the run-in during the two weeks when only the early teams have rewards. Leave article suggestions open by default at close, without auto-linking or blocking resolution. Stop prizes on schedule and leave the workflow and feedback in place.

Four weeks after each team’s prizes end, decide using **quality-adjusted coverage**: credited tickets divided by eligible resolutions, multiplied by the audit pass rate. Compared with the run-in, **keep** the workflow and feedback without prizes at a gain of at least 10 percentage points; **change** the workflow and retest at a smaller positive gain; **drop** the incentive at no gain. Don’t scale a programme with unresolved service or privacy harms, even if coverage rises.
