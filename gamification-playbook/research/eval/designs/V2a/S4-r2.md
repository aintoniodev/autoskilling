## 1. Diagnosis

**Known:** Agents link or write a knowledge-base article on 12% of resolved tickets. That figure establishes a starting point. The reason for low adoption is unverified.

Before launch, the Support Operations lead walks two cases with agents. On a routine reset, check whether the agent can find and link the approved article from the resolution screen. On a sensitive billing exception with no suitable article, check whether the agent can close the ticket safely and flag the gap for the knowledge-base editor. Time both paths and ask what agents skip when the queue is busy. Fix access, search, and review delays first. Defer the incentive if most tickets still lack a practical, safe path.

**Assumptions:** The ticketing system records resolution, reopen, and link history. An editor can review articles within two business days. The sponsor can fund protected time. Confirm all three before launch.

## 2. Credit rule

When a support agent resolves a ticket, it earns **one credit** if the agent links an approved, relevant article that actually supports the resolution. If there is a genuine knowledge gap, the agent can submit a reusable article for review. Credit posts once the editor approves and publishes it and the agent links it to the ticket. Agents close tickets on time and flag gaps without exposing customer information. The editor can reuse one approved article across distinct tickets where it genuinely applies.

The pilot measures credited tickets divided by all eligible resolved tickets. Eligibility comes from a predefined ticket classification and auditable exceptions, independent of whether an agent added a link. Report coverage across **all** resolved tickets and the exclusion rate alongside the pilot metric so changes in eligibility are visible.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Relevant approved article linked | One credit |
| Two links, repeat submission, or duplicate ticket | One credit for the underlying case |
| Ticket reopened or article withdrawn | Reverse provisional credit. Restore it once the case qualifies again. |
| Due date edited or article approved late | Use the actual resolution timestamp. Count eligible articles approved within five business days of resolution. |
| Eligible ticket closed using the old workflow | Zero credit. Ticket stays in the denominator. |
| Eligible ticket omitted from the programme feed | Recover it from the full resolution log and verify any credit before payout. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **At resolution:** “Link the approved article you used so the next agent can reuse the answer. Missing an article? Flag the gap and close the ticket as usual. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation:** “Approved article linked. This ticket counts. If you submitted a new article, we’ll confirm credit once it’s approved and linked.”
- **Private progress:** “You’ve covered 1 of your next 3 eligible tickets. On the next one, link the article you used.” Progress carries across weeks. Agents with zero eligible tickets see “No eligible tickets yet.”
- **Restart:** “Your previous work still counts. Start again with the next eligible ticket.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own tickets and credits. The existing team channel shows the pilot group’s verified coverage against its January baseline, with no names. Suppress any breakdown with fewer than five contributors or where someone’s activity can be inferred. Managers see aggregates. Support Operations grants logged, time-limited access to individual records for corrections, disputes, or agent-requested coaching. Programme data stays out of discipline and CSAT bonus calculations.

## 5. Reward

If the pilot group finishes **10 percentage points above its January eligible-ticket baseline** and clears the quality check, everyone in the group gets **30 minutes of paid, queue-covered learning time**, including agents with few eligible tickets. Test the reward and threshold with a few agents before launch. Share one concrete workflow fix each week so staff can see what their feedback changed.

## 6. Operator card

Support Operations owns the resolution-log reconciliation, score, and private correction route. Budget **two hours weekly** for reconciliation and QA, plus article editing. An independent Support QA reviewer samples 20 credited pilot tickets weekly for relevance, accuracy, and safe handling. At **3 failures in 20**, or **any exposure of sensitive data**, the Support Operations lead pauses scoring and payout, corrects the issue, and requires a clean recheck before resuming. Agents can dispute a credit privately and get a response within two business days.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 4–31, 2027** as baseline, **February 1–28** as the pilot, and **March 1–28** as reward-free follow-up. Randomly assign comparable teams to pilot or comparison where staffing permits. Both get the workflow fixes and private feedback. Only the pilot gets shared progress and the reward. Settle credits after the five-business-day window.

Scale if the pilot improves by **10 points more than comparison during the pilot** and **5 points more afterward**, with quality passing and no sustained deterioration in CSAT or resolution time. Change the workflow if quality holds but lift fades. Stop if quality or service worsens and remains worse after correction. Without a credible comparison, report feasibility only.

## 8. Biggest risks

Irrelevant links inflate coverage. Documentation delays customer replies or puts CSAT earnings under pressure. Agents revert when paid learning time ends. Audit relevance, protect queue coverage, and use the reward-free follow-up to test durability.
