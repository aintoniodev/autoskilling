## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently have a digital checklist. That figure alone can’t tell us whether technicians lack access, find the app slower than the current process, or haven’t tried it. Before launch, the field safety lead and a union steward should ride along on one supported job and one blocked job. Check access, signal, device usability with gloves, time to complete, and whether the app handles a reported hazard. Give technicians paid time to complete the checklist. The field operations lead owns fixes, acknowledges blockers by the next working day, and posts a weekly “you reported it, we fixed it” update. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds material rework, fix the workflow before launching.

## 2. Credit rule

When a dispatched job is ready to start, the assigned technician earns **one credit per job** for completing the required checklist in the app before work begins. The checklist must record actual observations and any required escalation. A technician who finds a hazard can earn credit for completing the checklist and following the safety procedure, even if work never starts. If the app fails, follow the approved fallback and report the blocker. If the required safety check can’t be completed, hold the job under the existing safety procedure.

Measure adoption as verified digital completions divided by all dispatched jobs that reach the pre-work safety check, including hazard holds, fallback cases, and omissions. Report fallback and blocked jobs alongside adoption. Assume dispatch history and protected app timestamps can establish event order, including offline submissions. Verify that before attaching a reward.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal job | One credit for a valid checklist before the recorded work start. |
| Duplicate or reassignment | One credit per job ID, assigned to the technician who completed the valid check. |
| Recall or reversal | Remove provisional credit if the checklist is invalidated. Leave paid rewards in place. |
| Late or edited schedule | Use actual work start and event history. A schedule edit can’t turn a late checklist into an on-time one. |
| Approved fallback | Count separately as a fallback. It earns zero digital credit. |
| Omitted checklist | Keep the job in the denominator with zero credit, and refer it through the existing safety process. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at dispatch:** “Before you start this job, open Safety Checklist in the app. It saves your answers with the job so the crew can find the safety record. If the app fails, use the approved fallback. Team rewards end 7 March.”
- **Confirmation:** “Checklist saved for job 4821 before work started. One credit pending verification.”
- **Progress:** “3 of your 4 checklist goal this week. Open Safety Checklist on your next job.” Set the goal at 60% of expected opportunities, rounded up and capped at actual opportunities. For one job, the goal is one. For zero, show “No jobs needing a checklist this week.” The safety requirement still applies to every job.
- **Restart:** “New week, fresh goal. Open Safety Checklist before you start your next job.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress privately. The existing dispatch board shows weekly verified team completion against its own baseline, without names. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress teams and breakdowns with fewer than 10 technicians, including totals that could reveal a suppressed figure. A designated safety data steward handles individual corrections, disputes, and requested coaching through logged, time-limited access. Programme data stays out of discipline. Agree the access and retention terms with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Reward

A team that reaches **60% verified completion over four weeks** and passes the quality check earns a company-funded meal on paid time, with equivalent access across shifts and no attendance requirement. Everyone on the team shares the reward regardless of job volume. Test the reward and target with technicians and union representatives before launch. Assume approval and budget are available.

## 6. Operator card

The field safety lead reconciles dispatch, app event history, and fallback records. Each week a safety reviewer independent of the reward decision samples 20 credited jobs across teams and checks timing, observations, and hazard handling against available job evidence. **Any critical safety bypass or two invalid credits in a weekly sample** pauses promotion and reward qualification. The safety lead investigates with the union steward and resumes after correction and a clean recheck. Technicians can dispute a credit privately with the data steward, who resolves it within five working days. Budget three staff-hours weekly for reconciliation and review, plus blocker fixes.

## 7. End and decision

Use **11 January–7 February 2027** as baseline, **8 February–7 March** for the reward, and **8 March–4 April** for reward-free follow-up. Randomly assign comparable dispatch teams to the programme or to the same app support, fixes, and private feedback. Keep fixes and private feedback after rewards end.

Scale if the programme’s baseline-adjusted lift over comparison teams is at least 15 percentage points during the pilot and 10 points afterward, with no unresolved quality or privacy issues. Revise if the lift fades or blockers dominate. Stop if there’s no positive lift or safety or privacy problems recur. Treat results as feasibility evidence if too few independent teams support a credible comparison.

## 8. Risks

- Technicians rush through checks or downplay hazards to help the team qualify.
- Poor connectivity and uneven job mix make some teams’ targets harder to reach.
- Small-team reporting or manager access makes the programme feel like surveillance and discourages blocker reporting.
