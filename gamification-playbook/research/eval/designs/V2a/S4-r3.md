## 1. Diagnosis

The reported article coverage is **12% of resolved tickets**. That tells us how often agents use the tool, but the cause is unknown. Before launch, shadow one routine resolution with a usable article and one blocked resolution, such as a ticket with sensitive content and restricted publishing access. Compare the time and rework with the existing close process. Fix access, search, or approval delays before adding stakes. If most cases are blocked or the workflow still costs agents more time than it saves, defer the incentive. The Support Ops lead owns blockers and responds within one business day. Agents can always close the ticket normally.

## 2. Credit rule

When a support agent resolves a ticket, they earn **one credit per ticket** for linking an approved article that covers the actual resolution, or for writing an article that a KB editor approves and links. The agent sees provisional credit immediately after linking or submitting. Credit locks seven calendar days after first resolution if the article qualifies and the ticket is resolved. The closing agent receives the credit. Article views, drafts alone, and additional links earn zero.

Measure credited tickets divided by **all unique resolved tickets**, using the ticket system to supply the denominator. Keep blocked and omitted tickets in it, and report the blocked share separately. Recalculate the reported 12% under this rule before setting targets.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Relevant approved article linked | One credit at lock |
| Multiple links, duplicate events, or merged tickets | At most one credit for the canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopened or article withdrawn | Remove provisional credit. Restore it if the resolution and article qualify by lock. Correct verified errors in the audit record. |
| Late approval or edited due date | Approval after lock earns zero. Due-date edits have no effect. |
| Agent resolves the ticket the usual way | Ticket stays in the denominator and earns zero without a qualifying link. |
| Ticket missing from the KB feed | Reconcile against ticket-system records. Keep the ticket in the denominator and correct any documented credit. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution:** “Have an article that covers this fix? Link it now so the next agent can reuse your answer. If it’s missing, submit one for review. If you’re blocked, close the ticket as usual and flag it to Support Ops. The team challenge ends March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked to ticket #4821. One provisional credit. We’ll confirm it seven days after resolution.” For a submission, say “Article submitted for review” and show its status.
- **Progress:** “2 of 3 verified this week. On your next resolution, check for a matching article.” Start personal goals at one in four forecast supported resolutions, rounded up. For one to three opportunities, aim for one. For zero, show “No opportunities this week.” Adjust goals when volume changes.
- **Restart:** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Your earlier credits still count.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own tickets, credits, and corrections. The incentive group sees its aggregate verified rate against its baseline in the existing weekly support huddle. Managers receive aggregates. Suppress any breakdown covering fewer than 10 agents or otherwise identifying someone. A designated Support Ops reviewer can open individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log access, close it when the case is resolved, and restrict it to that purpose.

## 5. Motivator

Test **one paid hour of protected time** with a few agents before launch. Everyone in the incentive group earns it if the group improves verified coverage by **10 percentage points** over baseline and passes the quality gate. Schedule coverage so agents can take the hour. The existing CSAT bonus is calculated independently. Share one concrete fix from agent reports in each weekly huddle.

## 6. Operator card

**Assumptions:** Ticket and KB histories retain stable IDs, timestamps, approvals, and reversals. Editors can review submissions inside seven days, and leadership can fund coverage. If history cannot support the table test, run the workflow without stakes.

Support Ops reconciles the ticket and KB records and handles corrections through the existing support channel. An independent QA lead samples 24 credited tickets weekly across both groups for relevance, accuracy, and safe content. Pause reward accrual at three failed checks in a weekly sample or one privacy incident. QA and Support Ops investigate, correct records, and resume after a clean recheck. Budget two operator hours weekly plus article editing.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline **Jan 11–Feb 7, 2027**. Run **Feb 8–Mar 7**. Assuming comparable queues or shifts, match and randomly assign them to the incentive or comparison group. Both get the same workflow fixes and private feedback. End reward accrual March 7 and measure through **Apr 4**. Apply the seven-day lock before scoring.

Scale if coverage improves at least 10 points during the programme and retains a five-point improvement over the comparison group’s change after rewards end, with at least 90% QA passes and no more than a two-point relative decline in CSAT or increase in reopens. Revise if uptake fades or evidence is thin. Stop if customer outcomes breach those limits or privacy failures recur.

## 8. Risks

- Agents link generic articles to earn credit. Sample the fit against the actual resolution.
- Agents rush closures or skip valuable authoring to protect CSAT. Track reopens, CSAT, and approval delays.
- Approval backlogs make the milestone feel arbitrary. Publish blocked counts and fix editor capacity before awarding stakes.
