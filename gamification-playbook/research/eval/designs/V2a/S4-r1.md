## 1. Diagnosis

The reported 12% attachment rate tells us where to look. Before launch, the Support Ops lead shadows two cases with agents. On a repeat issue, find an approved article, check that it supports the fix, and link it at resolution. On a case with no usable article, walk through drafting and approval, then use the fallback if approval would delay closure. Time both paths and confirm with agents whether the link would help the next person handle the issue.

Support Ops owns search, permissions, and approval blockers and responds within one business day. Agents resolve tickets on time and flag blockers in the ticket. If most cases are blocked or the supported path adds effort agents see no value in, fix the workflow before starting rewards. **Assumptions to verify** include reliable ticket and article histories, an independent reviewer, comparable support pods, and approval for paid time.

## 2. Credit rule

When a support agent resolves a ticket, the ticket earns **one credit** if the agent links an approved article that accurately documents the resolution, or submits a reusable article at closure that passes editorial review and is linked within seven days. Never delay closure for credit. Exclude cases barred by policy from documentation using auditable reason codes. Missing articles, tool failures, and skipped prompts stay in the denominator. Measure credited tickets divided by all eligible resolved tickets, and report coverage across all resolved tickets alongside it. Recalculate the 12% baseline under this rule.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Approved article matches the fix | One credit |
| Two links, a duplicate ticket, or multiple agents | One credit per canonical ticket, assigned to the final resolver |
| Ticket reopens or article is withdrawn during verification | Reverse provisional credit. A subsequent qualifying resolution can earn one credit. |
| SLA due date changes or a link is added after closure | Use the recorded resolution timestamp. Only an article submitted at closure gets the seven-day approval window. |
| Ticket follows the old workflow | No credit. Eligible ticket stays in the denominator. |
| Ticket is omitted from the programme feed | Reconcile against all resolutions from the ticket system. Include it and verify any credit from history. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution.** “Help the next agent solve this faster. Link the article that explains your fix, or submit a short article if it’s missing. If you’re blocked, flag why and close the ticket as usual. The team reward ends March 14.”
- **Confirmation.** “Article linked to ticket #4821. One provisional credit.” For a submission, show “Article submitted. Credit pending approval.”
- **Progress.** “2 of your 4 eligible resolutions this week have a verified article. Look for a match on your next ticket.” Use actual opportunities as the denominator. Show “No eligible tickets yet” at zero. With one or two, show the count and next action without a percentage.
- **Restart.** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Find the article that explains the fix.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own ticket-level progress. Publish the pilot team’s verified rate against its baseline weekly in the existing support channel, without names or pod breakdowns. Suppress any view with fewer than five contributing agents or where an individual can be inferred. Managers see aggregates. Support Ops and the reviewer get logged, case-specific access for QA or corrections, removed after follow-up. Programme data stays out of pay and discipline.

## 5. Motivator

If the pilot team improves by **15 percentage points** over its own baseline and clears the quality check, each pilot member gets **one scheduled paid hour off queue**, including agents with few or zero eligible tickets. Check the reward with several agents across shifts and arrange coverage before launch. Post the fixes made in response to blocker reports in the same channel. Existing CSAT bonus rules continue.

## 6. Operator card

Support Ops reconciles ticket, duplicate, reopen, and article approval histories weekly. Agents dispute credit from the ticket using a private route. Support Ops records corrections and responds within two business days. An independent QA reviewer samples 20 credited tickets and 10 uncredited or excluded tickets weekly across groups, checking relevance, accuracy, and eligibility. Pause reward tracking if at least 3 of 20 credited tickets fail or any article exposes restricted information. The Support Ops lead removes unsafe content, corrects counts, and restarts after a clean resample. Budget three staff-hours weekly.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 11–February 7, 2027 for baseline, February 8–March 14 for the pilot, and March 15–April 18 for follow-up. Randomly assign comparable pods to the reward or comparison group. Both get workflow fixes and private feedback. End reward earning and team posts March 14. Verify final credits within seven days and keep private feedback and fixes.

Scale if pilot adoption improves at least 15 points more than comparison during rewards and retains at least a 10-point advantage in improvement during follow-up, with at least 90% sampled quality and no CSAT drop greater than two points relative to comparison. Review survey response counts before applying that guardrail. Change and retest if adoption fades or results are inconclusive. Stop if there is no meaningful lift or quality or CSAT deteriorates.

## 8. Risks

- Agents attach generic articles to earn credit.
- Agents delay closure to secure approval, putting customer experience and their CSAT bonus under pressure.
- Agents rely on the reward to justify extra work and stop documenting once it ends.
