## 1. Diagnosis

The reason nurses skip kiosk logging is unknown. During the baseline, walk two cases with day- and night-shift nurses on each ward. In a routine case, the nurse cleans their hands, completes the care task, then logs the moment at the next safe break. In a blocked case, urgent care or an unavailable kiosk prevents logging. The nurse follows normal hand-hygiene practice and skips the log if there is no safe chance that shift.

Ask nurses whether the entry is quick enough and whether seeing ward-level gaps lead to fixes would make it worthwhile. The ward charge nurse routes blockers to the kiosk owner, who responds within one working day. Fix recurring detours, queues or access problems before adding incentives. Defer launch if most observed actions lack a safe logging opportunity.

## 2. Credit rule

One ward credit for one completed hand-hygiene action at a hospital-approved indication, accurately logged on the same shift and matched to an independently observed action in the pilot sample. One action can cover multiple indications and earns one credit. Unsampled entries receive a private receipt.

**Logging coverage** is matched actions divided by eligible completed actions in the sample, including missing logs. Observers record eligibility before checking kiosk records and report blocked actions separately. **Clinical compliance** comes from the monthly observer audit and uses all observed opportunities as its denominator, including missed hygiene and kiosk outages. [WHO observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Observed action, accurate same-shift entry | One credit |
| Duplicate entry or multiple indications for one action | One credit total |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Remove the withdrawn credit. Recheck corrections against the observation. |
| Delayed entry | Accept a match later that shift. Preserve timestamps and exclude entries first made after shift end. |
| Existing audit or paper route only | Clinical audit counts the observed action. Zero kiosk credit. |
| Eligible action omitted | Included in logging denominator. Zero credit. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation, at shift huddle and on the kiosk.** “After your next hand-hygiene moment, log it when care allows. It takes one quick entry, and the ward can use the results to fix logging problems you flag. The reward window ends March 28. If care is urgent or the kiosk is down, carry on with care.”
- **Confirmation.** “Saved your after-patient-contact entry. You can correct it here if needed.”
- **Private progress.** “1 of your 2 practice logs this shift. Log the next completed moment when you have a safe break.” Nurses can choose one or two based on expected volume. A shift with zero safe chances has no goal.
- **Restart.** “Start with your next safe chance.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries and practice progress after sign-in. The existing ward huddle board shows verified logging coverage against the ward’s own baseline, with sample sizes. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress any breakdown that could identify someone. Collect no patient identifiers. A designated data steward gets logged, time-limited individual access for corrections or disputes. Programme records are excluded from performance reviews and discipline. [Ravid et al., monitoring meta-analysis, Grade A](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12514)

## 5. Motivator

Offer one hospital-funded meal per qualifying ward, available across shifts, including to staff who never logged. Test the choice with nurses from each shift before launch. The ward qualifies by closing at least one-third of its baseline logging gap, capped at a required 10-point gain, while clearing the quality gate. Fund kiosk fixes separately and post what staff reported and what was fixed.

## 6. Operator card

Infection Prevention owns the pilot. Trained observers rotate across days, nights and weekends, record actions before seeing logs, then reconcile by temporary session code, approximate action time and indication. Ambiguous matches earn zero credit. Keep the monthly clinical audit separate. The Infection Prevention lead pauses reward scoring immediately for any credible care delay, or if monthly audited compliance falls 5 percentage points below baseline. Investigate before resuming and hold reward approval while a pause is unresolved. Nurses submit corrections privately to the data steward.

Allow about two observer hours per ward per week and 30 minutes for central review. Launch assumes approved sampling, reliable matching and edit history, and budget for fixes and meals. Validate these during baseline before attaching stakes.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed dates are January 4–31, 2027 for a supported kiosk baseline, February 1–March 28 for rewards, and March 29–May 23 for follow-up with fixes and private feedback retained. Use the same sampling plan throughout, targeting at least 100 eligible observed actions per ward per phase.

Expand only if at least two wards reach their milestone both during and after rewards, all three have adequate samples, and no ward breaches the clinical safety gate. Revise and retest if coverage fades or sampling is insufficient. Stop incentives if care delays recur or clinical compliance stays below the safety threshold. The baseline comparison establishes feasibility.

## 8. Risks

1. **Logging interrupts care.** Use the safe-break rule and pause on credible delays.
2. **Entries rise faster than actual hygiene.** Verify sampled entries individually and keep the clinical audit independent.
3. **Observed shifts look better than routine practice.** Rotate sampling and check whether gains hold after rewards end.
