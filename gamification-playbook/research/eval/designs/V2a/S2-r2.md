## 1. Diagnosis

The median review response takes 2.6 days. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the engineering lead walks two recent PRs with their authors and reviewers. For a reviewable PR with an available reviewer, trace when the request arrived, when it was noticed, and what delayed the response. For a blocked PR, trace a missing owner, inaccessible environment, or missing specialist and check how it was escalated.

The engineering lead owns blocker triage by the next working day and assigns a backup reviewer when needed. Sample 20 recent requests. If most are blocked or engineers lack time to review during normal hours, fix coverage and measure again before launching. The incentive is for reviewable work that gets overlooked or deferred.

## 2. Credit rule

When a non-draft PR first requests team review, the designated reviewer or backup has **eight staffed hours** to submit a substantive review. Use the team’s published coverage calendar, excluding nights, weekends, and team holidays. The review must approve the PR after checking the change, request specific changes, or identify a concrete issue or question the author needs to resolve. An acknowledgement, emoji, or draft review earns nothing. For sensitive or unfamiliar code, use the backup or specialist route. A late review can still help the author.

Each eligible PR earns at most one credit. The team score is the percentage of eligible requests that earn credit, grouped by request week. Include eligible requests with no response. Report median response time alongside the score using the same definition throughout the pilot.

| Case | Scoring |
|---|---|
| Review submitted after six staffed hours | One credit if the review qualifies |
| Duplicate request, reassignment, or reopened PR | Original clock and one-credit limit apply |
| Review dismissed, withdrawn, or found invalid | Remove provisional credit. A valid replacement qualifies only within the original window |
| Due date edited or review arrives late | Original deadline applies. Late response earns zero |
| Review happens in chat or live | Counts if the reviewer records the substantive outcome on the PR before the deadline |
| Request missing from the feed or unanswered | Reconcile against repository history. Restore omitted requests and score unanswered ones zero |
| PR withdrawn before review | Keep it in the denominator and report withdrawals separately |

**Assumption:** Repository history preserves request, submission, dismissal, and withdrawal events. The operator verifies the table cases against real history before attaching the reward.

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation:** “PR #482 is ready for your review. Please review it by Tuesday at 14:00 so the author can move forward. If you’re unavailable or need a specialist, use the backup route. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation:** “Review submitted for #482 within one working day. One provisional credit.”
- **Progress:** “You answered 3 of your 4 review requests within one working day so far this week. #491 is next, due Thursday at 11:00.”
- **Restart:** “Your next review is #506, due Tuesday at 10:00. Use the backup route if you’re tied up.”

The private goal is sized to the requests each engineer actually receives. With one request, show its deadline and status. With zero, show “No reviews assigned this week.” There are no streaks.

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress and the team’s verified rate against its baseline in the existing engineering channel. Managers receive the same aggregate. Publish no names or subgroup breakdowns, and hold any weekly result with fewer than ten eligible requests or fewer than five reviewers. Individual records are accessed only for requested coaching, corrections, or disputes. Access is logged, expires after resolution, and is excluded from performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Motivator

If the team reaches **80%** across the reward window and clears the quality check, each engineer gets **one paid hour of their choosing** back on their calendar. Test the reward and wording with five engineers before launch. The sponsor approves the paid hour and funds fixes to routing or backup coverage. Post those fixes in the engineering channel as they ship.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead owns the repository event log, weekly reconciliation, and private corrections. A rotating senior engineer independently samples 10 credited reviews per week, excluding their own, and checks for rubber stamps and material misses. If two sampled reviews fail or one misses a critical security or correctness issue, the lead pauses scoring and payout, corrects the records, and resolves the cause before resuming. Budget 75 minutes per week across both roles. Engineers can message the lead with a PR link for a correction within two working days.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 4–31, 2027** as the baseline with the same routing, coverage, and private feedback. Run rewards **February 1–28**, then track the same measures **March 1–28** with workflow support and private feedback. Settle the reward after the final quality check.

Evaluate with at least 40 eligible requests per window. **Scale** if follow-up reaches 80%, improves at least 10 percentage points over baseline, and clears quality checks. **Change** if follow-up improves but misses either threshold, or volume is insufficient. **Stop** if follow-up fails to improve or quality failures recur. The before-and-after comparison measures feasibility.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamping:** Engineers approve prematurely to meet the clock. Independent sampling can suspend payout.
- **Review load concentrates:** The same engineers absorb urgent requests. The lead checks assignment load and adjusts backup coverage.
- **The lift fades:** Engineers revert once the reward ends. The four-week follow-up determines whether to scale.
