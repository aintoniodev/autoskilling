## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs reportedly get a digital checklist. Use that as a planning estimate and verify what it counts. Before launch, shadow one job where the app works and one with a reported blocker, from dispatch through the pre-job safety check. Ask technicians and a union steward where the app saves time, adds work or fails. Check access, connectivity and duplicate paperwork. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds material rework, fix the workflow and defer the incentive.

The field operations lead owns blockers. Dispatch gives the technician the approved fallback immediately, and the lead gives a fix or an update by the next business day. Existing safety procedures govern whether work can start.

## 2. Credit rule

When a technician opens an assigned job in dispatch, the app prompts them to complete the safety checklist before starting work. Award **one credit per required checklist per job** when the technician submits all required items before the independently recorded work start and records any required hazard action. A completed checklist that correctly puts a job on hold can earn credit once dispatch confirms the hold.

Measure verified digital completions divided by all app-eligible jobs where a checklist was required, using the dispatch roster as the denominator. Omissions stay in it. Report verified safety-check completion across all jobs, including approved fallback, alongside digital adoption. Publish the count and reasons for exclusions so outages cannot silently improve the score.

| Case | Scoring |
|---|---|
| Complete before work starts | One credit after verification |
| Duplicate submission or sync retry | One credit total |
| Recalled or invalid checklist | Remove pending credit and offer correction. Keep any reward already given. |
| Late submission or edited schedule | Use original submission and actual start records. Schedule edits do not change credit. Offline submissions qualify if the original timestamp is verifiable. |
| Approved paper fallback | No digital credit. Count it toward safety completion. Exclude the job from digital eligibility only for a documented blocker. |
| Checklist omitted | Zero credit. Keep the job in the eligible denominator and follow the existing safety process. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at dispatch** “Open the safety checklist for this job before you start. Your submission stays with the job record. If the app is unavailable, use the approved fallback and tell dispatch. The team reward runs through February 21.”
- **Confirmation** “Checklist submitted for job 4821. We’ll confirm credit once the dispatch record is matched.”
- **Private progress** “3 of your 5 eligible jobs this week have a verified digital checklist. On your next job, open the checklist before you start.”
- **Restart** “Start fresh on your next job. Open its checklist before you begin.”

With zero eligible jobs, show “No eligible jobs this week.” With one or two, show the actual count. There is no minimum volume for sharing in the team reward.

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own job status and progress. The existing crew briefing shows the team’s verified rate against its own baseline and the reward target, with names and small or identifiable groups suppressed. Managers see the same aggregates. A designated coordinator may access individual records for corrections, disputes or worker-requested coaching, with logged, time-limited access. Programme records are excluded from discipline. Agree access and retention with the union before launch.

## 5. Reward

Offer everyone on a qualifying team **30 minutes of paid release time**, scheduled across shifts, if the team finishes the four weeks at least **15 percentage points above its baseline** and clears the quality gate. Agree the target and release-time arrangements with the union and test whether technicians value the reward before announcing it. Give the operations lead a budget for app and access fixes. Use briefings to report which technician-raised blockers were resolved.

## 6. Operator card

The operations coordinator matches dispatch, app and approved fallback records weekly. Technicians can dispute a result privately through the coordinator or their steward and get a response within two business days. Budget three staff-hours per week for reconciliation, review and corrections.

A safety lead independent of scoring samples 10 digital checklists per pilot arm each week against job and hazard records. Any critical hazard missed or bypassed, or two material defects in an arm’s weekly sample, pauses scoring and payout pending review. Approved safety procedures continue. **Launch assumptions** include reliable job IDs, verifiable submission and start history, and union and payroll approval. Attach rewards only once those are confirmed.

## 7. End and decision

**2027 pilot.** Baseline January 11–24. Run January 25–February 21. Follow up February 22–March 21 with the fixes and private feedback still in place. Randomly assign comparable crews to reward plus support or the same support alone. If crew assignment is impractical, use the predeclared baseline and treat the result as feasibility evidence.

Scale if follow-up adoption improves at least **10 points more than the support-only crews**, overall safety completion stays at or above baseline, and all quality pauses are resolved. Revise and retest if adoption rises during rewards but fades afterward. Stop the incentive if safety completion falls or critical defects recur.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rushed checks or underreported hazards.** Correctly held jobs earn credit, and independent review can pause payouts.
- **Unequal access or pressure from supervisors.** Track outage exclusions by crew, protect individual results and include low-volume technicians in the reward.
- **Adoption fades after payout.** Keep the workflow fixes and test whether usage holds for four reward-free weeks.
