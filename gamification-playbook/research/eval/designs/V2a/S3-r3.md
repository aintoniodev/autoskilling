### 1. Diagnosis

**Test whether logging is blocked, too costly, or simply unfamiliar.** During baseline, walk a routine case from hand hygiene to kiosk entry with a nurse, then walk an urgent case or a kiosk outage on the busiest shift. Test whether the kiosk fits the work and whether its feedback helps nurses spot logging gaps. If it adds a detour without a useful return, redesign it first. Nurses follow standard hand hygiene and continue care whenever logging would delay them. The nursing informatics lead owns blockers, responds within one shift, and posts fixes in the ward huddle. Defer the reward if most opportunities are blocked.

**Assumptions to verify before launch** include reliable timestamps and edit history, safe kiosk access, staffing for independent sampling, and privacy and paid-time approval.

### 2. Credit rule

A nurse completes hand hygiene at a required care moment, then records it at the first safe pause within five minutes, where the walkthrough confirms that window is practical. **One ward credit per independently observed action with one accurate matching entry.** Credit is calculated only in sampled windows.

The **logging rate** is matched entries divided by all observed, completed hand-hygiene actions eligible for kiosk use, including actions with no entry. Track excluded actions and reasons separately. **Clinical compliance** is measured in the monthly independent audit as observed hand-hygiene actions divided by all observed hand-hygiene opportunities. [WHO observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Routine action, accurate entry | One credit |
| Duplicate or multiple indications for one action | At most one credit |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Withdrawal removes credit. A correction keeps credit only if the original submission was timely and the corrected entry matches the observation. |
| Late entry or edited timestamp | No credit. Use the original submission time. |
| Hand hygiene done without the kiosk | No credit. Include in the logging denominator if eligible. |
| Required hand hygiene omitted | No credit. Include in the clinical compliance denominator. A kiosk claim for it fails verification. |
| Kiosk down or care cannot wait | Continue care. Record the exclusion in the audit. |

### 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at the shift huddle and kiosk** “After your next hand-hygiene moment, log it at the first safe pause. The kiosk shows what you’ve recorded this shift. If care needs you, keep moving. Your ward can earn paid break time through February 28.”
- **Confirmation** “Saved at 10:42. You can correct this entry here.”
- **Private progress** “You’ve logged a moment on 2 of your 3 planned shifts this week. On your next shift, start with the first safe chance.” Size the goal to the nurse’s worked shifts with safe opportunities. With none, show “No logging goal this week.”
- **Restart** “Start with your next safe chance to log a completed moment.”

### 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own saved entries. Staff and managers see their ward’s verified rate against its baseline in the weekly huddle, pooled across shifts. Suppress results that could identify someone. Use temporary staff codes for matching and collect no patient identifiers. Only the designated audit analyst may link records for verification or a nurse-requested correction. Log access, delete the linkage 30 days after follow-up or when an open dispute closes, whichever is later, and prohibit disciplinary use.

### 5. Motivator

Each ward can earn **one extra 20-minute paid break per nurse**, available across shifts and covered by the sponsor’s staffing budget. Before launch, test whether day, night, and float nurses value it and can actually take it. Award it to the whole ward after verification. Fund kiosk fixes separately and share what changed at the huddle.

### 6. Operator card

The infection prevention lead owns the pilot. Trained auditors sample across day, night, and weekend shifts, recording observed actions before viewing kiosk data. Match records using temporary staff code, ward, moment, and time. Treat ambiguous entries as unverified. Budget four auditor hours and one analyst hour weekly across the three wards, plus the existing monthly audits. Nurses request corrections privately through the kiosk’s **Report an entry** option.

The lead pauses reward scoring and investigates if fewer than **95% of assessable entries** match an observed action in a review of at least 20 entries, monthly observed compliance falls **5 percentage points** below baseline, or any logging-related care delay is reported. Resume only after the lead verifies the fix.

### 7. End and decision

**2027 schedule.** January 4–31 baseline with kiosk support on all wards. February 1–28 reward period. March 1–28 follow-up with the same support, private feedback, and sampling. Rewards stop accruing February 28.

Each ward earns the break by improving its verified logging rate by **15 percentage points or half its remaining gap to 100%, whichever is smaller**, while clearing the quality and safety gates. Require at least **60 eligible observed actions per ward per phase** for a decision. Scale in stages if all three wards reach and sustain their targets through March and clear the gates. Revise if gains fade or samples are insufficient. Stop if care delays or lower compliance persist. The baseline comparison supports a feasibility decision.

### 8. Risks

- **False or duplicate logs** inflate apparent adoption. Match sampled actions individually and check omissions.
- **Kiosk trips interrupt care.** Keep logging skippable at the point of care and review exclusions by shift.
- **Observer presence changes behavior.** Vary sampling times, keep the audit method consistent across phases, and report the sample sizes.
