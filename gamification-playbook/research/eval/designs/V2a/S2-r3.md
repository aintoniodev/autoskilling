# One working day for a useful review

## 1. Diagnosis

The median review response is 2.6 days. Find out where that time goes before offering a reward. In the week before baseline, the engineering lead walks two real PRs with their authors and reviewers. On a reviewable PR, trace when it became ready, who picked it up, what interrupted them, and when the author got an answer. On a blocked PR, such as one with failing required CI, trace who spotted the blocker and who could clear it.

If reviews get buried, surface the due time in the existing review queue. If reviewers lack time, protect a daily review slot and name a backup. The engineering lead owns these fixes, responds to reported blockers within one working day, and posts what changed in the team channel. Delay launch if most cases remain blocked or reviewers lack coverage.

## 2. What earns credit

When a human-authored PR becomes ready in the team’s queue, the assigned reviewer or backup has **eight staffed hours** on the team calendar to submit a useful review. A qualified review gives an approval that explains what was checked, an actionable change request, or a specific question the author needs to resolve before proceeding. Award **one credit per PR**. Assume the repository can provide reliable readiness, assignment, CI, review and withdrawal history, and the team can agree on a shared staffed calendar.

The team score is the percentage of eligible PRs that earn credit, grouped by when their deadline falls. Eligible PRs count once their deadline passes, including unanswered PRs. An unassigned PR still counts. If tooling fails, use the existing review channel and have the operator verify the timestamped response.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Useful review within eight staffed hours | One credit |
| Second review, reassignment, or reopen | One PR, one deadline, one possible credit |
| Review withdrawn or found invalid | Remove provisional credit unless another qualified response met the original deadline |
| Late review or edited due date | Record the response. Score against the original deadline |
| Review submitted through the usual repository UI | Same credit rule |
| PR omitted from the queue or left unanswered | Reconcile against repository history and count it if eligible |
| Required CI blocks review, or author withdraws the PR before the deadline | Log and audit the exclusion. For CI, start the clock when the PR first becomes reviewable |

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation in the review queue:** “PR #482 is ready. You’re the reviewer and it’s due tomorrow at 11:00. Give the author a decision or a specific next step. Use your review slot or hand it to the backup. The team reward runs through March 28.”
- **Confirmation:** “Your review on #482 was recorded on time. You’ve answered 2 of your 4 reviews due this week on time.”
- **Progress:** “Your goal is 3 of 4 this week. PR #491 is next, due Thursday at 14:00.” Size goals to assigned opportunities. With one, aim for that one. With zero, show “No reviews due.”
- **Restart:** “PR #491 missed its deadline. Your next review is #503, due Tuesday at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The team channel shows the weekly team rate, eligible PR count, and baseline, with no names. Suppress weekly figures below 10 eligible PRs and roll them into the next update. Managers receive the same aggregates. Individual programme records are accessible only for requested coaching, corrections or disputes, with logged, time-limited access. Programme data is excluded from performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Reward

Ask five engineers across different review loads whether **one protected paid hour for everyone** is worth earning. Use it if they say yes and the sponsor approves the 25 hours and backup coverage. The team earns it for improving by at least 10 percentage points over baseline, subject to the quality check. Schedule the time after verification.

## 6. Operator card

The review rotation lead reconciles repository and queue events weekly, handles corrections privately within two working days, and publishes the aggregate. A senior engineer outside the sampled reviews checks eight randomly selected reviews weekly for a useful decision or next step, with timing and credit hidden. If two fail, the engineering lead pauses reward credit and investigates before resuming. Budget 45 minutes total per week.

## 7. End and decision

Use February 1–28, 2027 as the baseline with the workflow fixes in place. Run rewards March 1–28 and measure March 29–April 25 with the fixes and private feedback still active. Decide April 30. Scale if both periods beat baseline by at least 10 points and follow-up reaches 80% on time with no unresolved quality pause. Change the workflow if gains fade or coverage remains uneven. Stop the incentive if it produces no lift or quality problems recur. The baseline comparison supports a feasibility decision. It cannot isolate the reward’s effect.

## 8. What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamp approvals.** Audit review substance before confirming the reward.
- **Hard PRs disappear from the score.** Reconcile omissions and audit exclusions.
- **The programme feels like a performance ranking.** Use one shared reward, private feedback and team-only reporting.
