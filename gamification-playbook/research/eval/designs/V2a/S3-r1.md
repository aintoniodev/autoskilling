## 1. Diagnosis

The hospital needs to establish whether nurses can log a completed hand-hygiene moment without delaying care or adding enough work to make routine use impractical. Before launch, the ward educator and a bedside nurse walk through one routine case and one blocked case, such as an urgent bedside task with the kiosk out of reach. Use the kiosk at the next safe break in care, within the same shift. If that is impractical, skip the entry. Hand-hygiene practice and monthly observation continue as usual.

The nursing informatics lead triages access, placement and usability issues within one working day and owns the fix. If most cases are blocked or the walkthrough shows the kiosk interrupts care, fix the workflow before offering a reward.

## 2. Credit rule

A bedside nurse earns one ward credit when an independent observer confirms a completed hand-hygiene moment and a same-shift kiosk entry can be matched to that moment. Use the existing monthly audits across day, night and weekend shifts. The kiosk can give an immediate receipt, with credit confirmed after audit reconciliation.

**Reward metric:** verified kiosk entries divided by independently observed, completed hand-hygiene moments eligible for logging. The observer records eligibility before seeing kiosk data. Track **actual hand-hygiene compliance** separately as completed hand hygiene divided by all observed opportunities, using the hospital’s existing audit protocol.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Observed hand hygiene, one matching same-shift entry | One credit. |
| Duplicate entries for the same moment | One credit total. |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Withdraw credit if the match no longer holds. Preserve the edit history. |
| Late entry or edited timestamp | Use the original system timestamp. An entry created after shift end earns zero. |
| Hand hygiene done, recorded through the old route or skipped at the kiosk | Zero kiosk credit. The observer still counts the completed act toward compliance. |
| Eligible observed moment omitted from the kiosk | Stays in the reward denominator. |
| Kiosk entry with no matching observed moment | No credit. Entries outside observation coverage can still serve ordinary documentation. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at shift handover:** “When you finish hand hygiene, log it at the kiosk at your next safe break in care, before shift end. If the kiosk is unavailable or you need to stay with a patient, keep caring for the patient and skip the entry. The ward can earn a meal across all shifts. Rewards end 31 March 2027.”
- **Confirmation:** “Entry saved. Ward credit is confirmed after the monthly audit.”
- **Private progress:** “Last month, 3 of your 4 eligible observed moments matched a kiosk entry. Your next goal is 3 of 4 if you have four eligible observed moments again. On your next case, log it at a safe break in care.” Size goals to available opportunities. With fewer than three audited moments, show “Too few observed moments for a goal,” with no penalty.
- **Restart:** “Missed an entry? Start with your next eligible moment.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own progress privately. The existing ward huddle board shows each ward’s verified rate against its own baseline and milestone. Managers see aggregates. A named audit steward can access individual records for corrections, disputes or coaching requested by the nurse. Log and time-limit that access, and prohibit disciplinary reuse. Suppress any breakdown that could identify someone.

## 5. Reward

Each ward can earn one modest, hospital-funded meal, available across all shifts, by improving verified logging by **15 percentage points** over its baseline and clearing the quality check. Include every shift regardless of who was sampled. Test the meal and goal with nurses from different shifts before launch. Fund kiosk fixes separately and report them at huddles. If a ward’s baseline leaves fewer than 15 points of headroom, keep the workflow support and omit the contest there.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner:** Infection prevention lead. **Inputs:** Observer audit records and kiosk creation, edit and withdrawal history. A designated steward reconciles them using an approved, time-limited linkage without patient identifiers. Trustworthy matching, kiosk access and reward funding are launch dependencies.

Nurses flag corrections privately to the steward, who responds within two working days. Budget 45 minutes per ward per week for reconciliation and feedback, plus existing audit time. Pause awards and investigate if audited compliance falls **5 percentage points** below the ward’s baseline, more than **5%** of entries reviewed in covered observation windows are confirmed inaccurate, or anyone reports that kiosk use delayed care.

## 7. End and decision

Use **December 2026–January 2027** for baseline with the same kiosk support, **February–March 2027** for rewards and **April–May 2027** for reward-free follow-up. Keep workflow fixes and private feedback. Require at least 30 eligible observed moments per ward per phase, with comparable shift coverage. With less coverage, withhold the decision.

Advance to a controlled rollout only if at least two wards gain 15 points during rewards, retain at least 10 points in follow-up, and no ward triggers the quality pause. Revise if the gain fades. Stop incentives if care delays or compliance deterioration persist. The baseline comparison establishes feasibility.

## 8. Main risks

1. Nurses log mainly when observers are present, inflating measured adoption.
2. Kiosk use interrupts care or makes proper hand hygiene harder.
3. Sparse audits make individuals identifiable or leave some shifts feeling unfairly judged.
