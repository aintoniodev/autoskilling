## 1. Diagnosis

The 25-engineer team reports a median review wait of 2.6 days. That tells us there is a delay. Before launch, the engineering manager and two reviewers walk through one routine PR and one stalled PR using their event history. Check whether the request reached an available reviewer, whether the PR was ready to review, and whether the reviewer had time to pick it up.

**Working assumption:** some ready PRs sit because ownership is unclear or review time gets crowded out. Give every ready PR a named reviewer, a backup, and a protected review slot. The engineering manager owns blockers and resolves or reroutes them that working day. If most cases lack prerequisites or review capacity, fix those first and defer the incentive.

## 2. What earns credit

When a human-authored PR is ready and review is requested, the assigned engineer or backup earns **one credit per PR** for submitting the first substantive review within one working day. A substantive review is an approval after inspection or a review with specific, actionable feedback. Acknowledgments and bot comments earn zero credit. Subsequent review rounds earn zero additional credits.

Measure **qualified reviews within one working day ÷ all eligible PRs whose deadlines have passed**. Start at the first recorded ready-for-review request and add eight working hours on the team’s published calendar. Handoffs and status edits preserve the original deadline. If blocked, tell the author what is missing and route the issue to the manager. Keep already-eligible PRs in the measure.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Reviewer submits actionable feedback in six working hours | One credit and an on-time result |
| Backup submits a second review | Original credit stands |
| Approval is withdrawn because a material issue was missed | Invalidate the credit and recalculate the rate |
| Reviewer is reassigned or a due date is edited | Original deadline stands |
| Reviewer submits through the usual PR workflow, without the reminder | Same credit if qualified and timely |
| Ready PR is omitted from the queue or never answered | Recover it from PR history and count it as a miss once overdue |

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation in the existing PR notification:** “Review requested for #482. Please submit a review by Tuesday at 14:00 so the author can keep moving. If you’re tied up, hand it to the backup today. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation:** “Review submitted for #482 before its deadline. One credit pending verification.”
- **Private progress:** “2 of your 3 reviews due this week answered on time. Next up is #491, due Thursday at 11:00.” With one opportunity, show 0/1 or 1/1. With zero, show “No reviews due this week.”
- **Restart:** “Your next review is #512, due Tuesday at 10:00. Use the backup if your calendar is full.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel shows the whole team’s verified weekly rate, volume, and baseline in one line. Suppress the line when fewer than five distinct reviewers contributed. Managers get the same aggregate. The programme grants temporary individual access for a named correction, dispute, or requested coaching session. Log access and prohibit disciplinary reuse.

## 5. Team reward

If the pilot reaches **85% on time** and passes the quality check, the manager clears **one paid hour per engineer for self-directed technical work**, with staggered review coverage. Test the reward with five engineers before launch and secure the manager’s commitment to remove an hour of planned work. Post routing and capacity fixes in the same weekly update.

## 6. Operator card

A rotating engineer reconciles the queue against PR event history weekly. An engineer independent of the sampled reviews audits five randomly selected credited PRs for evidence of substantive review and missed material issues. If **two of five fail**, the engineering manager pauses credit and reward decisions, corrects the record, and resumes after a clean five-review sample. Engineers can raise corrections privately with the operator, who responds within two working days. Budget **60 minutes per week** across reconciliation and audit.

**Launch assumptions:** PR history reliably captures ready, request, review, and withdrawal events. The team agrees on the calendar, backup coverage, and reward. Verify these before attaching stakes.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 11–February 5, 2027** as the baseline, **February 8–March 5** as the pilot, and **March 8–April 2** as the follow-up. Apply the same measure throughout. End reward earning March 5 and retain routing, protected review time, and private feedback.

Keep the workflow and consider a wider trial if the pilot reaches 85%, follow-up stays at **80% or higher and at least 10 percentage points above baseline**, and quality holds. If only the pilot clears the bar, end the reward and fix the remaining bottleneck. Stop the incentive if quality deteriorates or the pilot yields no improvement. The before-and-after comparison measures feasibility. It cannot isolate the reward’s effect.

## 8. What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamp approvals.** Sample review quality and invalidate failed credits.
- **Difficult PRs disappear from the queue.** Reconcile against source events and retain the original clock.
- **Engineers experience the pilot as surveillance or extra work.** Keep individual progress private and fund review time by reducing planned work.
