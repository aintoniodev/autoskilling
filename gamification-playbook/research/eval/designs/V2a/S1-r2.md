## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently get a digital checklist. Before launch, ride along with a technician on one routine job and one job with poor signal, missing access, or another reported blocker. Time the app and the approved fallback from arrival to clearance to start. Ask technicians and union stewards what causes the other 65% and whether the app saves them work, for example by eliminating duplicate entry. Give the field operations lead a repair budget and two working days to resolve or document each blocker. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds work without a technician benefit, defer the incentive and fix the workflow.

**Launch assumptions** are union agreement on the reward and data rules, a safe fallback, and reliable records of job starts, checklist submissions, and edits. Confirm all three before attaching a reward.

## 2. Credit rule

When a technician arrives at a dispatched job, the app prompts them to complete the safety checklist before work starts. A job earns one credit when the required answers are recorded against its dispatch ID, a trustworthy timestamp puts completion before the actual start, and any stop-work response is handled under the existing safety procedure. The reward never depends on finding zero hazards. If the app is unavailable, use the approved fallback. If neither route is available, hold the job.

Measure credited jobs divided by all started jobs where the app was supported, using the dispatch roster to find missing checklists. Log and verify exceptions separately, and report digital coverage across **all** started jobs alongside the reward metric so exclusions stay visible.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal pre-start completion | One credit after verification. |
| Duplicate or resubmission | One credit per dispatch job ID. |
| Checklist recalled or invalidated | Remove provisional credit. Correct reporting after payout and leave paid time intact. |
| Late submission or edited schedule | Use the original completion and actual start records. Late completion earns zero. An offline sync can count if the original completion time is trustworthy. |
| Approved fallback | Record safety completion separately. Zero digital credit. Exclude from the reward denominator only for an independently verified blocker. |
| Omitted checklist | A started, supported job stays in the denominator and earns zero. A cancelled job that never started is excluded. |

## 3. Worker copy

Assuming the ride-along confirms that the app removes duplicate entry:

- **Invitation in the dispatch app** “At the job? Open Safety Check before you start. Your answers save to the job record, so you only enter them once. If the app fails, use the approved fallback. The team challenge ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation** “Checklist saved before start for job 4821. Your team has one provisional credit, pending verification.”
- **Private progress** “You completed 2 of 3 eligible jobs in the app this week. Open Safety Check when you arrive at your next job.” Use the technician’s actual volume, even for one job. With zero eligible jobs, show “No eligible jobs yet this week.”
- **Restart** “Start fresh at your next job. Open Safety Check before work begins.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing weekly safety huddle shows crews their verified rate against their own baseline, with no names. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress any breakdown with fewer than 10 technicians or 20 jobs, including totals that would reveal a suppressed result. A named reviewer may access individual records for a correction, dispute, or worker-requested coaching. Log access, close it when the case is resolved, and bar programme data from disciplinary use. Agree and communicate the rules with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Motivator

Test the reward with a few technicians and stewards before launch. Offer every member of a qualifying crew **30 minutes of paid personal time**, scheduled with coverage, if the crew improves by 20 percentage points over its baseline after verification. At a 35% baseline, that means 55%. Crew members with few or zero eligible jobs share the reward. The huddle also names the app problems technicians reported and operations fixed.

## 6. Operator card

Field operations reconciles dispatch starts, checklist history, and fallback records weekly. An independent safety lead reviews 20 sampled jobs across credited, fallback, and missing cases against work records and technician confirmation. One critical safety failure or two material discrepancies in the weekly sample pauses scoring and payout pending investigation. Safe checklist procedures continue. Technicians can dispute a record through dispatch or a steward. Field operations responds within two working days. Budget roughly three staff-hours per week, excluding fixes.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline January 11–31, 2027. Run February 1–28 and follow up March 1–28 with rewards off. Where crew numbers allow, randomize comparable crews to immediate challenge or the same app fixes, training, and private feedback without a challenge. Keep the fixes and feedback afterward. Scale if improvement over baseline exceeds the comparison crews’ improvement by at least 20 points during the challenge and 10 points afterward, with no unresolved quality pause. Revise if the lift fades. Stop the incentive if there is no lift or it pressures unsafe starts. Without a comparison, treat results as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Risks

- **Rushed checks or suppressed hazards.** Reward verified completion and uphold stop-work procedures.
- **Excluded jobs inflate the rate.** Reconcile against dispatch and publish all-start coverage.
- **Crews perceive surveillance or lose interest after payout.** Enforce access limits and measure use for four weeks after rewards end.
