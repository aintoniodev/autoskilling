## Knowledge base rally

Reward teams for resolving customer tickets with knowledge that another agent can reuse. Each team earns **one hour of paid personal time per person**, booked with coverage, if it raises its share of eligible resolutions with a verified article by **15 percentage points** over its own baseline during a four-week rally. A team starting at 12% would aim for 27%. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place and give agents 30 minutes of protected work time each week to learn the tool or write articles.

### What earns credit

**One eligible ticket earns one credit when its resolution is verified and the approved article linked to it accurately covers the fix.** An existing article and a newly written one earn the same credit. Use the support system’s existing independent closure check and the knowledge base’s publication approval. Before launch, confirm that the closure check can verify article relevance. If only some queues have that check, run the rally in those queues and give everyone the workflow improvements.

Count resolved tickets where the fix could help with another case, including cases where an article still needs to be written. Exclude duplicate records, spam and cases with no reusable resolution, such as a one-off account adjustment. Use existing disposition codes for exclusions and sample them in QA. Before launch, walk through these cases with agents and QA:

- Two references to the same ticket earn one credit. A merged duplicate drops out of both counts.
- A reopened ticket loses provisional credit. It can earn one credit after the final verified resolution. Settle the tally seven days after each team’s rally ends.
- An eligible ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the denominator. A draft article earns credit once approved and verified against the resolution.

### What agents see

Ask agents from different shifts what makes linking or writing slow or risky. Fix the biggest blockers in the ticket workflow, such as poor article search or a slow approval queue, and tell agents what changed in response to their feedback.

Show agents private confirmation in the ticket tool when a resolution qualifies, with a reason when credit is pending or reversed. Give each agent a private goal of 15 percentage points above their own eligible-ticket baseline, capped at 100%. If someone has fewer than 10 baseline cases, use their first 10 eligible cases to establish a goal. Personal goals guide feedback. The team milestone determines the reward, and someone returning from leave can pick up with their next case.

Put the team’s baseline, current share, target, reward and deadline in the support dashboard people already use. Managers see team totals. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews and leave named rankings out of the rally. Agents can flag a wrong count through the existing QA channel. The support operations lead decides disputes and corrects the tally.

### Timing and decision

Give all teams the fixes, private feedback and protected time first. Use four weeks under those conditions to establish baselines. Pair comparable teams by queue and baseline, then randomly assign one team in each pair to start first. Start the second group two weeks later. Each group gets four reward weeks. Before the first group’s reward ends, make article search the default in the resolution screen, with suggested articles agents confirm before linking.

Use **14-day same-issue repeat contact per 100 eligible resolutions** as the decision metric. Compare the groups during the two weeks when only the early group can earn the reward. Recheck resolutions from weeks three and four after each group’s reward ends, allowing the full 14 days for repeat contacts to appear. Agree before launch to keep the rally as a repeatable intervention if the early group improves by at least **two repeat contacts per 100** relative to the change in the later group and retains that improvement after rewards end. Revise if the improvement fades or the volume is too low to judge. Drop the reward if repeat contacts stay flat or worsen with enough volume to assess the result.

### What could go wrong

Irrelevant links, unnecessary articles and convenient exclusions can inflate the count. Reuse 10 cases from routine weekly QA across credited, uncredited and excluded tickets. Pause reward accrual if two of the 10 have a material article, resolution or eligibility error, or immediately if an article exposes customer data. The support operations lead corrects the issue and resumes accrual after a clean sample. Watch the existing team CSAT report for signs that article work is slowing replies or hurting service, and use protected time to clear writing backlogs. Automate the tally from existing records so one lead can review the sample, resolve count disputes and update the dashboard in under an hour a week.
