## Support knowledge base pilot

**Reward one durable resolution with a useful article.** A ticket earns one credit when it passes the normal closure checks and the resolution record contains a published article that supports the fix. An article the agent writes qualifies once it passes the usual publishing review. Keep article review separate from ticket closure so customers get their answers on time. Duplicate and merged tickets count once under the surviving ticket ID. A same-issue reopen within seven days reverses the credit. A ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the eligible total and earns zero credit. Cases with no reusable guidance or a policy restriction on knowledge capture are excluded from both sides of the calculation. The existing QA reviewer checks those exclusions.

**Give agents a reason to use it.** Before launch, walk through real tickets with agents from different queues and shifts. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice. Fix the slowest steps they identify, show what changed, and put article search and linking directly in the resolution screen. Keep a clear exception path for cases where an article would be inappropriate. Test the counting rule on a duplicate, a reopen, an old-workflow closure, an irrelevant link, and a valid exclusion. Confirm that the existing closure record can independently verify the resolution and article link before activating rewards.

**Show progress where people already work.** On closure, tell the agent which article was recorded and whether the ticket is pending credit. Confirm the credit after seven days and explain any reversal. In their private view, agents see their credited share of eligible tickets and a goal of 15 percentage points above their own four-week baseline, capped at 100%. Agents with too few baseline cases start with a goal of three qualifying resolutions in their next ten eligible cases. A missed week has no penalty. The shared support dashboard shows team totals, the team's baseline, and the milestone. A team that finishes its six-week reward window at least 15 percentage points above its baseline, with the quality gate clear, earns 30 minutes of paid learning time per member in the following two weeks. Managers receive team totals only. Individual progress stays out of performance reviews, and the existing CSAT bonus continues under its current rules.

**Run six reward weeks per team, staggered by three weeks.** Turn on the workflow fixes, default article picker, and feedback for everyone before rewards begin. Pair existing teams by queue mix and baseline use, then randomly assign one team from each pair to start in week 1 and the other in week 4. Rewards end in weeks 6 and 9 respectively. Finalize each team's result after its last tickets clear the seven-day reopen window. Keep the picker and private feedback in place, and check results again four weeks after the final reward window.

**Make the decision on durable resolutions.** Use the share of resolved tickets with no same-issue reopen or repeat contact within seven days. Compare the early and late teams during weeks 1–3, using the four-week baseline and comparing similar queues. Keep the reward for future rollouts if the early teams improve by at least 2 percentage points more than the late teams, the improvement holds four weeks after rewards end, and the guardrails stay clear. Change the programme if the signal is too uncertain or fades. Drop the reward if article use rises while durable resolutions stay flat or worsen.

**Catch the ways it can backfire.** Agents may attach irrelevant articles, overuse exclusions, or rush closure to hit the milestone. Each week, the QA lead samples ten tickets across credited cases and exclusions as part of routine QA. Two or more incorrect credits or exclusions in the sample pause awards for the affected team pending review. A drop of 3 percentage points or more in team CSAT against its baseline also triggers a pause and a check of response volume and queue mix. The support lead decides when awards resume. The support operations owner corrects counts through the existing QA channel and tells affected agents privately. Automate the totals and use the existing QA sample to keep programme administration under an hour a week.
