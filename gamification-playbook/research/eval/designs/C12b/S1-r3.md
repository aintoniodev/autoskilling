# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Give each crew three weeks to earn a company-paid meal on paid time. The milestone is a 15-percentage-point increase over the crew’s own baseline share of eligible jobs with a verified pre-job checklist in the app. Cap the target at 90% for crews starting below 90%. Crews already at or above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. Everyone on a qualifying crew gets the reward, including technicians who were on leave or had few eligible jobs.

## Before the pilot

Have the safety lead and union stewards walk through a few jobs with technicians from different shifts, including jobs with poor connectivity. Fix the barriers they find, such as duplicate entry, missing job details or unreliable offline saving. Show crews what changed and give them paid time to practise. Agree on the counting rule, data access and corrections with the union before collecting any incentive data.

## What counts

**A job counts once when its digital checklist clears the existing safety check before work starts.** Use an existing independent record, such as a permit or dispatch release that confirms the checklist was reviewed, and verify the timing against the recorded start of work. A technician’s own completion tick is insufficient. If only certain job types have that independent check and reliable timing, pilot on those job types. If none do, hold the reward until there is an outcome the existing workflow can verify.

Use the dispatch job list to establish eligibility. A cancelled job or one that genuinely requires no checklist counts neither way. A job completed safely using the approved paper fallback still counts as eligible and passes the safety audit if the check was valid and timely. It earns no digital credit. Keep the fallback available and investigate recurring outages as an app problem.

Before launch, test the rule against a duplicate submission, a checklist voided before work starts, a late submission, an offline checklist synced later and a paper fallback. Offline completion earns credit when the existing records establish that the check cleared before work began. Hold ambiguous credits for review. The safety coordinator corrects counts through an existing support channel, and the safety lead decides disputed cases with a union steward.

## What technicians see

After each submission, the app confirms whether the job counted or is awaiting verification. Each technician sees their own eligible jobs, verified completions and a personal goal based on their baseline using the same rule as the crew target. The goal is for feedback. Missing a week has no penalty, and the next eligible job still counts.

Put crew progress, the meal and the earning deadline on the dispatch screen or board crews already use. Managers see crew totals only. Skip named rankings and manager drill-downs. Individual incentive data stays out of performance reviews.

## Timing and decision

- **Weeks 1–2:** Give every crew the fixes and feedback, then establish baselines. Assign comparable crews to early and late starts by lottery where practical, grouping by job mix.
- **Weeks 3–5:** Early crews can earn the meal. Late crews continue with the same fixes and feedback. Compare both groups during these weeks.
- **Weeks 6–8:** Late crews get the same three-week reward window. Before each crew’s window ends, make the prefilled checklist the first step when opening an eligible job. Keep the approved fallback accessible.
- **Week 12:** Check whether the improvement held four weeks after the last reward window closed.

The decision metric is **the share of sampled eligible jobs with a valid, complete safety check before work began**, via app or approved fallback. Use the existing QA sample across both groups and check a few claimed exclusions for denominator gaming. Before launch, agree to keep the incentive for future rollouts if early crews improve by at least five percentage points more than late crews during the overlap and the pooled week-12 rate stays at least five points above baseline. Change the rollout if the lift fades or the sample is too small to judge. Drop the incentive if digital credit rises while audited safety completion stays flat or falls.

## What could go wrong

Rushed checklists, backdating and false exclusions can inflate progress. Pause reward counting if fewer than 90% of sampled credited jobs pass QA over the latest 10 reviewed credits, or immediately if QA finds a missed critical check on a credited job. The safety lead investigates with a steward, fixes the cause and restores any valid credits. Make clear at launch that stopping unsafe work and reporting hazards never cost a crew its reward. Use the existing QA and dispatch routines so the coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
