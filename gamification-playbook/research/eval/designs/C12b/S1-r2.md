## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Prepare with technicians and union stewards.** Use the first two weeks to shadow a few jobs across shifts and routes, agree on what makes a job eligible, and check where the app adds time or fails offline. Give technicians paid practice time. Fix the biggest barriers before rewards start and show crews what changed. Put the checklist first in the job flow, prefill job details, and retain the approved fallback for outages. Keep the existing stop-work process in force.

**Reward a verified pre-job safety check.** A job earns one credit when the technician completes the digital checklist before work starts and the existing safety review verifies that required checks were done and any hazards were resolved or escalated under the current procedure. Use the job ID to deduplicate and the existing dispatch record to check timing. A submission alone earns zero. Late entries, duplicates, and voided records earn zero. An approved paper fallback during a verified outage can pass the safety audit and is excluded from the digital adoption score. Eligible jobs completed on paper for other reasons stay in the digital denominator. A job cancelled before work starts is excluded. If existing records cannot independently verify digital checks at scale, base the reward on jobs already verified through routine safety QA and set the baseline and goal on that same pool.

**Give crews a reachable goal.** Use each technician’s verified share of eligible jobs during the two-week baseline. Set their private goal at 15 percentage points above that share, capped at 100%. Use an eligible-job-weighted average of those goals for the team milestone, with leave and ineligible jobs carrying zero weight. Confirm reachability with crews before launch. If the team reaches its milestone over its four-week reward window and clears the quality gate, everyone on the team earns 30 minutes of paid release time, scheduled around coverage. A missed week simply leaves the next eligible job as another opportunity.

**Show progress where people work.** After submission, the app confirms receipt for that job and shows credit once it is verified. Technicians see their own verified share, goal, and reasons for excluded or uncredited jobs. The existing crew huddle view shows team progress, the reward, and the date it can be earned. Managers get team totals without individual drill-down. Programme data stays out of performance reviews. Technicians can raise a wrong count with the programme owner or through their steward. The owner resolves it against the original job record and corrects the team total before payout.

**Run a staggered pilot and end it on schedule.** Give every crew the app fixes, paid practice, and feedback first. Pair similar crews by job mix and baseline, then randomly assign one crew in each pair to weeks 3–6 and the other to weeks 5–8. Use weeks 5–6 to compare crews in their reward windows with their own pre-reward baselines, and use weeks 3–4 for the direct early-versus-late comparison while only the early crews are earning rewards. End rewards after each crew’s four-week window. Keep the prefilled checklist first in the app and recheck results four weeks after each crew’s rewards end.

**Use one result to decide whether to repeat the reward.** Measure the share of sampled eligible jobs that pass the existing pre-job safety audit, including jobs done on paper and jobs with no checklist record. Draw the sample from dispatch jobs so missing submissions can surface. Use a five-percentage-point improvement as the pilot decision threshold. Repeat the reward in future rollouts only if the early crews improve by at least five points more than the late crews during weeks 3–4 and audited compliance remains at least five points above baseline four weeks after rewards end. If the lift fades or the sample is too small to support a decision, keep the app improvements and revise the pilot. If compliance is flat or worse with enough audit coverage to judge, drop the reward.

**Pause for bad counts or unsafe shortcuts.** Before launch, have a steward and the safety lead walk through a duplicate, a voided submission, a paper fallback, a late entry, and a stop-work case. Use routine QA to spot-check credited jobs and exclusions for rushed checks, copied answers, false outage claims, or pressure to start with an unresolved hazard. The safety lead pauses rewards if more than 5% of sampled credits fail verification or if one credited job is confirmed to have started with an unresolved stop-work hazard. The programme owner fixes counts and publishes the team update through the existing huddle, keeping reward administration under an hour a week.
