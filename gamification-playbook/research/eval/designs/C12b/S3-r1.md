## Three-ward hand hygiene pilot

**Reward rule.** Each ward earns one hospital-funded team meal, available to every shift, if it closes at least 20% of the gap between its baseline audit score and 100% over its two reward months. A ward starting at 80% earns the meal at 84%. A ward starting at 100% earns it by maintaining that score. Each ward can earn the meal independently.

The existing monthly observer audit determines the score. Divide compliant hand-hygiene actions by eligible opportunities observed, counting each opportunity once. Pool the two reward months by adding their numerators and denominators. Kiosk entries earn zero points. A correctly performed action still counts if an observer sees it and the nurse documents it later, uses the downtime procedure, or misses the entry. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**Make the kiosk worth using.** In month 1, give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk during a simulated workflow. Ask where access or logging would interrupt care. Fix the biggest blockers before rewards begin, such as slow sign-in, awkward placement or too many taps. Post the fixes at the shift huddle. Put the hand-hygiene form first on the kiosk and keep the existing downtime procedure available. Patient care takes priority, and nurses can return to logging at the next safe opportunity.

**What nurses see.** After a save, the kiosk confirms the selected moment and offers a way to correct or undo the entry. At the existing shift huddle, show the ward’s latest audit result, the number of opportunities observed, its target, and the meal decision date. Update the result after each monthly audit. Staff and managers see the same ward totals. Use aggregate kiosk volume to spot workflow problems. Individual scores, rankings and performance-review use are out of scope.

**Timing and decision.** Use month 1 as a common baseline after the kiosk fixes and feedback are in place. Draw the ward start order before results come in. Run rewards in months 2–3, 3–4 and 4–5. Publish each ward’s meal decision after its second reward-month audit. Keep the kiosk shortcut and aggregate audit feedback in place after the reward ends. Use each ward’s first post-reward audit, in months 4, 5 or 6, to check whether the gain holds.

Infection Prevention uses **audited hand-hygiene compliance** to decide whether to repeat the reward. Compare each ward’s change from baseline with the change on wards still waiting during the same month. Repeat the reward only if the overlap favors the rewarded wards and at least two wards still meet their own improvement target at follow-up. If gains fade, retire the meal and keep the workflow fixes. If audit coverage is too sparse or uneven to interpret, revise the sampling plan before another rewards round. Three wards give a directional result.

**What could go wrong.** Before launch, the Infection Prevention lead and a ward nurse walk through a duplicate entry, an undone entry, an observed action with no entry, and an entry with no observed action. An observer’s duplicate is removed, kiosk corrections affect the log only, and an unobserved kiosk claim earns no credit. The lead checks a small sample of existing audit tallies for duplicate opportunities and questionable exclusions and keeps shift coverage comparable across wards. If an award-period audit falls **5 percentage points or more below baseline**, pause the ward’s award decision and review care interruptions, supplies and audit coverage. The Infection Prevention lead owns the pause and corrects audit tallies. The kiosk owner fixes logging issues. Use the existing audit and huddle so one coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
