## Six-week knowledge-base pilot

**Start with the work.** Spend two weeks giving every team the same fixes, training, and feedback. Ask agents from different shifts and queues to show where they lose time or risk giving a customer the wrong answer. Use paid practice time to improve article search, remove duplicate results, and agree on when an article helps. Give agents scheduled time to draft articles and tell them what changed because of their feedback. Proceed once agents can complete the new flow within normal ticket-handling time, including scheduled writing time.

**Reward a verified resolution.** Tell agents the rule in one sentence: “Your team gets one credit when a resolved ticket has a useful, approved article attached and the ticket record shows it was used to resolve the issue.” Use the helpdesk’s delivery record to verify customer-facing links. For internal articles, count cases only where existing closure QA verifies that the article supported the resolution. A new article earns credit on the originating ticket once it clears the normal publication review and meets the same resolution rule. Use the ticket ID to count once. One-off account actions and cases where sharing or documenting the answer would expose restricted information are ineligible. A missing article for a reusable issue is eligible and can be addressed by writing one.

**Make progress visible.** Give agents private confirmation at closure, with a pending status where review is required. Show their share of eligible tickets credited and a goal of closing one-quarter of the gap between their own baseline and 100%. Use each agent’s four-week baseline under the same eligibility rule. For new hires or agents with fewer than ten baseline cases, use the queue baseline until they have ten cases. Show managers and agents the team’s aggregate progress in the existing support dashboard. A team earns one paid 45-minute break per member if it closes one-quarter of its own baseline gap by the end of its six-week window. Schedule breaks with queue coverage. If the eligible baseline is 12%, the team target is 34%. Put the reward and deadline on the dashboard. Restrict individual results to the agent and the person correcting a disputed credit. Exclude pilot data from performance reviews and fund the reward separately from CSAT pay. Progress accrues across the full window, so a missed week carries no penalty.

**Test the rule before launch.** A duplicate link earns one credit. A mistaken closure that is reversed loses its credit. A valid resolution through the old workflow stays in the denominator if an article would have helped. Each week, the existing QA lead samples eight credited and four excluded tickets across queues. Two or more incorrect credits or exclusions in that sample pause reward accrual for the affected team. The QA lead decides when to resume. The helpdesk administrator fixes counting errors and backfills valid credits. Use the existing QA session and an automated dashboard to keep programme administration under an hour a week.

**Stagger the start and set the end date.** Pair existing teams by queue mix and baseline, then randomly assign one of each pair to start first. Start the later teams three weeks after the early teams. Each gets six reward weeks. Before the first reward window ends, put article search and suggestions directly in the closure flow for everyone, with a quick way to record a valid exclusion.

**Decide on customer outcomes.** Use one primary measure: the share of resolved tickets with no reopen or identifiable same-issue recontact within seven days. Compare early and late teams during the three-week overlap, accounting for baseline performance and queue mix. Measure again four weeks after each team’s rewards end, allowing seven days for the last tickets to mature. Set a two-percentage-point improvement as the minimum worthwhile gain before launch. Keep the workflow and end the prizes if the gain reaches that mark during the overlap and persists afterward. Change the workflow and retest if results are inconclusive or the gain fades. Drop the incentive if the comparison rules out a worthwhile gain or shows harm.

**Watch for shortcuts and customer friction.** Irrelevant links, premature closures, and convenient exclusions can inflate credit. QA checks all three. Review existing team CSAT weekly as a guardrail, since an article can make a reply worse even when the ticket stays closed. Pause incentives if CSAT drops by five points from baseline for two consecutive weeks and QA finds that article use contributed.
