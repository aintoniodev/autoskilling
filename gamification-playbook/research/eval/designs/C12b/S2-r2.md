## One working day for a first code review

Give every eligible pull request a substantive first response within one working day. An approval or a review that identifies a concrete issue and what needs to change qualifies. Authors can act on that response while the rest of the review continues.

### Fix the queue first

In weeks 1–2, ask engineers who regularly submit and review PRs what slows them down. Give each review group a rotating primary reviewer and a backup, with protected review time each working day. Route review-ready PRs to the reviewer on duty and put the oldest requests first in the existing queue. Share which friction the team raised and what changed. Both groups get these fixes and private feedback from week 1.

### What earns credit

Publish this rule in the team’s existing engineering channel. **A review-ready PR earns one credit when an eligible reviewer submits a substantive approval or actionable change request in the PR within eight working hours.**

Use the PR platform’s existing ready-for-review, review submission, and reviewer eligibility records. Publish the team’s working-hours calendar before launch. Start the clock when a PR first becomes ready. Reassignment, duplicate requests, and reopening the same PR preserve its original clock and can earn at most one credit. A review discussed on a call qualifies once the reviewer records the decision or requested change in the PR within the window. A withdrawn or superseded PR leaves the denominator only if the author records the reason before the deadline and links the replacement where applicable. Once the deadline passes, an unanswered PR counts as a miss.

The engineering lead samples six cases a week, including credited approvals, change requests, and exclusions. The lead checks whether the response gave the author a usable decision and whether exclusions were justified. Two material failures in the last ten sampled cases pause rewards for the affected group. The lead decides when counting can resume. The rotation owner corrects wrong counts from the PR record and tells the affected engineer privately. Budget 45 minutes a week for the sample, corrections, and update.

### What people see and earn

Give reviewers private confirmation after a qualifying review, with a link to the PR. Show each reviewer their own share of assigned eligible reviews answered on time. Agree on a reachable goal using their prior four weeks as a baseline. An engineer who already meets the service target can aim to sustain it. After a missed week, the next PR is a fresh chance.

Post group totals, the eight-hour target, and the reward window in the existing channel. Managers receive the same group totals. Keep individual figures private and out of performance reviews.

Each group can earn one paid hour for an activity it chooses, with coverage arranged, by meeting its milestone during its four-week reward window. Set the milestone before launch from each group’s prior on-time rate. Ask groups below 80% to improve by 15 percentage points, capped at 80%, and groups already at or above 80% to sustain their baseline. The quality check must be clear before the group claims the reward.

### Timing and decision

Use two existing review groups with roughly comparable workload. Assign the earlier start by coin flip. The first group’s reward runs in weeks 3–6 and the second’s in weeks 5–8. During weeks 3–4, compare the change from weeks 1–2 in both groups to estimate what the reward added after the queue fixes. Attribute PRs to their original review group so reassignment preserves the comparison. Leave the rotation, backup, and oldest-first queue in place after prizes end.

The decision metric is **the percentage of eligible PRs with a substantive first response within eight working hours**. Keep the workflow if it reaches 80% across weeks 9–11 with the quality check clear. Change the workflow if the rate improved from weeks 1–2 but falls short of 80%. Retire the reward if the early group shows no clear additional gain over the waiting group or the gain disappears after prizes end. Treat small differences cautiously with only two groups.

### What could go wrong

Rubber-stamp approvals and token comments can improve the reported rate while giving authors little help. The sample and pause rule catch those cases. Easy reviews can crowd out difficult ones, so the duty reviewer starts with the oldest eligible PR. Absences and release spikes can overwhelm a rotation, so the backup takes the queue when the primary is away or at capacity. For engineers skeptical of gamification, keep the rollout focused on the review service, paid time, and a clear end date.
