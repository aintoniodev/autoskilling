## Hand hygiene on three wards

**Reward hand hygiene that observers verify.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, available across all shifts, during its two-month reward window. The ward earns it at the first monthly audit that meets its target. The rule for staff is simple: “Your ward earns a team meal when its monthly hand-hygiene audit reaches the target shown on the ward board.” Kiosk entries carry no reward. The monthly audit verifies hand hygiene, and a kiosk entry alone cannot verify that it happened.

**Fix the workflow before starting.** Ask nurses from day and night shifts on all three wards to try the kiosk on paid time. Watch where it adds a trip, interrupts care, or makes a correction hard. Fix those issues before the baseline month and report back at huddles on what changed. Make the correct ward and the next entry screen the kiosk defaults, with an easy way to undo a mistaken entry. Agree with nurses on when they can log without interrupting care. Keep those defaults and the correction path after the programme ends.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** The infection-prevention lead calculates each ward’s compliance as observed compliant moments divided by observed eligible opportunities, using the hospital’s existing audit criteria. Count each observed opportunity once and exclude ineligible opportunities from both totals. Set each ward’s target from its own baseline: improve by five percentage points or close half the gap to 100%, whichever is smaller. A ward starting at 100% can earn the meal by maintaining it. Publish the targets before ward order is drawn.

Before launch, walk through three cases with the audit lead. A duplicate kiosk entry adds zero audit credit. An undone entry leaves the observed result as recorded by the auditor. A compliant moment performed without using the kiosk still counts if an observer saw it. Each month, the lead spot-checks 10 audit records per ward against the observer’s source notes, including exclusions where available. Two or more scoring, duplication, or eligibility errors pause that ward’s award until the audit owner corrects the count and the lead signs it off. If the audit falls below the hospital’s existing minimum coverage, carry the milestone forward to the next usable audit.

**Show ward progress at existing huddles.** Put the latest audit result, sample size, ward target, meal, and next audit month on the existing ward board. Give immediate private confirmation when a kiosk entry is saved or corrected. Managers see ward totals. Keep individual kiosk and audit data out of manager reports and performance reviews. There are no individual rankings or streaks, so a nurse returning from leave simply picks up with the next eligible moment.

**Stagger the rewards and decide whether to keep them.** Give all three wards the kiosk fixes and audit feedback from the start. After one baseline month, draw the order of the wards and start their two-month reward windows in months 2, 3, and 4. Compare changes from baseline on reward-active wards with wards still waiting in the same month. Keep the board and kiosk workflow in place, and check each ward at its first monthly audit after prizes stop.

Use **audited compliance** as the decision metric. Drop the reward if wards improve about as much while waiting as they do while eligible, or if the improvement holds after prizes stop. Change and retest if improvement appears only during the reward window or the audits are too sparse or uneven to interpret. Keep a short reward window for future rollouts only if reward-active wards show a clearer improvement than waiting wards and retain some of that gain afterward. With three wards, treat the comparison as a pilot signal. The infection-prevention lead owns the decision and runs the programme through the existing audit and huddles, with less than an hour of added administration per week.

**Watch for distorted results.** Kiosk counts can rise through duplicate or retrospective entries while hygiene stays flat. Nurses may also change their behaviour when they see an observer, and a shift mix change can move the audit score. Keep the existing observation protocol, distribute observations across shifts and opportunity types, and flag changes in coverage alongside the result. If nurses report that logging delays care or hygiene, fix the workflow before continuing the incentive.
