## One-working-day code reviews

Give authors a substantive answer within one working day. The current median is 2.6 days. Run this as a short trial of a better review workflow, with one shared reward and no individual rankings.

### Make reviews easier to do

Before launch, spend 30 minutes of paid work time with five engineers who regularly write and review PRs. Ask what makes reviews wait and what would make a quick approval risky. Use the answers to fix the queue before rewards start. Begin with a rotating primary and backup reviewer for each existing review group, a protected daily review block, and oldest-ready-first ordering in the team’s existing PR view. Tell the team which changes came from their feedback. Make that queue the default for everyone before the trial starts.

### What earns credit

**A ready PR counts once when it gets a substantive approval or a specific request for changes within eight staffed working hours.**

Start the clock when a PR becomes ready and enters the team’s review queue. Use the group’s published working hours and holidays. A handoff, new commit, or second review request never resets the clock. Drafts and bot-only updates that require no human review are excluded. A ready PR that is later withdrawn remains in the denominator unless the review lead confirms it was a duplicate or opened in error.

Use submitted reviews from the existing PR system. An approval qualifies when it remains valid under the repo’s existing merge rules. A request for changes qualifies when the existing PR thread shows the author addressing or resolving a concrete issue. Credit the original review timestamp once that evidence exists. Acknowledgments, duplicate reviews, and dismissed approvals earn nothing. Before launch, test the rule against a duplicate, a dismissed approval, a Friday handoff, and a PR answered in chat. A chat answer counts when the substantive review is also recorded on the PR within the window.

### What engineers see and earn

Send the reviewer a private confirmation when credit is verified. Show each engineer their own on-time share for eligible cases assigned to them through the rotation. Set their personal goal by closing one-quarter of the gap between their baseline and 100%. Someone at 60% aims for 70%. People with fewer than five baseline cases see their own results and use the team target until they have enough cases. A missed case or week carries no penalty into the next one.

In the existing review channel, pin each group’s aggregate on-time share, eligible PR count, and reward window. If a group reaches **80% on time** during its two-week window and clears the quality check, everyone in the group earns **one paid hour for self-directed learning**, booked within the following two weeks. Publish only aggregates. Managers have no individual drill-down, and programme data stays out of performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

Measure a two-week baseline using the same rule. Then give both existing review groups the queue fixes and private feedback for two weeks. Randomly choose which group gets the reward in trial weeks 1–2. The other gets the same reward opportunity in weeks 3–4. Keep review coverage stable and compare the groups during weeks 1–2, taking their baseline rates and PR volumes into account. Treat the comparison as directional if volumes are low.

End rewards after week 4 and leave the queue and protected review blocks in place. The decision metric is the **share of eligible PRs receiving a substantive answer within one working day**. Measure it again across the four weeks after rewards end. At **80% or above**, keep the workflow as standard. At **65% to under 80%**, adjust coverage and recheck. Below **65%**, end the programme and revisit where reviews stall before trying another incentive.

### What could go wrong

Fast approvals can hide shallow reviews, reviewers can favour easy PRs, and teams can improve the number by excluding awkward cases. Each week, the review lead samples five credited PRs and two exclusions across the groups. **Two or more errors in the weekly sample, or one confirmed rubber-stamp approval that lets a material defect through, pauses reward awards.** The review lead decides when awards can resume. The queue owner corrects disputed counts in the weekly tally. Batch the sample, corrections, and channel update into one 45-minute weekly check.
