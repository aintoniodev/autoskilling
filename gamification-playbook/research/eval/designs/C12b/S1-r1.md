## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Reward crews for completing a job-specific digital safety checklist before work begins and addressing any issue it flags. Each crew can earn one 30-minute paid team break, scheduled across shifts, once it reaches its agreed milestone. Keep credit when a technician correctly postpones or stops a job. A missed week has no penalty, and the next eligible job counts as usual.

### Before launch

Meet with union reps and technicians from different shifts and routes. Give technicians paid time to try the app on real jobs and identify where it adds time or risk. Fix issues such as unreliable offline access, repeat data entry and unclear stop-work instructions. Show crews what changed based on their feedback. Agree with the union on eligibility, access to data, corrections and the reward before starting. Keep the approved paper fallback available when the app is unavailable.

### What counts

Tell technicians **“One job earns one credit when its digital checklist is completed before work starts and any flagged hazard is handled through the usual safety process.”**

Use the dispatched job ID to deduplicate. Match the checklist’s completion time to the existing dispatch record of work starting, and check any flagged hazard against the existing clearance or stop-work record. For a job stopped before work starts, use the existing stop-work record. Offline submissions count once synced if the app preserves the original completion time and it can be verified. A duplicate earns nothing, a voided checklist loses its credit, and a late checklist earns nothing. Paper fallbacks earn no digital credit and still count in the safety audit. Track app outages at team level so the pilot owner can correct the milestone denominator for jobs where digital completion was unavailable. If dispatch and safety records cannot independently verify an outcome, resolve that before attaching a reward.

Measure each technician’s baseline share of eligible jobs completed digitally during two weeks after the fixes and feedback are in place. Set a private target 15 percentage points above that baseline, capped at 100%. Weight those targets by eligible job volume to set the crew milestone, so crews with different starting points have a reachable goal. Jobs outside the existing safety checklist requirement are excluded from both numerator and denominator. Check a sample of exclusions.

### What people see

After submission, the app confirms the job and shows whether credit is recorded or pending verification. Technicians see their own progress and can request a correction privately. The existing crew dashboard shows the team’s progress, its milestone, the paid break and the date it can be earned. Managers see crew totals only. Keep individual records out of performance reviews and discipline. The pilot owner resolves count disputes with a union rep and corrects the tally.

### Timing and decision

After the two-week baseline, start comparable crews in two waves, three weeks apart. Each wave has four weeks to earn the same reward. Both waves get the fixes and feedback from day one. Before each wave’s reward ends, make the job-specific checklist the first screen on opening a dispatched job, with known job details prefilled. Keep that default and the feedback after the reward ends.

Use **the share of audited jobs that met the pre-job safety standard** to decide what stays. Count approved paper and digital checks equally in that measure. During the three-week overlap, compare how much each wave improved from its baseline. Recheck four weeks after each wave’s reward ends. Use a five-percentage-point greater improvement during overlap and a five-point improvement over baseline at follow-up as the pilot thresholds. If both hold, keep the workflow and retire the prize as scheduled. If the early improvement fades, fix the remaining friction. If the reward adds no clear improvement, drop it. Treat a sample too small to distinguish those outcomes as inconclusive.

### What could go wrong

Technicians may rush through boxes, backfill forms, avoid reporting hazards or classify hard jobs as ineligible. Use the existing safety QA process to spot-check 10 jobs across crews each week, including paper fallbacks and exclusions. Pause rewards for the affected crew if two of the last 10 audited jobs fail the pre-job safety standard, or immediately if an audit finds work began with an unresolved critical hazard. The safety lead decides when rewards resume after the underlying issue is fixed. Reporting a hazard, stopping work or using the approved fallback during an outage never puts earned recognition at risk.
