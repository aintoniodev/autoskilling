## Three-ward hand-hygiene programme

**Reward independently observed hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one team reward during its two-month incentive window. The hospital funds a team meal with equivalent options for night and weekend staff. Everyone on the ward shares it, including staff who were on leave. The ward earns it when its audited compliance across those two months reaches its preannounced target. Kiosk submissions earn zero points.

**Fix the workflow before launch.** Walk through a shift with nurses from each ward, including nights, and give them paid time to try the kiosk. Fix placement, slow screens and unclear fields before starting. Give all three wards the same improvements and feedback, then collect two monthly audits as the baseline. Make the short logging form the kiosk’s default screen and keep it there after the programme. Nurses can skip an entry when care needs their attention or the kiosk is unavailable.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** The measure is the share of eligible observed opportunities where hand hygiene was performed, using the hospital’s existing audit definitions. Pool the counts across the two baseline audits and across the two reward audits. Set each ward’s target at five percentage points above its own baseline, capped at halfway from baseline to 100%. A ward starting at 88% aims for 93%. A ward starting at 96% aims for 98%. A ward starting at 100% aims to maintain it. Hand hygiene counts in the audit even if the kiosk was skipped. A duplicate kiosk entry, a corrected entry or an entry for an ineligible moment has zero effect on the reward.

**Show staff what they can act on.** The kiosk confirms each save immediately and lets the nurse correct it. The existing ward huddle board shows the team’s latest audited rate, the number of observations, its target, the meal and the decision date. Managers see the same ward totals. Collect no nurse or patient identifiers in the kiosk, publish no individual scores and exclude programme data from performance reviews. Each audit covers fresh opportunities, so a missed shift or week creates no catch-up work.

**Stagger the rewards and check what lasts.** After the two baseline months, randomly assign ward start order. Run the two-month reward windows in months 3–4, 4–5 and 5–6. In months 3 and 4, compare each ward’s change from baseline while some wards are eligible for rewards and others are still waiting. Check each ward again at its first monthly audit after rewards end. Keep the kiosk workflow fixes and ward feedback in place. Use audited compliance to decide whether to repeat the incentive. Repeat only if at least two wards reach their targets and sustain them at follow-up, and the staggered comparison favours the reward. Revise the programme if audit coverage prevents a fair comparison. Drop the reward if the lift fades or waiting wards improve just as much. With three wards, treat the comparison as a local decision aid.

**Watch for distorted results and extra work.** Staff may log duplicates, rush to the kiosk or change behaviour mainly when observers are present. Keep the usual audit schedule, ask the audit lead to spot-check a small sample of records for eligibility, and check whether coverage shifted across shifts. Before launch, test duplicate, correction, outage and skipped-log cases. Pause a reward decision if either monthly audit misses the coverage minimum set by the infection prevention lead before launch, or if audited compliance falls five percentage points below baseline. The infection prevention lead reviews the result and decides when to resume. The audit coordinator corrects counting errors and posts the revised ward total. One coordinator can update the board and triage issues in under an hour a week using the existing audit.
