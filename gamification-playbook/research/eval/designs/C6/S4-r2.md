# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Give agents a way to find, preview, and attach an approved article from the ticket resolution screen. Before launch, spend 30 minutes of paid time with agents from several queues to watch them resolve real cases, identify where search or article approval slows them down, and confirm that an hour of paid time off is a reward they want. Fix the biggest obstacle, tell the team what changed because of their feedback, and give everyone paid practice time. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged.

## What earns the reward

**Launch message:** “Earn one hour of paid time off by reaching your personal ticket goal in two weeks. A resolved ticket counts once when you attach an approved article that helps resolve it, submit a usable article that passes the usual review, or correctly record why an article is unnecessary.”

Use a short, agreed list of valid reasons for “article unnecessary,” such as a customer-specific issue with no reusable guidance. A missing article on a repeatable issue goes to the existing knowledge backlog. It can earn credit when the article passes review. Credit it to the original pilot window so approval delays don’t cost the agent their reward.

Before each agent’s reward window, use recent ticket volumes and queue mix to set a private goal at 40% of their expected qualifying resolutions over two weeks, rounded up. Adjust the goal for leave, queue changes, or unexpectedly low ticket volume. A missed day carries no penalty. Agents earn the reward from their own work and can book it during paid hours with normal coverage. The sponsor thanks each recipient personally and privately.

## What people see

The ticket screen immediately shows whether the resolution counted, is awaiting article review, or needs correction. Agents see their own progress and can flag a wrong count from that view. Post team-level progress in the existing support huddle or dashboard. Managers see team totals without individual drill-down. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews and omit named rankings.

## Timing and ending

Give all 60 agents the improved workflow and private feedback for two weeks. Then start the reward in one group of existing teams for two weeks while the other group continues with fixes and feedback alone. Start the second group’s two-week reward window as the first ends. Use teams with similar baseline adoption and case mix where possible. Before the first reward ends, make article suggestions part of the default resolution flow, with a clear way to dismiss irrelevant suggestions. Keep the improved workflow and feedback after the final reward.

The decision metric is the **share of resolved tickets where reusable guidance was warranted and a relevant, approved article was linked or written**. Sample across all resolutions, including tickets marked “article unnecessary,” so misclassified exceptions stay in the denominator. Establish the baseline under this definition during the first two weeks. The current 12% is the reported starting figure. Compare the two groups during the staggered start, then measure again four weeks after the final reward. Keep the workflow if adoption is at least 10 percentage points above baseline and quality holds. If adoption lifts during rewards then fades, fix the remaining friction before considering another reward. Drop the incentive if the staggered start shows no meaningful lift.

## Owner’s note

One support operations owner runs the pilot in under an hour a week. Use existing ticket, article approval, reopen, and QA records. Count each ticket ID once, after final resolution. Each week, review 10 credited tickets through the existing QA process and use a sample of all resolutions for the adoption measure. Check for irrelevant links, duplicate or unusable articles, false exceptions, and premature closes. **Pause payouts if two or more of the 10 credited tickets fail.** The owner corrects counts, tells affected agents privately, and resumes payouts after the next sample clears. If the existing records and QA process can’t verify the work, run the improved workflow and feedback without prizes.

Watch CSAT and reopen rates by team through the existing dashboard. A drop in CSAT or a rise in reopens triggers a review of ticket quality and workload before continuing payouts. Rare cases and review queues can make the reward harder to earn, so the owner checks goal fairness and clears delayed approvals before closing each window.
