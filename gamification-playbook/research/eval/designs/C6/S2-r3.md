### One-working-day code reviews

Give engineers a protected 25-minute review slot each working day and put the oldest ready PRs first in the queue they already use. A rotating backup covers absences and picks up requests that would otherwise miss the deadline. Before launch, ask five engineers who regularly author or review PRs what slows reviews down, which requests need specialist coverage, and whether an hour of paid time back is a reward they’d use. Fix the top routing or workload problem and tell the team what changed. If reviewers still lack time or coverage, delay the reward.

**Reward rule.** Earn one hour of paid time off by meeting your private two-week goal for substantive first reviews submitted within one working day. Set a goal of one, two, or three reviews based on expected opportunities, specialist workload, and scheduled time off. If someone gets fewer eligible assignments than their goal, lower it to the number they received. With zero assignments, carry eligibility into the next window. Each engineer can earn the reward once during the pilot. The owner confirms it privately so the engineer can book it that week.

### What counts and what people see

Use existing PR records. The clock starts when a non-draft PR is ready and review is requested. One working day means eight hours on the team’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. Reassignments and new commits leave the original clock running. A PR withdrawn before its deadline is excluded. A PR already late stays in the measure.

The first substantive submitted review counts once per PR. It may approve, request specific changes, or identify a concrete blocker and the next action needed to clear it. A routine approval is eligible when it reflects an actual review. Acknowledgments and handoffs leave the request open. Credit goes to the engineer who submitted the qualifying review.

Reviewers get a private confirmation after each qualifying review and can see their own progress. The team sees its weekly share of ready PRs answered within one working day in the existing engineering channel. Managers get the same team total. The owner handles individual corrections privately, and reward records stay out of performance reviews.

### Run and ending

- **Weeks 1–2:** Give everyone protected time, backup coverage, the ordered queue, and private feedback. Record the one-day response rate alongside the current 2.6-working-day median.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Start the reward in half the existing review groups, chosen by lot. Compare the change in their on-time rate with the groups still using the same workflow and feedback. Keep cross-group coverage available and treat the comparison as directional given the team size.
- **Weeks 5–6:** Give the remaining groups their two-week reward window. Make the ordered queue and backup rotation the defaults before the last reward window closes.
- **Weeks 7–10:** Keep the workflow and feedback running. At week 10, use the on-time share over the preceding four weeks to decide. At **85% or higher**, keep the workflow as is. At **70–84%**, adjust review capacity or routing. Below **70%**, replace the workflow with designated review coverage and measure again. The reward ends at week six.

### Owner’s note

The engineering lead spends up to one hour a week checking five credited reviews against the team’s existing review checklist, looking at the oldest overdue requests, correcting disputed credits from the PR record, and posting the team rate. Pause payouts if **two of five** sampled reviews fail the quality check, or if a sampled review skipped a required safety or security check. Correct affected credits and resume once a fresh sample passes.

Watch for easy PRs jumping the queue, perfunctory blocker comments, and review work spilling into evenings. Route by age and expertise, treat a blocker as substantive only when it identifies a concrete next action, and use protected time and backup coverage when the queue grows. If the existing PR records and spot checks cannot support reliable credit within the owner’s hour, continue the workflow and feedback without payouts.
