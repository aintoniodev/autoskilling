## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Fix the app, then reward verified safety checks.** Before launch, have union stewards and technicians from different shifts and routes try the checklist on paid time. Fix the problems they encounter in the field, especially poor signal, slow loading and duplicate entry. Make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens a dispatched job, pre-fill job details and let them save it offline. At the next toolbox talk, show what changed because technicians reported it. Agree on the reward, counting rules and data access with union representatives before anyone enrolls.

**Offer 30 minutes of paid time off**, once per technician, earned within one of two reward weeks. Let technicians book the time through the usual leave process. Confirm the reward at a toolbox talk or privately, according to the technician’s preference.

Tell technicians the rule in one sentence: “Earn 30 minutes of paid time off when at least 90% of your eligible jobs in either reward week have a verified pre-job checklist or a documented safety hold.”

Calculate the goal from each technician’s own eligible jobs, rounding up, with no minimum job count. One eligible job means one verified check earns the reward. A technician with no eligible jobs in either week gets the next working week as their earning window. Every job still requires the safety check. The 90% threshold applies to the reward.

### What technicians see

After saving, show “Checklist saved before start” or “Safety hold recorded,” with a pending-sync message where needed. Show each technician their own verified count, eligible job count and any records awaiting sync or correction. Share the team’s aggregate completion rate at the existing toolbox talk. Managers see team totals, with small groups combined to protect privacy. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. The pilot uses existing job and safety records and adds no location or activity tracking.

### How it runs and ends

Give every team two weeks with the fixes, the new default and private feedback. Then start the two-week reward window for half the teams, selected by lottery across comparable routes or shifts. The other half starts two weeks later. During the first reward window, compare the change in verified completion rates for teams with and without the reward. Both groups have the same app, default and feedback.

Say at launch when the reward ends. Keep the checklist first in the app and preserve offline saving and private confirmation. Four weeks after each team’s reward window, compare its **verified pre-job checklist or safety-hold rate** with its own two-week fixes-and-feedback baseline. Use the combined, job-weighted change to decide whether to repeat the reward on future rollouts. Keep it as a time-limited launch tool at **10 percentage points or more** of sustained improvement, change the design at **5 to under 10 points**, and drop the reward **below 5 points**. The staggered comparison estimates what the reward added during the pilot. The follow-up shows how much improvement lasted.

### Owner’s note

One safety coordinator runs the pilot in under an hour a week using an existing records export and a union-agreed correction channel.

- **Count once per dispatched job.** Match the required checklist fields and saved timestamp to the existing job-start record. Use the offline save time once the record syncs. Count a documented pre-start safety hold, including a job cancelled because of the hazard. Exclude jobs cancelled before a check was due. If existing records cannot establish that a check happened before work began, run the fixes and feedback without a prize.
- **Check for box-ticking and missed credit.** Review 10 credited jobs each week using the existing safety audit criteria, including any holds or offline records in the sample. Pause new payouts if **two or more of the 10** are unsupported, or immediately if a review finds anyone was pressured to start unsafely. The coordinator corrects disputed counts before payout and checks sync or outage records so a tech who followed the approved fallback can receive credit.
- **Protect the decision to stop work.** A valid hold earns the same credit as a completed checklist. Review discrepancies against dispatch records so reclassifying a difficult job or logging a late check cannot improve the score. If completion rises during the reward window and falls afterward, use technician feedback to identify what still makes the app hard to use in the field.
