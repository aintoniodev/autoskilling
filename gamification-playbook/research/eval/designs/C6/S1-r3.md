## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Give technicians a paid window to complete the checklist, make it available offline, and put it first when they open a job in the app. A technician who spots a hazard can record a safety hold and get help from dispatch. Offer one hour of paid time off for meeting a personal checklist goal in either of two two-week windows. Launch the reward only after technicians have tested those fixes on real jobs.

### What technicians earn

Agree the reward, eligibility and data rules with union representatives before launch. Test the workflow with technicians across shifts, job types and areas with poor coverage. Give everyone paid practice time and publish the fixes that came from their feedback.

Use one rule in the launch message: **“Earn one hour of paid time off when you complete the checklist before work on at least 80% of your eligible jobs in either two-week window. A correctly recorded safety hold counts.”** The reward is earned once. Show the goal as a job count so each technician knows what they need to do. Carry a window with fewer than five eligible jobs into the next two weeks and allow a make-up window for technicians whose leave or job volume kept them from qualifying. A missed job leaves the next job available to count.

### What counts and what people see

Use the existing job record and safety review. Count each eligible job once when its checklist is saved on the device before work starts, including when the checklist records a valid safety hold and work never starts. Use the device completion time for offline submissions and reconcile them on sync before closing the window. Exclude jobs cancelled before the safety check was due and jobs the existing safety procedure exempts. Keep started jobs with missing or late checklists in the denominator. If existing records cannot reliably establish the order of completion and work start, run the workflow fixes and feedback without payouts until the records can support the rule.

After saving, technicians see “Checklist recorded” or “Saved offline, awaiting sync,” plus their own progress and the reason a job is pending or did not count. Post team completion rates in the existing safety huddle, pooling small teams where totals could identify individuals. Managers receive aggregate programme results only. Keep individual reward counts out of performance reviews. The programme owner handles private corrections through the existing safety or payroll channel and confirms earned time off privately.

### When it runs and how it ends

Give every team two weeks with the fixes and private feedback first. Then randomly assign comparable teams to start the four-week reward period two weeks apart. During the overlap, compare teams with and without the reward on the same measure: **the percentage of eligible jobs with a verified pre-start checklist**, including valid holds. Use the reported 35% as a starting estimate and recalculate it using the pilot’s counting rule.

Keep the checklist first in the job workflow, along with offline access, paid completion time and private confirmation, when rewards end. Check the same completion rate four weeks after each team’s reward ends. Use **80%** as the target for keeping the workflow as-is. Below 80%, use technician feedback and the existing safety audit to fix the remaining barriers. Consider another reward round only if the staggered comparison shows a meaningful lift during the reward period.

### Owner’s note

The safety coordinator runs the pilot in under an hour a week using an automated count and the existing safety audit. Review 10 credited jobs per week across participating teams for timing and checklist quality, including holds where available. Pause payout processing for the affected team if two of the 10 jobs were incorrectly credited, or immediately if any credible report indicates pressure to start unsafe work. Correct the counts before releasing earned payouts.

Watch for rushed answers, checklists completed after work starts, valid holds being discouraged, and technicians losing credit because of sync delays or edited dispatch records. Give technicians a private route to flag errors or pressure through the coordinator or a union representative. Review holds through the existing safety process and leave them out of any speed target for the pilot.
