## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Give agents a way to find and link an article from the ticket, draft a missing article from a template, or record why an article would add no value. Resolve the customer’s issue on the usual timeline. Give agents 30 minutes of paid queue cover to learn the workflow and protected writing time when they find a genuine gap. Before launch, ask agents from different shifts what slows them down, which tickets rarely need an article, and whether an hour of paid time off is a reward they want. Publish the fixes they helped identify.

**Launch rule.** Earn one hour of paid time off by meeting your private two-week goal for resolved tickets with a relevant article linked, a new article approved, or a valid reason to skip.

### What counts

Use existing ticket IDs, resolution records, knowledge-base links or approvals, and support QA. Each resolved ticket counts at most once. A link must help answer the issue or help another agent handle it. A new article must pass the usual editorial review and be linked back to the ticket. A skip counts when the case genuinely warrants it, such as a one-off account adjustment with no reusable guidance. Hold draft credit pending review and apply it to the original window once approved. Remove credit when a reopen or QA review shows the resolution or article was wrong.

Set a reachable goal before each agent’s window using their usual ticket volume and case mix, with 70% of resolved tickets as the starting target. Prorate for leave and low-volume queues. Agents with too few cases get their next active week to qualify. Each agent earns the reward from their own work, once per pilot, regardless of team results. Budget up to 60 paid hours, arrange queue cover, and have the lead confirm the reward personally. The existing CSAT bonus keeps its current rules.

### What people see

After closing a ticket, agents see private confirmation that credit was recorded or that an article is pending review. They can see their own progress and flag a wrong count. Post the team’s weekly completion rate in the support channel. Managers get team totals only. Omit named rankings and streaks, suppress small-group breakdowns, and keep pilot data out of performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

Run two weeks of workflow fixes, paid practice, and private feedback for everyone. Then randomly assign existing teams to two waves. The first wave gets the reward in weeks 3–4 and the second in weeks 5–6. Compare the waves’ change from the shared baseline during weeks 3–4, when both have the same workflow and feedback. Both waves get the same two-week earning window.

Before week 6 ends, make article suggestions and the skip option part of the standard resolution screen. End prizes after week 6, keep that workflow and private feedback, and measure again four weeks later.

Use one decision metric, the **QA-validated share of resolved tickets with a correct knowledge-base outcome**. This includes a relevant link, an approved new article, or a justified skip. Establish the baseline in weeks 1–2 using that definition. The current 12% is the reported link-or-write rate. Keep the workflow if the post-prize measure is at least 15 percentage points above baseline. Revise and retest if the gain is smaller. End the incentive experiment if the gain disappears.

### Owner’s note

The support operations lead runs the pilot in under an hour a week using the existing QA review. Review 12 randomly selected closures across teams and outcome types, including skips and tickets with no recorded outcome. Report the link, write, and skip rates alongside the decision metric so rising skips cannot hide stagnant article use. **Pause new payouts if three or more of the 12 closures have an incorrect credited outcome.** Check existing CSAT and reopen reports for signs agents are rushing closures or delaying replies to write articles. If service quality deteriorates, pause the wave and fix the workflow or workload before restarting. The lead resolves count disputes privately within two working days and honors valid earnings once the review clears them. If existing records cannot reliably distinguish real work from a tick, run the fixes and feedback without prizes.
