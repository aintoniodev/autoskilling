## One working day for a useful code review

Give engineers a clear review queue, a named reviewer for every ready pull request, and 30 minutes of protected review time each workday. Before launch, spend 20 minutes with a few authors and reviewers to find where requests get stuck and confirm that a half-day of paid time off is a reward they’d value. Use the existing repository ownership rules to route requests, put the oldest ready review first, and assign a backup when the primary reviewer is away. Show the team which bottlenecks their feedback helped fix.

**The offer to engineers:** “For two weeks, give a useful first review within one working day on at least 80% of the ready pull requests assigned to you, and earn a half-day of paid time off.”

### What counts

A pull request earns credit once, when its assigned reviewer or designated backup submits a timely first review that lets the author move forward. An approval after checking the change, a specific request for changes, or a concrete blocker the author can act on all count. An acknowledgment or a promise to look later leaves the review open.

Use the pull request system’s existing ready-for-review, assignment, and submitted-review records. The team clock starts when a ready pull request enters the review queue and runs for eight hours on the team’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Reassignment keeps the original deadline and transfers responsibility to the backup. Personal targets use the engineer’s actual assigned cases, including late cases, with the required number rounded up. The rotation gives low-volume reviewers a fair chance to receive an assignment. Anyone with zero eligible assignments gets another opportunity in the next scheduled reward window. A late review affects only that case.

### What people see

After each submitted review, the reviewer gets a private confirmation with the pull request, whether it met the deadline, and progress toward their target. Authors see the assigned reviewer and due time in the pull request. The team sees its aggregate on-time rate and case count in the channel it already uses for engineering updates. Managers receive the same aggregate view. Individual results stay private and out of performance reviews. The sponsor hands over the reward personally and helps the engineer book the time off.

### Timing and decision

Run the queue, protected time, and feedback for everyone for two weeks. Then randomly assign the two existing review rotations to reward windows in weeks 3–4 and 5–6. Compare their on-time rates in weeks 3–4, taking each rotation’s first two weeks into account. With 25 engineers, treat that comparison as a directional estimate of what the reward added.

Before the last reward window closes, make assignment and the oldest-first queue the default in the review tool. Keep protected review time on the calendar. In weeks 8–9, measure the **percentage of ready pull requests receiving a useful first review within one working day**. At **85% or higher**, keep the workflow. If it falls short but beats the first two weeks, fix the largest remaining source of delay. If it returns to or falls below that baseline, retire the reward programme and revisit routing and reviewer capacity.

### Owner’s note

The engineering operations lead runs the pilot in under an hour a week. They sample five credited reviews using the existing pull request history and check for rubber-stamp approvals, vague blockers, and avoidable rework using the team’s normal review standards. **Pause payouts if two of the five sampled reviews fail the quality check.** The same owner handles disputed counts privately, corrects the underlying assignment or review record where appropriate, and resolves the disputed credits before payouts resume.

Watch for easy reviews crowding out complex ones, authors holding changes in draft to protect a colleague’s score, and review duty eating into delivery time. Keep the oldest ready work first, make substantial blockers eligible, and use the protected time and backup rotation to spread the load. If the existing records and spot checks can’t support reliable credit, continue with the workflow fixes and feedback and suspend the prize.
