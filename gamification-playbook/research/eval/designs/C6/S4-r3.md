# Knowledge base adoption pilot

Make the article step part of closing a ticket. Put suggested articles beside the resolution field, let agents link one in a click, and open a prefilled draft from the same screen when there’s a genuine gap. Before launch, give agents 30 minutes of paid practice and ask a mix of shifts and queues to try the workflow on real cases. Fix the two biggest sources of friction they identify and tell the team what changed. Give agents paid time to write articles during their shift.

**The offer.** “Meet your personal target for verified knowledge-base resolutions in a two-week window and earn one hour of paid time off.” Confirm with agents that they want the hour and can schedule it before announcing the offer. The team lead thanks each earner in person and helps book the time. Run the award separately from the existing CSAT bonus.

**What counts.** A resolved ticket earns one credit when the agent links a relevant existing article, links a new article that passes the normal editorial review, or correctly records that an article would add no value under the existing QA rubric. For example, a ticket closed after a customer withdrew a duplicate request may need no article. Settle credit once per ticket ID, with reopened tickets evaluated on final resolution. Once an article covers an issue, later tickets can link it. Use the existing ticket, article, and QA records. If those records can’t distinguish meaningful work from a box tick, start with the workflow fixes and feedback and defer the award.

**What agents see.** At close, show a private confirmation such as “Article linked · 7 of 10 toward your paid hour.” Show drafts awaiting editorial review as pending and credit them back to the original window once approved. Give each agent a reachable target based on their recent volume, scheduled hours, and queue mix. For example, an agent expected to handle 25 article-relevant tickets might have a target of 10 verified article links or publications. Correct “no article needed” decisions count as completed work and reduce the volume used to size the target. Adjust for leave and queue changes. Each window starts fresh. Post team progress in the existing support channel and give managers team totals only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews.

**Timing and decision.** Give all teams the improved workflow and private feedback for two weeks. Then start the award in two comparable groups of teams, two weeks apart, with two earning windows per group. During the stagger, compare the change in article coverage for teams earning the reward with the change for teams receiving feedback alone. Keep article suggestions and the draft shortcut in the normal close flow after the award ends. Four weeks after the last award window, measure the share of article-relevant resolutions with a relevant link or approved new article, using the same QA sampling rule throughout. Recalculate the starting rate on that basis because the reported 12% covers all resolved tickets. Use the post-award improvement over the fixes-and-feedback baseline to decide what to do with the award next time. At 10 percentage points or more, keep it as a short launch option if the staggered comparison showed a lift. At 5–9 points, revise it before another trial. Below 5 points, retire it.

**Owner’s note.** The support QA lead runs the weekly check in under an hour using 10 tickets from the existing QA sample, spread across queues and including “no article needed” decisions. If two or more credits are invalid, pause the affected team’s payouts, correct the counts, and resume after a fresh sample clears the threshold. Agents can send a ticket ID privately to the QA lead for a correction. Watch for irrelevant links, duplicate articles, overused exceptions, and slower resolutions that put CSAT at risk. Use the existing CSAT and reopen reports to spot deterioration and investigate it before the next window. Give editors a two-business-day review target so agents can earn credit for work that is waiting on someone else.
