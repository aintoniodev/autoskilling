**Approve a six-week pilot with 30 minutes of paid time off for each technician who earns it.** First, give technicians paid time to try the checklist on real jobs. Ask technicians from different shifts and routes, together with union reps, what slows them down, what happens when they lose signal, and whether paid time off is a reward they want. Fix the biggest barriers before launching the reward. Make the checklist the first step on the job screen, prefill job details, and support offline completion. Show crews what changed because of their feedback.

### What earns the reward

**Earn 30 minutes of paid time off when you record a valid safety checklist or a documented safety hold before work begins on at least 90% of your eligible jobs during your two-week reward window.**

Count each job once. A valid checklist can clear the job or document why it must stop. A hold earns the same credit as a clearance, even if the job never starts. Exclude jobs cancelled before a technician reaches the safety decision and approved outage exceptions handled through the existing safety procedure. Agree the eligibility rules, reward and privacy terms with union reps before launch. The reward depends on each technician’s own work, and a missed week carries no penalty into the next eligible job.

### What technicians see

After each submission, the app confirms whether the checklist or hold counted. For offline jobs, it shows that credit is pending until sync and uses the recorded completion time. Technicians see their own eligible jobs, credited jobs and target. Supervisors see team totals in the existing weekly huddle, with small groups combined where needed to protect privacy. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. Name the reward at launch and have supervisors confirm it privately when earned.

### How it runs and ends

In weeks 1–2, everyone gets the fixes, paid practice and feedback. In weeks 3–4, half the teams get the reward while the other half continue with fixes and feedback. Pair teams with similar job volumes and baseline completion rates, then randomly choose which team in each pair goes first. In weeks 5–6, the second group gets the same two-week opportunity. The checklist stays first on the job screen after rewards end.

Use one decision metric, **the percentage of eligible jobs with a valid checklist or documented hold before work begins**. Recalculate the reported 35% baseline using that definition and report the post-fix rate separately. Compare the groups during weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds. Check the rate again four weeks after rewards end. With the quality check clear, keep the workflow if the rate is at least 80%. If it’s below 80% but above the post-fix rate, revise the workflow with technicians. If it’s at or below the post-fix rate, drop the prize and revisit the barriers.

### Owner’s note

The safety lead uses existing dispatch records, app timestamps and the routine safety audit to verify credit. Where those records can’t establish whether a checklist or hold came before work, launch the fixes and feedback first and defer the prize. Budget up to an hour a week for the lead to review the automated tally, check exceptions in the existing audit and resolve disputed counts privately.

The main risks are rushed tick-box submissions, backdating and pressure to clear an unsafe job. Use the routine audit to check checklist quality and timing. **One confirmed falsified checklist or incident of pressure to bypass a safety hold pauses payouts** while the safety lead and union rep investigate. Review outage exceptions and reward attainment by team for signs that coverage or job mix is making the reward harder to earn.
