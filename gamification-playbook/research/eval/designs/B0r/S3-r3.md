# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward honest logging, not the number of taps.** Each ward can earn one team commendation and a staff-chosen, funded skills session, with paid time offered across shifts. Every ward can qualify; there is no leaderboard, individual prize, or penalty for missing the target. Training, kiosk repairs and hygiene supplies are provided regardless of results.

### What earns recognition

During the final four weeks, a ward qualifies if **at least 80% of eligible nurse-shifts have a valid entry, with at least 70% coverage on each shift type**, and the ward has fixed and tested one staff-reported logging barrier. For example, night-shift staff might verify that moving a kiosk closer to their normal route removes a detour. Publish the thresholds before launch.

Ask nurses to log real opportunities as soon as safely practical, by the end of the shift. Record the moment type and whether hand hygiene was performed or missed. Either answer counts equally toward logging coverage. One entry is enough for a shift to count toward recognition; further entries still inform workflow improvement but earn nothing extra. **Perform hand hygiene when needed. Log it when safe.** Never delay care to enter a record or guess to fill a gap.

### How it is measured

- **Kiosk adoption:** Divide rostered patient-care nurse-shifts with at least one valid entry by all eligible patient-care nurse-shifts worked. Count each nurse-shift once, including float and agency staff working on the ward. Exclude only independently documented shifts without a working kiosk or approved fallback, and report those separately. If outages leave too little coverage to judge a shift type, mark it *insufficient data*, not a pass. This measure shows whether nurses are using the kiosk, not whether they logged every opportunity.
- **Clinical safety:** Infection prevention staff continue monthly audits independently of the kiosk. Trained observers sample unannounced windows across day, evening, night and weekend work using the same sampling plan at baseline and during the pilot. Report observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities, with the underlying counts. Never use kiosk entries to fill audit gaps. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241598606)
- **Safety gate:** Pause recognition and review promptly if an audit falls five percentage points or more below the ward’s baseline, staff report that logging delays care, or there is credible evidence of fabrication. Five points triggers investigation, not a finding of harm; check the sample size and shift mix and collect more observations if needed. Do not award while a safety or integrity concern is unresolved.

### What staff see

The kiosk confirms **“Entry saved”** and offers an easy correction and a way to report a problem. Each week, a staff-only ward board shows shifts covered out of eligible shifts, gaps by shift type where counts are large enough to protect privacy, and one barrier being fixed with an owner and due date. Monthly observer results appear separately, labelled **“Observed hand hygiene,”** with sample size and baseline. No patient identifiers, named scores, or use of pilot data for individual performance appraisal.

### Duration and exit

Take one month of baseline observations before launch. Then run an eight-week pilot: four weeks to train staff across all shifts and fix access problems, followed by four weeks that count toward recognition. At week eight, close scoring as announced and confirm awards once the final audit is reviewed. Four weeks later, check whether kiosk use held up without rewards and whether audits remained stable. Keep the kiosk only if the records serve a defined improvement purpose and the workload is acceptable; otherwise simplify or retire logging. Monthly audits continue either way.

### What could go wrong

- **Token entries, duplicates or batch fabrication:** Cap credit at one per shift. Review duplicate patterns and unusual entry bursts privately before accepting the figures. A spike in logs is not evidence of better hygiene.
- **Pressure to hide misses or blame colleagues:** Count honest misses equally, offer confidential reporting and never publish individual scores. Investigate pressure as a programme failure, not a reason to rank staff.
- **Unequal access or extra work:** Give night, weekend and float staff training and access from launch. Track downtime and ask weekly whether logging interrupts care. Fix access or pause scoring rather than asking nurses to compensate.
- **Observer effects or a misleading trend:** Vary observation windows and keep sampling comparable. Treat sparse or uneven samples as inconclusive; a three-ward pilot cannot show that the reward caused a change in compliance or infections.
