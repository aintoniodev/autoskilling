## Three-ward pilot: Clean first, log when safe

### What earns recognition

Reward wards for **accurately logging hand hygiene that actually happened**, not for generating entries. Each ward can earn one team recognition at the end of the pilot: a thank-you at the ward huddle and a small, fixed budget for a staff-chosen break-room improvement. All three wards can qualify. No individual prizes, points, streaks, or leaderboard. Soap, sanitiser, staffing support, and kiosk fixes are never contingent on winning.

Nurses clean their hands at the required moment and log the completed action at the next safe break in care, before the shift ends. The kiosk captures ward, moment type, and approximate time of the action separately from submission time. It confirms receipt without implying compliance. Never interrupt patient care, walk away from a patient to log, or reconstruct entries you cannot recall.

### How it is measured

Infection Prevention runs the existing monthly observer audits. Trained observers sample unannounced windows across day, evening, and night shifts, including weekends. During those windows, they record opportunities and completed actions independently of kiosk data. After the shift, an authorised reviewer matches entries one-to-one using a temporary staff token, ward, moment type, and action time within five minutes. No patient identifiers. Flag ambiguous matches and show how many were excluded; do not assume a match.

Keep three measures separate:

| Measure | Calculation |
|---|---|
| **Logging coverage** | Observed completed actions with a matching entry ÷ observed completed actions |
| **Logging accuracy** | Matched entries ÷ assessable entries claiming an action during a period when the nurse was continuously observed |
| **Clinical compliance** | Observed hand-hygiene actions performed when indicated ÷ all observed opportunities, including missed ones. [WHO observation method](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/infection-prevention-and-control/hand-hygiene/monitoring-tools) |

Use proposed pilot thresholds of **80% coverage and 90% accuracy**, pooled over the final two incentive months, with at least **50 observed completed actions and 50 assessable entries per ward**. Require coverage across shifts as well. An undersampled ward is *not yet assessable*, not a failure. Before issuing recognition, Infection Prevention checks compliance against the ward’s own baseline and reviews shift-level results. Hold recognition and investigate if pooled compliance drops more than five percentage points, declines in both months, or a credible safety concern arises. A stable result in a small sample is not proof of safety.

### What staff see

The kiosk shows only confirmation, a correction option, and a way to report an outage or workflow problem. At weekly huddles, staff see what they reported and what management fixed. After each monthly audit, a staff-only ward dashboard shows all three measures separately, with numerators, denominators, baseline, targets, and any sampling gaps. No named records, shift rankings, or comparisons between wards. Temporary matching data is restricted to the audit team, not used for individual performance scoring, and deleted after reconciliation.

### When it ends

Run **one baseline month** with training and no rewards, **three incentive months**, then **two months without rewards**. Publish the end date and rules before launch. At closeout, recognise qualifying wards once and remove reward messaging. Keep monthly audits and useful workflow fixes. After the two follow-up audits, retain routine kiosk logging only if coverage and accuracy hold, observed compliance has not deteriorated, and nurses report that logging fits their work. Otherwise simplify or stop the logging workflow rather than renewing prizes.

### What could go wrong

- **Duplicate, invented, or selectively logged entries:** Give no credit for volume. Check duplicate and unusual backfill patterns, reconcile against observed windows, and never classify entries outside the sample as false.
- **Audit-day behaviour or an unrepresentative sample:** Vary observation times, keep observers independent of ward managers, report sample gaps, and don’t interpret a rise in logs as a rise in compliance.
- **Unequal access or extra burden:** Test kiosk placement with night and float nurses. Record outages and emergencies separately; disclose affected windows rather than silently dropping them or treating them as failures.
- **Patient care displaced or pressure to falsify:** Give nurses a confidential reporting route. Pause incentives immediately on the affected ward if logging delays care or staff are pressured to misreport. Continue normal hand hygiene and clinical audits.
