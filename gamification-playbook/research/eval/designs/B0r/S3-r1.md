# S3: Log it when safe

**Reward accurate logging, not more taps.** Run a three-month pilot on all three wards. Nurses perform hand hygiene as usual and log completed moments at the next safe pause, by the end of the shift. The kiosk accepts the approximate time and moment type; it never asks for patient details. A missed log is a documentation gap, not evidence that hygiene was missed. Never backfill from guesswork.

Each ward can earn one team thank-you at its monthly huddles, repeated across shifts. Name the workflow improvement that helped, such as moving a kiosk out of a queueing bottleneck. All three wards can qualify. No cash, points per entry, individual streaks, leaderboards, or scores in performance reviews. Equipment and workflow fixes are available whether or not a ward qualifies.

## Measurement and eligibility

Infection Prevention uses the existing monthly audit, with trained observers sampling short, varying windows across days, evenings, nights, and weekends. Staff know the method, not the exact windows. Observers record findings before seeing kiosk data. Keep three measures separate:

| Measure | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Observed compliance** | Hand-hygiene actions performed at the appropriate time ÷ all observed opportunities, including misses. Kiosk entries never enter this calculation. [WHO observation method](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf) |
| **Logging coverage** | Observed completed actions with one matching kiosk record by shift end ÷ all completed actions in the validation sample. |
| **Logging accuracy** | Uniquely matched records ÷ all assessable kiosk records for the same sampled nurses and windows. Review unmatched records; count confirmed duplicates as errors and show ambiguous cases separately. |

For validation, use temporary staff codes, moment type, and approximate event time to match records one-to-one. Only assess windows in which the observer had sufficient visibility. Delete the matching key after reconciliation; retain ward totals.

Set the pilot rules before launch: **at least 80% coverage and 90% accuracy**, with at least 50 observed completed actions and 30 assessable kiosk records per ward-month, spanning day, evening, and night shifts. These are pilot logging targets, not clinical standards. If the sample is too small or ambiguous cases could change whether the ward qualifies, mark it **not enough evidence**, not a fail. Hold recognition for Infection Prevention review if observed compliance drops five percentage points or more from the ward’s pre-pilot baseline, or if logging appears to delay care. A smaller drop is not proof of safety.

## What staff see

The kiosk says **“Saved”** and allows a correction. A weekly staff huddle shares kiosk downtime, one reported barrier, and who is fixing it. After each audit, a staff-only ward dashboard shows the three measures separately, with numerators, denominators, shift coverage, and the ward’s own trend. No public display, individual breakdowns, or cross-ward ranking.

## Duration and exit

Use the preceding month’s comparable audit as the compliance baseline; if none exists, collect one before launching. Announce the reward end date at kickoff. At the end of month three, recognition stops automatically. Keep the kiosk only where the last two audits meet the logging targets, Infection Prevention finds no unresolved safety concern, and staff confirm it fits the workflow. Fix and retest elsewhere rather than extending the reward. Recheck retained wards after 30 and 90 days without incentives; routine clinical audits continue regardless.

## What could go wrong

- **Fabricated, duplicate, or selectively logged entries.** Give no credit for volume. Review anomalies privately and check for timestamp or workflow errors before assuming misconduct.
- **Logging competes with care.** Put kiosks on existing staff routes. Tell nurses explicitly to skip the log rather than interrupt care. Pause the pilot on a ward if logging causes delays or unsafe workarounds.
- **The sample flatters the ward.** People may act differently while watched, and quieter shifts can be overrepresented. Rotate windows, show gaps, and never present sample results as whole-ward compliance.
- **Busy shifts get blamed for broken access.** Review outages and missed shift coverage separately. Make up lost sampling when possible; never treat an unavailable kiosk as a nurse’s failure.

## Key Learnings:

1. More entries can mean better documentation without better hand hygiene. Keep the two measures separate.
2. The post-reward checks show whether logging fits the job without a prize.
