# Before Every Job: an eight-week adoption programme

**Goal:** Raise valid digital pre-job checklist completion from about 35% to 85% of eligible jobs, without making technicians less willing to stop work or report hazards. The checklist is expected before every job; the temporary reward is for learning and practising the routine.

### What earns the reward

Offer a **one-time award equal to one hour of base pay** after a technician completes three valid pre-job checks on separate shifts. Pay it in the next payroll cycle, on top of normal pay for training and checklist time. A properly documented **“stop and escalate” earns exactly the same credit as “safe to start.”** There is no extra pay for more jobs, faster checks, fewer hazards, or injury-free shifts.

Before launch, agree the award, eligibility, data access, and appeals in writing with union representatives. Dispatch schedules paid time for checks, and the company provides supported devices and paid practice. An approved outage fallback earns equal award credit. Technicians who cannot get three field opportunities because of leave, low job volume, or access barriers can demonstrate the remaining checks in paid practice. Practice does not count toward field adoption.

### How it is measured

Use dispatch records, not app submissions, to count eligible jobs: **one record per dispatched job that reaches the pre-work decision point**, including jobs held or cancelled because of a hazard. Exclude only duplicates and jobs cancelled before that point. Count one check per job, with a named lead for multi-technician jobs.

A digital check counts if it is linked to the work order, covers the applicable hazards, records controls or escalation, and is saved before work begins or at the stop-work decision. An offline app save counts when it syncs with its original timestamp; a late entry does not become on time. Approved paper or dispatcher-logged fallbacks count toward **safe-process completion**, but not **digital adoption**. Report both rates against the same denominator, plus the number and reasons for fallbacks.

Each week, a joint union-management safety group reviews 20 randomly sampled records across shifts and job types for meaningful answers and appropriate action. It checks a small subset with technicians or dispatch rather than assuming a timestamp proves an inspection happened.

### What people see

The job screen opens directly to **“Start safety check.”** After submission, it shows **“Check recorded”** or **“Work on hold”**, with the next step and dispatcher contact. Technicians privately see award progress, which records counted, and how to challenge a missing credit through a steward or pilot coordinator.

Everyone sees weekly company-wide counts, both completion rates, and a short **“You reported / we fixed”** update. No leaderboards, public names, GPS tracking, or individual productivity scores. Pilot scoring data is not used for discipline; access to identifiable award records is limited to verification, payroll, and appeals.

### How it runs and ends

- **Two weeks before launch:** Recalculate the baseline using the dispatch denominator. Test offline and fallback workflows with technicians from different shifts.
- **Weeks 1–8:** Run the award. Review results and fix blockers weekly. Announce the end date at launch and remind crews in week 6.
- **After week 8:** End the award regardless of the score, pay earned awards, and allow 10 working days for appeals. Keep paid checklist time, the app prompt, and fallback. Measure four more weeks without payouts. Call it sustained if digital completion is at least 85% in both the final two reward weeks and the four-week follow-up, without a drop in audit quality. If it slips, fix the causes before considering another incentive.

### What could go wrong

- **Box-ticking or backfilling:** No pre-filled “all clear.” Investigate implausibly fast or repeated answers through sampled review, not automatic penalties.
- **Pressure to start or stay quiet:** Protect stop-work decisions and remove safety holds from dispatch productivity targets. Keep an independent reporting route through the union. A credible report of retaliation or pressure to bypass a check triggers a joint review and a pause in new awards, not a forfeiture. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation)
- **Outages or unequal access:** Never start work just to clear an app problem. Use the approved fallback, record the cause, and assign recurring device or connectivity issues to operations.
- **A high average hides a struggling shift:** Review results by shift and job type where groups are large enough to protect privacy. Fix scheduling, access, or checklist problems rather than blaming the crew.
