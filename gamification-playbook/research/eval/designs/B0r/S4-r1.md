## S4: Close the ticket, leave the answer

**Goal:** Make checking the knowledge base part of every resolution. Raise verified link-or-write coverage from the reported 12% without degrading customer service. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and survey rules unchanged. Knowledge-base metrics do not affect individual pay, performance ratings, or rankings.

### What earns credit

At resolution, the ticket form prompts the agent to search the KB and record one outcome:

- **Link:** Attach the current article that actually supports the fix. An internal link counts; agents should not send customers irrelevant articles just to get credit.
- **Write or update:** If no article fits, submit a reusable, anonymised draft or a substantive update. The KB editor checks accuracy, duplicates and privacy within two business days. It counts when approved, against the original resolution week. Agents close the ticket without waiting for review.
- **Record an exception:** If there is genuinely no reusable resolution, choose a reason. If there is a reusable fix but the draft needs more time, log the gap for follow-up. Neither earns coverage credit. A confirmed tool outage is also recorded separately.

A valid link and an approved draft each earn **one credit per ticket**, never more. There are no prizes for article volume. If the organisation meets the finish criteria, all 60 agents get a paid, 60-minute team-chosen learning session, staggered across shifts. Regardless of results, roster 30 minutes per agent per week for drafting and cleanup so knowledge work does not compete with queue time.

### How it is measured and what people see

Support Ops counts unique resolved customer tickets from the ticket system, not from agent submissions. Report **closeout completion** (any recorded outcome over all resolved tickets) separately from **verified coverage** (valid links plus approved drafts over all resolved tickets). Exceptions stay in the denominator. Count reopened tickets once and check the final resolution.

Before launch, apply the same definitions to the prior four weeks; the reported 12% is a starting point, not necessarily a verified baseline. Each week, QA samples 60 tickets across queues, shifts and outcome types, including exceptions and tickets with no entry. Weight the sample back to the actual ticket mix. A valid link must match the issue and the fix, not merely the product. Publish sample sizes and mark results provisional until editorial review closes.

Agents see their own ticket outcomes, pending reviews and correction notes. Everyone sees the org-wide trend, target, audit pass rate, service guardrails and the most common missing articles. The weekly huddle highlights one useful reuse example and one gap to fix. No public individual scores or queue league tables.

### Run, stop and hand off

Run for **eight weeks**. Use weeks 1–2 to train agents and fix search and review friction. Aim for 50% verified coverage by week 4. Award the shared reward if weeks 7–8 together reach **95% closeout completion, 70% verified coverage and a 90% audit pass rate on claimed credit**, with service guardrails met. These are pilot milestones, not permission to skip the KB on the remaining tickets.

Compare service metrics with the four-week baseline by queue and channel. Pause reward scoring if, for two consecutive weeks, adjusted CSAT falls more than 3 percentage points, seven-day reopens rise more than 2 points, or median time to resolution rises more than 10%. Show survey response counts and rates; if there are too few responses to assess CSAT, mark it inconclusive and defer the award decision rather than treating it as a pass. Investigate immediately if sensitive data is published.

End the incentive at week 8 whether or not it succeeds. Keep the closeout prompt, paid writing time and named KB editor. Recheck adoption and service four weeks later without a reward. If coverage falls, fix the biggest workflow or content bottleneck before considering another campaign.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, duplicate drafts or exception dumping:** Audit all three, remove invalid credit and coach from examples. Route repeat gaps to one article owner.
- **Hard queues carry more of the writing burden:** Review results by queue and ticket type. Direct editor capacity and protected time to gap-heavy queues rather than pressuring agents to match each other.
- **Agents delay closure or cherry-pick easy tickets:** Let tickets close while drafts are pending. Monitor backlog age, routing changes and reopens alongside coverage.
- **Review capacity becomes the bottleneck:** Show pending drafts separately. If the editor misses the two-day commitment, add backup coverage and defer scoring the affected cohort; do not count review delays as agent failures.
