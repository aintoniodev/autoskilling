### Make the knowledge base easier to use at ticket close

Give agents a paid 30-minute session to try the workflow on real tickets. Ask agents from each queue where they lose time, which cases need no article, and whether an hour of paid time off is a reward they would use. Fix the biggest blockers before the reward starts. Put suggested articles in the ticket close screen, make linking a one-click action, and route article drafts to a named reviewer. Show agents what changed based on their feedback. Agents can close tickets while drafts are under review.

### Reward one correctly completed ticket

**Launch message.** “Earn one hour of paid time off when you hit your personal goal over the next two weeks. A resolved ticket counts once when you link a relevant article, write one that gets approved, or record a valid reason in the close note for why an article isn’t needed.”

Use existing ticket IDs, resolution records, article links, approvals, and close notes. Credit the resolving agent once per ticket. Duplicate links earn no extra credit. A reopened ticket loses its credit and can earn it back on final resolution. A draft is pending until approved. A ticket closed through the old workflow can count if its existing record meets the same rule. Valid exceptions include a case-specific resolution with no reusable guidance or a privacy restriction that prevents safe documentation. An empty note or failed search earns no credit.

Set and show each agent’s target at the start using scheduled shifts and recent volume in their queue. Start at roughly half their expected resolutions. For example, an agent expected to close 16 tickets needs eight qualifying resolutions, and an agent expected to close four needs two. Prorate targets after absences and let agents who miss the window use their next active week. Each agent can earn the hour once. The programme owner confirms it in person and arranges paid coverage. The existing CSAT bonus formula stays in place.

### Show private progress and one team number

At close, tell the agent whether the ticket counted, is pending, or needs a correction. Agents see their own progress and can ask the programme owner to fix a count using the ticket ID. Managers see the team’s weekly share of resolved tickets with a qualifying outcome in the dashboard they already use, with no individual drill-down. Keep reward data out of performance reviews.

### Run two waves and check what sticks

Give all 60 agents the workflow fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Measure the baseline using the new rule, since today’s **12%** covers linking or writing and excludes valid exceptions. Randomly assign comparable teams to earn rewards in weeks 3–4 or 5–6. Compare the early and later teams during weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds. End new earning after week 6, with week 7 reserved for absence makeups and pending approvals. Keep article suggestions in the close screen.

In week 11, use one decision metric, the **audit-adjusted share of resolved tickets with a qualifying outcome**. Count invalid sampled claims against the reported rate. Set the decision before launch. At **40% or more**, keep the workflow and end prizes. At **25–39%**, fix the most common remaining blocker. Below **25%**, drop the incentive and investigate where the workflow still costs agents time.

### Catch gaming and customer harm

The programme owner samples 12 credited tickets each week across queues, including exceptions. If **three or more** have an irrelevant link, weak article, or unjustified exception, the Support Ops lead pauses new reward credit and assigns the fix. The owner corrects disputed counts. Also watch team CSAT and reopen rates for signs agents are rushing to close. Use the existing support review to investigate any deterioration while keeping the bonus formula intact. Budget one hour a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.
