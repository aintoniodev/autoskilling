## One-working-day code reviews

Give engineers a clear review queue and protected time to use it. Run a short pilot with one reward: a paid hour of the engineer’s choosing, covered by the team’s review rota.

### Fix the workflow first

Ask engineers from different parts of the team to walk through several PRs that waited longer than a day. Find out where ownership, interruptions, large diffs, or specialist availability slowed them down. Confirm that the paid hour is worth earning and can be taken without catching up later.

Give every ready PR a primary reviewer and a backup, with coverage for leave and specialist areas. Put the oldest ready PRs first in the review queue engineers already use. Reserve a daily review block within working hours and give the team paid time to practise the workflow. Share what engineers reported and what changed. Start rewards once the rota and queue are working.

### What earns the reward

**Launch rule:** “Complete your assigned share of on-time first reviews in the next two weeks and earn one paid hour of your choosing. An approval, a substantive request for changes, or a documented reason the PR cannot safely proceed all count.”

Set each person’s target privately before their reward window. Start with two reviews for people expected to receive at least three routine assignments and one for people with lighter volume or complex assignments. Route a real assignment to anyone who wants to participate and has none. Extend the window if leave or a lack of eligible work gets in the way. Each engineer qualifies through their own work and can earn the reward once. The engineering lead thanks them privately and arranges coverage when they redeem it.

### How it’s measured and what people see

Use existing PR timestamps and submitted reviews. The clock starts when a PR first becomes ready for review and expires after one working day on the team’s published working calendar. Count the first qualifying human review once per PR. An acknowledgement, bot comment, or self-review does not qualify. A valid hold must name the blocker and next step. A duplicate submission adds zero, and a dismissed review loses credit unless another qualifying review was submitted by the deadline. An eligible PR handled off-platform still enters the denominator and counts as on time if the review is recorded on the PR by the deadline. Exclude a PR withdrawn before its deadline only if the author records that the work was abandoned. Returning to draft keeps the original clock.

The success metric is **the percentage of eligible PRs that receive a qualifying first review within one working day**, including still-unanswered PRs whose deadlines have passed. Use the same definition to reconstruct the four-week baseline where timestamps allow. The current 2.6-day median gives context, but the pilot decision uses the on-time percentage.

After each qualifying review, send the engineer a private confirmation with their progress toward the reward. Show the team one weekly percentage and its denominator in the existing engineering channel. Managers see that same aggregate. Pilot counts stay out of performance reviews. The review rota owner handles count corrections privately.

### How it runs and ends

Give all 25 engineers the workflow fixes and feedback for two weeks. Then start the two-week reward window in one existing review group and start the second group two weeks later. Attribute each reward to the reviewer’s group. Compare each group’s on-time rate during the fixes-only and reward periods, treating the comparison as directional given team size and shared PRs.

After the second window, stop offering new rewards, honour earned hours, and leave the queue order, routing, and protected review time in place. At launch, commit to an **80% on-time target** across the four weeks after rewards end. Keep the workflow if it meets the target. If the rate improves over the fixes-only period but falls short of 80%, adjust the bottleneck and remeasure. If it falls short and shows no improvement, retire the reward programme and use the late PRs to decide what to fix.

### What could go wrong

- **Fast approvals or bogus holds.** The rota owner samples five credited reviews each week, including holds and approvals, using the existing review standards. If two or more fail, the engineering lead pauses new reward credit, corrects affected counts, and resumes after reviewing a fresh sample that clears the threshold. Test duplicates, dismissals, and off-platform reviews before launch.
- **Engineers cherry-pick easy work or review after hours.** Use the existing assignment rota to distribute work, with explicit specialist coverage. The working-hours clock gives no deadline advantage to reviewing on weekends.
- **The programme feels like surveillance or a quota.** Keep participation in rewards voluntary and individual progress private. Missing a week carries no penalty. The next eligible review can still count.
