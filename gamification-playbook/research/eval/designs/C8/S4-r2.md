## Make knowledge part of resolving a ticket

Give agents a knowledge-base search inside the ticket, with suggestions based on the issue category and a one-click link. If there’s a gap, let agents start an article draft from their resolution notes and send it to the KB editor. Make this the default closeout flow before rewards start. Ask agents across shifts and queues to test it on real cases, fix the slowest steps, and show the team what changed. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and ask whether the proposed reward is worth earning.

## What earns the reward

Announce **one hour of paid time off** at launch. Each agent earns it once from their own work during a two-week window. The programme runs alongside the existing CSAT bonus, with its calculation and eligibility unchanged. The programme coordinator hands over the award privately, and workforce planning covers the hour.

**Counting rule.** A resolved ticket earns one credit when it has a relevant published article linked, whether reused or newly written, or a valid “no reusable knowledge” close reason.

Use the ticket ID, resolution status, article link and existing close reason to count automatically. An article gap stays pending until the editor publishes the article and it’s linked. The customer’s ticket can close immediately, and drafts submitted during the window have five business days to clear review. Set a reachable personal target based on scheduled hours, expected ticket volume and case mix. For example, an agent expected to close ten tickets needs six credits, while an agent expected to close three needs two. Adjust the target for leave or a light queue. Progress carries across both weeks.

## What agents see

At closeout, agents get private confirmation of what counted or what’s pending, plus progress toward their target. The existing team dashboard shows one aggregate, the percentage of resolved tickets with a completed knowledge closeout. Managers have access to the team total only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews. The coordinator handles corrections through the existing support channel and tells the agent when the count is fixed.

## Timing and decision

Run the improved workflow and private feedback for everyone for two weeks. Give half the existing teams their two-week reward window first, then start the other half two weeks later. Compare the groups during the stagger using the same measure, with queue mix and the pre-reward baseline in view.

Four weeks after each team’s reward window ends, use one decision metric: **the share of KB-applicable resolved tickets with a relevant published article linked**, estimated from a random sample across queues. Measure the same rate before rewards, since the reported 12% covers all resolved tickets. Set the decision bands at launch. At 60% or above, keep the closeout flow as the default. At 30–59%, fix the biggest remaining obstacle and remeasure. Below 30%, drop the programme and revisit the workflow with agents. Paid-time-off awards end on schedule in every case.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, duplicate articles or overused exceptions.** The coordinator checks ten random credited tickets each week, including exceptions, using existing ticket records. A valid exception might be a one-off account correction with no reusable guidance. At two or more invalid credits in ten, the support lead pauses awards, the coordinator corrects affected counts, and the KB editor fixes the content or guidance. Resume after a fresh sample falls below the threshold. Automate counting so the weekly sample and corrections fit within an hour.
- **Inflated counts from reopens or old workflows.** Before launch, test a duplicate close event, a reopened ticket, a removed link and a ticket resolved through the old workflow. Count each ticket once. A reopen or removed link reverses provisional credit until the ticket qualifies again. An old-workflow ticket earns credit when its existing record meets the same rule.
- **Slower service or pressure to protect the CSAT bonus.** Keep article review out of the customer’s wait. Check existing resolution time and CSAT by queue during the stagger. If either worsens, inspect the sampled tickets for delays caused by closeout and have the support lead fix that step before the next wave.
