## One working day for a first code review

Give engineers a protected daily review slot and a clear queue in the code host. Reward a substantive first review within one working day with **one hour of paid time off**. Run it as a short trial with a private tally for each engineer and one team-wide number.

### Fix the review queue first

Ask a few engineers who regularly author and review PRs where reviews get stuck, what makes a review worth receiving, and whether they’d use an hour of paid time off. Give everyone paid time to try the workflow and agree on what a good first review looks like.

When an author marks a PR ready and requests review, route it to one available reviewer using the team’s existing ownership rules. Put the oldest pending requests first. Protect 30 minutes a day for reviews and name a backup for absences and overload. Treat that slot as part of planned capacity. Use the first two weeks to fix routing and workload problems, and tell the team which changes came from their feedback.

### What earns the reward

Tell engineers at launch: “Earn one hour of paid time off by completing your private review goal within two weeks. A review counts when you give the author a substantive answer on a ready PR within one working day.”

Count the first qualifying human review recorded in the code host, once per PR. An approval, a request for changes, or a specific hold that explains the blocker and next step qualifies. A quick acknowledgment, emoji, bot response, or self-review does not. Reviewers can use their usual workflow as long as the author gets a durable review in the PR by the deadline.

Start the clock at the first review request on a ready PR. Allow eight hours on the team’s agreed working calendar, skipping evenings, weekends, and team holidays. Reassigning the PR or toggling draft status keeps the original deadline. A duplicate review earns nothing. A withdrawn review loses credit if it was recorded in error or left the author without an answer. Exclude a genuinely abandoned PR only if it was withdrawn before the deadline and before any review. A late review still helps the author and leaves the engineer free to earn credit on the next case.

Set private goals of one to four reviews per engineer based on recent assignment volume, planned availability, and case complexity. Give low-volume reviewers a genuine assignment through the normal queue if one is available. Carry earned credit through absences during the trial. Each engineer earns the reward once from their own work. The programme owner hands it over in a brief private conversation as soon as it’s verified.

### What people see and how it runs

After a qualifying review, send a private confirmation such as “Review counted. Two of three toward your paid hour.” Show the whole team one weekly number where they already plan work: the percentage of eligible PRs that received a substantive first review by the deadline. Include unanswered PRs once their deadline passes. Managers get the same aggregate, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews.

Give everyone the queue, protected time, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Start the two-week reward window for one existing ownership group in weeks 3–4 and the other in weeks 5–6. Assign groups before launch and keep normal backup coverage. Compare each group’s on-time rate before, during, and after its reward window to see what the reward adds. The groups are small, so treat the comparison as directional.

### Quality and the ending

The programme owner samples five credited reviews a week for evidence that the reviewer engaged with the change and gave the author a usable decision. Pause new credits if two of the last ten sampled reviews fail that check. The owner decides whether to restart after addressing the cause with the team’s engineering lead, and corrects disputed counts privately. Watch for rubber-stamp approvals and easy PRs jumping the queue. Keep the oldest request first and use the backup when a difficult review needs more time.

Before launch, calculate the on-time rate for the previous four weeks using the same rules. Rewards end after week 6. Keep automatic routing, oldest-first ordering, and protected review time in place. Decide using the on-time rate across weeks 7–10. At **90% or higher**, keep the workflow without rewards. Below 90% but above baseline, fix the remaining bottleneck. At or below baseline, drop the incentive and revisit review capacity and ownership.
