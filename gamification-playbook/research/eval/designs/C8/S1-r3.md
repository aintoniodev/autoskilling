# Safe Start pilot

Make the checklist the first step on the mobile job card, prefill the job details, and leave every safety answer blank. Technicians get an hour of paid personal time for reaching a private goal within two weeks. A properly recorded safety hold earns the same credit as a completed checklist on a job that proceeds.

## Before launch

Give technicians from different shifts, routes, and job types paid time to try the app with their union stewards, dispatch, and Safety. Walk through a weak-signal job, a repeat visit, and a stop-work decision. Fix the barriers they find, test the approved fallback, and tell crews what changed. Give everyone paid practice time. Have the union and company agree on the reward, eligibility, data access, and appeals before announcing the pilot.

## What earns credit

**Tell crews:** “Earn one credit per eligible job visit when you save a complete safety checklist before work starts or record a valid safety hold before proceeding.”

Use the existing job-visit ID, checklist or hold record, and dispatch work-start timestamp. An offline entry earns credit when it syncs if its saved time can be verified. A duplicate earns one credit total. If a checklist is withdrawn as invalid, reverse the credit. A hold followed by a restart still earns one credit, and the usual requirement to recheck before starting applies. A routine paper checklist earns no pilot credit. An approved fallback during an app or device outage earns credit if existing records verify it. A visit cancelled before any safety decision is due is ineligible. If the records cannot reliably establish timing or distinguish corrections from duplicates, fix that before rewards start.

Set the private goal at five verified visits for a technician expected to have at least five eligible opportunities over two weeks. Use a lower goal for technicians with fewer opportunities because of their schedule or case mix. Recalculate downward if leave, cancellations, or outages reduce those opportunities. A missed visit leaves the next eligible visit available. Announce the paid hour at launch and have a coordinator confirm it privately when earned. Each technician earns it from their own work.

## What people see

After saving, show the technician the recorded outcome and their progress, such as “Safety hold saved. 3 of 5 visits credited.” For offline entries, show “Saved on device. Credit pending sync.” Show one weekly team completion rate in the existing dispatch briefing and roll small crews into a larger group. Managers see team totals. Technicians see their own progress. Restrict individual records used for the pilot to the coordinator and existing authorized safety reviewers. Payroll receives only what it needs to grant the hour. Pilot data stays out of performance reviews.

## Timing and ending

Use weeks 1–2 to fix the workflow, make it the default for everyone, and establish a baseline with private feedback already on. Check the reported 35% against the same definition used in the pilot. Assign comparable crews to start rewards in weeks 3–4 or weeks 5–6. Both waves get the same two-week earning window. Compare the waves during weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds once crews have the improved app and feedback.

At the end of week 6, stop issuing new credits, honor earned hours, and keep the checklist first on the job card. Four weeks later, use one decision metric: **the percentage of eligible visits with a valid digital checklist recorded before work or a valid digital hold recorded before proceeding**. Include approved paper fallbacks in the denominator so outages remain visible. Agree the bands before launch. A proposed target is 75% or higher to keep the workflow and feedback as designed, 55–74% to revise and retest, and below 55% to drop the incentive approach and investigate barriers. Safety-check requirements continue throughout.

## What could go wrong

- **Box-ticking, backdating, or pressure to start.** Safety reviews ten randomly selected records a week through its existing review process, with holds and corrections represented when available. One critical defect, such as work starting before a valid check or a hold being bypassed, or two materially incomplete records in ten pauses reward issuance. The Safety lead makes the call, informs the union steward, and resumes issuance after the fix is verified. Valid credits carry forward.
- **Bad counts or pressure over individual numbers.** The pilot coordinator checks disputes against existing records, corrects counts, and replies privately within two working days. Technicians can raise a dispute through their steward.
- **Unfair access or a short-lived lift.** Paid training, approved outage credit, and goals based on actual opportunities address differences in access. The four-week follow-up tests whether completion holds after the reward ends.
