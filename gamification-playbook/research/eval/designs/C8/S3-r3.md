## Three-ward kiosk pilot

**Make the kiosk easy to use before offering a reward.** In week 1, the nurse educator walks through the workflow with day- and night-shift nurses on all three wards. Ask where logging interrupts care, where the kiosk causes queues, and whether 30 minutes of paid release is a reward they’d use. Fix placement, sign-in, or duplicate paperwork before starting the reward. Give every shift protected practice time and post the fixes at the kiosk and in the usual ward huddle. Patient care and hand hygiene come first. If logging would delay care, use the hospital’s approved fallback.

**Reward one correctly documented real case.** At launch, tell nurses: “Save and check your first real hand-hygiene record during a supported shift and earn 30 minutes of paid release.” The nurse educator verifies the saved record during ordinary coaching and confirms it privately on the spot. A correctly handled downtime or safety exception qualifies if the approved fallback documents the case and the educator can verify it. Each nurse earns the reward once. The ward scheduler arranges cover and books the time within two weeks of earning it. One case is the goal across all shifts and case mixes. A nurse who misses the window can complete it on their next supported shift.

**Use the existing records.** The educator checks the kiosk’s saved confirmation and uses the existing training sign-off for reward eligibility. One case earns one credit. A second submission earns zero, a voided entry can be corrected before sign-off, and an old-route entry qualifies only when it was the approved fallback. Test those three cases and a kiosk outage before launch. Each week, the educator checks five existing records per ward, or all if fewer, for duplicates, voids, and missing required fields. Nurses can raise a disputed credit privately with the educator, who corrects the sign-off. The kiosk owner fixes system errors.

**Show one ward view.** The kiosk gives immediate “Saved” feedback and a clear way to correct an entry. Nurses get their own reward confirmation privately. The usual ward huddle board shows the share of staffed shifts with at least one valid kiosk entry and the latest observed compliance rate with its denominator. Managers see ward totals only. Keep reward fulfilment with the educator and scheduler, and keep pilot data out of performance reviews. Kiosk records measure use. Infection Prevention calculates clinical compliance separately from the existing monthly observer audit as hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Self-reports can overstate compliance, and observer results reflect the opportunities sampled. [WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Stagger the reward and set an end date.** Give all three wards the fixes, practice time, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Run two-week reward windows in weeks 3–4, 5–6, and 7–8. Compare each ward’s usage before, during, and after its window and use the later-starting wards as a rough comparison. Before each window closes, make the short logging form the kiosk default and retain the downtime route. Four weeks after each window, use one adoption measure to decide what to do with the workflow: keep it at **80% or more of staffed shifts with a valid entry**, fix the workflow and retest at **60–79%**, and drop the kiosk pilot below **60%**. Set those pilot thresholds before launch. Shift coverage measures whether the kiosk is in routine use. The monthly audit remains the clinical safety check.

**Pause for harm or bad data.** If two of five sampled records on a ward are invalid, pause new sign-offs on that ward while the kiosk owner investigates. Infection Prevention can pause the pilot immediately if logging delays care or creates an infection-control hazard. A monthly audit drop of five percentage points from that ward’s pre-pilot baseline triggers a pause and review of the sample size and shift mix before restarting. Rising entry counts alongside flat or falling observed compliance are a reason to investigate, especially for backfilling or duplicate entries. If paid release is hard to schedule on nights, reserve cover before opening that ward’s reward window.
