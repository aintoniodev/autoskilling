## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Make the kiosk fit the shift first.** Ask nurses from all three wards, including night and float staff, to walk through where and when logging would fit, what would make them skip it, and whether they’d value 30 minutes of paid time off. Give them protected time to try the workflow. Fix access, location and sign-in problems before launch, and post a short “you reported it, we fixed it” update. Have infection prevention approve kiosk placement and the logging workflow. Patient care takes priority, and a kiosk outage or an urgent clinical interruption never costs someone eligibility.

**Reward one completed shift record.** Put this rule in the launch message: “Complete your hand-hygiene record on two of your first three eligible shifts during the pilot and earn 30 minutes of paid time off.” A complete record covers the moments the nurse can reliably report. An accurately recorded missed moment or a shift with no applicable moments can qualify. An incomplete record stays editable and can be completed later. Extra entries and higher self-reported compliance earn nothing extra. Offer one eligible shift as the goal for nurses scheduled for only one during the pilot. Extend the window after leave or an outage so people have a fair chance to qualify. Arrange coverage and hand over the reward privately in person.

**Count each shift once.** Use the kiosk’s saved records and the existing roster to identify the nurse and shift. Add a simple end-of-shift review and submit step if the kiosk needs one, using those same records. A duplicate submission or edit keeps the same shift record. A withdrawn record stops counting until resubmitted. A paper-only shift during an outage is excused and replaced by the next available shift. Before launch, test those cases along with an overnight shift and a shift with no applicable moments. Each week the pilot coordinator checks a small sample for duplicates, roster mismatches and inconsistent records. These checks verify the documentation. The existing monthly observer audit measures hand-hygiene compliance independently.

**Give immediate feedback and keep individual progress private.** The kiosk confirms each save and shows the nurse their own progress at submit, for example, “Shift saved. One more eligible shift earns your paid time off.” Show each ward’s completed-record rate in the usual staff huddle or channel. Combine reporting periods if a small group would make individuals identifiable. Managers get ward totals. The coordinator handles private corrections and reward delivery. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews.

**Stagger the reward and make the kiosk the default.** Give every ward the fixes and private feedback first. Run those for two weeks, then start two-week reward windows on wards A, B and C two weeks apart. Compare completed-record rates before and during each window to see what changed when the reward arrived. Before the first reward window ends, make the current shift’s record the first screen after sign-in, with ward and shift prefilled. Keep that workflow and feedback when rewards end.

**Use observed compliance to make the decision.** Before launch, record each ward’s latest comparable observer-audit baseline and agree the minimum observation count with infection prevention. A drop of five percentage points or more on any ward in a comparable audit pauses new rewards while the infection prevention lead investigates. The coordinator fixes counting errors, and earned time off is honoured. At the first routine audit at least four weeks after the final reward ends, use the largest ward-level drop from baseline to decide: under five points, keep the kiosk workflow; five to under ten, revise it and recheck; ten or more, drop the pilot workflow and return to the approved prior process. If the sample is too small, hold the decision for the next audit.

**Watch for made-up entries, rushed logging and a post-reward drop.** A rise in completed records can reflect better documentation or easier box-ticking. Review it alongside the observer audit, keep the target tied to shift completion, and give nurses a private route to flag pressure to overreport or problems that interrupt care. Check the ward completion rate again after rewards end to see whether the documentation habit lasted.
