## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Start by making it possible to find or contribute an article without leaving the ticket. Ask six agents across shifts and queues to walk through recent closures and confirm that an hour of paid time off is a reward they’d want. Give all 60 agents 30 minutes of protected practice, and schedule queue coverage for article writing. Put article search first in the resolution form, offer a short draft template, and have the knowledge-base owner review drafts by the next business day. Show agents which problems they reported and what changed before the pilot starts.

### What earns the reward

Tell agents at launch: **“Earn one hour of paid time off by completing your personal ticket goal within two working weeks. A ticket counts when you resolve it with a useful article, an approved new article, or a valid reason why no article applies.”**

Count one completion per resolved ticket ID using the existing resolution record, article link, and resolution note. A useful article must support the actual fix. A new article counts once approved and linked. Valid exceptions include duplicate tickets, administrative closures, and cases where policy bars documenting the resolution. If a ticket is reopened, remove its provisional credit and evaluate it at reclosure. A ticket closed through the old workflow qualifies if its existing records meet the same rule.

Set the usual goal at **five tickets**, adjusted before launch to **one to four** for agents with lower expected volume or queues with fewer opportunities, using recent volume, case mix, and scheduled hours. Agents who miss time resume from their existing count when they return. An article submitted during the earning window can count when approved. The pilot coordinator checks milestone records and hands over the paid-time-off award in person. Administer the existing CSAT bonus separately and cover the leave in the staffing plan.

### What people see

After closing a ticket, the agent sees private confirmation such as **“Ticket counted. 3 of 5 toward your hour off.”** If a draft is awaiting approval or a ticket is reopened, show its status and the reason. Post one team-wide number in the existing support channel each week: the percentage of article-eligible resolutions with a useful article. Managers see the team figure. Individual counts stay with the agent and the coordinator who handles corrections and awards. Exclude pilot data from performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

Give everyone the workflow improvements and private feedback for two weeks. Then randomly assign existing teams to two start dates, two weeks apart, with the same two-week reward window for each team. Compare the teams during the first reward window to estimate what the reward adds to the improved workflow. Leave article search first in the resolution form and keep private ticket feedback after the reward ends.

Use **the share of article-eligible resolved tickets with a useful linked or approved new article** as the decision metric. Audit the reported 12% baseline using the same eligibility and quality rules, and check the metric again four weeks after the last reward window. Use these pilot thresholds for the lift that remains over the verified baseline: **10 percentage points or more**, keep the workflow and feedback; **5 to under 10**, fix the most common remaining barrier and retest; **under 5**, retire the incentive and investigate why agents still struggle to use the knowledge base.

### What could go wrong

- **Agents attach loosely related articles or overuse exceptions.** Before launch, test a duplicate, a reopened ticket, an old-workflow closure, and a privacy exception. Each week the coordinator samples 12 resolutions across linked, exception, and uncounted cases. If **three or more** are misclassified or have an unusable article, the support lead pauses new awards while the knowledge-base owner fixes the rule or workflow. The coordinator corrects the records and tells affected agents privately.
- **Writing slows support or puts the CSAT bonus under pressure.** Keep article drafting out of the live customer response and use the covered writing time. Check team CSAT alongside time to resolution by queue. If either worsens, inspect the affected cases and adjust coverage or the writing workflow. Keep a ticket ineligible for credit if its article would expose customer information.
