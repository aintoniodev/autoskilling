# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Give day- and night-shift nurses paid time to try the kiosk during routine work. Ask where logging interrupts care, whether the kiosk is easy to reach, and what would make it worth using. Before launch, fix the biggest reported barrier. Set the ward automatically, put the hand-hygiene entry screen first, and make saving and undoing an entry quick. Post the fix in the ward huddle with a specific example of what nurses reported and what changed. Perform hand hygiene when indicated and log it at the next safe opportunity. During urgent care or an outage, follow the ward’s existing downtime process and resume with the next eligible moment.

**Recognize verified improvements to the workflow.** At launch, promise a specific thank-you from the director of nursing at a shift huddle within two weeks when a nurse identifies or tests a fix that the ward lead verifies and puts into use. Thank people privately if they prefer. Each nurse can earn recognition through their own contribution, and nurses on every shift get paid time to take part. Kiosk entries, audit scores, and peers’ participation carry no individual rewards. The monthly audits provide an independent check on hand hygiene, while a saved entry confirms documentation.

**Show one ward view.** After each save, the kiosk displays “Entry saved” with an Undo button. In the existing ward huddle, show the ward’s weekly kiosk entry count labeled *self-reported entries*, alongside the latest monthly observer result with its numerator, denominator, and date. Nurses and managers see the same totals. Keep names, patient identifiers, individual rankings, and performance-review use out of the pilot. Absences carry no penalty. The next eligible moment is a fresh start.

**Measure adoption and watch for harm.** Infection Prevention continues its monthly audit and calculates observed compliance as correctly performed hand hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Sample across shifts using comparable observation windows before, during, and after the pilot. In a small subset of those rounds, check whether an observed completed action has one correct kiosk entry by the end of the observation window, using existing kiosk records and no nurse or patient identifiers. Record ambiguous matches as unverified and report the sample size. Before launch, rehearse a double tap, an undo, and a case handled through downtime. A double tap counts once, an undo removes the entry, and downtime is reported separately. Observation can itself change behavior, so interpret small swings cautiously. [WHO observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf), [SHEA/IDSA/APIC practice recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)

**Run it for eight weeks, then end recognition.** Give all wards the fixes and feedback in week 1. Open a two-week recognition window on each ward starting in weeks 3, 5, and 7. Keep the simplified kiosk screen in place after week 8. Compare the wards’ early experience before and after recognition starts, treating the stagger as a practical check with three wards.

**Decide at the next monthly audit.** Use one adoption measure for the decision: the share of sampled, observed completed actions with one verified kiosk entry. Set **80%** as the pilot target before launch. Keep the kiosk workflow if it reaches that target four weeks after recognition ends and observed compliance holds. If coverage falls short, fix the reported barrier and retest once. If coverage still falls short, drop the kiosk logging requirement. If a ward’s observed compliance falls by **5 percentage points** from baseline on a comparable audit, Infection Prevention pauses the pilot on that ward and investigates. A credible report that logging delayed care triggers an immediate pause. The ward lead routes duplicate or incorrect records to the kiosk administrator and brings queues, outages, and privacy concerns to Infection Prevention.
