# One-working-day code reviews

**Fix the review queue first.** Ask five engineers across time zones and experience levels where reviews stall and whether an hour of paid time off is worth earning. Give the team protected time to try the new workflow. In the first two weeks, set up a rotating reviewer and backup for each code area, route ready PRs to the rotation automatically, and put the oldest request first in the existing review queue. Protect a daily review block and cover absences through the backup. Show engineers which blockers they raised and what changed. Start rewards once the routing and coverage work.

**Reward a useful first response.** Each engineer can earn one hour of paid time off for answering two assigned PRs within one working day during their two-week reward window. Set the goal at one for engineers with light rotations, planned leave, or mostly complex reviews. If someone receives no eligible PRs, give them an equal window on their next staffed rotation. A missed case leaves their earned credit intact.

Use this rule in the launch message: “Earn one hour of paid time off by giving your assigned PRs a useful first review within one working day. Your private progress shows your goal and what counted.” Announce the reward up front and have the engineering lead thank each recipient in person, privately.

**Count once per PR.** Use the existing ready-for-review, review-request, and submitted-review events. Start the clock when the PR is ready and review is requested. One working day means eight hours on the published team coverage calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. Reassigning a review carries over its original deadline.

Credit the first reviewer who submits a substantive approval, requests changes with specific feedback, or records a justified hold with a reason and next step. A hold can be the right response when a dependency or safety concern prevents review. A canned acknowledgment earns zero. Follow-up reviews and duplicate PRs earn zero additional credit. A review withdrawn as erroneous loses credit, and the next qualifying response is measured against the original deadline. Log decisions reached in a call or chat on the PR before the deadline so the author has a durable answer. Ordinary late reviews still belong in the team’s service measure.

**Keep feedback private.** After a qualifying review, send the reviewer a private confirmation with the PR, the credit, and their progress toward the hour off. Pin one team view in the existing engineering channel with the weekly share answered within one working day and its denominator. Managers see that same view. Individual progress stays out of performance reviews. The rotation coordinator handles disputes privately and corrects wrong counts.

**Run it for six weeks.** Give both review rotations the routing fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Start the reward for one rotation in weeks 3–4 and the other in weeks 5–6. Compare their week-by-week rates to see whether the reward added anything after the workflow improved. With 25 engineers, treat the comparison as directional. At the end of week 6, pay out earned rewards and end the campaign. Keep automatic routing, the oldest-first queue, protected review time, and backup coverage as the defaults.

Use one decision metric: **the percentage of eligible PRs that received a qualifying first response within one working day**. Include unanswered PRs once their deadline passes. Recalculate the pre-launch baseline from PR history using the same rule, since the current 2.6-day median measures something different. Pool weeks 3–4 after the prizes end. At **90% or above**, keep the workflow as configured. At **75–89%**, adjust routing or coverage and recheck. Below **75%**, retire the incentive design and revisit where review capacity is breaking down.

**Watch for rushed approvals, easy-PR cherry-picking, and holds used to stop the clock.** The coordinator samples five credited PRs each week, including holds and complex changes, for evidence that the response helped the author proceed. If two of five fail, the engineering lead pauses new rewards while the coordinator corrects counts and fixes the rule or routing. Before launch, dry-run a duplicate, a withdrawn review, a reassignment, and a review answered in chat. Budget under an hour a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.
