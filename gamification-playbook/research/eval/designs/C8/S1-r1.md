## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Make the checklist easy to do on paid time.** Before offering a reward, meet with union representatives and technicians from different shifts and routes. Walk through a routine job, an urgent call and a low-signal site. Put the checklist first on the mobile job screen, prefill job details and test offline saving. Give technicians 20 minutes of paid practice and build checklist time into dispatch schedules. At launch, show technicians which problems they raised and what changed. Start the reward once technicians can reliably complete the checklist in the field.

**Offer one reward.** Give each technician one hour of paid personal time if they digitally complete the checklist before work on at least 80% of their eligible jobs during their two-week reward window. Confirm with technicians and union reps that the reward is wanted, and agree with dispatch how people can use it. Name it at launch and have the crew lead confirm it in person. Each technician earns it through their own work, regardless of team results. A technician with two eligible jobs can earn it with two completions. Someone with no eligible jobs gets a two-week window when they return to field work. A missed job leaves the next job available for credit. The safety checklist remains required on every applicable job.

**Count one completion per job.** Use the app’s existing checklist record and the dispatch system’s existing job-start record. Count a completed checklist saved before work starts, including one that records a legitimate safety hold before any work takes place. Use the original saved time for an offline submission if the app already captures it. Agree with technicians on how to verify the order of events using existing records before launch. Test duplicate submissions, reopened jobs, late submissions, cancellations and the existing fallback. A duplicate or reopen earns no extra credit. A cancelled job with no field assessment is out of scope. The approved fallback stays available and documented. A verified app failure removes that job from the technician’s reward calculation, while the job stays in the digital adoption measure.

**Show people their own progress.** After each save, show the technician whether the job counted or is awaiting sync. Privately show their credited jobs, eligible jobs and progress toward the paid hour. Put one weekly team completion rate in the existing crew huddle or channel. Managers see team totals only. Individual results stay out of performance reviews. Name the pilot coordinator as the contact for disputed counts, and have them resolve discrepancies against the existing job records before rewards are finalized.

**Run a staggered pilot and measure what sticks.** Roll out the fixes and feedback to everyone for two weeks and record a fresh baseline alongside today’s approximate 35%. Then give the first group of crews its two-week reward window, followed by the second group. Compare the crews during the first window to estimate what the reward adds beyond the workflow fixes. Keep the checklist first on the job screen after rewards end. Four weeks after the final window, use the share of applicable jobs with a valid pre-work digital checklist to decide what to do. Keep the rollout at **75% or above**. At **50–74%**, fix the remaining barriers and remeasure. Below **50%**, retire the incentive and revisit the workflow with technicians and union reps.

**Watch for rushed checks and hidden hazards.** Each week, the safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected job records across crews for checklist quality, including holds when available. One confirmed critical omission or recorded start despite an active hold pauses reward decisions while the safety lead investigates and the coordinator corrects affected counts. Review the rule with union reps before launch. Give a valid hold the same credit as a cleared check, and keep dispatch from treating a hold as a missed productivity target.
