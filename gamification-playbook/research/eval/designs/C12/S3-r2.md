## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward rule.** Each ward earns one hospital-funded meal, budgeted at €15 per nurse and available across all shifts, if it meets its hand-hygiene target in either of its two monthly audits during the reward period. The target is five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain at least 95%. A missed first month leaves the second month open. Everyone on the ward shares the reward, including staff who were on leave.

### Make the kiosk easy to use

Before launch, give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts paid time to try the kiosk and identify where it adds steps or interrupts care. Fix the barriers they raise, check kiosk placement with Infection Prevention, and post what changed. Give all three wards the same fixes, training and feedback before any reward begins. Make the kiosk the default logging screen, with the ward prefilled, a quick confirmation after saving, and an undo option. Use the existing fallback during downtime or urgent care. Patient care takes priority over logging.

### Count what observers verify

Use the existing monthly audits. The ward score is **compliant observed opportunities divided by all eligible observed opportunities** under the hospital’s audit protocol. Count each observed opportunity once, including observed misses in the denominator. An observed compliant act counts even if the nurse used the fallback or made no kiosk entry. A duplicate, an undone entry, or an entry without an observed act has no effect on the score. Exclude only opportunities the audit protocol classifies as ineligible. Agree on these cases with observers before launch. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

Each nurse gets immediate confirmation that their entry was saved or undone. The ward’s existing huddle board shows its latest audit percentage and sample size, its target, the two qualifying months, and the meal on offer. Managers see ward aggregates. Keep kiosk entries separate from audit records and performance reviews. Use team goals because the audits provide ward-level evidence.

### Stagger the rewards and check what lasts

After the fixes, collect one baseline audit on all three wards. Randomly assign wards to start rewards in months 2, 3 and 4. Each gets two monthly audits with rewards active and a follow-up audit about four weeks after its reward window closes. Keep the kiosk defaults and ward feedback in place throughout.

Use **audited compliance** to decide whether to repeat the reward. Compare each ward’s change from baseline with the change on wards still waiting in the same month. Repeat only if the early wards improve more than the waiting wards and that improvement is still visible at follow-up. Revise the pilot if samples are thin, findings are mixed, or nurses report workflow friction. End the reward if comparable audits show no added or lasting lift. With three wards, treat the result as directional.

### Watch for distorted results and added work

Use the same observation protocol and a comparable mix of shifts and opportunities each month. Set the minimum usable sample with Infection Prevention before launch and keep exact observation times off the reward board. Staff may behave differently when watched, and a change in which shifts are sampled can move the score. [SHEA/IDSA/APIC hand-hygiene practice recommendation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)

Each month, the Infection Prevention lead spot-checks 10 de-identified audit records per ward, including exclusions where available. If two or more have a scoring or eligibility error, the lead pauses the award and the audit owner corrects and rechecks the tally before release. The ward champion routes kiosk problems to IT and escalates any interference with care immediately. Use the existing audit report and huddles to keep administration under an hour a week.
