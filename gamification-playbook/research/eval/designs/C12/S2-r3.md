# One-working-day code reviews

Start by making reviews easier to answer. In week 1, ask a few engineers across the 25-person team which reviews sit longest and why. Give reviewers a protected 20-minute block each working day and put the oldest ready PRs first in the review queue they already use. Assign a primary reviewer and a backup when a PR becomes ready. Fix the biggest routing or workload problem engineers identify and tell the team what changed before rewards begin.

## What earns the reward

**One ready PR earns one credit when another engineer submits a substantive review within one working day and the PR record confirms it.** An approval after required checks pass, a specific request for changes, or a concrete blocking question can qualify. For a change request or blocking question, an author reply that addresses it or a linked revision confirms the review. The timestamp is the reviewer’s submission time, so a slow author reply cannot make an on-time review late. A greeting, emoji, or “looking soon” earns no credit.

Start the clock when a human-authored PR is marked ready and enters the review queue. One working day means eight hours on the team’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Reassign to the backup when the primary is away and retain the original deadline. Drafts and duplicates are ineligible. A genuine withdrawal before the deadline can be excluded and sampled. Reopening a PR or moving it back to draft does not reset its deadline. A review dismissed as invalid loses its credit. Once an eligible PR reaches its deadline, it counts in that week’s denominator even if it is still unanswered.

## What people see

Use the existing review notification to give the reviewer prompt, private feedback, such as “Review submitted in 5 working hours. Credit pending author follow-up.” Each reviewer sees their own share of eligible assignments answered on time and a personal goal of 15 percentage points above their prior four-week baseline, capped at 100%. For engineers with fewer than five baseline assignments, show the count and timing until there is enough history to set a goal.

Pin squad progress in the existing engineering channel, with the eligible PR count, the verified on-time share, and the reward. Each squad earns a company-paid team lunch if it improves its verified on-time share by at least 15 percentage points over its baseline during its four reward weeks, capped at 100%, and passes the quality check. Everyone in the squad is included. Managers get aggregate numbers only. Individual figures stay private and out of performance reviews. Every eligible PR is a fresh opportunity after a missed week.

## Timing and decision

Use two existing review groups and randomly choose which starts first. Give both the routing fixes, protected time, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Run rewards for the early group in weeks 3–6 and the later group in weeks 5–8. Announce both windows up front. Keep the queue order and backup assignment as defaults after week 8.

Use one decision metric: **the share of eligible PRs that get a substantive review within one working day**, checked against a sample of actual PRs. Compare the groups’ change from weeks 1–2 during weeks 3–4, then measure both groups again in weeks 11–12. Set an initial target of 80%. If the reward adds at least 10 percentage points in the overlap and the team holds 80% after rewards end, keep the workflow and retire the lunch. If the team holds 80% with little apparent reward effect, also retire the lunch. If the team falls short, use the review sample to identify what to change before repeating the experiment. Treat the comparison as directional if there are few PRs or reviewers frequently work across both groups.

## What could go wrong

Rushed approvals, token comments, and questionable exclusions can make the numbers look better while reviews get worse. Each week, a rotating engineer checks up to 10 PRs across credited, late, and excluded cases, using the existing PR history. Pause reward credit if two of the sampled records are wrongly credited or excluded, or one sampled approval misses a material issue. The engineering lead decides when to resume, and the programme owner corrects counts and handles private disputes. Automate the weekly tally from existing PR events and cap administration, including sampling, at one hour a week.
