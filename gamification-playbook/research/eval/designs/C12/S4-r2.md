## Six-week knowledge-base pilot

**Reward a resolved ticket backed by a useful article.** Before launch, spend 30 minutes of paid time with agents from different queues. Find out where search, linking, or publishing slows them down or puts their CSAT bonus at risk. Give everyone paid practice time, fix the biggest blockers, and show agents what changed. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility rules.

**Counting rule.** A ticket earns one credit when it reaches verified closure and an approved article that addresses the issue is recorded in the customer reply or in a resolution record already reviewed through QA. A new article earns credit through the same rule once it is approved and attached. Use the existing ticket and knowledge-base records. If neither existing workflow can verify that an internal link was used, start with customer replies that contain an approved article.

Multiple links, repeat sends, and duplicate tickets earn one credit per underlying case. A same-issue reopen within seven days cancels the credit. If the case is resolved again, it can earn one credit after a fresh seven-day window. An eligible ticket solved without an article stays in the denominator. Cases such as sensitive requests that cannot be documented or shared are excluded under a short, agreed list of reasons. A missing article on an otherwise eligible case is a gap to fix and remains in the denominator. Test these cases with agents before launch.

**Feedback and reward.** At closure, show agents a private confirmation such as “Password reset article linked. Credit pending for seven days.” Show each agent their own confirmed share of eligible tickets. Use their own two-week baseline to set a goal of closing one fifth of the gap to 100%, rounded to the nearest percentage point. An agent at 12% would aim for 30%. A missed week carries no penalty or reset.

Put aggregate progress in the team’s existing dashboard, alongside the reward and deadline. If the team closes one fifth of its own baseline gap over its four reward weeks, every team member gets one paid hour for learning or stretch work, scheduled with queue coverage. Managers see team totals only. Individual figures stay out of leaderboards, manager reports, and performance reviews.

**Timing and decision.** Give all 60 agents the fixes and private feedback for two baseline weeks. Pair teams with similar queues and ticket volumes, then randomly assign one from each pair to start rewards in pilot week 1 and the other in week 3. Each team gets four reward weeks. Before its reward ends, make article suggestions appear by default in the resolution screen, with an easy skip for exceptions. Confirm the reward after the last seven-day window closes.

The decision metric is the **percentage of resolved tickets that stay resolved for seven days**, using existing same-issue reopen records. Include tickets with exclusions so reclassification cannot improve the result. Compare the early and late teams’ changes from baseline during weeks 1–2. Check again four weeks after the final reward ends. Keep the workflow if the post-reward rate is at least 2 percentage points above baseline and the quality and CSAT checks pass. Change it if the gain is smaller or uneven by queue. Drop the incentive if resolution worsens or the early comparison shows no gain from rewards. A gain that persists after rewards end supports keeping the workflow default.

**What could go wrong.** Agents may attach generic articles, close cases early, or overuse exclusions. Each week, the support QA lead samples 12 tickets across credited cases, exclusions, and reopens. If two or more fail the existing resolution-quality rubric or have an invalid article or exclusion, the support operations lead pauses rewards and owns the fix. Agents can flag a wrong count privately to that lead. Review existing CSAT by queue alongside the sample. If it falls 3 percentage points below baseline for two consecutive weeks, pause rewards and check response volume, case mix, and whether article use is hurting replies. Budget 45–60 minutes a week for the sample, corrections, and dashboard check.
