## One working day for a code review

Run a six-week pilot for the 25 engineers. Give reviewers protected time, make ownership clear, and offer each review rotation a team lunch if it meets its milestone. Use the existing pull request queue and team channel.

### What earns credit

A pull request earns one credit when an assigned human reviewer submits a substantive review within one working day of the request. An approval must record what the reviewer checked. A request for changes must identify a specific issue the author can act on. The submitted review and the author’s existing merge or revision workflow provide the evidence for a small quality audit. Acknowledgments, bot comments, and bare approvals earn zero.

Use the team’s published working hours and holiday calendar. A request made outside those hours starts at the next opening. Count each ready PR once, including PRs that receive no answer. Reassignments, repeat requests, and new commits leave the original clock running. Exclude drafts, bot-only maintenance PRs, and PRs the author genuinely withdraws before the deadline. Audit withdrawals and exclusions. An approval withdrawn because the review was incomplete loses its credit.

### What engineers see

After a qualifying review, the reviewer gets a private confirmation in the existing PR workflow. Their private weekly view shows the share of eligible requests they answered on time and a reachable goal based on their own baseline. For example, someone starting at 50% might aim for 65%. People with too few cases for a reliable baseline see their counts and can set a goal once they have enough cases. A missed week has no carryover penalty.

The existing team channel shows the team-wide rate and pins the reward rule. Each rotation earns a company-paid team lunch if it answers at least 80% of eligible PRs on time across its two reward weeks and clears the quality check. Publish both rotations’ earning dates up front. Managers see only the combined team rate and earned milestones. Individual data stays private and is excluded from performance reviews.

### How it runs and ends

- **Weeks 1–2.** Ask a few frequent authors and reviewers where reviews stall. Use their answers to assign a primary and backup for each queue, protect two short review windows per day, and fix routing or oversized handoffs where possible. Show the team what changed. Give everyone feedback from week 1, and use recent PR history to establish baselines. Before rewards start, test the counting rule on a duplicate request, a withdrawn approval, and a review completed through the existing workflow.
- **Weeks 3–4.** Start the reward for one existing rotation, chosen by lot. The other has the same workflow and feedback. Compare the change from baseline in the two rotations’ on-time rates over these same weeks.
- **Weeks 5–6.** Give the second rotation the same two-week reward window. Before week 6 ends, make primary and backup routing automatic and put the oldest unanswered requests first in the queue. Award any earned lunches and end the incentive.
- **Weeks 9–10.** Check the same decision metric, the percentage of eligible PRs with a substantive answer within one working day. Keep the workflow if it sustains 80% or more. Below 80%, change the workflow if it still beats the pre-pilot baseline. If it returns to baseline or worse, drop the pilot setup and revisit the bottleneck. Use the staggered comparison to judge whether the lunch added enough to repeat in a future rollout. Treat the comparison as directional given the two small rotations.

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber stamps and convenient exclusions.** The rotation lead samples five cases weekly, including credited reviews and exclusions. If two or more are wrong, the engineering manager pauses rewards for the affected rotation until the lead corrects the count and checks a fresh sample. Engineers can flag disputed counts privately to the lead.
- **Easy PRs jump the queue.** Sort by deadline and keep ownership with the assigned rotation. The backup covers absences and busy days. Complex PRs can earn credit through concrete review findings while staying open for further review.
- **Review duty crowds out delivery.** Protect review time in sprint planning and rotate the duty. If reviews regularly overflow those windows, fix capacity or PR size before raising targets.

The rotation lead should be able to run the weekly sample, resolve routine corrections, and post the aggregate in under an hour.
