## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Reward teams for using an approved knowledge-base article to resolve a real customer issue. The reported link-or-write rate is 12%. Recalculate the baseline using the pilot’s eligibility and counting rules before setting goals. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place.

### What earns credit

**One credit per eligible resolved ticket when an approved article helps resolve the issue and its use is verified by a delivered customer reply or existing closure QA.** If an agent writes an article, it earns credit only after normal approval and verified use on the ticket. A draft, a pasted link, or a self-entered checkbox earns no credit on its own.

An eligible ticket is one where a reusable article could help resolve the issue. Exclude spam, duplicate tickets, and account-specific cases where an article would add no value. A ticket resolved without an article still goes in the denominator if an article would have helped. Merge duplicates into one case. If a ticket reopens for the same issue within seven days, reverse the credit and reassess after the next resolution. Before launch, have agents and QA walk through those cases and one legitimate exclusion.

### What agents see and earn

Spend two weeks asking agents across shifts what makes articles hard to find, use, or write. Give them 30 minutes of protected learning time. Fix the biggest blockers, such as poor search results or a slow approval queue, and post what changed in the support channel. Give every team the fixes and feedback before rewards start.

Show agents a private confirmation in the ticket tool when a resolution qualifies, with credit marked provisional until the seven-day window closes. Give each agent a private goal of 10 percentage points above their own four-week eligible-ticket baseline, capped at 100%. Use a rolling four-week view so a missed week carries no penalty. For agents with fewer than 10 eligible tickets in the baseline, start with case-level feedback and set a goal once they have enough cases.

Pin one team view in the existing support channel. Show the verified share, eligible ticket count, milestone, deadline, and reward. Set each team’s milestone at 10 percentage points above its own baseline, capped at 100%, across its four reward weeks. If a team reaches the milestone and passes the quality check, everyone on it earns one hour of paid personal development time, scheduled with queue coverage. Managers see team totals only. Individual data stays out of performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

Randomly assign existing teams to an early or late start. Run four reward weeks per team, with the late teams starting two weeks after the early teams. During the first two weeks, compare early teams with late teams that already have the same fixes and feedback. Before the first reward period ends, put article suggestions in the normal resolution screen for everyone, with an easy way to skip irrelevant suggestions. Confirm milestone results after the final tickets have had seven days to mature, then retire the reward and keep the workflow improvements.

Use **the share of resolved cases with no same-issue reopen or repeat contact within seven days** as the outcome that decides the programme’s future. Measure it across resolved cases, including those excluded from reward credit. Compare each cohort’s change from baseline during the early-versus-late window, then check again four weeks after the last reward period. Set a practical threshold before launch. Use two percentage points as the pilot threshold. Keep the improved workflow if the gain persists, revise it if the gain fades, and drop the incentive if verified article use rises without a corresponding improvement in durable resolution. Treat a mixed or noisy result as inconclusive.

### What could go wrong

Agents could attach irrelevant articles, mark difficult tickets ineligible, or rush closure under pressure from overlapping incentives. Each week, the QA lead samples five credited cases and five exclusions across teams through the existing QA process. If more than one of the ten has an incorrect credit or exclusion, the support ops lead pauses reward confirmation, fixes the counting rule or workflow, and resumes after a passing sample. Agents can flag a wrong count privately in the existing QA channel. Support ops owns corrections before the payout.

Review team CSAT alongside survey response volume during the pilot. If CSAT drops below its usual range, inspect the affected cases for rushed closures or article links that displaced a direct answer. Use the existing ticket report and QA queue so one owner can run the weekly checks in under an hour.
