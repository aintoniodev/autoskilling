## One working day for a useful first review

Give authors a substantive first review within one working day. Run this as a review-service trial with a modest team reward. Before launch, give engineers from both review rotations 30 minutes of protected time to identify what slows reviews down and choose a reward they would actually want. Start with a shared ready-for-review queue, a named backup for each rotation, and protected review time. Tell the team which problems they raised and what changed.

### What earns credit

A pull request earns credit once when an eligible human reviewer submits a substantive review within eight working hours of the PR becoming ready. An approval identifies what the reviewer checked. A request for changes identifies a concrete issue the author can act on. Use the existing repository review record and reviewer permissions. Acknowledgments, bot comments, and self-reviews earn no credit.

Start the clock when the PR is marked ready, even if nobody has picked it up. Use the owning rotation’s published work hours and holidays. The backup covers individual absences. Exclude drafts and PRs that genuinely require no human review under existing policy. A duplicate review earns nothing extra, reopening or redrafting the same PR does not reset the clock, and a still-eligible PR answered through chat needs a qualifying review in the repository to count. If a review is dismissed as invalid, remove its credit and use the timestamp of the next qualifying review.

### What people see and earn

Show each engineer a private confirmation when their review counts, plus a personal target based on their own baseline share of assigned, eligible PRs answered on time. Start at one additional timely review per ten eligible assignments for engineers below 90%, and use maintenance goals for engineers already at or above 90%. Show the denominator and wait for ten assignments before displaying a rate. A missed week carries no penalty.

In the existing team channel, post each rotation’s aggregate result, its milestone, and the reward date. Each rotation earns a team lunch during paid work time if, over its two reward weeks, it gets **10 percentage points more eligible PRs reviewed on time than during its baseline** and clears the quality check. Everyone in the rotation shares the reward, including people who were on leave. Managers receive aggregates only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

- **Weeks 1–2.** Give both rotations the workflow fixes and feedback. Measure their baselines using the same rules as the trial.
- **Weeks 3–4.** Randomly select one rotation to start earning the reward. Compare its change from baseline with the other rotation’s change over the same weeks.
- **Weeks 5–6.** Give the second rotation its reward window. By the end of week 6, make ready PRs ordered by deadline the default queue view for both rotations.
- **Weeks 7–10.** Keep the queue, backup coverage, and protected review time. End prizes and check the result again in weeks 9–10.

Decide using one number, **the quality-checked share of eligible PRs that receive a substantive first review within one working day**. Before launch, commit to keeping the workflow if that share stays at least 10 percentage points above baseline at follow-up. If the gain is smaller, revise the workflow around the delays found in the audit. If the gain disappears, drop the incentive design. Use the staggered comparison to judge whether the reward added anything to the workflow fixes. With two rotations, treat that comparison as directional.

### What could go wrong

Engineers may rubber-stamp reviews, pick easy PRs, or mark difficult PRs ineligible. Keep unassigned and late PRs in the denominator, route by ownership, and put the oldest eligible work first. Each week, the review lead samples five credited reviews and up to five exclusions across both rotations, checking the review against the diff and the exclusions against existing policy. Pause rewards if two of the last ten audited records are wrongly credited or excluded. The engineering lead decides when to resume, and the review lead corrects the counts and reruns the milestone calculation. Use existing PR records and cap routine administration at 45 minutes a week.
