## Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets

Run an 11-week pilot. Each team can earn a team lunch or equivalent meal credit during its four-week reward window by raising its share of eligible resolutions with a verified article by **15 percentage points above its own baseline**, capped at 100%. Everyone on a qualifying team gets the reward, including agents who were on leave. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules in place. A missed week has no penalty.

### What counts

**A resolved ticket earns one credit when normal closure QA accepts a relevant article link or the knowledge-base editor approves and publishes an article written from that ticket.** An existing article must support the actual resolution. A new article must document a reusable fix and pass the existing editorial check. Give equal credit for either route. Use the ticket ID to deduplicate and reverse credit if the ticket reopens for the same issue within seven days. Agents can close tickets on the normal schedule while article approval is pending.

The denominator is all resolved tickets where an article could safely help with the issue. Document exclusions such as account-specific actions with no reusable guidance or sensitive cases where even a sanitized article would be inappropriate. Eligible tickets handled the old way stay in the denominator. Use existing ticket and knowledge-base records. Confirm before launch that existing closure QA can verify links. If it cannot, count only editor-approved articles tied to resolved tickets and reset the baseline and target for that measure before advertising the reward.

Have the QA lead and agents score a duplicate, a reopened ticket, an eligible ticket closed without an article, and a sensitive exception before launch. Each week, use eight cases from the existing QA sample across the pilot, including credited tickets and exclusions. **Pause rewards if two or more of the eight are wrongly counted or excluded.** The QA lead decides when the rule is working again. The support operations owner corrects affected counts before payout. Agents can flag a wrong count in the existing QA channel.

### What agents and managers see

Give agents a private confirmation in the ticket tool when credit is verified, plus their eligible-case share and personal target. Set the target at 15 percentage points above their own baseline, capped at 100%. Post team totals, the milestone, the lunch or meal credit, and the earning deadline in the existing support dashboard. Managers see team totals only. Keep individual figures out of performance reviews.

### Timeline and decision

- **Weeks 1–2.** Ask agents across shifts which tickets are hard to document and where the tool adds time. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice. Fix the biggest workflow blockers, post what changed, and give all teams private feedback. Measure each team’s baseline using the pilot’s eligibility and verification rules. The current 12% is the starting reference.
- **Weeks 3–8.** Where possible, pair existing teams on baseline coverage and ticket mix, then randomly assign one in each pair to start in week 3 and the other in week 5. Both get four reward weeks. In weeks 3–4, compare the change from baseline in the early and later teams while both have the same fixes and feedback.
- **Before each team’s reward ends.** Make article suggestions the default in the resolution panel, with an easy skip for exceptions. Award the meal after the last tickets’ seven-day review period. In week 11, check both teams again with rewards off.

Use one decision metric, **the share of all resolved tickets with no same-issue reopen or recorded repeat contact within seven days**. Set a two-percentage-point improvement as the pilot threshold. Repeat the reward only if the early teams improve by at least two points more than the later teams during the comparison and the gain remains at least two points above baseline in week 11. If the gain appears only while rewards run, revise the workflow and retest before offering another reward. If the early comparison misses the threshold, drop the reward and keep workflow fixes that helped agents.

### What could go wrong

Irrelevant links, duplicate articles, and inflated exclusions can make coverage look better while support gets worse. Equal credit for reuse and writing reduces pressure to publish, and the QA pause catches bad counts. Agents could also delay closures or spend too long writing to earn credit. Keep closure independent of publication, give agents paid time for article work, and review existing team-level CSAT and resolution time weekly. If either deteriorates alongside the pilot, check sampled tickets and pause the affected team’s rewards when the added work is the cause. Use the existing dashboard and QA review to keep weekly administration under an hour.
