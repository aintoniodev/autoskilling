## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward observed hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn a team meal, with equivalent options for night and weekend staff, after its four-week reward period. The rule staff see is “Your ward earns the meal if its routine audit shows five percentage points more hand-hygiene compliance than its baseline, or at least 95% if it started at 90% or higher.” Every ward can earn it independently. Kiosk entries carry no points and do not affect the audit score.

**Make the kiosk fit the shift.** Before launch, ask nurses from day and night shifts on each ward to walk through when they can safely log a moment and what gets in the way. Give them paid practice time. Fix access, placement or slow entry first and post what changed. Make the hand-hygiene form the first screen on the ward kiosk, with the ward prefilled and the actual moment selected by the nurse. During urgent care or an outage, staff follow normal hand-hygiene practice. They can resume logging at the next eligible moment without catching up or losing progress.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** Compliance is the share of eligible opportunities in which observers record that hand hygiene was performed. Use the hospital’s existing eligibility rules and audit process across all three wards. Before launch, infection prevention sets a minimum usable sample and checks that planned observations cover a comparable mix of shifts and opportunities. An insufficient sample defers the reward decision to the next usable audit. Duplicate kiosk entries, corrected entries and hygiene performed without a kiosk entry leave the audit score unaffected. Check those cases before launch, along with an ineligible opportunity. An opportunity the observer could not assess stays out of both numerator and denominator.

**Show staff what they need.** After each submission, the kiosk confirms the moment recorded and offers a correction. Nurses may set a private logging goal based on their own starting routine, such as logging the next applicable moment after a round. The ward huddle board shows the ward’s baseline, latest usable audit result, reward target and decision date. Managers see ward totals only. Individual kiosk activity stays out of rankings and performance reviews. The pilot lead checks aggregate kiosk entries and downtime to spot workflow problems. Entry volume measures use of the kiosk and cannot establish compliance.

**Run one baseline month, three staggered reward months and two follow-up months.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback in the baseline month. Draw the ward order, then activate the reward for one ward per monthly audit cycle. Compare the change in audited compliance on the active ward with the change on wards still waiting during the same cycle. Keep the simpler kiosk workflow and feedback in place after the meals end. Use audited compliance to decide the outcome. Keep the workflow and feedback if wards that reached their target still meet it at the second monthly follow-up. Revise and retest if gains fade or results vary by ward. End the incentive if there is no credible improvement during the reward period. Treat the three-ward comparison as directional because audit samples and patient mix can vary.

**Pause if care or measurement suffers.** A usable audit result five percentage points below the ward’s baseline pauses reward decisions. The infection-prevention lead checks the audit sample, corrects verified counting errors and decides when to resume. The kiosk owner fixes entry and access problems. The pilot lead spends up to 45 minutes a week reviewing existing totals and issues raised in huddles. Watch for retrospective bulk logging, staff feeling watched, and the kiosk interrupting care. If entries rise while observed compliance stays flat or falls, investigate the workflow and keep the reward tied to observed care.
