## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward one verified digital safety check per eligible job.** A check earns credit when the technician completes it in the app before work starts and the existing dispatch or safety record independently confirms the job start or safety hold. A correctly documented safety hold earns the same credit as a job that proceeds. Credit follows the job ID, so duplicates and resubmissions earn once. A voided check loses credit. An approved paper fallback satisfies the existing safety procedure and counts toward the audited safety result.

**Set up the pilot with technicians and union representatives.** Include different shifts, job types, and low-signal routes. Walk through real jobs together to find where the app adds time or gets in the way. Fix the main blockers, show crews what changed, and give every technician 20 minutes of protected paid practice. Before launch, confirm that the existing records can independently verify the sequence. If they cannot, leave rewards off until an existing safety sign-off can verify a suitable outcome. Test the counting rule on a duplicate, a void, a paper fallback, and a safety hold.

**Measure each technician against their own baseline.** Use four weeks of eligible jobs after the fixes and feedback are in place. The company’s current 35% is a starting reference. Set each private goal at 15 percentage points above their own verified digital completion rate, capped at 95% for anyone below it. Technicians already at or above 95% aim to maintain their rate. Use the same eligibility rules throughout. Jobs cancelled before the safety check is due and jobs outside checklist policy are excluded. Eligible jobs handled on paper stay in the denominator. A missed shift or week carries no penalty.

**Show progress where crews already look.** The app confirms each verified check privately and shows the technician their own eligible jobs, credited checks, and goal. Managers and crews see a team total in the existing shift briefing or dispatch board. The board shows the team’s baseline, its four-week target of a 10-percentage-point lift in verified digital completion (capped at 95%, with teams already there aiming to maintain), and the reward. If the team reaches its target and clears the quality gate, everyone on the crew gets a 30-minute paid break scheduled across shifts the following week. Individual data stays out of manager views and performance reviews. Technicians can flag a wrong count privately to the pilot coordinator, who checks the existing record and corrects it before rewards are issued.

**Run two four-week reward waves, starting three weeks apart.** Give everyone the fixes, practice, and private feedback first. Assign comparable crews to early and late starts by lottery, balancing for shift and job mix. During the three-week overlap, compare the groups’ change from baseline on one primary measure: the share of audited eligible jobs with a complete, valid safety checklist in place before work started, or a correctly documented safety hold. Count approved paper and digital checks in this measure. Before rewards end, put the checklist first when an eligible job opens in the app and prefill job details. Leave safety answers for the technician to complete.

**Set the quality and ending rules before launch.** Use the existing QA review to sample jobs across both waves each week, including exclusions, holds, paper fallback, and digital completions. Pause rewards for an affected crew if QA finds any job that started without a valid check, or if more than 10% of sampled reward credits or exclusions are wrong. The safety lead decides when to resume. The coordinator fixes counts, and the dispatch or app owner fixes the underlying issue. Use the existing QA sample and dispatch report so one coordinator can run the programme in under an hour a week.

Four weeks after each wave’s prizes end, check the same audited safety measure again. Set a five-percentage-point improvement over baseline as the threshold for keeping the app default and routine feedback. If the gain is smaller or the audit sample is too thin to judge, adjust the workflow and recheck. If audited compliance falls, drop the incentive design and review the workflow with technicians and the union. End prizes on schedule in all cases.

Watch for rushed answers, paper checks being backfilled into the app, difficult jobs being marked ineligible, and pressure to proceed on a safety hold. Sampling and independent timestamps address the first three. Equal credit for a verified hold, a private correction route, and paid recovery time after an outage help keep the reward from working against the safety procedure.
