## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Give each crew four weeks to earn **30 minutes of paid time per technician** by raising its share of verified pre-job digital checklists by **15 percentage points** over its own baseline, capped at 100%. Offer the time across shifts, with scheduling agreed with union reps. A technician who misses a week can pick up on the next job.

### Before launch

Spend two weeks with union reps and technicians from different shifts and routes. Give technicians paid practice time. Test the app on actual jobs, including low-signal sites and shared devices. Fix and publicize the barriers they identify. Use the same two weeks to establish crew and individual baselines from eligible jobs. Treat the company’s estimated 35% as a starting point and verify it against job records.

### What earns credit

**One credit per eligible job when the digital checklist was completed before work started and the existing job-release or safety sign-off confirms the required checks were performed.** Verify during setup that the sign-off actually checks the work. If the company has no existing independent check that does this, hold the reward until a verifiable outcome can be used.

Use the dispatch job ID to count each job once. Before launch, test the rule on a duplicate, a reopened job, a late submission, an approved paper fallback, and an offline checklist that syncs after the job starts. Credit the offline checklist if its completion time and independent sign-off establish that it was done before work started. Exclude canceled jobs, jobs outside the checklist requirement, and independently confirmed outages that require the approved fallback from the reward calculation. Audit a sample of those exclusions. Jobs done on paper when the app was available remain in the reward denominator.

### What people see

- **Technicians** get a private confirmation after each checklist showing whether credit is pending or confirmed. They see their own eligible-job count, verified completion rate, and a private goal of 15 points above their baseline, capped at 100%.
- **Crews and managers** see the crew rate, target, reward, and deadline on the existing dispatch board. Pool small crews for reporting where totals would identify individuals. Agree with union reps that managers get team totals only, with no individual drill-down or use of pilot data in performance reviews. Technicians can raise a disputed count privately with the pilot coordinator, who corrects it against the job record.

### Timing and decision

Give every crew the fixes, paid practice, and feedback first. Pair crews by job mix and baseline, then randomly assign one crew in each pair to start rewards in week 1 and the other in week 4. Each gets four reward weeks. Before its reward ends, make the checklist the first step when a technician opens an eligible job. Keep the approved fallback available.

Use **the share of audited jobs where all required pre-job safety controls were actually performed before work started** as the keep, change, or drop measure. Include approved fallback jobs in this audit. Compare early and late crews during weeks 1–3, then check each crew again four weeks after its reward ends. Keep the reward for future rollouts if the early crews improve by at least five percentage points more than the late crews and at least five points of improvement over baseline remain after the reward ends. If the gain fades or the sample is too small to judge, revise or extend the evaluation. If the audit shows no gain or worse safety execution, drop the reward and retain the app improvements.

### What could go wrong

Rushing, backdating, and reclassifying difficult jobs can all inflate the rate. Use the existing QA sample to check timestamps, evidence of completed controls, and exclusions. The safety lead pauses reward accrual if fewer than 90% of the last 20 sampled credited jobs pass verification, or immediately if a credited job missed a critical control. The pilot coordinator corrects the counts and the safety lead clears the restart. Make clear at launch that stopping unsafe work and reporting hazards never cost anyone earned recognition. Automate the tally and use the existing QA review so one coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
