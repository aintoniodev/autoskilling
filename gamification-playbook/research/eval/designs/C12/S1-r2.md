## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Give technicians and union stewards a say before launch.** Ask a small group across shifts and service areas to walk through real jobs with the app, including jobs with weak signal. Give them paid practice time. Fix slow sign-in, missing job details and any offline problems before starting the reward clock. Tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Agree with union representatives on eligibility, paid time, data access and how technicians can challenge a count.

**Reward one verified digital checklist per job.** The rule technicians see is “Your team gets credit once when you complete the digital safety checklist before work starts and it passes the existing pre-job verification.” Use the dispatch job ID, the checklist submission time and the existing job-start and safety verification records. A technician’s submission alone earns no credit. If the current records cannot independently verify completion and timing, choose a safety outcome the existing process can verify before offering a reward.

| Case | How it counts |
|---|---|
| Duplicate submission or reopened job | One credit per job ID. A reopened job earns no second credit. |
| Checklist withdrawn, invalidated or submitted after work began | Zero credit. Reverse any provisional credit and explain the correction privately. |
| Approved paper fallback during a verified outage | Exclude from the digital goal. Include in the safety audit and give full safety credit if it meets the same standard. |
| Paper used when the app was available | Include in the digital goal denominator, with zero digital credit. Audit it under the same safety standard. |
| Job cancelled before work or genuinely exempt under the existing safety policy | Exclude from the applicable denominator. Sample exemptions to catch misclassification. |

**Show progress where crews already look.** Confirm each verified job privately in the app. Show technicians their own share of eligible jobs and a personal goal of 10 percentage points above their two-week baseline, capped at 100%. Managers see team totals on the existing crew dashboard, with small groups pooled to protect privacy. Agree with the union that pilot data stays out of performance reviews. A missed week has no penalty. The next eligible job counts normally.

**Offer one reachable team reward.** Each team earns 30 minutes of paid release time per technician if its verified digital share rises by 10 percentage points over its own baseline by the end of its four-week reward window, with the target capped at 100% and payout subject to the quality check. At a 35% baseline, the target is 45%. Post the target, current share, reward and deadline on the crew dashboard. Schedule release time across shifts so everyone on the team can take it.

**Run two staggered four-week reward windows.** After the fixes, give all teams two weeks of baseline measurement and private feedback. Assign comparable teams by lottery to start rewards in week 3 or week 5. Compare them in weeks 3–4, when all teams have the same fixes and feedback and only the early teams have rewards. Before each team’s reward ends, make the checklist the first action when a technician opens an eligible job and prefill known job details. Keep that default and the private confirmation after prizes end. Check again four weeks after each team finishes.

**Use one result to decide whether to keep the incentive.** Measure the share of audited jobs that required a checklist and had a satisfactory one completed before work began, including approved fallback. Use the existing safety audit and sample both teams in each wave, including jobs with missing submissions. Agree up front to keep the incentive for a future rollout only if the early teams lead by at least 5 percentage points during the overlap and both waves remain at least 5 points above their own baseline at follow-up. If the sample is too small to judge that difference or the gain fades, keep the workflow fixes and revise the pilot before offering another reward. If digital completion rises while audited safety is flat or worse, drop the incentive.

**Put the safety lead in charge of pauses and corrections.** Each week, review a small sample of credited jobs, fallbacks and exclusions through existing QA. Pause the affected team’s payout if more than 10% of sampled credited checklists fail substantive QA, or immediately if QA confirms work began with a critical hazard unresolved. Review with a union steward before restarting. The safety lead decides the pause, and the dispatch administrator corrects disputed counts and tells the technician privately. Keep stop-work and hazard reporting outside the reward calculation so crews have no incentive to conceal a problem or rush a job. Use an automated weekly tally and existing QA to keep programme administration under an hour a week.
