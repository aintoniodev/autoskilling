## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Start with the workflow.** Give day, night and weekend nurses on each ward paid time to try the kiosk during a normal shift. Ask where logging would interrupt care, whether the kiosk is reachable, and what the ward could learn from the entries. Fix placement, sign-in and duplicate submissions before starting rewards. Post the fixes in the shift huddle. Nurses perform hand hygiene at the point of care and log afterward when care allows. If routine logging still means leaving patients, queuing or reconstructing events from memory, narrow it to a feasible sample before launch.

**Reward reliable ward-level logging.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, with equivalent provision for nights and weekends, plus specific thanks from the nursing director at handover. Announce the reward and the rule at launch. “Your ward earns the team meal when its kiosk records agree with at least four out of five eligible checks during routine audits, and Infection Prevention clears the safety check.” Every ward can earn it independently. There are no points per entry, individual quotas or streaks.

**Measure agreement during the existing monthly audits.** Before launch, test whether the kiosk and routine observer records can be compared for the same ward area, time window and staff group using data they already record. Infection Prevention reviews all comparable windows in the reward period, with at least five across two monthly rounds and coverage of day and night shifts. An eligible window contains at least five observed completed actions. It passes if the saved kiosk totals by moment category differ from the observer totals by at most one action overall, using the same counting convention for a single action that covers multiple indications. Sum absolute differences so overcounts in one category cannot cancel undercounts in another. Use a short, fixed grace period for entries after care, agreed during the baseline. Agreement is a sample-level check of logging reliability. The monthly clinical compliance rate remains observed actions divided by observed opportunities. If the systems cannot support a fair comparison with existing records, defer the reward and fix the measurement workflow. **Show progress at handover.** The kiosk confirms “Moment saved” and offers an undo. The existing huddle board shows the ward’s checks passed and reviewed, the next audit update and one workflow fix. Managers receive ward totals. The pilot adds no patient identifiers, staff tracking or manager drill-down. Pilot data stay out of performance reviews. The ward kiosk lead handles count disputes and IT corrects confirmed system errors.

**Stagger the reward and set an end date.** Give all three wards the workflow fixes and feedback for a four-week baseline. Start eight-week reward periods in weeks 5, 9 and 13, assigning the order by draw and aligning each period with two monthly audit rounds. Compare each ward with its own baseline and look at wards still awaiting rewards for context. Before prizes end, make the logging screen the kiosk’s default and keep the confirmation and huddle feedback. Repeat the agreement check in the first monthly audit at least four weeks after each ward’s prize ends. Keep the workflow if at least 80% of eligible windows pass, revise and recheck at 60–79%, and drop routine kiosk logging below 60%. If there are fewer than five eligible windows, use the next audit before deciding.

**Pause if care or data quality suffers.** Infection Prevention pauses rewards if observed compliance falls five percentage points below the ward’s pre-pilot baseline, or immediately if logging delays care. It reviews shift mix, observer coverage and reported workflow problems before restarting. Before launch, test a double tap, an undo, a legitimate repeat action and a completed hygiene action left unlogged during urgent care. A duplicate counts once, an undone entry is removed, a genuine repeat can count separately, and an unlogged action still counts in the clinical audit. Kiosk downtime makes a window ineligible and triggers a replacement check at the next routine audit.
