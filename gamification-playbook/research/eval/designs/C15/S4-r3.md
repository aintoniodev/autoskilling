## Knowledge base adoption programme

**Reward one useful knowledge-base contribution per resolved ticket.** A ticket earns one credit when the agent responsible for the resolution links an article that explains the actual fix or writes a reusable article that passes the existing publication review. Multiple links still earn one credit. Reopening a ticket reverses its credit until it is resolved again with an article that matches the final fix. A ticket resolved the old way stays in the denominator and earns zero until a valid article is attached. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged.

**Fix the workflow before rewards start.** In weeks 1–2, give agents across queues and shifts 30 minutes of paid time to try the workflow on real cases and flag friction. Make article search the first option in the resolution form, prefill search from the ticket category, and offer a short article template when there’s no match. Give agents protected writing time and assign a knowledge-base reviewer to clear drafts within two business days. Let agents close tickets while drafts are pending. Post the fixes in the support channel with the agent feedback that prompted them.

**Use existing ticket and article records.** Count distinct eligible resolved ticket IDs with a qualifying link and divide by all eligible resolved ticket IDs. Use current ticket categories to exclude only predefined cases such as spam, duplicate tickets merged into a parent, and cases whose handling rules prohibit documenting the resolution. Support Ops checks exclusion rates weekly so recategorizing tickets can’t quietly inflate the score. Before launch, test a duplicate link, a merged ticket, a reopened ticket, a pending draft, and an old-way resolution. Use the current 12% as the starting reference and audit a prelaunch sample to see how much of it represents useful coverage.

**Give agents immediate feedback and a reachable target.** In the ticket workflow, show “Counted,” “Pending article review,” or a specific reason the ticket earned no credit. Each agent privately sees their count and a goal of covering 25% of their own eligible resolutions over the four-week window. A missed week has no penalty. The existing support channel shows weekly team coverage, and managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. Announce the reward at launch: if the team reaches 25% coverage and passes the quality check, every team member earns one paid hour off, with shift coverage arranged. The support lead announces the result in the team huddle and recognizes a specific contribution with the agent’s consent.

**Run four reward weeks per team.** Split existing teams into two waves balanced where possible by queue mix and baseline coverage. Both waves get workflow fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. The first wave earns rewards in weeks 3–6 and the second in weeks 5–8. Use weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds while the second wave has the same workflow and feedback. Keep article search first in the resolution form after the reward ends, and retain protected writing time and the review queue.

**Pause for quality problems.** Each week, the knowledge-base lead randomly reviews 10 credited tickets per wave, or all if fewer, checking whether the article matches the fix, is reusable, and exposes no customer data. They also spot-check uncredited and excluded tickets for missed links and misclassification. If two or more credited tickets fail the weekly check, or any article exposes customer data, the support lead pauses rewards for that wave while the knowledge-base lead fixes the issue and verifies a fresh sample. Support Ops handles disputed counts privately. Watch team CSAT against its prelaunch level, with response volume visible, and investigate any sustained drop for rushed closes or agents delaying replies to do article work.

**Decide four weeks after each wave’s reward ends.** Use one decision metric: quality-adjusted coverage, calculated as recorded coverage multiplied by the pass rate in a random audit of up to 40 credited tickets from those four weeks. Set the decision bands at launch. At **25% or above**, keep the workflow and feedback with prizes ended. At **18% to under 25%**, investigate the biggest remaining workflow friction and adjust it. **Below 18%**, drop the reward programme and revisit why agents aren’t finding or writing useful articles.
