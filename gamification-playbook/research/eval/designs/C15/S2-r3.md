## One working day for a useful code review

Run this as a six-week trial of faster review handoffs. Launch with a concrete promise the team has agreed to: “When your review group gets 8 in 10 pull requests a useful first review within one working day over two weeks, everyone in the group earns a paid half-day off.”

### Make reviews easier to pick up

Before launch, ask engineers from both sides of the handoff what makes reviews hard to start. Give them paid time to try the workflow. Set up a shared review queue ordered by oldest request, a rotating primary and backup reviewer, and a protected daily review block. Route requests to the backup when the primary is away. Tell the team which problems they raised and what changed. Start the reward trial once both groups have working coverage.

### What earns credit

**Count one on-time review per pull request when a reviewer submits a useful approval, a concrete request for changes, or a specific finding or question the author can act on within one working day.** A bare “looking,” an emoji, or a bot response earns nothing. The author can still have work to do before the PR is ready to merge.

Use the repository’s existing ready-for-review, review-request, and submitted-review events. Start the clock when a non-draft PR first has a human review request. One working day is eight hours on the group’s agreed coverage calendar, excluding weekends and group holidays. Measure the percentage of eligible PRs whose deadline fell in the reporting window and received a qualifying review by that deadline. Unanswered and late PRs both count as misses.

Before launch, replay awkward cases with the engineers. Two reviewers answering the same PR earn one credit. A review withdrawn before the deadline needs a qualifying replacement by the original deadline. Reassigning or reopening a PR keeps the original clock. A genuine review done in chat earns credit when its substance is recorded in the PR by the deadline. Give engineers a private way to flag a wrong count. The review rotation owner resolves disputed eligibility, and the reporting owner corrects the tally.

### What people see and earn

Confirm credit promptly in the existing review workflow, visible privately to the reviewer. Each engineer can see their own eligible requests and timely answers, with a personal target of four out of five across their next five requests. Those requests can span weeks, so a missed week or time off has no penalty. Only requests assigned during a reviewer’s coverage count toward their personal view.

Post each group’s on-time percentage and denominator weekly in the team channel. Managers see those totals, with no individual drill-down. The programme data stays out of performance reviews.

Each group earns the half-day once when it reaches 80% across two consecutive weeks with at least 10 eligible PRs. If volume is lower, extend the measurement window until it contains 10. Hand over the reward at the team meeting with specific thanks, and let engineers schedule their time off around review coverage.

### Timing and ending

Use two existing review groups with mostly separate queues. Give both the queue, coverage and feedback in week 1. Start the reward in week 2 for one group and week 4 for the other, giving each three weeks of eligibility. Compare the groups’ weekly rates during the stagger alongside their own rates before launch. With 25 engineers, treat the comparison as directional.

All reward eligibility ends after week 6. By then, make the oldest-first queue and primary-to-backup routing the defaults. Four weeks after rewards end, use the team’s on-time useful review rate for those four weeks to decide whether to keep the workflow as configured (80% or higher), change coverage or routing (65% to below 80%), or end the trial’s scorekeeping and revisit the bottleneck with engineers (below 65%).

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamping or token comments.** Each week, a rotating senior engineer samples five credited reviews per group against the credit rule. If two or more fail in either group, the engineering lead pauses that group’s reward decisions, corrects the tally and resumes after a fresh sample passes.
- **Easy PRs get picked first or requests get delayed.** Keep the queue oldest-first and spot-check long waits, including PRs marked ready well before anyone requested a review. Use the findings to fix routing or coverage.
- **Review work crowds out delivery.** If the queue regularly exceeds the protected review block, the lead adjusts reviewer capacity or planned work before asking for faster responses.
