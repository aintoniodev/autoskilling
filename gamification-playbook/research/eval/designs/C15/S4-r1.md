## Support knowledge-base pilot

**Reward a resolved ticket with a useful article.** Give one credit when an agent resolves a ticket and links an existing article that helped resolve it or links a newly published article that captures the resolution. The rule agents see is “One resolved ticket earns one credit when its linked article explains the fix or the next step.” Reuse a good article across tickets. Give no extra credit for writing one. Keep the existing CSAT bonus in place.

**Make it easy during ticket work.** Before launch, sit with agents from different queues and shifts and watch them close a few tickets. Fix the biggest barriers they identify, such as poor search, missing permissions or a slow publishing queue. Show agents what changed and give them 30 minutes of paid practice. Use the existing ticket and knowledge-base records to count credits. If a case needs an article, give agents paid time to write it and an owner who can review it promptly.

**Count each ticket once.** The ticket ID is the unit. Multiple links earn one credit. Reopening reverses the credit until the ticket is resolved again. Merged duplicates count through the surviving ticket. A ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the denominator and earns credit if it has a qualifying link in the existing record. Agents can close a case without an article when linking would expose sensitive information or no useful article can be made. Those tickets stay in the denominator, and QA uses them to identify gaps. The support operations lead owns the count and corrects disputes privately. Before launch, test the rule on a duplicate, a reopened ticket, an old-workflow closure and a sensitive case.

**Give agents private feedback and teams a shared milestone.** At resolution, confirm the credit in the ticket and show agents their own credited share of resolved tickets. Start with a personal goal of roughly one in three, expressed in tickets based on their own volume over the pilot. An absence carries no penalty. Post team totals weekly in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals only, and the programme data stays out of performance reviews. Announce at launch that every member of a team that reaches **35% credited coverage over its four reward weeks** earns **one hour of paid time off**, scheduled across shifts. The support lead announces the result at the team huddle and thanks the team for a specific improvement.

**Run for 12 weeks.** Give every team the fixes and private feedback in weeks 1–2. Randomly assign existing teams to reward windows in weeks 3–6 or 5–8. Compare the change in coverage during weeks 3–4, when both groups have the same workflow and only the first group is earning a reward. Before week 6 ends, make article suggestions and the link field part of the default close screen for all teams. Stop rewards after week 8. In weeks 9–12, measure **valid article coverage across all resolved tickets**, using the same counting rule to verify the reported 12% baseline. Keep the workflow at **30% or higher**, revise it and retest at **20–29%**, or end the programme and revisit the workflow below **20%**.

**Pause for quality problems.** Each week, QA randomly reviews 20 credited tickets across teams for relevance, accuracy and customer-safe content. The support lead pauses rewards if three or more fail or if any linked article exposes sensitive information. QA also monitors the existing CSAT measure. A five-point drop from the pre-pilot baseline over two weeks with at least 30 responses triggers a pause and review of rushed closures and queue mix. Resume after the support lead verifies the fix. The risks to look for first are generic links added for credit, duplicate articles, tickets closed too early, and low-volume queues running out of time to write.
