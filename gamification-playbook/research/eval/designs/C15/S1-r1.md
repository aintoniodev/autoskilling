## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Make the checklist practical first.** In weeks 1–2, give every crew paid practice time. Have technicians from different shifts and routes walk through the app with union stewards, Safety and Dispatch. Fix slow sign-in, poor offline access and duplicate entry before rewards begin. Give crews enough time in the job schedule to complete the check and publish what changed in response to their feedback. Agree the counting, privacy, correction and reward rules with union representatives before launch.

**Reward one timely safety check per job visit.** Use this launch message: “If our crew records a completed mobile safety checklist before work begins or before we stop or defer a job for safety, on at least 75% of eligible visits during our four-week pilot, everyone on the crew earns one hour of paid time off.” Arrange coverage in advance so every shift can take the time. Count a completed check that identifies a hazard the same way as one that clears the job. Follow the existing stop-work and paper fallback procedures throughout. The safety check remains required on every eligible visit.

**Measure completed checks against Dispatch records.** The denominator is every dispatched visit that reaches a pre-job safety decision, including visits stopped for safety and visits using paper fallback. Exclude cancellations before that point. The numerator is the first valid completed digital checklist per visit, linked by visit ID and recorded before the existing work-start or safety-stop event. Credit offline submissions using the original completion time once synced. Exclude duplicates, voided submissions and late entries. Report paper fallback separately in the team totals so app failures are visible. Before launch, verify that existing records can establish the order of events and test a duplicate, a void, an offline sync, a paper fallback and a safety stop. Delay rewards if the records can’t support reliable counting.

**Show feedback where crews already work.** After submission, the app confirms the visit and shows whether the check is counted or awaiting sync. Each technician can see their own eligible visits and credited checks, with a rolling goal of eight out of their next ten visits. A missed week has no effect on progress. Use the existing crew huddle to share the team rate and progress toward 75%. Managers get aggregate results only. Group small crews where totals could identify individuals. Exclude incentive data from performance reviews and discipline. A named pilot coordinator handles missing-credit requests privately and resolves them within two working days.

**Run four reward weeks per crew, then remove the prize.** Give everyone the app fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2, and measure the baseline after those changes. Randomly assign comparable crews to start rewards in week 3 or week 5. Compare the groups in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds. Before each crew’s reward window closes, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job, with the visit details prefilled. At launch, commit to using the same measure throughout: the share of eligible visits with a valid, timely digital check. Measure it again four weeks after each crew’s reward ends. Keep the default if the rate is at least 75%, fix the workflow and retest if it’s above the post-fix baseline but below 75%, and retire the reward approach if it returns to baseline or lower.

**Watch for rushed checks, delayed start records and pressure to proceed.** Each week, Safety and a union representative review a random sample of up to 10 credited checks per crew against the existing job records. Check for skipped required items, implausible answers and timing discrepancies. Two materially invalid checks in a sample pause reward credit pending review. A credible report of pressure to work around a hazard triggers an immediate review. The Safety lead decides when credit resumes, and the pilot coordinator corrects the affected counts. Use crew feedback and paper fallback totals to spot routes where connectivity or scheduling makes the target harder to reach.
