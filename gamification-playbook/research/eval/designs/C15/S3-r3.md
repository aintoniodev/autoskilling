## Three-ward hand hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward the ward for reliably recording hand hygiene that nurses actually performed.** Announce one milestone at launch. If at least 80% of sampled, performed hand-hygiene actions are correctly recorded during the ward’s eight-week reward period and audited compliance passes the safety check, the chief nursing officer hosts a meal for the ward, with equivalent sessions and staffing cover for day and night shifts. Each ward can earn it independently. The rule for nurses is simple. “Clean your hands when care calls for it, then record the action once at the next safe pause.”

### Set up the workflow

Give day, night and float nurses on all three wards 20 minutes of paid practice. Walk through a busy shift with them to find where kiosk placement, sign-in or the screen slows care. Fix those barriers before launching rewards and tell staff what changed. Preselect the ward and open the kiosk directly to the moment picker. Agree with nurses and Infection Prevention on a practical logging window. If urgent care takes priority, nurses resume with the next eligible action.

### Measure and show progress

- **Kiosk adoption** is the percentage of sampled, performed actions that the existing monthly audit can confirm were recorded correctly within the agreed window. Add an anonymous tally to the audit for *recorded*, *missing* or *unverifiable*. Unverifiable actions stay in the denominator, and Infection Prevention reviews their frequency before certifying the result. A duplicate counts once, an entry undone without a correction earns no credit, and an action performed but recorded only through the old workflow counts toward compliance and earns no kiosk credit. Test these cases with observers before launch.
- **Patient safety** is measured through the existing audit as hand-hygiene actions performed divided by observed opportunities. Infection Prevention sets the minimum sample size and uses a comparable mix of shifts and opportunities each month. Pause the reward if ward compliance falls five percentage points below its pre-pilot baseline, audit coverage is insufficient, or staff report that logging delayed care. The Infection Prevention lead decides when to resume. The kiosk owner fixes counting errors and posts the corrected ward total. Observer presence can affect behaviour, so treat results as estimates from the sampled shifts. [WHO hand hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)
- **Feedback** is immediate at the kiosk with a brief confirmation such as “Before patient contact recorded” and an Undo option. At the existing ward huddle, post the latest adoption and compliance rates with their sample sizes and progress toward the meal. Managers see ward totals. The pilot collects no new staff or patient identifiers and bars use of its data in performance reviews.

### Run it and end it

Give all three wards four weeks with the improved kiosk, paid practice and feedback. Then start eight-week reward windows four weeks apart. Use the wards still awaiting rewards as a rough check on what the reward adds. Three wards and monthly audits give a directional comparison.

Award the meal once per qualifying ward, then retire the prize. Keep the kiosk on the moment picker and continue audit feedback at huddles. Recheck the **confirmed kiosk recording rate** four and eight weeks after each ward’s reward ends. Keep the workflow if the rate holds at 80% or higher and the safety check passes. If it drops below 80%, use shift feedback to fix the workflow and recheck at the next audit. End the kiosk rollout if it remains below target after that fix or safe use cannot be restored.

### What could go wrong

Raw kiosk taps could rise through duplicates, fabricated entries or selective logging on easy shifts. Base the reward on anonymous audit confirmation and include nights and weekends in sampling. If staff feel pressure to leave care to log a moment or to make up entries after a missed shift, reinforce that care comes first and the next eligible action is a fresh start. If an outage or observer coverage gap makes the result unreliable, hold the milestone decision until the next adequate audit.
