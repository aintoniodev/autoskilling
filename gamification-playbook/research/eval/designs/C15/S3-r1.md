## Three-ward kiosk pilot

**Reward one completed improvement to the logging workflow per ward.** Give each ward a fixed reward when its nurses identify a barrier, get it fixed, and show that the fix works on both a day and a night shift. For example, if the kiosk queues at handover, IT can simplify sign-in and nurses on both shifts can confirm that they can save an entry without waiting or delaying care. At launch, announce the reward: a hospital-funded team meal with the chief nurse, offered across shifts on paid time, with specific thanks for the improvement. Each ward can earn it independently. Prize eligibility is independent of entry volume and audit scores.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Give day, night, and float nurses 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk and identify what gets in the way. Before opening the reward window, fix any barrier that makes routine logging slower or riskier than the prior workflow. Use the ward huddle to tell nurses what they reported and what changed. Care and hand hygiene come first. If the kiosk is down or logging would interrupt care, follow the existing downtime procedure and resume with the next safe opportunity. There is no catch-up quota.

**Measure kiosk adoption and safety.** Use existing kiosk timestamps and ward IDs, scheduled shift blocks, and the IT outage log. Track the percentage of staffed shift blocks with at least one valid saved entry. Count each block once and report outages alongside the rate so downtime stays visible. This measures how widely the kiosk is used across shifts. The existing monthly observer audit remains the measure of hand-hygiene compliance: observed actions divided by observed opportunities. Keep the audit method and schedule consistent, retain the opportunity counts, and give observers no access to kiosk reports. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

Before launch, test a double tap, an undone entry, two legitimate entries close together, and a moment handled during downtime. A retry should save once, an undo should remove the entry from the tally, and two legitimate entries should both save. The shift still counts only once. The ward’s nurse champion submits correction requests to the kiosk owner, who fixes the record or tally. Infection Prevention reviews a small sample of de-identified records weekly for duplicates and invalid entries and signs off on the workflow milestone.

**Show nurses what happened.** After each submission, show “Saved” with an Undo option. At the existing weekly huddle, show the ward’s aggregate shift coverage and progress toward its chosen fix. Managers get the same ward-level view. Keep individual histories out of dashboards and performance reviews. There are no named rankings or streaks.

**Time-box the reward.** Start all three wards with two weeks of paid practice, workflow fixes, and feedback. Open four-week reward windows in weeks 3, 5, and 7, drawing the ward order in advance. Use the stagger to see whether coverage changes when the reward opens, accounting for outages and staffing changes. Before each window closes, make the quick-entry screen the kiosk default, keep the approved fix in place, and stop the prize on schedule.

Four weeks after each ward’s prize ends, use **at least 80% of staffed shift blocks with a valid entry over those four weeks** as the proposed keep threshold, agreed before launch. Keep the workflow if it clears the threshold and the safety check. Below 80%, fix a specific barrier if nurses can identify one. If coverage fell back to baseline and there is no fixable barrier, retire the incentive.

**Pause when the numbers or the workflow deteriorate.** Pause rewards immediately if logging delays care. Also pause if more than 10% of entries in a weekly quality sample are invalid or if the monthly observed compliance rate drops by at least five percentage points from that ward’s pre-pilot baseline. Infection Prevention reviews the opportunity counts and shift mix before deciding whether to resume. If kiosk coverage rises while observed compliance falls, investigate at the ward level. Kiosk records cannot establish whether hand hygiene happened.
