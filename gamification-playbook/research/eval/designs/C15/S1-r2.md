## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward a completed safety check.** Each crew earns a 30-minute paid break, scheduled during normal shifts with coverage, if at least 75% of its eligible job visits have a valid digital checklist over its four reward weeks. Everyone on the crew earns the same break, including people who were on leave. There are no rankings or individual payouts. Announce the milestone and break at launch, then have the supervisor and union rep recognize the crew in person and confirm when people can take it. Agree on the reward, counting rules, and data access with union reps and technicians from different shifts and routes before launch.

**Make the check practical first.** Give technicians protected, paid time to try the app on real routes. Fix the problems they surface, especially weak signal, duplicate entry, and job schedules that leave too little time for the check. Show what changed in the crew briefing. Make the checklist the first step on the job screen, prefill job details, and let technicians record it offline with its original completion time. Keep the approved paper fallback available. If neither method can be completed or a hazard remains unresolved, stop the job. Reward credit never determines whether work can proceed.

**Count one check per job visit.** The rule technicians hear is “Your crew gets one credit when the assigned technician completes a valid digital safety checklist before the job starts.” Match existing checklist and job-start records using the visit ID and original timestamps. Confirm with technicians that the recorded start corresponds to work beginning. A record that cannot establish the order does not earn credit, and the pilot adds no location tracking or clock-in step.

- A duplicate or replayed sync counts once. A voided checklist earns no credit unless a valid replacement was completed before the start. A checklist entered after work began earns no credit.
- A visit stopped before work begins stays outside the denominator. A valid paper check permits work under the existing safety procedure. Paper visits count in the overall adoption measure, while visits affected by a confirmed app outage or unsupported offline workflow are excused from the crew’s reward calculation.
- The safety lead samples 10 credited visits per crew per reward week, or all if fewer, using routine safety records to check timing and whether flagged hazards were handled. Pause milestone awards if more than 10% of the sample has a material defect or if any sampled job began with an unresolved critical hazard. The safety lead and union rep decide when awards can resume. A designated data steward fixes disputed counts and tells the technician the outcome.

**Show people their own progress.** After submission, the app confirms the visit and shows whether the checklist is saved offline or synced. Once the start record is available, it shows whether the visit earned credit. Each technician sees their own counts and a personal target of three in four eligible visits, scaled to their actual workload. A missed visit or week leaves the next visit available. Post crew-level progress in the existing shift briefing. Managers get crew totals with no named drill-down. Limit access to individual records used for pilot reconciliation to the data steward and keep pilot metrics out of performance reviews and discipline.

**Run two waves and stop the reward on schedule.** Give every crew the same fixes, default, and feedback for two weeks. Assign comparable crews to two waves by lottery, with the second starting two weeks later. Each wave gets four reward weeks. Compare the waves during the two weeks when only the first can earn the break to estimate what the reward adds. Keep the app improvements and private feedback when prizes stop.

Four weeks after the final reward period, use the **share of all eligible job starts preceded by a valid digital checklist** to decide what to do. At **75% or higher**, close the incentive pilot and keep the workflow as standard. At **55–74%**, fix the remaining barriers and recheck. **Below 55%**, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and the union. The reported baseline is about 35%. Recalculate it using the same counting rule before launch.

**Watch for rushed checks, delayed start entries, and pressure to proceed.** The safety sample can flag implausible timing and missed hazard follow-up. Use an existing union or safety reporting channel for pressure to rush, avoid paper, or start unsafe work. Review adoption by route and shift in aggregate so that poor coverage or tight scheduling gets treated as a workflow problem.
