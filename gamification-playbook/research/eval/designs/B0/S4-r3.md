# Knowledge at Close: eight-week pilot

### What earns the reward

On every resolved ticket, the agent records one of three outcomes in the closeout screen:

- **Link** an existing article that accurately explains the resolution. An internal link counts; agents should only share it with customers when it helps.
- **Write or improve** an article when there’s a reusable answer but no adequate article. Attach the draft and resolve the ticket without waiting for publication. A knowledge steward reviews it within two business days. It counts once approved, including if the steward merges it into an existing article.
- **Record an exception** when there’s genuinely nothing reusable or safe to document, with a reason. Exceptions are visible and audited; they never earn credit.

Each ticket earns at most one credit. Reuse and approved writing count equally. If the org reaches **70% verified coverage across weeks 7–8**, with at least **90% of sampled claimed credits passing QA** and no unresolved service guardrail breach, all 60 agents earn **one hour of paid discretionary time**, scheduled across shifts. No individual prizes, rankings, or changes to the existing CSAT bonus.

### How it’s measured

**Verified coverage** is the estimated share of unique resolved customer tickets with a relevant, accurate link or an approved contribution. Missing actions, pending or rejected drafts, and exceptions stay in the denominator; spam and merged duplicates do not. Reopening and reclosing a ticket never creates extra credit; QA checks the final resolution. Report valid exceptions separately so reviewers can see why some tickets remain uncovered.

Before launch, Support Ops applies the same rules to the previous four weeks to check the reported 12% baseline. Each week, QA samples 60 tickets across queues and shifts, including claimed credits, exceptions, and tickets with no action. Weight the results back to actual ticket volumes. Reviewers check whether the article matches the fix, is accurate and reusable, and contains no customer-sensitive information. Allow two business days after week 8 for draft review before closing the score.

### What people see

The closeout screen shows **Link / Draft / Exception** and the status of any submitted draft. Agents get a private weekly view of their tickets, missed opportunities, and QA corrections. The org sees aggregate verified coverage versus baseline and target, the exception rate, and service guardrails. The weekly huddle showcases one article another agent reused and one gap the knowledge steward will address. No public individual scores.

### How it runs and ends

Weeks 1–2 are for practice: demonstrate closeout, fix search friction, and roster 30 minutes of protected writing time per agent per week. Weeks 3–6 are for coaching and filling recurring knowledge gaps. Weeks 7–8 determine the reward. Publish the result and retire the incentive on schedule, whether or not the target is met. Keep the closeout workflow, steward, and QA. Four weeks later, check whether verified coverage is still **at least 60%** without a reward. If it falls short, fix the biggest observed blocker rather than restarting the prize.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, duplicate articles, or inflated exceptions.** QA rejects irrelevant links; stewards merge duplicates; queue leads review exception spikes. Never pay per article or per click.
- **Slower service or premature closure.** Compare CSAT, seven-day reopen rate, and SLA attainment against the prelaunch baseline by queue. Investigate if CSAT or SLA attainment drops **3 percentage points**, or reopens rise **2 points**, for two consecutive weeks. Show CSAT response counts and check ticket mix before attributing the change. Pause reward scoring if the programme is harming service; don’t change CSAT pay.
- **Hard queues or shifts carry more work.** Review coverage, exceptions, and drafting load by queue and shift. Move steward capacity and protected time to where work accumulates rather than imposing individual quotas.
- **Sensitive information or a publishing backlog.** Keep drafts internal until approved. Remove exposed data immediately. If the review queue breaches its two-business-day target, add reviewer capacity instead of lowering the quality bar.
