# Safe Start: a 12-week checklist pilot

**Goal:** Raise verified digital checklist completion from about 35% to 85% of eligible jobs by the end of week 8, without encouraging rushed checks, unsafe starts, or under-reporting.

### What earns the reward

Agree the pilot in writing with the union before launch. Give each technician a **one-time bonus worth one hour of base pay** for correctly completing the pre-job checklist on three separate jobs during the six-week reward window. A check earns equal credit whether the technician proceeds, makes the job safe first, or stops and escalates. Documented use of the approved fallback earns the same credit. A missed check does not reset progress. Offer an equivalent paid, observed exercise for technicians who do not get three job opportunities, including those returning from leave.

The bonus is separate from paid work time. Dispatch schedules time for every check, and the app opens the right checklist from the work order on a company device. No prizes for speed, volume, perfect streaks, low incident counts, or keeping jobs moving. This follows [OSHA’s worker-participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation): reporting a safety concern should never put a reward at risk.

### How it is measured

- **Digital adoption:** Divide valid pre-work app checks by dispatched jobs that reached the pre-work decision, including jobs stopped for safety. Exclude duplicates and cancellations before arrival, not outages. Count one check per work order. For offline submissions, use the recorded completion time, not the upload time. Compare it with the work-start record; for stopped jobs, verify the stop and escalation instead.
- **Safety coverage:** Use the same denominator, but count both valid digital checks and verified fallbacks. If the device or app fails, use a timestamped paper or dispatch check before starting. Report fallback reasons separately so an outage is visible as an app problem, not a technician failure.
- **Quality:** Each week, a safety lead and union steward review 20 randomly selected checks across shifts and job types, and observe a small rotating sample in the field. Verify that the check reflects conditions on site and that any blocking hazard was handled before work began. A submission timestamp alone is not proof.

### What people see

For each job, the technician sees **check due**, **recorded**, or **stopped: next action**. A private view shows bonus progress, pending offline records, and how to correct a missing credit through a steward. At weekly huddles, everyone sees company-wide adoption and safety coverage, the number and causes of fallbacks, and what management fixed in response. No individual leaderboards or depot rankings. Limit access to named pilot records, prohibit their use for discipline or productivity scoring, and delete the reward tracker after payout and appeals close. Keep required safety records under the existing policy.

### How it runs and ends

**Weeks 1–2:** Reconcile the 35% baseline against dispatch data, shadow technicians across shifts, and fix obvious barriers such as login failures, weak signal and schedules with no time for checks. **Weeks 3–8:** Run the bonus and review the data weekly with union representatives. **Weeks 9–12:** End bonus earning on the announced date, pay earned bonuses, resolve appeals, and keep the checklist workflow and paid time in place.

At week 12, retain the workflow if digital adoption is at least **80% in each of the final two weeks**, at least **90% of sampled checks pass quality review** over those weeks, and no serious safety concerns linked to the pilot remain unresolved. If adoption falls or quality slips, fix the cause with technicians before considering another incentive. Do not extend the bonus automatically.

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamping or backfilling:** Blank, copied, or late records can inflate completion. Reconcile with dispatch and use field observations. Investigate patterns before assuming misconduct.
- **Pressure to hide hazards or skip checks:** Protect stop-work and provide a confidential reporting route through the union. A credible complaint of retaliation or incentive-driven unsafe work triggers a joint review and a pause on new bonus earning, not on safety checks or earned payouts.
- **Unequal access or customer delays:** Review fallback rates and start delays by shift and job type. Fix devices, training, accessibility, or booking time where the burden falls. Never solve a schedule problem by shortening a safety check.
