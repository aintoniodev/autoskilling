# Check before work: eight-week adoption pilot

**Goal:** Raise verified pre-job digital checklist completion from about 35% to 80% by the last four weeks of the pilot, without encouraging rushed checks or discouraging stop-work. A joint union–management safety committee signs off on the reward, paid time, data use, and appeals before launch. Technicians test the workflow on paid time across shifts and low-connectivity routes.

## What earns the reward

Offer up to **25 in local currency per technician per four-week block**, for two blocks. The payout is 25 multiplied by the share of the technician’s eligible jobs with a verified pre-work check. For example, 18 of 20 earns 22.50. There is no minimum job count, threshold cliff, or reward for taking extra jobs. The maximum is 50 per technician, or 15,000 if all 300 employees are eligible, excluding payroll overhead.

A check earns the same credit whether it clears the job to start or identifies a hazard and puts the job on hold. An approved paper or dispatch-assisted check earns the same credit if the device or app is unavailable. Injuries, near misses, reports, and time spent resolving a hazard never reduce the payout. There are no speed prizes, streaks, team quotas, or injury-free bonuses. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management%2Fworker-participation)

## How it is measured

- **Start with dispatch records, not app submissions.** Count one opportunity per eligible job visit, assigned in advance to the responsible technician. On crew jobs, the lead submits one checklist after reviewing it with the crew. Include jobs attended but stopped for safety. Exclude duplicates and jobs canceled before anyone attends. Reconcile reassignments and cancellations against dispatch records.
- **Verify timing and substance.** Match the job ID, completed required items, and recorded go/no-go decision to the dispatch work-start event. For held jobs, use the logged hold before work began. Use the app’s offline completion timestamp, not upload time. For paper fallback, dispatch logs the check before work starts. Late entries stay in the safety record but do not earn credit. Flag missing or conflicting timestamps for review rather than treating them as automatic misses.
- **Keep two rates.** The reward rate includes verified fallback checks; the digital adoption rate does not. Both use all eligible visits as the denominator. Track late, missing, fallback, and held jobs separately. Each week, a safety rep and union rep review a sample of 20 visits across shifts and job types, including occasional field observations to check whether the assessment actually happened.

## What people see

The job screen shows **Check due**, **Saved offline**, **Ready to start**, or **On safety hold**. A private weekly summary shows credited jobs, pending or disputed records, and estimated payout. Workers have 10 working days after each block to challenge records through a steward or directly with the committee. Pay undisputed amounts on the next payroll; correct upheld disputes on the following run.

Crews see company-wide progress and a short “you raised it / we fixed it” update, not rankings or names. Supervisors see current job safety status, not individual reward scores. Restrict identifiable reward data to authorised reviewers and payroll; no GPS tracking or use of pilot scores for discipline, scheduling, or performance reviews.

## Timeline and exit

Use two baseline weeks to validate the reported 35% against dispatch and fix access problems. Then run eight reward weeks. From day one, publish the end date. At week eight, stop accrual, settle payouts, and remove the reward display. Keep the checklist prompt, paid time, and fallback. Measure four more weeks without rewards. Call the pilot successful if digital completion reaches **80% in weeks 5–8 and stays at or above 75% in weeks 9–12**, with no deterioration in sampled check quality or evidence of reporting pressure. If it misses, fix the causes with the union rather than automatically extending the bonus.

## What could go wrong

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| **Box-ticking, backdating, or hiding missed jobs** | Check patterns against dispatch and field observations. Investigate anomalies before changing anyone’s credit. |
| **Pressure to start unsafe work or stay quiet** | Protect paid stop-work time and keep a confidential reporting route through the union. Pause the incentive if credible evidence of pressure or retaliation emerges; keep the safety workflow running. |
| **Poor signal, inaccessible app, or overloaded routes** | Provide company devices, accessible training and forms, offline capture, and paid time in dispatch schedules. Review fallback and missed-check rates by shift and route; fix access before drawing conclusions about adoption. |
