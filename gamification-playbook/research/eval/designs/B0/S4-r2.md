## Close the ticket, capture the fix

Run an **eight-week, org-wide pilot** for all 60 agents. Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets, not article volume. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula, survey process, and eligibility unchanged. No individual quotas, rankings, or pay adjustments.

### What counts and what people earn

At resolution, agents choose one of three options:

- **Link an existing article** that accurately describes the fix used. Link it internally unless it is appropriate to share with the customer.
- **Submit a new article or substantive update** when no current article covers a reusable fix. The draft is marked pending until a knowledge editor approves it. Give approved work the same credit as a correct link: one covered ticket, never extra credit for writing. Related tickets can link to the same draft rather than creating duplicates.
- **Record an exception** for a genuinely one-off or sensitive case, or a tool outage. “No article found” is a drafting prompt, not an exception. Exceptions count as documented closeouts, not article coverage.

Give agents 30 minutes of protected drafting time per week, with shift coverage. Assign a rotating editor to review drafts within two business days. Resolve the customer’s ticket without waiting for editorial approval.

If verified coverage reaches **80% in both weeks 7 and 8**, at least **90% of sampled article links and contributions pass QA** across those weeks, and service guardrails hold, everyone gets **one paid hour for learning of their choice**, scheduled across shifts. Recognise useful examples during weekly huddles with contributors’ consent. No prizes per link or article.

### How it’s measured and what people see

Count each distinct resolved customer ticket once per weekly cohort. Exclude only spam, test tickets, and merged duplicates using fixed system rules. Keep exceptions and pending drafts in the denominator. Credit approved drafts back to the original cohort and allow five business days after week 8 for review and appeals. A reopened ticket does not earn a second credit; if the final fix changes, the link must match it.

**Verified coverage** is the share of resolved tickets with a qualifying article, adjusted for the pass rate in a random QA sample. Each week, QA reviews 60 records across queues and shifts, sampling article links, contributions, and exceptions separately and weighting article results by their actual mix. A pass requires an accurate, current article that matches the fix, is usable by another agent, and contains no customer-sensitive data. Publish sample sizes and label estimates as estimates.

Agents see their own missing, pending, approved, and returned items, with reasons and a correction path. The team sees aggregate coverage, the reported **12% starting point**, the 80% target, QA results, exception rates, and editorial backlog. Managers see queue and shift breakdowns to identify gaps in the knowledge base or protected time, not to rank people.

### Timing, safeguards, and exit

Before launch, remeasure the prior four weeks using the pilot definitions and establish queue-level service baselines. Use weeks 1–2 for coaching and fixing workflow friction, and weeks 3–6 to tackle the most common missing articles. Check guardrails weekly: a CSAT drop of more than **3 percentage points**, a seven-day reopen increase of more than **2 points**, or a first-response SLA attainment drop of more than **5 points** versus baseline triggers review. Check survey counts, queue mix, and ticket volumes before attributing a change to the pilot. Confirmed deterioration linked to the pilot pauses reward qualification while the cause is fixed.

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate drafts:** remove failed credit, coach with examples, and merge drafts. Investigate repeated mismatches rather than penalising one honest error.
- **Premature closure or exception dumping:** review reopen and exception spikes by queue. Never delay resolution to earn credit or pressure customers for survey responses.
- **Review bottlenecks or privacy leaks:** add reviewer capacity if the two-day turnaround slips. Remove sensitive material immediately and follow the incident process. Do not make reward eligibility depend on a decision that is still stuck in the review queue; clear it before deciding.

Announce the end date at launch. After the final review, report whether the team earned the reward and retire the reward and campaign dashboard either way. Keep the closeout workflow, editorial ownership, and routine QA. Recheck coverage and service four weeks later without incentives. If coverage falls below **70%**, fix the biggest workflow or content gap before considering another campaign.

## Key Learnings:

1. Reward relevant reuse and approved writing equally; track exceptions without counting them as coverage.
2. Keep the CSAT bonus separate and test whether the habit holds after the reward ends.
