# S4: Resolve it, then make it reusable

**Fix the closeout first.** Ask six agents across queues and shifts to walk through recent tickets: one where search failed, one where writing would have delayed a reply, and one where no article belonged. Give all 60 agents 30 minutes of paid practice and reserve 20 minutes per week for writing, with queue coverage. Put the article picker first in the resolution panel, prefill its search from the ticket topic, and offer “Draft from resolution” and “No suitable article.” Never block a customer resolution. Show agents what changed in response to their feedback. Start rewards only once linking is quicker than the old workaround and writing fits into paid time. **Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula unchanged.**

**Reward useful coverage, not article volume.** Tell agents: “A resolved ticket counts once when it has a relevant, accurate published article attached, whether you reused it or wrote it.” Use existing ticket resolution, article link and publication records. A draft counts only once published and linked. Multiple links earn no extra credit; merged tickets count under the surviving ID; reopening or removing the link reverses provisional credit. A ticket resolved the old way or marked “No suitable article” stays in the denominator but earns no credit. Test those cases before launch. If the existing records can’t support the rule, simplify it rather than adding employee tracking.

Each team earns one **30-minute paid break per person**, scheduled with coverage, if at least **35% of its resolved tickets** qualify during its four reward weeks and the quality check passes. No prize per article and no individual quota for earning the team reward. After a link or draft submission, show private confirmation in the ticket: “Article linked · provisional credit” or “Draft saved · counts when published.” Show agents their own progress toward a suggested goal of about one in three resolutions, based on actual ticket volume. Post only team totals and progress in the existing weekly huddle or channel. No manager drill-down, leaderboard, performance-review use, or streak to lose after time off.

**Run the pilot in waves.** In weeks 1–2, everyone gets the improved closeout and feedback, but no prize. Pair teams with similar queues and starting coverage, then randomly assign one in each pair to reward weeks 3–6 and the other to weeks 5–8. Compare the change in weeks 3–4 against each team’s weeks 1–2 baseline to see what the reward adds while the later wave still has feedback alone. Treat the reported 12% as raw usage, not verified useful coverage.

**Catch bad links and protect service.** Each week, the support ops lead samples 10 credited tickets across teams and checks that each article matches the actual resolution, is accurate and reusable, and contains no customer-sensitive information. Fewer than 9 passes pauses payouts; exposed customer data triggers an immediate pause and removal. The lead also pauses if rolling two-week CSAT drops more than 3 percentage points versus the pre-pilot baseline with at least 30 responses, or if the queue breaches its existing SLA for two consecutive weeks. Check reopen rates for rushed closes. If agents are linking filler, fix search and examples; if writing is hurting service, adjust coverage before restarting. The ops lead owns pause and restart decisions; the KB owner fixes publication issues and support ops corrects counts privately. Budget 30 minutes for sampling, 15 for the existing dashboard and huddle update, and 10 for corrections each week.

**End the prizes, keep the shortcut.** When each wave ends, turn off reward messaging but leave the article picker first, the prefilled search, and protected writing time in place. Four weeks after the last prize ends, decide using one number: **the share of resolved tickets with a useful article**, estimated as recorded coverage multiplied by the audit pass rate over those four weeks. At **35% or higher**, keep the workflow and private feedback; at **20–34%**, fix the biggest remaining blocker and recheck; below **20%**, drop the blanket closeout prompt and focus it on repeat issues. Don’t extend prizes to rescue the number.
