# Before You Start: a pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Fix the work before offering a reward.** The safety lead and union stewards sit down with technicians from different shifts and routes, including low-signal areas. Walk through a real job from dispatch to start. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and build checklist time into job schedules. Open the checklist from the assigned job, prefill job details but never safety answers, and test offline use. Keep the approved paper fallback and stop-work route available. Publish what changed: “You reported losing checklists when signal dropped; drafts now save offline.” Don’t start rewards until technicians confirm the main blockers are fixed and the union and company agree in writing on the rules, data access and appeals.

**Reward the safety decision, not a green checkmark.** Tell technicians: “One credit per job for completing the checklist before work starts, whether you proceed or stop and report a hazard.” Earn one union-agreed reward, such as 30 minutes of paid personal time, for qualifying on at least 80% of assigned jobs that reach the pre-job decision point during your four-week reward window. No prize per job, no ranking and no streaks. Five qualifying jobs out of six earns the same reward as 20 out of 24. Absence creates no missed jobs; someone with no eligible jobs gets a make-up window on return. The reward never depends on finishing a job, finding no hazards or having no injuries.

**Use records the company already has.** Match the app’s checklist completion record to the dispatch work-order ID and compare completion time with the existing job-start or safety-hold record. Validate those fields before launch; if they can’t establish timing reliably, don’t launch the incentive on that data. Count one valid record per job, not each submission or crew member; credit the designated checklist owner. Before launch, test a duplicate, a voided checklist, a late submission, an offline sync, a hazard stop and a paper fallback. A voided record needs a valid replacement before start; a late checklist doesn’t earn credit. Count offline completion by its recorded completion time, not upload time. Exclude jobs cancelled before the safety decision, not jobs stopped because of a hazard. An approved, documented paper fallback earns reward credit but stays in the denominator and out of the numerator for **digital adoption**.

**Show people their own progress.** After submission, show “Pre-job check recorded,” “Safety hold recorded,” or “Saved offline; credit pending sync,” as appropriate. The technician sees their own count and what they need to reach the goal. The existing weekly crew huddle shows team totals only; combine small groups where an individual could be inferred. No manager drill-down, named leaderboard, new location tracking or use of pilot data for discipline or performance reviews. A technician can challenge a count privately through a steward. The safety lead decides disputed eligibility, and the app administrator corrects the count within five working days. Delete pilot-specific individual tallies after payout and appeals; leave ordinary safety records alone.

**Run it for eight weeks, then check whether it stuck.** Weeks 1–2: give all crews the fixes and feedback, and measure adoption using the pilot definition rather than assuming the reported 35% is directly comparable. Randomly assign comparable crews to rewards in weeks 3–6 or 5–8. Compare the change in weeks 3–4, when both groups have the same workflow but only one has rewards. By week 8, the checklist is the first step on every assigned job. End prizes as announced; keep the default, paid checklist time, feedback and fallback. In weeks 11–12, use one decision number: **the share of eligible jobs with a valid digital pre-job checklist**. Agree before launch: **80% or more**, keep the workflow without prizes; **60–79%**, fix the remaining barriers and retest; **below 60%**, drop the incentive approach and redesign the digital workflow. The safety check itself stays in place.

**Guard against pencil-whipping and pressure to proceed.** Each week the safety lead spends no more than 50 minutes reviewing the automated report, sampling 10 records against existing job or hazard notes, and handling exceptions. If two sampled records have material safety defects, or there’s any credible report of pressure to bypass a check or conceal a hazard, the lead pauses new reward accrual immediately and reviews the cause with the steward. Preserve valid credits already earned. If the records can’t tell whether the check was real, don’t treat a timestamp as proof.
