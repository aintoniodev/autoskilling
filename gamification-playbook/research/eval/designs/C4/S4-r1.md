# S4: Make useful knowledge part of closing a ticket

**Goal:** Move from 12% recorded knowledge-base use toward useful article coverage on every eligible resolved ticket. Pilot a modest team reward. Leave the existing CSAT bonus, formula, and eligibility untouched.

### Fix the workflow before offering a prize

Ask six agents across queues and shifts to walk through recent tickets: one where they found the answer, one where search failed, and one that needed a new article. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and protect queue time for writing. Fix the biggest blockers before the reward starts: put suggested articles at the top of the resolve screen, make a relevant result attachable in one click, and prefill a short draft when nothing fits. Require the agent to confirm the match; never attach automatically or block resolution. Post “You reported it; we fixed it” with the actual changes.

### Reward useful resolutions, not article volume

**Agent-facing rule:** “A resolved ticket counts once if you link a current article that supports the fix, or write one that gets approved, published, and linked within seven days.”

Use existing ticket status, ticket-to-article links, and publication records. The denominator is unique resolved support tickets, excluding only pre-agreed categories identifiable from existing dispositions, such as spam and merged duplicates. A ticket with no relevant article stays in the denominator. Drafts are pending, not credit; agents should resolve the customer’s issue without waiting for publication. Reuse and writing earn equal credit. No credit for searches, clicks, drafts, or extra links.

Before launch, replay four cases: two links on one ticket count once; reopening or removing the only valid link reverses credit, and re-resolving can restore it but never double it; an old-way close with no link stays in the denominator; a draft published after seven days earns no pilot credit. Agents flag errors privately to Support Ops, which checks the source record and fixes the tally. The support lead decides disputed eligibility and whether to pause or resume rewards.

### Show progress without ranking people

After close, show a private confirmation: “Counted: password-reset article linked” or “Pending: draft awaiting publication.” Show each agent a cumulative goal of 40% of their own eligible volume: 8 of 20 tickets, not the same quota as someone closing 60. Miss a week? Start with the next case. No streaks or individual payout.

Post each team’s aggregate progress in its existing weekly support huddle or channel. A team that reaches **40% quality-adjusted coverage** in its four-week reward window earns **30 minutes of paid time per person**, scheduled with queue coverage. Agents see only their own counts; managers get team totals with no individual drill-down. No leaderboard, new activity tracking, or use in performance reviews.

### Run two waves, then stop paying

Give all teams two weeks with the workflow fixes and feedback but no prize. Split existing teams into two waves with comparable queue mix and baseline coverage. Wave A earns in weeks 3–6; wave B earns in weeks 5–8. In weeks 3–4, compare each wave’s change from the shared run-in to estimate what the reward adds over fixes and feedback alone. Announce the end dates up front. Allow the final seven-day publication window to clear, then settle rewards. Keep the article picker, draft shortcut, and feedback after prizes end.

### Guard against gaming and customer harm

Support Ops samples 10 credited tickets weekly for relevance, accuracy, and whether the article actually supports the resolution. Duplicate or placeholder articles fail. Pause rewards if **2 or more of 10 fail**, or immediately if an article exposes customer data. Check existing aggregate CSAT and seven-day reopen rates against the pre-pilot baseline. If either worsens by five percentage points for two weekly readings with adequate volume, pause and check for rushed closes, queue shifts, or writing crowding out customer work. Never change CSAT pay to compensate. Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, reviewing totals, and correcting counts.

**Keep, change, or drop:** Use quality-adjusted coverage: the share of eligible resolutions with recorded credit multiplied by the audit pass rate. Pool the samples across the four weeks after the last reward window closes. At **40% or above**, keep the workflow and retire prizes. At **25–39%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck without prizes. Below **25%**, drop the incentive model and investigate why the workflow still loses to the old way.
