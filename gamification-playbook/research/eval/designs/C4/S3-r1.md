## Three-ward pilot: clean hands first, log when safe

**Fix the workflow before offering a reward.** In weeks 1–4, give day, night and float nurses on each ward 15 minutes of protected, paid time to try the kiosk. Ask where logging would interrupt care, create a queue or duplicate existing work. Put the kiosk on the normal staff route, prefill the ward and make “Log a moment” the first screen. Aim for a save in under 10 seconds when there’s no queue. Post one visible “you reported it, we fixed it” update. If logging still delays care or requires double entry, don’t start rewards.

**Reward one ward milestone, not individual entries.** Tell staff: “Your ward earns a meal across all shifts when it reaches its logging target without a drop in hand-hygiene safety.” Nurses choose the modest reward in advance. Each ward can earn it once; there’s no competition between wards. The target is a 10-percentage-point improvement over the ward’s feedback-only baseline, capped at 90%; wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. No reward for logins, raw counts or individual quotas.

**Measure documentation and hygiene separately.**
- **Documentation coverage:** During existing monthly audits, divide correctly performed hygiene opportunities with one accurate, verifiable kiosk record by all correctly performed opportunities in the audit sample. Use the existing save record and anonymous observation details; count a match only when the observer can verify it without guessing, interrupting care or linking a nurse or patient identity. Test this during the run-in. If routine audits can’t verify a usable sample this way, keep the workflow fixes and feedback but don’t launch the reward. Require at least 30 sampled completed opportunities per ward before deciding a milestone; otherwise report “not enough data.”
- **Clinical safety:** Keep the observer audit independent: hygiene actions performed divided by all observed opportunities. A kiosk entry is never evidence that hygiene happened. Keep the audit method and mix of shifts comparable, and show the sample size beside each rate. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144056/)
- **Test the awkward cases:** A double tap counts once; an undone or incorrect entry earns no credit; correctly performed hygiene documented the old way still counts in the clinical audit but not as kiosk adoption. In urgent care or downtime, use the approved fallback. Never delay care or guess to fill a gap.

**Show people useful feedback without ranking them.** After a save, show “Saved: [moment type]” with an undo option, not “compliant.” Nurses can privately choose a practice goal sized to their shifts, such as trying the flow at the next safe opportunity on each shift they work. A missed shift or week resets nothing. Post only ward totals, the latest audited coverage and the milestone in the existing staff huddle. Managers get the same aggregates, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews. Don’t add badges, sensors or a leaderboard.

**Pause before bad data or unsafe work earns a prize.** Each week the coordinator samples 10 existing records across active wards for obvious duplicates, reversals and invalid entries; this checks record quality, not whether hygiene happened. Infection Prevention pauses the affected ward’s reward if two or more of 10 records are defective, if audited compliance falls five percentage points below baseline or below the hospital’s safety floor, or if logging interferes with care. Nurses can report errors privately to the ward champion; the kiosk administrator corrects the record and the coordinator updates the tally. Budget 45 minutes a week for the coordinator, using the existing audit rounds.

**Stagger the start and set the end in advance.** All wards get fixes and feedback in weeks 1–4. Randomise the order and start eight-week reward windows in weeks 5, 9 and 13, just after the relevant monthly audit. Compare changes with wards still on feedback alone. Treat that comparison as directional: three wards and monthly samples cannot cleanly separate a reward effect from staffing changes or people behaving differently while watched.

Before prizes end, make the kiosk the default documentation route, retaining the approved fallback. End rewards on schedule. Four weeks later, use **audited documentation coverage** to decide: keep the default if coverage still meets the milestone and safety is intact; fix the workflow and recheck if coverage improved but fell short; return to the approved alternative if there’s no gain or the kiosk compromises care. Hand hygiene and independent audits continue in every case.
