# Pre-job safety checklist: eight-week pilot

**Make the app easier before offering a reward.** Have the safety lead and union stewards sit down with technicians from different shifts, including low-signal routes, on paid time. Watch them run a real pre-job check. Fix the problems they identify: open the checklist from the work order, prefill job details but never safety answers, support offline completion, and build checklist time into dispatch schedules. Keep the approved paper fallback. Show crews what changed at their next toolbox talk: “You reported it; we fixed it.” Agree the reward, data access, and appeals process with the union before launch.

## What earns the reward

**The rule crews see:** “One job counts when you finish the safety check before work starts, or document a safety hold before starting; each work order counts once.”

Earn **30 minutes of paid time off**, once, for three qualifying jobs during your four-week reward window. If you have only one or two eligible jobs in that window, your goal is one or two respectively. A missed check or a week off never resets progress. There’s no prize for doing more jobs, finishing faster, reporting fewer hazards, or proceeding instead of stopping. Give a verified outage fallback the same reward credit, but don’t count it as digital adoption.

## Measurement and feedback

Use existing work-order IDs, checklist completion times, job-start events and safety-hold records. For offline submissions, use the recorded completion time, not the sync time. If those records can’t reliably establish that the check happened first, fix the workflow before starting the incentive; don’t add GPS or worker monitoring.

The adoption measure is **the percentage of eligible work orders with a valid digital check before job start or documented safety hold**. Include safety-held jobs, even if later cancelled, and keep paper-only jobs in the denominator. Exclude jobs cancelled before the technician reached the point of needing a check. Before launch, replay a duplicate, a voided check, a late submission, an offline sync and a paper fallback. A duplicate earns nothing extra; a voided check loses credit unless a valid replacement was completed in time; a late check never earns credit.

Show a private receipt immediately: “Safety hold recorded. 2 of 3 toward your paid time.” If offline, say “Saved on device; credit pending sync.” Post team adoption totals at the existing toolbox talk, combining small crews where a total could identify someone. Managers get no individual drill-down. No leaderboard, disciplinary use or performance-review use. Delete the pilot’s individual tally after the appeals window; leave required safety records untouched.

## Timing and decision

Give every crew the fixes, paid practice and feedback in **weeks 1–2**, and measure that reward-free baseline rather than assuming the reported 35% is directly comparable. Start matched groups of crews by lot in **weeks 3 and 5**, each for four weeks. Compare their change from baseline during weeks 3–4, when only the first group has rewards. End prizes after week 8. Keep the checklist first in the job workflow, with offline access and paper fallback.

Use the same adoption rate in **weeks 9–12** to decide: **80% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback without prizes; **65–79%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck; **below 65%**, drop the incentive approach and redesign the workflow, not the safety requirement. Don’t renew prizes just because adoption rose while they were available.

## What could go wrong

- **Box-ticking or pressure to start.** A safety lead reviews five sampled records a week against existing job and hold records. Pause new reward accrual if even one sampled record shows a critical check skipped or work proceeding despite an unresolved hold. The safety lead and union steward review the cause before restarting. Reporting a hazard never triggers the pause.
- **Wrong counts or unequal access.** Technicians can challenge a count privately through their steward or the pilot owner. The safety lead decides disputed eligibility with the steward; the pilot owner corrects the tally by the next weekly update. Dispatch protects check time on every shift and arranges paid time off without adding workload to the technician’s next shift.
- **Too much administration.** Automate receipts and totals from existing records. Budget one pilot owner 50 minutes a week: 25 for the sample, 15 for exceptions and appeals, and 10 for team updates.
