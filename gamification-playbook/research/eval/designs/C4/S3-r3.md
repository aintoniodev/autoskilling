# Three-ward pilot: clean hands first, log when safe

**Fix the workflow before offering a prize.** Ask two bedside nurses from each ward, including night and weekend staff, to walk through a real shift with Infection Prevention. Fix kiosk placement, queues and sign-in delays before launch. Give every nurse 15 minutes of protected, paid practice, and post what changed in the existing ward huddle: “You reported it; we fixed it.” If logging still competes with care or hand hygiene, don’t start the incentive.

## What earns recognition

**The rule nurses see:** “Your first valid record of a real hand-hygiene moment on an eligible shift counts once toward the ward goal; extra entries and ‘done’ answers earn nothing extra.”

An eligible shift is a worked bedside shift with a functioning kiosk. Use the existing kiosk submission record and worked roster; don’t add sensors, patient identifiers or staff tracking. If those records can’t reliably assign one credit per nurse-shift, fix the counting rule before launch. Count a truthfully logged missed moment the same as a completed one if the kiosk supports it. Never mark a missed moment as done.

Each ward earns one modest reward, a catered meal available to every shift, if **75% of eligible nurse-shifts** have a valid submission over its eight-week reward window and the quality and safety checks pass. Check with staff during the run-in that the target is reachable. There are no individual prizes, no competition between wards and no extra credit for more entries.

## What people see

After a submission, the kiosk says “Record saved” and, when applicable, “This shift counts toward the ward goal.” It never says “compliance verified.” Nurses can privately see their own eligible shifts and progress toward logging on three out of every four worked shifts. A missed shift doesn’t reset anything; the next shift is a fresh chance.

The existing weekly huddle shows ward progress as a numerator and denominator, with **kiosk use** and **observed compliance** clearly separated. Managers see ward totals only, with no individual or small-shift breakdowns. No leaderboards or use in performance reviews.

## Checks and failure modes

- **Duplicate, undone or fallback entry:** A retry doesn’t add credit. Undo removes credit if no valid entry remains. The approved downtime process still applies, but fallback entries don’t earn kiosk credit; documented outage shifts are excluded and reported separately. Test all three cases before launch. The kiosk administrator fixes wrong counts through the existing support route; Infection Prevention decides when to pause or resume rewards.
- **More entries, same hygiene:** Each week the pilot coordinator samples 20 credited records across the wards for duplicate, voided, test or wrong-shift entries. Three or more invalid records pauses the affected ward’s payout pending review. This checks record quality, not whether hands were cleaned. Shift coverage also can’t tell whether every moment was logged.
- **Worse hygiene or delayed care:** Keep the existing monthly observer audit independent. Report observed compliant actions divided by observed opportunities, with sample sizes and comparable shift coverage. Observer presence and sampling can skew results. [WHO hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/) Hold payout if the audit sample is too small to interpret or the rate falls five percentage points below the ward’s pre-pilot baseline, using a minimum sample agreed before launch. Pause the incentive immediately if logging credibly delays care or hand hygiene. **Clean hands and care come first; the kiosk can wait.**

## Timing and sunset

Give all three wards the fixes and feedback for a four-week run-in. Randomize the order and start the eight-week reward windows two weeks apart. Compare each ward with its own run-in and with wards still awaiting rewards. Treat the comparison as directional, not proof. Budget 45 minutes a week for one coordinator to review the automated totals, sample records and post the huddle update, beyond the existing monthly audits.

Before prizes end, make the short logging form the kiosk’s first screen, with the ward prefilled but no hygiene answer selected. Keep the easy form, save confirmation and ward feedback; remove the prize meter on schedule. Four weeks later, use **the percentage of eligible shifts with a valid kiosk submission** to decide: **keep** the workflow at 75% or above, **change** it at 50–74%, or **drop** this kiosk workflow and return to the approved alternative below 50%. An unresolved safety concern overrides a “keep.” Don’t restart prizes to prop up the number.

## Key Learnings:

1. Shift coverage tells you whether nurses are using the kiosk. It does not tell you whether hands were cleaned or every moment was logged.
2. The test is whether nurses still use the easier workflow after the meal is gone, without compromising care.
