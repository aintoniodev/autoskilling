# Put the right article one click from resolution

**Fix the workflow first.** In weeks 1–2, give six agents across queues and shifts 30 minutes of paid time to walk through real closes. Find out where search fails, which articles they don’t trust, and what writing costs them when the queue is busy. Fix the biggest blockers before offering a prize: put relevant articles first in the resolution screen, let agents attach one in a click, and offer a prefilled draft when nothing fits. Give agents protected writing time and have the KB editor review drafts within two business days. Tell the team what changed because of their feedback. Keep “no suitable article” available so nobody has to delay a resolution or attach a bad link.

**Reward a resolved ticket backed by useful knowledge.** Tell agents: “Your team gets one credit per resolved customer ticket when you attach an article that supports the fix, or when a new article drafted from that ticket is approved.” Reuse and writing earn the same credit. Each team earns one 30-minute paid break, scheduled with queue coverage, if at least 35% of its resolved tickets qualify over its three-week reward window and it passes the quality check. The existing CSAT bonus stays unchanged. There’s no prize for the highest score and no individual payout.

**Use records the tools already keep.** Join ticket resolution, ticket-to-article links and KB approval records by ticket ID. Divide credited tickets by all unique resolved customer tickets in the window. Count a merged case once, under its surviving ID. Multiple links earn one credit; reopening reverses provisional credit, and re-resolution can restore it once. An old-way close or “no suitable article” stays in the denominator and earns no credit. A draft tied to the ticket at close can earn credit if approved within seven days, without holding up the customer. Dry-run those cases before launch. If a required event isn’t already recorded, don’t add employee tracking: leave that path out of the reward until it can be verified from ordinary work records.

**Show progress without rankings.** At close, show a private confirmation such as “Article attached: one provisional credit” or “Draft awaiting approval.” Give each agent a private target of roughly one qualifying ticket in three of their actual closes, not a fixed quota. A missed week costs nothing; the next case counts. Put team totals in the existing support channel. Managers get team totals only, no individual drill-down, and programme data never goes into performance reviews. Agents can flag a wrong count privately to Support Ops, which checks the source record and corrects the tally.

**Catch gaming and customer harm early.** Support Ops samples 10 credited tickets per week across active teams for relevance, accuracy and safe handling of customer information. Below 90% passing pauses reward payouts; a privacy exposure pauses them immediately. The Support Ops lead decides when to resume; the KB editor fixes or removes bad content. Also review existing team CSAT and reopen rates weekly. A CSAT drop of five percentage points versus the pre-pilot baseline, on at least 30 responses, pauses rewards while Support Ops checks whether the workflow is hurting service. Pool weeks if survey volume is low. Watch for filler links, duplicate articles and rushed closes, not just rising counts. Budget 30 minutes for the sample and 20 minutes for metrics and corrections each week.

**Stagger the prize, then remove it.** Everyone gets the workflow fixes and feedback in week 1–2. Randomly assign existing teams to start their three-week reward windows in weeks 3, 5 or 7, balancing queue mix where possible. Compare changes for rewarded teams against teams still waiting in the same weeks to see what the prize adds beyond the fixes. The last window ends in week 9. Keep article suggestions first in the close flow, keep the skip option, and remove prize messaging once pending credits settle.

**Decide before launch what success means.** Use *quality-adjusted article coverage*: the share of resolved tickets with a recorded qualifying article, multiplied by the audit pass rate. Recalculate the baseline with that same definition rather than assuming the reported 12% is verified. Four weeks after the last prize window, keep the workflow as-is if coverage is at least 30%, revise it if coverage is above baseline but below 30%, and drop the pilot-specific changes if coverage is back at or below baseline. Don’t expand a workflow with unresolved customer harm, and don’t restart prizes to rescue the number.
