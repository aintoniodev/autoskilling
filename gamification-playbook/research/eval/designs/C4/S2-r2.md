# One working day to a useful code review

**The deal:** Authors get a useful answer without chasing reviewers. Reviewers get protected time and clear ownership, not another target in their performance review. The goal is a first review within one working day, not a merge within one day. Today’s median wait is 2.6 days.

### Fix the queue before offering a reward

Spend 30 minutes of work time with six engineers who regularly submit or review PRs, including a sceptic and a specialist. Walk through three recent slow reviews. Ask what made them hard to pick up, what would make the new routine worse, and whether the proposed reward is worth having.

Start with a rotating primary and backup reviewer for each existing workstream. Give the primary a protected 30-minute daily review block by taking equivalent work off their plate. Put the oldest ready PR at the top of the existing review view, route specialist changes to a qualified owner, and make the backup responsible when the primary is out. Use a short PR template that says what changed, what’s risky, and how it was tested. Post one concrete “you reported it, we fixed it” example before launch. If ownership or protected time still isn’t working, delay the reward.

### What counts and what people see

**The rule:** One credit per ready PR for its first useful review within eight working hours: an approval after checking the change, or a submitted review that tells the author what to fix or decide.

Use existing PR readiness and submitted-review events. Use a published team working calendar, excluding evenings, weekends and holidays. The clock starts when a non-draft PR opens or a draft first becomes ready. A bot, self-review, emoji, or “I’ll look tomorrow” doesn’t count. A qualifying review through the existing workflow counts even if the reviewer didn’t pick it up from the new queue. A second review adds nothing; a dismissed review doesn’t count. Reassignment, returning to draft, or reopening doesn’t restart the clock. An eligible PR closed or merged without an answer is a miss, not an exclusion.

The team’s number is **the percentage of eligible PRs whose deadline has passed that received a useful review on time**, including unanswered PRs. Recalculate the pre-pilot baseline using this rule; the 2.6-day median doesn’t tell you the starting percentage.

After a qualifying review, send a private confirmation: “PR #418 got a first review in 4 working hours. It counts.” Reviewers can set a private goal based on their expected assignments, such as two on-time reviews out of three this week. No minimum for people with no assignments, no streaks, and no catch-up debt. The existing team channel gets one weekly aggregate, such as “31 of 40 on time; target 30.” Managers see team totals only, with no drill-down, rankings, or performance-review use. Don’t store a separate history of individual scores.

### Eight-week pilot and exit

- **Weeks 1–2:** Everyone gets the queue fixes, protected time and private feedback. No prizes.
- **Weeks 3–4:** One existing workstream starts the reward; the other starts in **weeks 5–6**. Randomly pick which goes first. Each earns one company-paid team lunch during work hours if at least 75% of eligible PRs get an on-time first review across its two-week window and the quality gate passes. Confirm with engineers that they’d want the lunch; don’t make attendance mandatory. Compare the early group’s change with the delayed group’s over the same weeks. With 25 engineers and shared reviewers, treat the comparison as directional.
- **Weeks 7–8:** No further rewards. Keep the preassigned rotation and oldest-ready-first queue as defaults. At the end of week 8, use the same on-time percentage: **85% or more**, keep the routine; **70–84%**, fix the biggest remaining bottleneck and retest without prizes; **below 70%**, drop the incentive experiment and redesign review coverage. Recheck the same measure four weeks after the last reward window. Don’t restart prizes to mask a slide.

### What could go wrong

**Rubber stamps or easy-PR picking:** Keep normal merge protections. The pilot owner samples five credited reviews each week, including a larger change where possible. If two of the last ten sampled reviews are clearly perfunctory or miss an obvious material issue, the engineering lead pauses rewards and fixes the review process before resuming. The queue stays oldest-first; a complex PR gets a qualified reviewer and time, not a token reply.

**Wrong counts or extra work:** Before launch, replay a duplicate review, a dismissed approval, a Friday request, a reopened PR, and a review submitted outside the new queue. Engineers flag errors privately; the engineering lead rules on disputed cases and the pilot owner corrects the tally. If the queue routinely exceeds the paid review block, shift sprint capacity rather than expect evening work. Budget 45 minutes a week for one owner to sample reviews, handle corrections, and post the aggregate.
