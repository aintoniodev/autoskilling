# Safe Start: a pre-job checklist pilot

**Make the checklist easier before paying anyone to use it.** Bring together union stewards, dispatch, safety staff and technicians from high-volume, rural and on-call routes. On paid time, walk through real jobs and find where the app costs time or creates risk. Fix the biggest barriers first: open the checklist from the dispatched job, prefill job details but never safety answers, and make offline completion work. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice and build checklist time into dispatch schedules. Post the fixes at the crew huddle: “You reported it; we fixed it.” Agree with the union on the reward, data access and appeals before launch.

## What earns credit

Tell technicians: **“One credit per job when you complete the digital safety check before starting work, or use it to record why the job must be held for safety.”**

Use existing checklist and dispatch records: work-order ID, checklist completion time, and actual work-start or safety-hold event. Use the saved completion time for offline submissions, not the sync time, if the app can verify it. Before launch, replay these cases:

- **Duplicate or resubmission:** one credit per work order, not one per submit or technician.
- **Voided checklist or late entry:** no credit unless a valid replacement was completed before work began. A safety hold earns the same credit as a safe start; an unrelated cancellation before assessment is not an opportunity.
- **Paper fallback or app outage:** use the approved fallback; never delay a safety check to earn credit. Paper counts as safe procedure, not digital adoption. Verified outages stay visible in the adoption denominator but don’t count against personal reward targets.

If existing records can’t reliably establish timing or distinguish a safety hold from a routine cancellation, fix the workflow before launching rewards. Don’t add GPS, photos or individual monitoring to settle the count.

## What technicians see and earn

On submission, show a private confirmation: “Checklist complete before start. 7 of 10 toward your paid break.” If offline, show “Saved on device; credit pending sync.” At the start of each four-week reward window, set a personal target at 70% of expected eligible jobs, rounded up. Adjust it downward for leave, fewer assigned jobs or verified outages. One missed job or week never resets progress; the next qualifying job counts. Reaching the target earns one extra 30-minute paid break, scheduled with coverage and redeemable on shift. No prizes for speed, job volume, all-clear answers or incident-free days.

Technicians see only their own records. At the existing crew huddle, show the team’s pre-job digital completion rate and what’s being fixed. Combine small crews where a total would reveal who missed. Managers get aggregates only, with no drill-down; no leaderboard, performance-review use or disciplinary use of pilot data. Delete individual reward tallies after the agreed appeals window; leave normal safety-record retention unchanged.

## Timing and decision

**Weeks 1–2:** Give all crews the fixes, paid practice and private feedback. Verify the reported 35% baseline against dispatch records. **Weeks 3–6:** Start rewards for half the crews, selected by lottery among comparable routes or shifts. **Weeks 5–8:** Give the other half the same four-week reward window. Compare the groups in weeks 3–4 to see what rewards added beyond fixes and feedback. Before each crew’s rewards end, make the checklist the first action on the dispatched job, with the approved fallback still available. Pay out earned rewards and keep the default and feedback.

Recheck four weeks after the last rewards end. Use one decision metric: **the percentage of eligible jobs with a valid digital checklist before work starts or at a documented safety hold**, including paper-fallback jobs in the denominator. Agree thresholds up front: **80% or more**, keep the workflow without prizes; **60–79%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and retest; **below 60%**, drop the incentive approach and redesign the workflow. The safety requirement stays in force either way.

## What could go wrong

The main risk is faster box-ticking, not safer work. Each week, the pilot owner samples eight jobs across crews, including starts and holds, and checks whether recorded hazards and actions match existing safety or dispatch records. Use observations from routine safety checks where available; a timestamp alone doesn’t establish quality. **Pause new rewards if two of eight checks have material omissions or contradictions, or immediately if any critical hazard was missed or bypassed.** The safety lead and union steward decide when to restart; the app owner fixes the cause. Technicians can dispute credit privately with the pilot owner, who corrects the tally or explains the decision within five working days.

Use one weekly report: 15 minutes for counts and appeals, 30 for sampling, and 10 for the crew update. Handle safety incidents through the existing safety process, not the reward programme.
