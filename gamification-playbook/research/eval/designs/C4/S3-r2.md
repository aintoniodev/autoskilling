# Three-ward pilot: clean hands first, log when safe

**Fix the workflow before offering a reward.** Give day, night and weekend nurses from each ward 20 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk during a normal shift. Ask what makes it slower or riskier than the current process. Fix the barriers they find: put the kiosk on an existing route, open directly to the logging screen, prefill ward and shift, and make mistakes easy to undo. Post “You reported it, we fixed it” updates in the shift huddle. Don’t start rewards until nurses can log without delaying care or entering the same information twice. Keep the downtime fallback and make clear that missing a log is preferable to interrupting care.

**Reward completed documentation, not the number of entries.** The rule staff see: “Log a hand-hygiene action you actually performed when it’s safe to do so; your first valid record that shift counts once toward your ward’s goal.” Nurses should follow the normal logging policy after that first entry, but extra entries earn nothing. No points for logins, no prize for a perfect compliance report, and no individual payout. Each ward that reaches **70% of eligible shifts documented over its six reward weeks** earns one hospital-funded meal, available across shifts. All three wards can earn it.

**Measure two things separately.**
- **Kiosk adoption:** Eligible nurse-shifts with at least one valid, non-voided record filed by shift end, divided by eligible nurse-shifts. Use existing kiosk records and roster data only. Eligible means a worked direct-care shift with the kiosk available; exclude documented downtime and leave, not busy shifts. Before launch, test a duplicate submission (still one credit), an undone sole entry (credit removed), and an old-method entry (no kiosk credit, no penalty). If the existing systems cannot deduplicate by nurse-shift without new staff tracking, don’t launch the reward on this measure.
- **Hand-hygiene safety:** Infection Prevention continues its independent monthly audit: observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities, not by kiosk entries. Keep the mix of shifts and observation conditions comparable and report the denominator. Observation is sampled and can change behavior, so don’t interpret a small or unrepresentative audit as proof of improvement. [WHO guidance on observation and compliance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/).

**Show progress without surveillance.** After a save, the kiosk says privately, “Record saved. This shift counts once.” A nurse’s private goal is to log on the next eligible shift they work, not to maintain a streak. The existing ward huddle shows only the ward’s weekly and cumulative adoption rates and its own target. Managers see the same aggregates: no named rankings, individual drill-downs, or use in performance reviews. Collect no patient identifiers for the pilot.

**Run it, then remove the prize.** Give all three wards four weeks of the improved workflow and feedback before rewards. Randomly assign reward starts in weeks 5, 7 and 9; each ward gets six weeks. Compare each ward with its own feedback-only period and with wards still waiting. With three wards, treat differences as directional, not proof. Before each ward’s prize ends, make the prefilled kiosk screen the default documentation route, with the fallback still available. Four weeks after each ward’s reward ends, decide on the **workflow**, not on renewing the prize: keep it at **70% or higher** sustained adoption, fix and retest at **50–69%**, or drop it as the default below **50%** and restore the prior route. A safety concern overrides any adoption result.

**Catch gaming and harm early.** Each week the pilot lead reviews 10 credited records across the wards for obvious defects such as duplicates, voids or entries outside the shift. That checks record integrity, not whether hands were cleaned. Pause the affected ward’s reward if **two or more of 10** fail, if its comparable monthly audit falls **five percentage points** below baseline, or if anyone reports that logging delayed care. Infection Prevention decides when to restart; the kiosk owner corrects wrong counts and confirms the correction privately. If logging rises while observed hygiene falls, treat it as a safety warning, not a success. Budget 45 minutes a week for the pilot lead using the existing reports and audit cycle.
