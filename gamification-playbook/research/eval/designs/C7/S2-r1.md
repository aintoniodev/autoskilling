# One working day for a useful code review

**Fix the review queue first.** Ask five engineers what delays reviews, which changes need specialist input, and whether an hour of paid time off is worth earning. Confirm the reward before launch. Give each squad a rotating reviewer and backup, a protected 30-minute review block, and an oldest-first queue in the tool they already use. Route specialist changes to someone qualified. Tell the team which delays their feedback helped fix, then run the queue for two weeks before rewards start.

**Reward a review that moves the work forward.** Count the first submitted review on an eligible PR if it gives a reasoned approval, requests specific changes, or explains a concrete blocker and gives the author a next step. A justified security or safety hold counts. The backup can earn credit by taking over and submitting the review. Each PR earns credit once across reviewers, reassignments, pushes, and reopens.

**Measure it from existing PR events.** Start the clock when a PR is ready and review is requested, using the later of those timestamps. One working day means eight hours on the owning squad’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Reviewer reassignment leaves the original deadline in place. Use existing timestamps to identify on-time submitted reviews and sample the review text for quality. Exclude drafts, bot-only PRs, and PRs withdrawn before the deadline. Record overdue PRs and PRs merged without a qualifying review as misses, including when someone used the old workflow. Recalculate the 2.6-day baseline on the same calendar and eligibility rules.

**Make the reward personal and visible.** At launch, announce one hour of paid time off for meeting a personal target within a two-week window. Set the target from the engineer’s expected assignments and case mix. Two timely reviews is the usual target, with one for a light rotation or a specialist queue. If too few eligible PRs arrive, carry progress to the next rotation. The existing PR tool confirms credit privately after each qualifying review. Post the team’s weekly on-time percentage in its usual channel. Managers get team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. The engineering lead thanks each recipient for a specific contribution and grants the time in person or on a call.

**Catch shortcuts early.** Before launch, walk through a duplicate review, a withdrawn approval, a justified hold, and a PR merged through the old workflow. The rotation owner samples up to ten credited reviews a week for cursory approvals, vague blockers, and inappropriate holds. If two sampled reviews fail in a week, the engineering lead pauses new rewards and fixes the workflow before resuming. The rotation owner corrects disputed counts from PR history. Assign reviews in queue order so easy PRs are harder to cherry-pick. Track the original deadline through handoffs so reassignments cannot improve the score.

**End the reward and keep the queue.** Start two-week reward windows two weeks apart across existing squads. All squads get the queue and feedback from day one. Compare squads with and without an active reward window to see whether the reward appears to add anything. Keep the queue, rotation, and protected review time as defaults after the last window closes. Four weeks later, check the share of eligible PRs receiving a timely, qualifying review. Keep the workflow at **80% or higher**, adjust routing or review capacity if it improved from baseline but is below 80%, and drop the reward format if there was no improvement over the two weeks with the queue and feedback alone. The rotation owner spends at most 45 minutes a week on sampling, corrections, and the team update.
