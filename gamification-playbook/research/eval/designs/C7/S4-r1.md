## Reward a useful knowledge step on resolved tickets

An agent earns one credit when they resolve a ticket and link an article that helps with the issue, publish a reusable article if none exists, or record a valid “no article needed” reason. Account-specific billing corrections are one example. A missing article for a recurring issue needs an article or a draft sent through the existing review queue. Drafts earn credit when published, including after the earning window if submitted during it. Deduplicate article ideas in the review queue so agents can use the same article for later tickets.

Give agents two protected 30-minute blocks in paid time to try the workflow. Ask a mix of agents across shifts and queues where linking or writing adds time and what reward they’d value. Fix the biggest friction before launch, such as making article search available in the ticket or routing drafts directly to the KB editor. Show agents what changed in response to their feedback.

## Measure it once per ticket

Use the help desk’s existing resolution event, article links, and internal notes, plus existing KB publication records. Count each ticket ID once. A duplicate or merged ticket earns no extra credit. Reopening reverses the credit until the ticket is resolved again with a valid knowledge step. A ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the denominator and earns credit if the existing record shows the same valid step. Test those cases, a pending draft, and a valid exception before announcing the rules.

Each agent earns **one hour of paid time off** by hitting a private target during their two-week window. Set it at 50% of the eligible resolutions expected for their scheduled hours and queue, using recent volume. Prorate for leave and give agents with very low volume up to two extra weeks to reach the target. Announce the reward at launch and have the programme owner confirm it personally when earned. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and survey process in place.

## Show progress where people already work

After a qualifying resolution, show a private confirmation such as “Article linked. 8 of 12 toward your paid hour.” Show pending drafts and reopened tickets in the same view, with a way to flag a wrong count. The programme owner resolves disputes using the ticket record and corrects the tally. Post the team’s aggregate completion rate in its existing weekly channel. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. A missed week carries no penalty.

## Stagger the reward and check what lasts

Give all teams the workflow fixes and private feedback for two weeks, then start half the teams’ reward windows in week three and half in week five. Pair teams with similar queues and baseline rates and randomize which starts first. Before the first reward window closes, make the knowledge step part of the default resolution form for everyone. Surface existing articles first and put drafting and the exception option on the same screen.

Use one decision metric **the share of resolved tickets with a valid knowledge step**, including valid exceptions. Establish a baseline under that definition first, since the current 12% measures linking or writing. Estimate validity by auditing a random sample of credited tickets. Four weeks after the last reward window closes, keep the default workflow if the quality-adjusted rate is at least 20 percentage points above baseline, revise it if the lift is positive but smaller, and drop the campaign if there’s no lift. Use the staggered launch to see whether the reward added enough to justify repeating it.

## Catch the ways it can go wrong

Agents could link irrelevant articles, publish duplicates, overuse exceptions, or rush closures to hit their targets. Each week the programme owner checks 12 credited tickets across queues, including exceptions and new articles. If three or more fail, pause new reward confirmations, fix the rule or workflow, correct affected counts, and resume after the next sample passes. Check existing team-level CSAT and reopen rates for signs that service is worsening, and check whether drafts are piling up with the KB editor. Automate the counts and reserve the owner’s weekly time for the sample, disputes, and the team update.
