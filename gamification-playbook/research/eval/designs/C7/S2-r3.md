## One working day for a useful code review

**Fix the review queue first.** Ask five engineers across the product team what delays reviews, which reviews interrupt focus, and whether an hour of paid time back is worth earning. Give everyone 20 minutes of protected time to try the workflow. Put review-ready PRs in the team’s existing queue, oldest first, with one assigned reviewer and a rotating backup for absences and overload. Reserve a short review block each working day. Start the trial once the queue and backup are working, and tell the team which friction their feedback helped remove.

**Reward a useful first response.** The assigned reviewer or recorded backup earns one credit for submitting a substantive review within one working day. A qualifying response is a considered approval, a request for changes with a concrete reason, or a specific blocker that tells the author what to do next. A documented decision that review is unnecessary counts when repository policy permits it and the reviewer explains why. Acknowledgments, emoji, and bare “looking later” comments earn no credit.

**Use the PR history.** The clock starts when a non-draft PR is marked ready and review is requested. Require that request when marking a PR ready, and surface ready PRs missing a reviewer in the queue. One working day means eight hours on the team’s published work calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Use the timestamp of the first qualifying response in the PR, including reviews entered through the team’s usual workflow. Reassignment leaves the clock running. Duplicate requests, repeat reviews, and reopened PRs earn at most one credit per PR. An approval withdrawn because the reviewer missed something material loses credit unless a qualifying correction landed within the original window. Before launch, test those cases plus an out-of-hours request and a review given in chat. An engineer who reviews in chat can post the substantive conclusion on the PR so the author and future readers have a record.

**Give engineers private feedback and a reachable reward.** Announce one hour of paid time back at launch, subject to confirming that engineers want it. Each engineer earns it with two on-time responses among their first three eligible assignments in their two-week reward window. For engineers expected to receive only one or two assignments, set the goal at one on-time response. Use normal backup coverage to give low-volume reviewers a genuine opportunity. A miss simply uses one opportunity, and approved leave shifts the window. Confirm each credit privately when it lands, show progress toward the personal goal, and have the team lead hand over the earned hour in person or on a call. The existing team channel shows only the team-wide share of PRs answered on time and the number overdue. Managers get those same totals. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews and leaderboards.

**Run six weeks, then check whether the lift holds.** Give everyone the queue, protected review time, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Start rewards with one review rotation in weeks 3–4 and the other in weeks 5–6. Compare their on-time response rates during weeks 3–4 as a directional check on what the reward adds. Keep the oldest-first queue, backup coverage, and review blocks in place after rewards end. Four weeks later, use the share of eligible PRs that received a qualifying response within one working day, counting unanswered PRs as misses once their deadline passes. Set the decision rule at launch. **At 80% or higher, keep the workflow. At 65–79%, adjust coverage or queue handling. Below 65%, drop the incentive and revisit why reviews wait.** Compute the same rate for the four weeks before launch so the team has a comparable baseline alongside today’s 2.6-day median.

**Watch for rushed approvals and uneven assignments.** The review rotation owner samples up to 10 credited PRs each week, including complex reviews. If two sampled responses lack substantive review, pause new credits while the owner checks the cause and corrects disputed counts against the PR history. Keep assignment order visible in the existing queue so easy cases cannot quietly jump ahead. The same owner handles sampling, corrections, and posting team totals in under an hour a week.
