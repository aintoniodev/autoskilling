## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot for three wards

Start by asking nurses from each ward, including night and weekend staff, to walk through a normal shift and show where using the kiosk would delay care or create duplicate work. Give every nurse 15 minutes of paid, protected time to learn and try the workflow. Fix the barriers before the incentive starts, and post what changed at the shift huddle. Patient care takes priority whenever recording would interrupt it.

**Reward.** Announce at launch that the nursing director will visit each qualifying ward’s shift huddles to give specific thanks for the work behind its independently audited hand-hygiene result. Offer the thanks on every shift within a week of the audit result. Each ward qualifies independently. Paid learning time is available to everyone from day one, and no one’s pay, leave, or appraisal depends on the ward result. The kiosk confirms each accepted entry immediately with “Moment recorded.”

**Measurement.** Use the existing monthly infection-prevention audit for recognition. The score is compliant observed opportunities divided by eligible observed opportunities. Before launch, use the two most recent comparable audits to establish each ward’s baseline. Set its milestone at five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%. A ward already at or above 95% qualifies by maintaining its baseline. Infection Prevention sets the minimum sample and shift coverage before launch. An audit below either minimum defers the recognition decision to the next adequate audit.

Track kiosk uptake separately using the weekly ward-level share of staffed shifts with at least one valid entry. That shows how broadly the kiosk is being used. It cannot establish how many moments were captured or whether hand hygiene occurred. Count a duplicate submission once, remove an undone entry, and keep an observed compliant moment in the audit score even if the nurse used the old recording method or skipped the kiosk during urgent care. Where no hygiene opportunity was indicated, exclude it under the existing audit protocol. Walk through those cases with nurses before launch. The ward educator owns kiosk corrections, and Infection Prevention owns audit corrections and recognition decisions.

**What people see.** Put the ward’s latest audit result, sample size, and milestone on its existing huddle board, with one short update on kiosk use or a reported barrier. Nurses see confirmation of their own entries. Managers see ward totals only, with no individual drill-down or ward ranking. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews and avoid collecting additional staff or patient identifiers. After an absence, the nurse picks up with the next eligible moment.

**Timing and ending.** Give all three wards the workflow fixes, training, and confirmation first. Start the one-audit-cycle recognition window on a different ward each month. Compare the wards’ changes during months with and without recognition, taking shift coverage into account. Before each window ends, make the kiosk’s recording screen the default landing screen and prefill the ward where possible. Keep those fixes and confirmation in place. Check each ward’s audited compliance in the next two monthly audits after recognition ends. Use the combined rate across those two audits to decide the pilot’s future. Keep the workflow if it meets the ward milestone, revise it if it falls between baseline and milestone, and withdraw the kiosk rollout on that ward if it falls below baseline. Defer a decision when coverage is inadequate.

**What could go wrong.** A rise in kiosk entries alongside falling audited compliance is a prompt to check for rushed charting, duplicate entries, or care interruptions. Pause recognition whenever an adequate monthly audit falls below baseline, and have Infection Prevention and the ward educator review the workflow before restarting. Review audit coverage for shift bias and treat small changes cautiously because staff may behave differently when observed. Use the existing audits and huddles, with one coordinator spending up to 45 minutes a week reviewing totals, routing corrections, and updating the boards.
