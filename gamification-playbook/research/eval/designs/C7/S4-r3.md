# Knowledge-base adoption rally

**Reward.** Each agent earns one paid hour off by correctly handling the knowledge-base step on 70% of their resolved tickets in a two-week window. Round the target up to a whole ticket. An agent who resolves three tickets needs three qualifying resolutions and can earn the reward as soon as their window closes. Everyone who meets their own target earns it, regardless of team results. Announce the paid hour at launch and have a support lead recognize the milestone in person, privately by default. Keep the CSAT bonus formula unchanged.

**Fix the workflow first.** Give agents 30 minutes of paid practice. Ask a small group across queues and shifts to complete real cases, flag slow searches and risky article writes, and confirm that the paid hour is worth earning. Fix the biggest friction before the rally and tell agents what changed. Give everyone the fixes and feedback at the same time. Put suggested articles and the article template in the ticket-resolution screen, and agree a turnaround with the existing article approver so agents can resolve customer issues while an article is reviewed.

**What counts.** Publish one rule: “A resolved ticket counts once when you link a relevant article, write and link an approved article, or record a valid reason no article is needed.” Valid reasons include a one-off account issue with no reusable guidance and a case where documenting the details would expose sensitive information. A missing article on a repeatable issue still needs a write-up. A new article earns credit when it is approved and linked, even if approval comes after the earning window.

Use existing ticket IDs, resolution history, article links, and the resolution reason or note. Attribute credit to the resolving agent. Multiple links, edits, and duplicate tickets for the same issue earn one credit at most. If a ticket reopens, withdraw provisional credit and reassess it at final resolution. An old-way closure with no valid article or exception stays in the denominator and earns zero. Before launch, run those cases and a valid exception through the count. The support operations lead owns the rule and corrects disputed counts from the ticket record before rewards are issued.

**What people see.** At resolution, show the agent whether the ticket counted, is awaiting approval, or needs a correction. Agents see their own totals and can ask for a correction through the existing support-ops channel. Post team totals in the support channel people already use, with article coverage and valid exceptions shown separately. Managers get team totals without individual drill-down. Keep individual results out of performance reviews. Agents who miss a week can pick up with their next ticket, and agents on leave can start their two-week window when they return during rollout.

**Timing and ending.** After two weeks with the fixes and feedback in place, start rewards for half the existing teams. Start the other half two weeks later. Give each team two weeks to earn the reward. During the stagger, compare the change in verified completion on rewarded teams with the change on teams still waiting. Before prizes end, make the knowledge-base step the first option in the resolution screen and keep the valid-exception option available.

Four weeks after the final reward window, use **verified completion rate** to decide what to keep. Estimate it from a random sample of resolved tickets, checking whether each has a relevant article or a valid exception. Take the same measurement after the fixes and before prizes begin. Keep the workflow if the follow-up rate is at least 70%. At 50–69%, fix the most common reason for missed completion and recheck. Below 50%, remove the added resolution prompt and revisit the workflow. End the paid reward on schedule in all three cases. The current 12% article rate is context for adoption and uses a different denominator rule for successful completion because it excludes valid exceptions.

**What could go wrong.** Agents may attach loosely related articles, overuse exceptions, publish thin or sensitive guidance, or delay closure to protect their score or CSAT. The support operations lead samples 10 credited tickets each week in the existing QA queue, including links, new articles, and exceptions. If fewer than 9 pass, pause new reward accrual, correct affected counts, and resume after a fresh sample passes. Route sensitive content through the existing incident process. Use the existing CSAT and time-to-resolution reports to spot tradeoffs, and remind agents to resolve the customer issue as soon as it is ready. Automate the count from ticket records so the weekly sample and corrections fit within one hour.
