# One working day for a code review

Run a short pilot for the 25 engineers. Give authors a useful decision within one working day, and give reviewers protected time to do the work.

### Make reviews easier first

Ask a few engineers who regularly write and review PRs what causes delays and whether an hour of paid time off would be worth earning. Give everyone 30 minutes of work time to try the new workflow. Put one primary reviewer and one backup on each ready PR using the existing review queue. Protect a 20-minute review slot each working day and put the oldest unanswered requests first. Fix the biggest source of delay before launching the reward, then tell the team what changed in response to their feedback.

### What earns the reward

Publish this rule at launch. “Give a ready PR a useful first review within one working day. Meet your personal goal during the two-week pilot and earn one hour of paid time off.”

A useful review approves the change after checking it, requests specific changes, or explains a concrete blocker and what would let the review proceed. A reasoned decision that the change should be withdrawn or reviewed by a specialist also qualifies. An emoji, a bot response, or “I’ll look later” earns no credit.

Set each reviewer’s goal privately before their two weeks begin. Use their expected review volume, scheduled availability, and case mix. For example, someone expecting five routine assignments might aim for four timely reviews, while someone expecting two complex assignments might aim for one. Adjust the goal for leave or lower incoming volume. A missed case stays missed and the reviewer can earn credit on the next one. Each person earns the reward through their own work. The engineering lead confirms it privately and gives the engineer an hour off during scheduled work time, with review coverage arranged.

### Measurement and visibility

Use the code host’s existing ready, review-request, review submission, and PR history. Start the clock when a ready PR has a requested reviewer. One working day means eight hours on the team’s agreed working calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. A handoff to the backup keeps the original deadline. Credit goes to the engineer who submits the first qualifying review.

Count each PR once. Re-requests, follow-up reviews and reopenings keep the original record. A review dismissed for being invalid loses credit. A substantive review given during a pairing session counts when its decision is recorded on the PR within the deadline. Exclude genuine withdrawals before the deadline. A PR merged without a qualifying review counts as a miss, as does a request still unanswered when its deadline expires.

Give the reviewer a private confirmation when the review is recorded, with their progress toward the goal. Post only the team-wide timely-review rate and its numerator and denominator in the existing team channel each week. Managers get the same aggregate view. Pilot counts stay out of performance reviews. The review rotation owner resolves disputed counts from the PR history and updates the reviewer privately.

### Timing and decision

Give everyone the workflow fixes and private feedback for two weeks before rewards begin. Use the 2.6-day median as context and calculate the baseline timely-review rate from recent PRs using the pilot rule. Split the existing review rotation into two balanced waves. Start the second wave two weeks after the first, so both get a two-week reward window and the first window gives a comparison against engineers using the same workflow without rewards.

Keep the assigned reviewer, backup, protected time, and oldest-first queue in place as the default when rewards end. Three weeks after the last reward window, check the **percentage of eligible PRs that received a qualifying first review within one working day**, including unanswered PRs whose deadlines elapsed. Agree on this decision rule before launch. At **80% or higher**, keep the workflow and retire the reward. Below 80% but above baseline, adjust the workflow at the main bottleneck. At or below baseline, drop the pilot setup and revisit what is causing the delay.

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamping or vague blockers.** Before launch, test the rule on duplicate requests, a dismissed approval, and a review done in pairing. Each week, the rotation owner samples five credited reviews, or all of them if there are fewer. If two lack a substantive decision, the engineering lead pauses new reward credit, checks the disputed cases, and fixes the rule before restarting.
- **Easy PRs get picked first.** Assign from the existing queue, keep the original deadline on handoff, and review overdue complex PRs at the weekly team check-in.
- **The pilot feels like surveillance.** Use existing PR records, keep personal progress private, and skip ranks and streaks. Budget the rotation owner 45 minutes a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.
