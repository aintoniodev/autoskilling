# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**First, make it fit the work.** Ask six agents across shifts and queues to walk through tickets where finding or writing an article is slow, risky, or pointless. Give agents 30 minutes of paid practice and protected time to draft articles during their shifts. Fix the biggest workflow blockers and show agents what changed. Keep the existing CSAT bonus terms. Agents should resolve and reply to customers on time, then finish any knowledge-base work during their shift.

**Reward five good resolutions in two weeks.** For each unique resolved ticket, count one completion when the agent links a relevant, current article, submits a usable new article through the existing review workflow, or records a valid “no article needed” reason in the existing resolution workflow. A one-off account correction may qualify for that exception. An unsuccessful search for an article on a recurring issue does not. Give agents in lower-volume queues a smaller goal based on their normal caseload. Each agent who meets their goal earns one paid hour off, announced at launch and handed over in person by the programme lead. Agents choose when to take it within normal coverage arrangements. The reward depends on their own verified work.

**Use the records the tools already keep.** Match ticket IDs to saved article links, draft submissions, or resolution reasons. Before launch, test that a duplicate link counts once, a withdrawn link or reopened ticket loses credit until the ticket qualifies again, and a ticket resolved the old way stays in the denominator. If the existing records cannot verify a completion path, fix the workflow before offering credit for it. Use the same rules throughout the pilot. Recalculate the baseline after the fixes, since the current 12% measures links and articles only.

**Show progress privately.** After a qualifying ticket, tell the agent in their existing workflow what counted and how far they are from their goal. Post team totals in the usual support channel. Managers see only team totals, and the data stays out of performance reviews. Offer a private route to the support ops lead for count corrections. Agents can make up a missed week on their next cases, with the window extended for planned leave.

**Stagger the reward and set the end date at launch.** Give all teams the workflow fixes, practice, and private feedback for two baseline weeks. Put article search and suggestions in the resolution workflow for everyone, with an easy way to record a valid exception. Then start the two-week reward window two weeks apart across three team cohorts. During each rollout, compare the change in adoption with teams still on feedback alone, where available. End prizes when the last cohort finishes and leave the workflow defaults and feedback in place.

**Watch for shortcuts and slower service.** Each week, the support ops lead samples ten credited tickets across links, drafts, and exceptions. Check for irrelevant links, duplicate or thin drafts, and overuse of “no article needed.” If two or more fail, the lead pauses new awards for the affected team, corrects counts privately, and resumes awards after a fresh sample passes. Review existing team CSAT and time-to-resolution reports for signs that agents are delaying customer replies or avoiding difficult tickets to earn credit. If service worsens alongside the pilot, pause the affected rollout and review those tickets before resuming. Use one existing report and the ten-ticket sample to keep routine administration under an hour a week.

**Decide four weeks after the final prize.** Use the share of all resolved tickets with a valid knowledge-base disposition, adjusted using the quality sample. Set the decision rule at launch. Keep the workflow if adoption is at least 15 percentage points above the post-fix baseline, revise it if the gain is 5 to under 15 points, and drop the programme if the gain is under 5 points. Review the link, draft, and exception mix before calling a gain real. Use the staggered comparison to judge whether the paid hour added enough adoption to repeat it in a future rollout.
