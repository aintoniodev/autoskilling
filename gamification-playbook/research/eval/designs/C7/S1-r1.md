# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Give technicians one hour of paid time off for completing a personal goal of verified pre-job safety checks.** Run the reward for two weeks per crew. A documented safety hold earns the same credit as a completed checklist that clears the job to proceed.

### Before launch

Ask union stewards and a small group of technicians from different shifts and routes to walk through a job on paid time. Find out where the app costs time or fails, especially at sites with poor signal. Confirm that paid time off is a reward technicians want and agree with the union on how it will be scheduled. Fix the reported blockers, show crews what changed, and give everyone paid practice time. Make the checklist the first action on the mobile work order and prefill the job details. Keep hazard answers blank.

### What earns credit

Tell technicians at launch: “Complete the safety checklist before starting the job, or record a safety hold before work begins, and earn one credit for that job.”

Set each technician’s goal from their expected dispatch volume and mix of jobs over the next two weeks. For example, set four verified checks for someone expected to get six eligible jobs and two for someone expected to get three. Adjust for leave, reassignment, and low dispatch volume. Credits carry forward within the pilot, and the next eligible job is always another chance. Each technician earns the hour independently. A supervisor confirms the award in person and arranges the paid time privately.

### What gets counted and shown

Use existing work-order IDs, checklist or hold records, and start timestamps. Count one valid result per dispatched job that reaches the pre-job safety decision. A duplicate or a resumed job earns one credit total. A voided submission loses its credit unless a valid pre-start record remains. A checklist completed after work began, or an undocumented bypass, earns zero. Exclude dispatch cancellations that never reached the safety decision. An approved paper fallback during an app outage earns personal credit if the existing record verifies timing, and it stays in the denominator when measuring **digital** adoption. Test these cases with dispatch and a steward before launch. If existing records cannot verify timing, fix that gap before starting rewards.

After sync, show technicians a private confirmation such as “Job 1842 counted. Two of four toward your paid hour.” Post crew-level digital completion rates in the existing weekly briefing. Managers see crew totals only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews and give technicians a private way to dispute a count with the pilot coordinator.

### Timing and decision

Give all crews two weeks with the fixes, paid practice, and feedback. Then start the two-week reward for half the crews and start the rest two weeks later. Assign the order by crew, balancing for baseline adoption and job mix. Compare the groups during the first reward window to estimate what the incentive added.

End rewards on schedule and keep the checklist first in the app. Four weeks after the final reward window, use the prior two weeks’ **share of eligible jobs with a valid digital check or hold recorded before work began** to decide what to retain. Recalculate the reported 35% baseline using that same definition. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow and routine feedback. At **50–69%**, fix the largest remaining barrier and remeasure. **Below 50%**, drop the incentive from future rollouts and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The safety checklist requirement continues in every case.

### What could go wrong

Technicians may rush answers or feel pressure to clear a job. Each week, the safety lead reviews 10 existing records, including holds and offline jobs when available, against the work orders. If **two or more** lack evidence for a required safety check, or any sampled record shows work proceeded despite an unresolved hold, the safety lead pauses new reward awards and investigates with a steward. The coordinator corrects counts and tells affected technicians privately. Review the sample, crew totals, and disputes in one weekly session budgeted at under an hour.
