## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward one accurate use of the kiosk.** Each nurse earns 30 minutes of paid release time after walking a ward educator through one real entry from their own shift. The nurse explains which hand-hygiene opportunity it represents, confirms the entry reflects what happened, and shows how to correct it. An accurately recorded missed action qualifies if the kiosk supports that status. The educator checks the entry and records only that the nurse earned the reward. Each nurse can earn it once, on any eligible shift in their two-week window. The educator confirms it on the spot and arranges cover so the nurse can take the time.

**Make it easy before launch.** Give day, night and float nurses ten minutes of protected time to try the kiosk and identify queues, access problems and interruptions to care. Fix those barriers and post what changed at the shift huddle. Set the kiosk to open directly to the entry screen, with ward and shift prefilled and the clinical fields blank. Agree with Infection Prevention on when a deferred entry is acceptable. During urgent care or an outage, nurses follow the hospital’s existing workflow and can qualify on their next eligible shift. Before launch, test a duplicate, a correction, one action that meets two indications, and an outage. One opportunity gets one record, and a corrected entry replaces the original.

**Measure adoption and clinical safety.** During the existing monthly audits, Infection Prevention checks what share of observed opportunities can be matched unambiguously to an accurate kiosk entry using the ward, time window and moment type already available in the records. Set the matching window before launch, include permitted deferred entries, and report ambiguous matches separately. Use the same sampling approach across wards and shifts before, during and after the pilot. Continue reporting clinical compliance as observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. An educator’s reward check verifies kiosk use, while the independent audit checks what happened during care. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**Show private feedback and ward progress.** The kiosk confirms each save and offers a correction path. Nurses see their own entries and reward status. Managers see ward-level results and sample sizes at the existing huddle. Educators handle disputed credit privately. Individual records stay out of performance reviews, and the pilot collects no patient identifiers or new staff tracking data.

**Run it once, then check what lasts.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback first. Start the two-week reward windows two weeks apart. Use the stagger to see whether uptake changes when the reward starts, treating three wards and monthly audits as a limited comparison. End rewards after the final window and retain the simplified kiosk and feedback. At the first routine audit at least four weeks later, use accurate capture of observed opportunities as the decision metric. Keep the workflow at 80% or above, revise it at 60–79%, and retire the kiosk pilot below 60%. Infection Prevention sets the minimum usable audit sample before launch and waits for the next routine audit if it falls short.

**Pause for harm or gaming.** Extra entries earn zero extra credit. If logging delays care, or if audited compliance drops five percentage points from that ward’s baseline on a usable sample, the Infection Prevention lead pauses rewards on that ward and investigates. The ward educator corrects disputed credit, and the kiosk owner fixes workflow or counting defects. Keep the weekly administration to one aggregate report and have educators verify milestones during existing protected time.
