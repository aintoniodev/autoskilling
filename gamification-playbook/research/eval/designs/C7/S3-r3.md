## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward one accurate entry during paid onboarding.** Each nurse earns one extra 20-minute paid break with arranged cover after walking a ward educator through a kiosk entry for a hand-hygiene moment from their normal work. The nurse cleans their hands at the point of care and uses the kiosk when patient care allows. The educator checks that the nurse selects the right moment and saves it once, then signs off through the existing training record. Announce the break at launch and have the educator confirm it in person. One entry earns the full reward. Extra entries, a perfect record, and coworkers’ results have no bearing on it. Nurses who miss their window can use their next staffed shift.

**Make the kiosk easy to use first.** Walk through the workflow with day, night, and float nurses. Fix the problems they report, such as a kiosk that requires a detour or a sign-in that times out, and post the fixes in the ward huddle. Give everyone protected practice time before rewards start. Infection Prevention and the ward educators should test a duplicate, a correction, an outage, and a case where urgent care prevents logging. A corrected entry counts once. If care comes first or the kiosk is down, the nurse can complete sign-off on the next suitable case.

**Measure adoption and safety.** Use existing authenticated kiosk records and staffing totals, if available, to calculate the weekly percentage of eligible nurse-shifts with at least one saved entry. Count each shift once and exclude training entries, deleted entries, and shifts when the kiosk was unavailable. The measure shows how widely the kiosk is used. Infection Prevention continues the independent monthly observation audit and reports observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities, with both counts and comparable day and night sampling. During existing rounds, the educator checks a small sample of de-identified kiosk records for duplicate and correction handling. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/infection-prevention-control/hand-hygiene/monitoring-tools)

**Show nurses what they need.** The kiosk immediately confirms the moment saved and provides a way to correct it. Nurses can see their own sign-off and request a correction from the educator. The ward huddle shows its weekly adoption rate and latest observer result. Managers receive ward totals, with small groups suppressed. Keep individual kiosk records out of manager dashboards and performance reviews. If measuring adoption would require new individual tracking, use a brief anonymous end-of-shift poll on whether nurses logged at least one moment and report the response rate alongside it.

**Stagger the reward and end it on schedule.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback first, then collect four weeks of baseline data. Randomly assign the wards to two-week reward windows starting in weeks 5, 7, and 9. Before each window ends, put the kiosk entry screen first after sign-in and retain the quick-save workflow. At the first monthly audit at least four weeks after the final reward window, check whether each ward’s four-week adoption rate has retained at least 10 percentage points above its baseline, or reached 85%, whichever target is lower. For wards already above 85% at baseline, require them to maintain their baseline. Keep the workflow where adoption holds and the safety check passes. Where adoption fades, review the remaining workflow friction before deciding whether to revise or drop kiosk logging.

**Pause for safety.** Infection Prevention pauses new rewards on a ward if its observed compliance falls five percentage points below baseline at a comparable monthly audit, or immediately if staff report that logging delayed care or displaced hand hygiene. Review the audit counts and shift mix before attributing a change to the pilot. The educator handles disputed sign-offs and kiosk corrections. With three wards, use the staggered rollout to see whether uptake changes around reward starts, and treat the pattern as directional. The ward educator owns the weekly review, capped at one hour outside onboarding and existing audit work.
