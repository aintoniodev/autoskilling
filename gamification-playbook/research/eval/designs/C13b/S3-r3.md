## Three-ward hand hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene confirmed by the existing monthly audit.** Each ward earns one hospital-funded team meal if its pooled audit result over two months reaches a target agreed before launch with Infection Prevention. Set the target at five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, or at the hospital’s existing target if the ward is already meeting it. The nursing director announces the meal at launch and thanks the team in person when it earns it. Offer the meal across shifts, with clinical coverage. Each ward can earn it independently, and staff who miss a week remain eligible.

**Count each observed opportunity once.** The audit rate is completed hand hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. One action can cover several simultaneous indications and still counts once. A correctly performed action counts toward the reward even if the nurse uses the old reporting route or the kiosk is down. A duplicate entry, a deleted entry, or a kiosk entry with no observed action adds nothing to the reward. Confirm those cases with the auditors before launch. Keep the usual audit schedule and shift mix as consistent as possible. Publish the sample size with each rate because observations cover only a portion of care and staff may behave differently when observed. [WHO hand hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Make the kiosk easy to use before the reward starts.** Ask day- and night-shift nurses on each ward to walk through a real shift and identify where logging would interrupt care. Give them 15 minutes of paid practice. Fix access and location problems, prefill the ward, and make the kiosk the first option wherever nurses already go to report. Keep the existing fallback for outages. Show nurses what changed because of their feedback. After an entry, show a simple confirmation such as “Entry saved,” with an option to correct it. At the existing ward huddle, show the ward’s audit result, sample size and progress toward the meal. Managers get the same ward-level view. Keep names, individual rankings and performance reviews out of the programme.

**Measure adoption separately.** Within the existing monthly audits, use short observation windows where the auditor can cover the same area and nursing activity as the kiosk records. Compare the anonymous kiosk count with the independently observed count of completed actions for each window. The adoption measure is the share of comparable windows where those counts are within 10% of one another. Include windows across shifts and record outages separately. Agreement is a check on reporting coverage and accuracy. It cannot verify individual entries or establish ward-wide compliance. If the team cannot align the scope of the counts, mark that sample unusable and fix the measurement before making a rollout decision.

**Stagger the reward and end it on schedule.** Give all three wards four weeks of the improved kiosk and ordinary feedback first. Randomly assign the order in which the wards start their eight-week reward periods, four weeks apart. This gives a rough comparison with wards using the same kiosk and feedback while waiting for their reward. Keep the kiosk default and feedback in place after each ward’s final meal decision. At the first monthly audit at least four weeks after rewards end, use the same adoption measure to decide the kiosk’s future. Set the pilot thresholds up front. If at least 80% of comparable windows meet the agreement rule, keep the workflow. At 60–79%, fix the reporting friction and recheck. Below 60%, drop the current workflow and retain the existing reporting route. Infection Prevention agrees the minimum sample before launch and defers the decision when coverage falls short.

**Give Infection Prevention the authority to pause.** Pause the reward if a ward’s observed compliance drops five percentage points below baseline, or immediately if a credible report links kiosk use to delayed care. Review large count gaps for duplicate entries, backfilling, missed shifts or outages. Nurses should clean their hands at the point of care and log when safe. The ward educator takes correction requests, the kiosk owner fixes reporting errors, and Infection Prevention resolves disputed audit counts and decides when to resume. Use the existing huddle and audit report so one coordinator can run the programme in under an hour a week.
