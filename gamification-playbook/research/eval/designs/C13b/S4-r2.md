# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets.** Offer everyone on a team two hours of paid time off if at least 60% of its eligible tickets earn credit during its four-week reward window and the quality check passes. Announce the reward at launch. Have the support lead thank the team in person with a concrete example of an article that helped resolve a case, and schedule the time off with queue coverage. The existing CSAT bonus continues under its current formula.

## What earns credit

**Tell agents:** “A resolved ticket earns one credit when it links to a published article that helped solve the issue, or to an article you wrote for the case that passes KB review and is published.”

Use the ticket system’s resolution record and the KB’s published article ID and approval record. A draft shows as pending and earns credit when approved. Give reviewers two business days and allow a two-business-day review window after rewards end so late-window drafts can qualify. Repeat links and multiple articles still earn one credit per ticket. A reopened ticket loses provisional credit until it closes again with a valid link. A ticket closed through the old workflow stays in the denominator and can earn credit under the same rule. Ask agents to reuse a relevant article before drafting one, and have reviewers merge duplicate drafts into the existing article.

Count all resolved tickets except categories agreed before launch where attaching or writing a reusable article is inappropriate, such as spam or privacy-restricted cases. Use existing ticket dispositions for exclusions and show the excluded share next to team coverage so a shift in classification is visible. A genuine gap in the KB stays eligible. The support operations lead owns the count and resolves disputes privately using the ticket and KB records.

## What agents see

Give agents immediate, private confirmation in the ticket workflow that credit was counted, is pending review, or needs a fix. Give each agent a private goal of three in five eligible tickets, expressed against their own volume. A missed week simply means picking up with the next case. Show each team its own aggregate progress toward 60% in the queue dashboard. Managers see team totals only. Keep personal counts out of performance reviews.

## Run and ending

Spend two weeks with agents from different queues testing the workflow on real cases during paid time. Use their feedback to fix the friction, such as slow search or an awkward draft handoff, and tell agents what changed. Put article search in the resolution screen, suggest relevant published articles where possible, and prefill drafts with safe ticket context. Give every team the improved workflow and private feedback before starting rewards. Start half the teams first and the rest two weeks later, pairing teams with similar baseline coverage and ticket mix. Compare coverage during the two weeks when only the first group has rewards to estimate what the prize adds.

End rewards after four weeks per team. Keep the resolution-screen defaults and private feedback in place. Four weeks after each team’s reward window ends, use **the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article over the preceding four weeks** to decide what to do next. Keep the workflow at 55% or higher, revise it and recheck at 30–54%, and drop the incentive design below 30% while investigating what still makes the workflow hard to use. Calculate the pre-pilot baseline with the same eligibility rule so it can be compared with the reported 12%.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles.** Fold ten randomly selected credited tickets into the existing weekly QA review. Check that the article helped resolve the case, is accurate, and contains no customer-sensitive details. If two of ten fail, or any exposes sensitive data, the support operations lead pauses rewards for the affected team, corrects the counts, and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Before launch, test counting on a duplicate, a reopened ticket, an old-workflow close, and a pending draft.
- **Slower support or pressure on CSAT.** Review team CSAT, response volume, and reopen rates alongside coverage. A five-point CSAT drop versus the pre-pilot baseline with at least 30 responses in a rolling two-week window triggers an investigation and a pause if the workflow is contributing. Keep authorized exceptions available in the resolution screen so agents can handle sensitive cases immediately.
- **Uneven opportunity.** Check coverage and exclusions by queue during the pilot. If a queue has few usable articles, fix its biggest content gaps and give agents paid drafting time. Use the existing dashboard and QA review so one support operations lead can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
