# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward.** Each team earns one hour of paid time off per person when it reaches 40% of resolved tickets with a qualifying knowledge-base article over its four-week reward window. Everyone on the team shares the reward, including people who were on leave. Announce it at launch and have the support lead award it at a team huddle. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules in place.

**What counts.** Publish one rule: “A resolved ticket earns one credit when it has a relevant, approved article attached in the ticketing system.” An existing article must already be published. A new article counts once it passes the normal knowledge-base review and is published. Use existing ticket and article records to verify both. Count each original ticket once, even if it has several links, is merged, or is reopened and resolved again. A reopened ticket loses provisional credit until it is resolved again. A removed link or rejected draft earns no credit. Tickets handled the old way stay in the denominator. Give agents a clear skip option when documenting a case would expose sensitive information or when there is no reusable guidance. Track skips in aggregate and keep them in the denominator so the adoption rate stays comparable.

**Make the workflow worth using.** Before launch, sit with agents from different queues and shifts to see where search, linking, or drafting slows them down. Give each agent 30 minutes of protected practice. Fix the biggest barriers and show agents what changed in response to their feedback. Put article search and linking directly in the resolution flow, surface likely matches, and provide a short draft template that leaves out customer details. Keep the skip option available so agents can resolve urgent or sensitive tickets promptly.

**What people see.** At resolution, show the agent which article counted or whether a draft is pending review. Give agents a private progress view with a target of four qualifying tickets per ten resolutions, measured across the full window. A missed week carries no penalty. Post team progress toward 40% in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews and leaderboards. Agents can flag a wrong count to the pilot owner, who checks the ticket and has the ticketing admin correct it.

**Timing and ending.** Give everyone the workflow fixes and private feedback first. Measure a two-week baseline using the same counting rule and reconcile it with the reported 12%. Randomly assign comparable teams, grouped by queue mix and baseline adoption, to start their four-week reward windows two weeks apart. Compare adoption during the overlap when only the early teams can earn the reward. End rewards on the announced dates and keep article search in the resolution flow. Four weeks after the final reward window, use the qualifying share of resolved tickets over the preceding four weeks to decide what to do. At **40% or higher**, keep the workflow as configured. At **25% to under 40%**, fix the largest remaining barrier and remeasure. **Below 25%**, drop the universal prompting approach and focus it on queues where articles reliably help.

**What could go wrong.** Agents may attach generic articles, rush replies to hit both incentives, or clog the review queue with thin drafts. Before launch, test counting against a duplicate, a reopen, a removed link, a rejected draft, and a ticket resolved without an article. Each week, the pilot owner samples ten credited tickets across teams for relevance, accuracy, and privacy. If fewer than nine pass, pause reward qualification for the affected team or workflow. The support lead decides when to resume after the knowledge-base owner fixes the issue and a fresh sample passes. Review existing team-level CSAT and resolution time alongside adoption to spot customer harm or extra work. Use the existing QA routine and an automated team tally to keep weekly administration under an hour.
