# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Start with paid time and a better workflow.** Have the field safety lead and a union steward walk through the checklist with technicians from different shifts, including crews with poor signal. Use paid time to try it on real job types. Fix the delays they find, test the approved fallback, and show crews what changed before rewards begin. Put the checklist first on the mobile job screen and prefill job details. Keep hazard answers blank for technicians to complete.

**Reward a completed pre-job assessment.** Announce the rule at launch: “Your crew earns one hour of paid release time per technician when 75% of its eligible jobs have a valid digital safety checklist completed before work starts.” Agree the reward with technicians and the union before announcing it. The field operations lead thanks qualifying crews in person and schedules the time with coverage. Everyone on a qualifying crew gets it, including people on leave during the pilot. A valid assessment that puts a job on safety hold counts toward the milestone.

**Count once per job.** Match the saved checklist to an existing dispatch job ID. Use the app’s completion time and the existing job-start record, or the recorded safety hold if the job never starts. The app must validate required answers and record a disposition before awarding credit.

- Duplicate submissions earn one credit. If a checklist is voided, remove its credit. A replacement counts only if it was valid before work started.
- For offline jobs, use the original completion time if the app preserves it reliably. Show credit as pending until sync. Where timing cannot be verified, technicians use the approved safety fallback.
- A job completed using the fallback stays in the adoption denominator. A cancellation before any site assessment is due is excluded. Agree the eligible job types and counting rules with the union before launch.

Confirm those checks against existing records before launching the reward. Test a duplicate, a void, an offline sync, a safety hold, and a job done using the fallback. Resolve timing gaps with the existing workflow before promising credit.

**Show technicians their own progress.** After submission, show “Checklist counted for this job” or explain why it is pending or needs correction. Give each technician a private goal of four in five eligible jobs, using their actual job volume. A missed week simply means picking up with the next job. Post crew progress toward 75% in the existing weekly safety huddle. Managers receive crew totals only. Agree with the union that programme data stays out of performance reviews. Use aggregate reporting for groups large enough to protect individual privacy.

**Run a ten-week pilot.** Give all crews the fixes, paid practice, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Assign comparable crews to two start dates, by lottery where practical. The first group earns rewards in weeks 3–6 and the second in weeks 7–10. Compare the groups in weeks 3–6 to see what the reward adds to the shared improvements. Each group gets four weeks to qualify. Keep the checklist first in the app after both reward windows close. Pay out earned time and close the reward display.

Four weeks after the final reward window, use one decision measure: the percentage of eligible jobs with a valid digital checklist completed before work starts. **Keep** the workflow at 70% or higher, **change** the workflow and retest at 50–69%, and **drop** the incentive design below 50% while retaining the required safety assessment. Compare the result with the roughly 35% starting rate.

**Watch for rushed answers, hidden hazards, and bad counts.** The safety lead reviews five credited jobs a week against existing job records, focusing on whether the assessment preceded work and whether the recorded action matched the hazard. One confirmed case of a credited checklist missing a critical hazard or allowing work to begin despite a required hold pauses rewards. The safety lead makes that call, reviews the cause with the union steward, and resumes rewards after verifying the fix. The field operations coordinator corrects disputed counts and tells the technician the result privately. Review the sample, pending counts, and crew totals together in one weekly block of under an hour.
