## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Before launch.** The safety lead and union stewards meet with technicians from different shifts, job types and coverage areas on paid time. Ask them to walk through a real first job of the day and show where the app slows them down or makes it harder to stop work. Put the checklist first on the dispatched job, prefill job details, leave safety answers blank, and test offline submission and the existing safety fallback. Give every crew paid practice time and publish what changed because of technician feedback. Agree with the union in writing on the reward, data access, appeals and pilot end date before scoring starts.

**What earns credit.** “Complete the in-app safety check before work starts, or use it to record a hazard and put the job on hold, and earn one credit for that visit.” The assigned checklist owner gets credit for the crew’s check. The app must accept a complete, job-specific submission and record either clearance to proceed or a hold routed to dispatch. Both outcomes earn equal credit. Injury and near-miss reports have no bearing on eligibility. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safeandsound/safety-and-health-programs/worker-participation)

**How it’s counted.** Match the accepted checklist to the existing dispatch visit ID and compare its timestamp with the existing job-start or hold record. Before launch, verify that these records can establish the order independently of the checklist. If they can’t, fix the workflow before offering rewards. Use a verified offline submission time even if it syncs later. Count each visit once, remove credit for a voided check, and reject backfilled checks. A visit cancelled before arrival is outside the denominator. A visit stopped on site stays in. During a confirmed outage, use the existing fallback and excuse that visit from the technician’s reward calculation. Keep it visible in the programme’s digital adoption rate. Dry-run duplicates, voids, late sync, paper fallback and stop-work cases with stewards before launch.

**What people see and earn.** Announce at launch that technicians who complete valid checks on at least **80% of their eligible visits** during their six-week reward window earn **one hour of paid time off**, subject to the union agreement. Use each technician’s actual assigned volume, with no minimum visit count. A missed week has no separate penalty, and the next visit counts as usual. The app confirms each accepted check immediately and shows technicians their own progress, including pending offline submissions. Share team totals and rates in the existing weekly huddle, rolling small groups into a larger total. Managers get aggregate programme data only. Individual scores stay out of performance reviews and discipline. At the closing huddle, a steward and the safety lead thank crews for specific examples of good safety decisions and confirm earned time privately.

**What could go wrong.** The safety coordinator spends up to 45 minutes a week sampling 10 records across crews, including holds when available, using existing job records to look for generic answers, missed controls and unresolved hazards. If **two or more of 10** have a material defect, pause scoring for the affected cohort while the safety lead and steward investigate. Act immediately on any unsafe release or pressure to proceed or suppress a report. Technicians can raise count errors or pressure privately through their steward. The safety lead decides count appeals with the steward, and the app administrator makes corrections. Schedule paid checklist time into dispatch so the incentive doesn’t turn into pressure to rush.

**Run and end.** Give all crews two weeks with the fixes and private feedback, then start six-week reward windows by crew, two weeks apart. Compare early and waiting crews during the overlap to estimate what the reward adds. End each window as announced and keep the checklist first in the job flow, with the same feedback and fallback. Four weeks after each cohort’s rewards end, calculate the share of all eligible visits with a valid pre-job digital check or documented digital hold, using the same definition to verify the reported 35% baseline. **At 80% or above**, keep the workflow and retire the incentive. **At 60–79%**, fix the remaining barriers and recheck. **Below 60%**, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians. The pre-job safety requirement applies throughout.
