## One working day for a first substantive review

Give each review queue a paid half-day off if at least **80% of its eligible pull requests receive a substantive first review within eight working hours** during its four-week reward window. Everyone in the queue’s rotation earns it, including people who were on leave. Announce the half-day at launch, recognize the team at its next regular meeting after it earns it, and stagger the time off so review coverage holds.

### Make reviews easier first

In weeks 1–2, ask engineers who frequently author or review PRs where reviews stall. Give each queue a rotating primary reviewer and backup, with two protected 20-minute review blocks each working day. Put the oldest requests first in the existing review view and route overdue requests to the backup. Use a protected team session to agree what a substantive review looks like and walk through a few real examples. Tell the team which blockers they raised and what changed before rewards start.

### Count one outcome per PR

The launch rule is **“A PR counts once when someone other than its author submits a substantive review within eight working hours of its first review request after it becomes ready.”**

Use timestamps and review events already recorded by the code host. Working hours follow the team’s published schedule and exclude weekends and team holidays. A substantive review is an approval after examining the change or a submitted review that identifies a concrete concern or asks a question the author can act on. Acknowledgments, bot reviews, and self-reviews earn no credit.

Count all eligible requests whose deadlines fall in the window, including requests still unanswered at their deadline. Reassignment, re-requesting, and moving a PR back to draft preserve the original clock. Multiple reviews earn one credit. A review withdrawn before the deadline needs a qualifying replacement. A Slack conversation counts when the reviewer records its substantive outcome on the PR within the deadline. Before launch, check those cases against real PR history, including a PR opened just before a holiday.

### Show people what they need to act

The existing review submission gives immediate confirmation on the PR. Engineers can see their own due requests and results in a private view, with a personal target of answering four out of their next five eligible requests on time. People with low volume can work toward that target over several weeks. A missed week carries no penalty.

Post the team’s on-time percentage, denominator, and milestone progress in its usual engineering channel once a week. Managers see team totals only. Keep individual results out of performance reviews and skip the leaderboard.

### Run it for eight weeks, then check whether it stuck

Assuming two existing code-area queues with distinct rotations, start rewards for one in weeks 3–6 and the other in weeks 5–8. Both get the workflow fixes and feedback from week 1. Compare the queues during weeks 3–4 for an early read on what the reward adds, alongside each queue’s pre-launch on-time rate. The current 2.6-day median gives context. Calculate the baseline percentage from existing PR history using the new rule.

Rewards end after week 8. Keep the oldest-first view, backup routing, and protected review time in place. Use the **team-wide on-time percentage in weeks 9–12** to decide what happens next. At **80% or above**, keep the workflow as the default. At **65–79%**, adjust coverage where requests are stalling. Below **65%**, drop the target in its current form and revisit reviewer capacity with the team.

### Watch for rubber stamps and uneven load

Each week, the engineering lead samples up to five credited PRs across the queues for signs that reviews engaged with the change. **Two quality failures in the sample pause reward credit for the affected queue.** The lead checks disputed cases with a peer reviewer, corrects wrong counts before the award, and resumes credit after a clean five-review sample. If reviews routinely spill past the protected blocks, the lead rebalances product work or reviewer coverage. Budget 45 minutes a week for the sample, count corrections, and team update.
