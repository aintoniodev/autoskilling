## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward verified hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, delivered across shifts, with the nursing director thanking staff in person for a specific improvement. Announce the reward at launch. The rule is simple. “Your ward earns a team meal when a routine monthly audit meets its hand-hygiene target.” A kiosk entry is a self-report, so it carries no points. The hospital is testing whether the award adds anything to kiosk adoption once the kiosk is easy to use.

**Fix the workflow first.** Give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk. Ask where logging adds a trip, creates a queue or interrupts care. Fix the main barriers on all three wards before the baseline month, and post what staff reported and what changed. Set the kiosk to open directly to the ward’s logging screen, with an immediate confirmation and an undo option. Nurses clean their hands at the required moment and log afterwards when care allows. During an outage, use the approved fallback and flag the affected period in reporting.

**Measure care and adoption.** Infection Prevention calculates ward compliance as correctly performed hand-hygiene actions divided by eligible opportunities in the existing monthly audit. Count each opportunity once, including correctly performed hygiene that went unlogged or was documented through the fallback. Set the reward target at five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Before launch, Infection Prevention sets a minimum audit sample and shift coverage. An audit that misses either requirement cannot trigger a payout.

For adoption, use the existing audit sample where the observer can naturally see both the completed hygiene action and its kiosk entry. Tally only ward-level counts. The adoption measure is the percentage of those completed actions with one valid kiosk entry. Keep the same sampling approach across months and report how much of the routine sample could be checked this way. If the layout makes that sample too small or skewed, treat kiosk totals as usage only and drop the reward as an adoption tactic. Monthly compliance audits alone cannot validate individual entries. [SHEA, IDSA and APIC hand-hygiene guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)

**Show progress without ranking people.** The kiosk says “Entry saved” and lets the nurse correct it. Post the ward’s latest audit result, target and sample size at the existing shift huddle. Managers see ward totals only. Collect no patient identifiers or new staff identifiers for the pilot, and keep pilot data out of performance reviews. Everyone on the ward shares the reward, including staff who were on leave. There are no individual quotas or streaks.

**Run two reward months per ward.** Month 1 gives all three wards the fixes, feedback and a baseline audit. Randomly assign the wards to start rewards in months 2, 3 and 4. Each gets two monthly audits to earn the reward, with a follow-up audit one month after its reward window closes. Keep the kiosk defaults and feedback in place throughout. Use wards still waiting for rewards as a rough comparison. Three wards provide a pilot signal, with limited ability to isolate the reward’s effect.

**Set the decision before launch.** Use a proposed adoption target of 10 percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%, or maintenance of baseline if it already exceeds 95%. If at least two wards meet it after rewards stop and compliance holds, keep the kiosk workflow and end the prizes. If adoption clears the target only during rewards, fix the remaining logging friction before retesting. If adoption never improves or cannot be verified, drop rewards for kiosk adoption.

**Pause if care or data quality slips.** Infection Prevention pauses payouts if audited compliance drops five percentage points below baseline or staff report that logging delayed care. Keep routine audit sampling spread across shifts to reduce the risk of rewarding behaviour seen mainly when observers are present. Before launch, test a duplicate tap, an undone entry and a correctly performed action recorded through the fallback. A duplicate adds no credit, an undo removes the entry, and the fallback still counts toward audited compliance. One ward champion reviews exceptions and posts updates in under an hour a week. Infection Prevention resolves audit disputes and decides when payouts can resume. The kiosk owner fixes entry or counting errors.
