## Pre-job safety checklist programme

**Reward a crew for reaching 75% verified pre-job digital checklist completion over four weeks.** Each technician in a qualifying crew earns one paid hour they can schedule with dispatch, including across shifts. Agree the reward and privacy rules with union representatives and technicians before launch. Name the reward at kickoff, and have the service lead present it at a crew huddle with specific thanks for what the crew did well. Tie the reward to the crew’s checklist completion rate. Keep job speed, job volume and incident counts out of it.

### Fix the workflow first

Give technicians from several shifts, including low-connectivity routes, paid time to try the checklist on real jobs. Ask where it slows them down or creates risk. Fix access, offline saving and duplicate entry before starting the reward. Pre-fill job details and put the checklist first when a technician opens a job. Show crews what changed in response to their feedback. Keep the existing safety procedure available during outages and preserve technicians’ authority to stop work.

### Count one verified outcome per job

Use this rule at launch: **A job counts once when its completed digital checklist is recorded before the job starts, including when the checklist leads to a safety hold.**

Join the app’s checklist record to the existing dispatch job ID and use an existing, independently recorded work-start event to establish timing. Verify that this event reflects actual starts before launch. If the available records cannot establish timing reliably, delay the reward until the company can verify it with existing records. A technician’s own “started” tap alone is insufficient. For an offline checklist, use its original completion time if the app preserves it reliably through sync.

Use all eligible jobs where work started as the denominator. Exclude cancelled jobs and report confirmed app outages separately under an agreed rule. A completed checklist that leads to a safety hold counts as a successful outcome once the hold is recorded in the existing workflow. Include that job once in the denominator too, even if work never starts. A paper checklist completed before work protects the technician under the existing procedure and earns zero digital credit.

Before launch, test a duplicate submission, a checklist voided before work starts, an offline submission synced late, a paper checklist, and a job stopped for safety. The programme coordinator corrects disputed counts from existing records, with a union steward available to help technicians challenge a result.

### Show progress where crews already work

After submission, the app confirms the specific job and shows whether verification is complete or pending sync. Technicians privately see their own verified jobs and a goal sized to their workload, such as three of their next four eligible jobs. After leave or a missed week, they resume with the next eligible job. Share crew totals at the usual weekly huddle. Managers get crew totals only, with small groups combined where individual results could be inferred. Keep programme scores out of performance reviews and use existing records for verification.

### Run it, then remove the reward

Roll out the fixes and private feedback to all crews for two weeks. Then start the four-week reward window for half the crews, balanced by baseline completion and job mix. Start the other half two weeks later. Compare the crews’ completion rates during the two weeks when only the first group can earn the reward. Freeze each result after the same short sync window and grant the paid hour at the next huddle.

The app keeps the checklist first after prizes end. Use the **verified pre-job digital completion rate** to decide what to keep. Measure it over the four weeks after the last reward window. At **75% or above**, keep the workflow and feedback and retire the prize. At **60–74%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck. **Below 60%**, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and the union.

### Catch problems early

Backdating, rushed box-ticking and pressure to start unsafe work can all inflate the score. Each week, the safety lead uses existing records to spot-check 10 counted jobs across crews for timing, job-specific answers and proper handling of flagged hazards. **One confirmed safety-critical miss or two material failures in the sample pauses reward sign-off.** The safety lead decides when to resume after the coordinator corrects counts and the underlying issue is addressed. Reporting a hazard or stopping work never triggers a penalty. Automate the tally from existing records and use the regular huddle so one coordinator can run the programme in under an hour a week.
