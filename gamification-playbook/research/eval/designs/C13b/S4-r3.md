**Reward one useful article on one resolved ticket.** Agents earn one credit when they resolve a real customer ticket and attach an approved knowledge-base article that helps solve the issue. A new article counts once it passes the normal editorial review and is linked back to the ticket. One ticket earns at most one credit, even if it has several links or is reopened and closed again. Reopening removes provisional credit until the ticket is resolved again with a useful article. Duplicate and merged tickets count under the surviving ticket. A ticket resolved outside the new workflow still counts in the denominator and can earn credit if it meets the same rule. Use existing ticket and article records to calculate credit. A self-entered checkbox earns nothing.

**Fix the workflow before launch.** Ask six agents across shifts to walk through recent tickets and show where searching, linking or drafting costs them time. Give every agent 30 minutes of paid practice. Fix the biggest blockers and tell the team what changed. Put article search and suggested links in the close screen. If there’s no suitable article or the case contains sensitive information, agents can close the ticket without a link and flag a knowledge gap through the existing review queue. Keep article approval out of the customer’s resolution path. Give agents scheduled time to write articles about recurring gaps.

**Show progress where agents already work.** At closure, show the agent which article earned provisional credit or whether a draft is awaiting approval. Give each agent a private count and a personal goal of linking a useful article on half their own eligible resolutions over the four-week reward window. A missed week creates no penalty or catch-up quota. Post the team’s weekly rate in its usual huddle channel. Managers see team totals only, with no individual ranking or drill-down. Keep knowledge-base credit out of performance reviews and CSAT pay.

**Put the prize in the launch message.** “If your team links a useful article on at least half its eligible resolutions during its four-week window and passes the quality check, everyone on the team gets a paid hour off.” Include agents who were on leave and let people schedule the hour around queue coverage. Announce the earned reward in the team huddle and recognize a specific example of an article that helped a customer.

**Check gaming and customer impact.** Before launch, have the support QA lead run the rule against five real edge cases: a merged duplicate, a reopened case, a ticket closed in the old workflow, a pending draft and a sensitive case. Define eligible resolutions using existing ticket types and privacy rules. A missing article stays in the denominator. Each week, use the existing QA review to spot-check five credited tickets across the programme for relevance, accuracy and appropriate visibility. Two failures in the latest ten checks pause awards. One exposure of sensitive information pauses awards immediately. The QA lead decides when to resume, and Support Operations corrects wrong counts from the source records. Watch existing team CSAT and resolution-time reports for signs that agents are delaying closure or forcing links. The support lead can pause a wave to investigate. Support Operations runs the weekly tally, audit and huddle update in under an hour using existing reports.

**End the prize on schedule.** Give all 60 agents the workflow fixes and private feedback in weeks 1–2. Start four-week reward windows in weeks 3, 5 and 7 for three groups of existing teams. Compare each group’s change with groups still waiting to start to see whether the prize adds anything beyond the fixes and feedback. Before each window ends, make article search the default in the close screen and keep it easy to dismiss. Four weeks after each group’s prize ends, use one number to decide what to keep: the share of eligible resolutions with a valid article, using the launch denominator and the same QA checks. At **40% or higher**, keep the workflow and private feedback. At **25–39%**, fix the biggest remaining obstacle and recheck in four weeks. Below **25%**, retire the reward programme and revisit the workflow with agents. Recalculate the reported **12% baseline** using the same eligibility rule before launch.
