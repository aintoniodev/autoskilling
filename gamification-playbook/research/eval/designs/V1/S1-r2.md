### 1. Diagnosis

The reported baseline is about 35% of jobs with a completed digital checklist. Before launch, reconcile a sample against dispatch records to confirm how many checklists were completed before work started. The reason for low adoption is unknown. The programme targets technicians who can use the app on a real job and have a reason to try it.

Walk two jobs with technicians and a union representative. On a supported job, time the app and the approved paper process through completion, including any duplicate entry. On a blocked job, such as a site with no signal, verify whether offline completion is reliable and whether the paper fallback works. Dispatch handles immediate fallback. The field operations lead owns blockers and responds within one business day. Fix persistent access, time or rework problems before launch. If most jobs are blocked, defer the programme.

### 2. Credit rule

When dispatch marks a job **On site**, the designated technician earns one credit for submitting the required digital safety checklist before work begins. The checklist must include required hazard responses and any escalation required at that point. A checklist that leads to a documented stop-work decision earns the same credit. Dispatch assigns one checklist owner per job requiring a checklist.

The primary measure is credited jobs divided by all dispatched jobs that reached the checklist cue and were independently classified as app-supported. Report blocked jobs, approved fallback use and observed pre-job checklist completion across all jobs alongside it.

- **Normal job** → One credit for a qualified pre-start submission.
- **Duplicate or edit** → One credit per job. An edit after work starts cannot make an incomplete checklist qualify.
- **Reversal or recall** → Remove credit if the submission is invalidated, preserve the audit history and route the correction to the technician.
- **Late submission or reschedule** → A reschedule preserves the original history. Use the actual work-start time or documented stop-work time to determine eligibility.
- **Paper fallback or old process** → Proceed under the approved safety procedure. Record paper completion separately. An otherwise supported job stays in the digital denominator.
- **Omitted case** → Reconcile against dispatch so jobs with no app record remain in the denominator. Verify claimed blockers independently.

**Launch assumption:** Dispatch and the app can supply trustworthy job IDs, event times and edit history, including reliable timestamps for offline submissions. Resolve gaps before launch.

### 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation, in the dispatch job card** — “On site? Open the safety checklist before you start. It saves to this job for you. If the app is unavailable, use the approved paper checklist before starting and tell dispatch. The pilot ends 21 February 2027.”
- **Confirmation** — “Checklist saved for Job 4821 before work started. That’s 3 of your 4 supported jobs this week.” If syncing is pending, show “Saved on this device. Your progress will update after sync.”
- **Progress** — “3 of 4 supported jobs completed in the app this week. Open the checklist when you arrive at your next job.” Use actual opportunities to date, including weeks with one job. For zero, show “No supported jobs this week.”
- **Restart** — “Start with your next job. Open the checklist when you arrive.”

### 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own job-level progress. Crews and managers see aggregate weekly progress against each crew’s baseline, with names and breakdowns suppressed wherever a person could be identified. Before launch, agree the data-use rules with union representatives. Limit individual access to the designated reviewer for corrections, disputes or coaching the technician requests. Log access, expire it when the case closes and bar use of programme data for discipline.

### 5. Add-on

Use immediate confirmation and private progress as the incentive. These give technicians a visible sense of completion while keeping the focus on the safety check itself.

### 6. Operator card

The field operations lead reconciles dispatch and app events weekly and accepts corrections through dispatch using the job ID. An independent safety lead samples 20 jobs weekly across pilot and comparison crews, including app, paper and missing records. Verify timing, substantive hazard responses and required escalation against available job records. Pause programme credit and feedback if two sampled checks are materially deficient or any sampled job bypassed a required stop-work action. Investigate with the union representative and resume after verifying the fix. Existing safety procedures continue. Budget two staff-hours weekly for routine operation.

### 7. End and decision plan

**Proposed dates:** Run 11 January through 21 February 2027. Follow up through 4 April. Randomly assign comparable crews after measuring a four-week baseline. Both groups receive the same workflow fixes and safety support. Pilot crews also receive the invitation and progress feedback. At close, retire pilot messaging and weekly goal counts. Keep workflow fixes and ordinary submission receipts in both groups.

Scale if the pilot improves digital completion by at least 10 percentage points more than the comparison group during the pilot and retains at least 5 points of that advantage during follow-up, with no deterioration in overall pre-job completion or sampled quality. Change and retest if gains fade or access explains the gap. Stop if there is no meaningful lift or safety quality worsens.

### 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamping or backdating** makes adoption look better while hazards go unchecked.
- **Perceived surveillance** undermines trust and discourages candid reporting.
- **Poor signal or extra admin** makes paper faster, and the lift disappears when prompts end.
