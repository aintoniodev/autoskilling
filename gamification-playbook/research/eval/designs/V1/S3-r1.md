## 1. Diagnosis

**Unconfirmed.** Test whether nurses lack access, find logging too disruptive, or simply need a cue. Before launch, shadow a nurse from patient-room exit through hand hygiene and kiosk submission. Time the walk, queue and entry. Repeat on a night shift with an unavailable kiosk. In both cases, hand hygiene and patient care take priority. If logging would interrupt care, skip it and flag the barrier at handover. The nursing informatics lead triages barriers within one working day. Defer launch if most eligible moments lack a practical logging route or if logging adds work with no benefit nurses can identify.

## 2. Credit rule and test cases

Pilot one cue on all three wards. After an eligible hand-hygiene action at patient-room exit, the nurse records the completed action at the next safe chance, by shift end. One valid record earns one tick toward a **private practice goal**. The tick acknowledges documentation. Infection Prevention measures clinical compliance separately.

**Launch assumptions.** The hospital approves kiosk placement, the workflow and the data use. The kiosk can tie entries to a stable, privacy-approved event reference and retain correction history without collecting patient identifiers. Test both capabilities before enabling progress counts. If either fails, run a workflow trial with simple submission confirmations.

- **Normal:** One completed action with a valid entry earns one tick.
- **Duplicate:** A retry or second entry for the same event leaves one tick.
- **Reversal:** A withdrawn or erroneous entry loses its tick and retains a correction record.
- **Late or edited:** Use the recorded event time and original shift boundary. A late correction can fix the record without adding pilot credit.
- **Old way:** Hand hygiene recorded only through the existing process can count in the observer audit. It earns zero kiosk ticks.
- **Omitted:** An observed completed action with no matching kiosk entry stays in the documentation denominator.

## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation, on the existing room-exit workflow:** “After cleaning your hands, log this exit at the kiosk when care allows. You’ll see whether your entry saved and can correct it yourself. The pilot ends 30 April 2027.”
- **Confirmation:** “Room-exit entry saved. 1 of 2 on your practice goal this week.”
- **Progress:** “1 of 2 this week. Log your next eligible exit when care allows.” Use a goal of two for usual-volume weeks and one for low-volume weeks. Suppress the goal for leave or weeks with no eligible opportunities. Nurses can adjust it privately.
- **Restart:** “Start with your next eligible exit.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries and progress after signing in. Staff and managers see each ward’s audited documentation coverage against its own baseline. Suppress breakdowns with fewer than 10 contributing nurses or where subtraction could identify someone. Only a designated data steward can open individual records for a requested correction or dispute, with logged, time-limited access. Programme data cannot be reused for discipline.

## 5. Add-on

None. Private feedback and ward progress are the incentive. Raw entry counts carry no stakes.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner and effort.** The nursing informatics lead owns kiosk access and the private “Correct an entry” route. Budget 30 minutes weekly per ward for reconciliation, issues and corrections, plus two hours monthly for Infection Prevention to match and review audit records. Confirm capacity before launch.

**Measurement.** In the existing monthly audits, sample comparable day, night and weekend windows across phases. Documentation coverage is the percentage of independently observed, completed, in-scope actions with one valid kiosk match. Clinical compliance is observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Use approved event references for matching and report ambiguous matches separately. [WHO hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Pause.** Infection Prevention pauses prompts and progress if any ward’s audited clinical compliance falls 5 percentage points below baseline, more than 10% of sampled kiosk entries are unmatched or ambiguous, or any care delay or privacy incident is reported. Investigate while routine hygiene and audits continue. Resume after Infection Prevention clears the fix.

## 7. End and decision

**January–February 2027:** Kiosk, training and neutral receipts on all three wards establish the baseline. **March–April:** Add prompts, private goals and ward progress. **May–June:** Remove those pilot features. Keep the kiosk, workflow fixes and neutral receipts.

Expand only if each ward sustains at least a 10-point coverage gain over baseline in May–June, passes the quality checks, and shows no clinical compliance decline beyond 5 points. Infection Prevention sets minimum audit denominators before launch. If sampling is insufficient or gains fade with safety intact, revise and retest. Stop for unresolved harm or continued lack of worker benefit. The before-and-after comparison supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Most likely failure modes

- **Logging displaces care.** Track queues and deferrals, especially on nights.
- **Entries rise while hygiene slips.** Review independent compliance and match quality before counting gains.
- **Progress feels like surveillance.** Enforce access limits and keep individual records out of manager reports.
