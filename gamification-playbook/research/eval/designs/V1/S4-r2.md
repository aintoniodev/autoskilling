## 1. Diagnosis

The 60-person support org records a knowledge-base link or article on 12% of resolved tickets. Before launch, the Support Ops lead walks two real tickets with agents and checks the examples below. The 12% figure establishes recorded usage. The walkthroughs establish what is getting in the way.

- **Supported case.** An agent resolves a password-reset ticket, finds the approved article, attaches it in the ticket tool, and closes the ticket. Time the lookup and attachment, and check whether the agent had to enter anything twice.
- **Blocked case.** An agent resolves an account-specific issue whose details cannot safely go into a reusable article. The agent closes the ticket on time and records the blocker privately. Support Ops owns blocker triage by the next business day.

Run the pilot if agents can complete the supported workflow with little friction and the main gap is that they overlook it or haven’t tried it. Fix access, search, or authoring friction first if it dominates. Stop the launch if most cases are blocked.

## 2. Credit rule and test cases

When a support agent resolves a ticket, they earn **one personal credit** if the ticket has a relevant, approved article attached, or a new article that passes normal editorial review and is attached within seven calendar days of resolution. Drafts show as pending. Reviewers can approve one reusable article for multiple genuinely matching tickets. Customer responses and resolution proceed on their usual timetable.

The primary measure is qualified tickets divided by **all resolved tickets**, including blocked and omitted cases. Report blocked cases and qualified coverage among eligible tickets alongside it, using predefined blocker reasons that Support Ops verifies. Recalculate the historical 12% using the same rule if the records allow.

- **Normal.** An approved article matches the resolution and is attached at close. One credit.
- **Duplicate.** Multiple links, repeated saves, or duplicate ticket records for the same case earn one credit for the canonical ticket.
- **Reversal.** Reopening voids credit until final resolution. A recalled or invalid article removes credit unless a valid replacement qualifies within the original window.
- **Late or edited.** Use the recorded resolution timestamp for the seven-day window. Editing a due date has no effect. Late links can still help future agents.
- **Old workflow.** A macro or copied article URL qualifies only if the ticket also contains the tracked, relevant article attachment.
- **Omitted.** A resolved ticket with no qualifying attachment stays in the denominator and earns zero.

## 3. What agents see

- **Invitation at resolution.** “Found the fix? Attach the article here so the next agent can reuse it. If there’s a reusable fix we haven’t documented, save a draft and resolve the ticket as usual. The pilot ends February 7.”
- **Confirmation.** “Article linked. This ticket counts toward your weekly goal.” For a draft, show “Draft saved. Credit appears after approval and attachment.”
- **Progress.** “3 of 5 eligible tickets documented. Start with your next resolution.” The weekly goal is half of verified eligible tickets, rounded up and adjusted to actual volume. With one eligible ticket, the goal is one. With zero, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart.** “New week. Start with your next eligible ticket.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own counts and can inspect their ticket history. Everyone sees weekly team coverage against that team’s baseline, without names. Suppress breakdowns with fewer than five contributing agents or where someone could still be identified. Managers receive aggregates. A designated Support Ops reviewer may open individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log access, expire it when the case closes, and bar disciplinary reuse.

## 5. Add-on

Use private progress and shared team feedback. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility unchanged. Knowledge-base credit has no effect on pay.

## 6. Operator card

The Support Ops lead reconciles ticket resolution and attachment history with knowledge-base approval and recall history. An independent QA reviewer samples 20 credited tickets weekly, or all if fewer, and checks relevance, accuracy, and confidentiality. At **three failures in 20**, **over 10% failures in a smaller sample**, or **one confidentiality breach**, the lead pauses new credits and team updates, corrects affected records, and requires a passing recheck before restarting. Agents dispute missing credit or eligibility through a private Support Ops queue. Budget two staff-hours weekly, excluding normal editorial review. Confirm event history, review capacity, and privacy approval before launch.

## 7. End and decision

Pilot January 11–February 7, 2027. If comparable teams exist, randomly assign teams to pilot or the same workflow support without programme feedback. Otherwise predeclare a four-week baseline and treat results as feasibility evidence. End the goal prompts and team display February 7. Keep workflow fixes and personal attachment confirmation for everyone. Measure through March 7 and allow seven more days for approvals.

Scale if follow-up coverage improves by at least 15 percentage points versus the comparison after baseline adjustment, QA failures are at most 10%, and CSAT falls by less than two points on a 100-point scale. Revise if coverage improves but misses the target. Stop if lift disappears or quality or CSAT breaches its limit.

## 8. Main risks

- Agents attach generic articles to earn credit. Independent QA checks whether the article matches the actual fix.
- Documentation delays responses and jeopardizes CSAT pay. Close tickets on time and track approval backlog.
- Coverage fades when prompts end. Use the four-week follow-up to decide whether to scale.
