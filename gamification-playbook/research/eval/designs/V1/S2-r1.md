## 1. Diagnosis

The reported median is 2.6 days. Recalculate it in working hours before launch and measure the share answered within one working day. The working hypothesis is that ready PRs sit because ownership is unclear or review time gets crowded out. Engineers’ skepticism favors a short trial with private feedback and protected review time.

Before launch, the engineering lead traces two real PRs with their reviewers. For a supported PR, follow the ready event, assignment, notification, and substantive review. For a blocked PR, check what happened when the reviewer was unavailable or lacked access. Give the review rota owner responsibility for finding coverage or escalating access within two staffed hours. If most cases lack a viable review path, fix coverage before launching.

## 2. Credit and measurement

When a teammate’s PR first becomes ready for human review, the assigned engineer earns **one credit per PR** by submitting a substantive review within eight staffed hours on the published team calendar. A substantive review is an approval that says what was checked or a change request that identifies a concrete issue. The rota backup can take ownership and earn the same single credit. The clock stays anchored to the first ready event.

The team metric is the percentage of eligible PRs with a qualified first review by the deadline, including PRs with no reviewer or response. Track late qualified reviews separately. Assume the repository records ready, assignment, review, dismissal, and closure events. Verify that history before launch.

**Rule tests**

- **Normal.** Ready Monday at 15:00 on a 09:00–17:00 calendar. A qualifying review by Tuesday at 15:00 earns one credit.
- **Duplicate.** A second reviewer submits or the first reviewer edits their review. The PR still earns one credit.
- **Recall.** A reviewer withdraws a review submitted in error. Remove its credit and use the next valid review’s original timestamp.
- **Late or edited.** Reassignment, a due-date edit, or a ready-to-draft toggle leaves the original deadline in place. A review qualified after the deadline is late.
- **Existing workflow.** A substantive review submitted through the usual repository interface counts automatically. A chat reply alone leaves the PR unanswered.
- **Omitted.** Reconcile the report against all ready PRs. Restore any missing PR with its original deadline. Exclude only verified cancellations before the deadline and audit those weekly.

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at assignment.** “PR #482 is ready. Please review by Tuesday at 15:00. Use your review slot to give the author a clear next step. If you’re unavailable or missing access, hand it to the rota backup here. The trial ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation.** “Your review of #482 counted. 2 of your 3 assigned reviews met the one-working-day target this week.”
- **Progress.** “Aim to answer your next assigned PR within one working day. Next up is #491, due at 14:00 tomorrow.” Show the count even with one opportunity. With zero, show “No reviews assigned this week.”
- **Restart.** “Start with your next assigned PR. #507 is due tomorrow at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The team and managers see the same weekly on-time rate against baseline, with no names. Suppress aggregates with fewer than five contributing reviewers or where an individual is identifiable. Only the named operator can open individual programme records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log access, expire it when the case closes, and exclude programme records from performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Incentive

Protect a daily review slot and give prompt, private confirmation of completed work. Use the team’s own baseline as the shared milestone. Skip prizes, rankings, badges, and streaks.

## 6. Operator card

The rota owner runs one weekly reconciliation from repository events and handles corrections in a private channel. Budget **60 minutes per week**, including review by a rotating senior engineer who did not author or review the sampled PRs. Sample 10 credited reviews weekly, or all if fewer. Check for evidence of a real review and missed material issues. If two sampled reviews fail or one misses a critical issue, the engineering lead pauses credit and progress reporting, investigates, and reruns the check before resuming. Routine reviews and backup coverage continue.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 11–February 5, 2027 as baseline with the rota and protected time in place. Run the programme February 8–March 5. End the shared milestone on March 5 and retain private confirmations, coverage, and review slots. Measure March 8–April 2 using the same rules and wait for the last deadlines to mature.

Roll out if both trial and follow-up hit **at least 80% on time and 15 percentage points above baseline**, with no unresolved quality pause. Change coverage and retest if results improve but miss either threshold. End the programme if follow-up returns to baseline or quality problems recur. The baseline comparison supports a rollout decision with limited causal confidence.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamping.** Fast approvals can conceal poor review. Use the independent sample and pause rule.
- **Hidden misses.** Unassigned or repeatedly redrafted PRs can disappear from reports. Reconcile from the original ready event.
- **Skepticism or workload imbalance.** Engineers may feel watched or absorb disproportionate review work. Keep personal feedback private and use the rota to distribute coverage.
