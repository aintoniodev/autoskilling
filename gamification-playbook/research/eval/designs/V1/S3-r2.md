## 1. Diagnosis

Test whether nurses skip the kiosk because it is easy to miss, hard to access, or adds work during care. Before launch, the ward charge nurse and infection prevention lead walk two cases on each ward, including a night shift. In a routine case, the nurse performs hand hygiene, completes care, then logs at the next safe kiosk visit. In a blocked case, such as an emergency or kiosk outage, the nurse follows usual hand-hygiene practice and skips the entry. The charge nurse triages barriers that shift and escalates access or placement issues to IT or Facilities within one working day. If most shifts lack a safe, practical chance to log, fix access before launching.

**Assumptions to verify** include a kiosk location approved by infection prevention, private sign-in, a reliable shift ID and correction history, and approval for limited audit matching without patient identifiers.

## 2. Credit and measurement

A nurse earns one private milestone credit per worked shift by saving the first valid entry for a hand-hygiene action already performed at an eligible moment. The cue is the first safe kiosk visit after care. The entry records the moment and action time within 15 minutes and before shift end. Use the 15-minute window only if the walkthrough confirms it fits routine care. One action can cover several indications. Further genuine actions can still be logged.

**Credit rule test**

- **Routine entry** earns one shift credit. A retry or second entry earns zero extra credit.
- **Duplicate or withdrawn entry** is removed from the count. If another valid entry exists that shift, the credit remains.
- **Late entry or timestamp edit** earns credit only if the original submission met the window and the corrected record is valid. Retain the edit history.
- **Hygiene performed without a kiosk entry** can count in the clinical audit. It earns zero kiosk credit.
- **Omitted hygiene action** earns zero credit and remains a missed opportunity if observed.

Measure **logging adoption** as the share of independently observed, completed actions on supported shifts that have an unambiguous, timely kiosk match. Keep unmatched actions in the denominator and report blocked opportunities separately. Measure **clinical compliance** through the existing monthly audit as actions performed divided by opportunities observed. ## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation at sign-in** — “After care, use your next safe kiosk visit to record a hand-hygiene action you just completed. You can check that it saved and correct it yourself. Skip the kiosk whenever care needs you. The pilot ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation** — “Entry saved. You’ve logged your first hand-hygiene action this shift.”
- **Progress** — “2 of 3 shifts with a safe chance to log this week. On your next eligible shift, log one completed action.” Display this only if eligible shifts can be identified reliably. Otherwise show “First entry this shift · 0 of 1” until saved. Shifts without a safe opportunity carry no target.
- **Restart** — “Start with your next safe opportunity.”

## 4. Visibility and reward

The reward is immediate private confirmation and progress toward a reachable goal. Staff and managers see their ward’s verified logging rate against its own baseline, with denominators and no names. Suppress cells with fewer than 10 contributing nurses or where subtraction could identify someone. A designated reviewer may access individual records for a specific correction or staff-requested coaching, with logged access that expires when the case closes. Pilot records stay out of performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Operator card

The infection prevention lead owns the pilot. Each month, trained observers sample comparable day, night, and weekend windows across all three wards. They record actions before seeing kiosk data. An authorized reviewer matches records using a temporary nurse code, ward, moment, and time. Delete the linkage after reconciliation. Nurses submit corrections privately to the charge nurse. Budget 30 minutes per ward per week for pilot administration, plus existing audit time.

Pause prompts and milestones on a ward if observed compliance falls at least five percentage points below baseline or a credible report links kiosk use to delayed care or missed hygiene. Infection prevention investigates before restarting. If audit matching is unreliable, withhold verified progress and resolve the measurement gap.

## 6. End and decision

**Proposed 2027 dates.** January–February is the kiosk run-in with ordinary support. March–April adds the invitation and milestones. May–June removes the prompts and goals while keeping basic receipts, workflow fixes, and monthly audits.

In July, expand if each ward’s follow-up logging rate holds at least 10 percentage points above its run-in baseline or reaches 90% while maintaining baseline, clinical compliance stays within five points of baseline, and safety issues are resolved. Require at least 50 observed completed actions per ward per phase for the adoption decision. Extend measurement if samples are sparse, revise if the lift fades, and stop if safety problems persist. Treat the comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 7. Main risks

- **The kiosk interrupts care.** Make safe deferral explicit and act on the first credible report.
- **Logs rise while hygiene slips.** Keep clinical compliance independent and enforce the pause rule.
- **Staff feel watched or audited shifts look unusually good.** Limit access, vary observation windows, and check whether gains persist after the pilot.
