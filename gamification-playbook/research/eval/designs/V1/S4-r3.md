## 1. Diagnosis

**Working hypothesis: agents haven’t tried the tool enough to see a benefit.** The reported 12% adoption rate doesn’t tell us whether agents lack access, can’t find a relevant article, or skip the step because it slows them down. Before launch, the Support Operations lead should shadow one ticket with a usable article from search to resolution, then one ticket with no article and a blocked publishing path. Record added time, rework and the reason for skipping. The KB lead owns access and publishing blockers and responds within one business day. Agents can always resolve the ticket through the usual workflow. If most cases are blocked, or supported cases routinely take longer without a clear benefit to agents, fix the workflow before launching.

## 2. Credit rule and case test

When a support agent resolves a ticket with a reusable answer, they earn **one credit per ticket** for linking a current article that covers the resolution or getting a new article published and linked. A draft earns credit only once it is published and linked. Agents resolve the ticket on time even if approval is pending. The headline measure is the share of **all unique resolved tickets** with a qualifying link within seven days of resolution. Also report the share among eligible tickets. Approved exceptions, such as account-specific actions with no reusable answer, stay visible in the headline denominator. Missing classifications stay in the eligible denominator until reviewed.

- **Normal and duplicate.** One relevant link earns one credit. Extra links, copied articles and a second close of the same ticket earn no additional credit.
- **Reversal.** If the ticket reopens or the article is recalled, mark the credit pending. Restore it once the ticket is resolved and the link is valid within the original seven-day window.
- **Late or edited.** Use timestamped ticket and KB history. An edited date cannot extend the window. A link added after seven days remains useful to the team and earns no pilot credit.
- **Old way or omitted.** A ticket closed without a link stays in the denominator with zero credit. Reconcile against the full resolved-ticket export so missing programme events are counted.

## 3. What agents see

- **At resolution:** “Can this answer help on another ticket? Link the article you used so you and your teammates can find the answer next time. If it’s missing, submit it for review. Resolve the ticket on time either way. The pilot ends March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked to this resolution. 2 of 4 toward your goal this week.” For a draft: “Article submitted for review. You’ll get credit once it’s published and linked.”
- **Progress:** “2 of 4 this week. On your next reusable resolution, search for an existing article first.” Set the weekly target at half the agent’s typical eligible volume, rounded up and capped at five. Lower it if actual opportunities fall short. With zero opportunities, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart:** “A new week starts with your next eligible ticket. Link the answer if it’s already in the KB.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own progress and their pilot group’s verified adoption rate against its baseline. Managers see aggregates, with groups under five suppressed. The Support Operations lead can open an individual record for a requested correction or dispute. Log access and close it when the case is resolved. Explain at launch that programme records are excluded from discipline and performance reviews. The existing CSAT bonus continues under its current rules.

## 5. Recognition

A QA reviewer sends each agent one private thank-you after their first sampled contribution passes review. Name the answer they made easier to find. Recognize useful work once, regardless of ticket volume.

## 6. Operator card

Support Operations reconciles ticket and KB event histories weekly and handles corrections through the existing support channel within two business days. An independent QA reviewer samples 20 pilot tickets weekly, including credited tickets, exceptions and tickets without links. Check whether links actually support the resolution and whether exceptions are justified. If more than 10% of sampled credited links fail, the Support Operations lead pauses recognition, corrects credits and fixes the cause before resuming. Budget two staff-hours weekly. **Assumptions:** timestamped histories, a QA reviewer and timely publication are available. With unreliable history, run a measurement-only pilot.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 11–February 7, 2027 as baseline. Run February 8–March 7. Randomly assign existing support teams to the programme or to the same KB access, training and blocker support. End recognition March 7. Keep private feedback and workflow fixes through the March 8–April 4 follow-up.

Scale if the pilot’s baseline-adjusted gain over the comparison group is at least **10 percentage points** during the pilot and **5 points** in follow-up, QA failures stay at or below 10%, and CSAT falls by no more than 2 points relative to comparison. Check CSAT response counts before deciding. Change and retest if adoption fades or evidence is inconclusive. Stop if quality or CSAT breaches its threshold and stays there after correction.

## 8. Main risks

1. Agents link generic articles for credit. Audit relevance and correct credits.
2. Agents delay closure to protect credit or their CSAT bonus. Keep publication asynchronous and review resolution times weekly.
3. Adoption fades after recognition ends. Use the full four-week follow-up to decide whether to scale.
