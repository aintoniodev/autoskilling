## 1. Diagnosis

Agents currently link or write a knowledge-base article on 12% of resolved tickets. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the Support Ops lead shadows two resolutions: one where an agent can find and link a useful article, and one where access, a knowledge gap, or sensitive content blocks the action. Compare time and rework with the current workflow. Fix access and search friction first. The Knowledge Base lead owns blockers and responds within one business day. If most cases are blocked or the new workflow adds material effort without a clear benefit to agents, defer the pilot. Agents can always close a ticket on time.

## 2. Credit rule and test

At resolution, the assigned agent earns **one credit per eligible ticket** by linking a relevant, accurate, approved article or submitting a reusable article that passes editorial review and is linked to the ticket. Reuse an existing article when it fits. Drafts stay pending until approval. A second article earns no extra credit.

Measure **qualified coverage** as credited tickets divided by all eligible resolved tickets, using the ticketing system’s resolution log as the denominator. Tickets requiring a new article stay eligible. Predefine exclusions for cases such as spam and resolutions that cannot safely be documented, and audit them. Recalculate the 12% baseline with the same rule before comparing results.

- **Normal:** An agent links the approved article that covers the fix → one credit.
- **Duplicate:** Two links, repeat saves, or merged ticket IDs → one credit for the canonical ticket.
- **Reversal:** Ticket reopens or its article is withdrawn → remove credit and restore it only after a qualifying resolution.
- **Late edit:** Due-date edits never move a ticket between weeks. Allow five business days after resolution for verification or editorial approval, then lock the cohort.
- **Old way:** Agent closes an eligible ticket without a qualifying article → zero credit, still in the denominator.
- **Omission:** A resolved ticket is missing from the dashboard feed → reconcile against the resolution log and include it, even if it earned zero.

## 3. What agents see

- **Invitation at resolution:** “Before you close, link the article that explains the fix so you and the next agent can reuse it. If there’s a gap, send a short draft for review. Close on time either way. The pilot ends 28 February. Your CSAT bonus stays the same.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked to ticket #4821. One ticket counted.” For drafts: “Draft sent for review. We’ll count the ticket once the article is approved and linked.”
- **Progress:** “3 of 4 toward your goal this week. On your next eligible ticket, link the article that explains the fix.” Set the weekly goal at one-third of each agent’s typical eligible volume, rounded up. Agents with one or two eligible tickets can aim for one. With zero, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart:** “New week. Start with your next eligible ticket.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own ticket credit and corrections. Teams and managers see verified team coverage against the team’s baseline, with no names. Suppress views with fewer than five contributing agents or where filters could expose someone. Support Ops can access individual records for a correction, dispute, or agent-requested coaching. Access is logged, expires when the case closes, and is barred from disciplinary use.

## 5. Reward

Private progress and shared team progress are the incentive. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula intact. Pilot credits have no cash value and do not feed performance reviews.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner:** Support Ops reconciles resolution, link, and editorial logs weekly, corrects credits through the existing support channel within two business days, and publishes verified aggregates. Allow about three staff hours weekly for reconciliation and sampling, plus editorial time.

**Quality:** A QA lead independent of the pilot reviews 20 randomly sampled credited tickets each week for relevance, accuracy, and safe content. If three fail, pause the pilot prompts and published counts. Correct affected records and resume after a fresh sample passes. Launch assumes reliable ticket and article history, editorial capacity, and approval for private reporting.

## 7. End and decision

Run **1–28 February 2027**, then follow the same teams **1–28 March** with campaign prompts, goals, and shared progress removed. Retain workflow fixes and private link confirmation. Assign comparable teams to pilot or comparison, with the same training and editorial support. If there are too few comparable teams, use January as a predeclared baseline and treat the result as feasibility evidence.

Scale if qualified coverage improves by at least **10 percentage points** over the comparison team’s change during the pilot and retains at least **5 points** in March, with at least **90%** of sampled links passing and no CSAT decline greater than **2 points** relative to comparison. Use the existing CSAT score and check response counts before deciding. Revise and retest if uptake fades or CSAT data are too sparse. Stop if quality or CSAT misses its threshold.

## 8. What could go wrong

- Agents attach generic articles to earn credit. Sample for whether the article actually explains the resolution.
- Drafting delays closure and puts CSAT bonuses under pressure. Keep on-time closure explicit and watch CSAT by queue.
- Agents feel monitored or adoption falls when the campaign ends. Keep progress private and use March ticket activity to test whether the workflow holds.
