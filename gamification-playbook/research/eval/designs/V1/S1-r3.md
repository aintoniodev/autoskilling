## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently have a digital checklist. Before launch, verify what that figure counts and shadow two jobs with a technician and union steward. On a supported job, walk from dispatch through checklist completion and record how long it takes compared with paper. On a job with poor signal or a failed login, walk through the approved paper fallback and verify that dispatch can reconcile it. Ask technicians what keeps them from using the app.

The pilot targets technicians who can use the app and would benefit from trying it. The field operations lead owns access and workflow blockers, with a one-business-day response target. If most jobs are blocked or digital adds time or rework, fix the workflow before launching.

## 2. Credit rule and measurement

When a job in the dispatch queue is ready to start, the assigned technician earns **one credit per job** by completing every required checklist item in the app before work starts. Any hazard must have the disposition required by the safety procedure. A properly recorded stop-work decision qualifies. Use the approved paper process if the app fails. If the safety check cannot be completed, hold the job.

Measure adoption as **verified digital checks divided by all dispatched jobs that reached the pre-start safety check**, including paper, blocked and missing cases. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. Reconcile against dispatch records and report blocked cases separately so the denominator stays visible.

- **Normal or duplicate:** A qualified pre-start submission earns one credit. Retries, edits and multiple technicians on the same job still yield a maximum of one.
- **Reversal:** If review invalidates a submission, remove the credit, record why and tell the technician privately.
- **Late or rescheduled:** A checklist submitted after actual start earns zero. Editing the scheduled time cannot make it eligible. Delayed sync qualifies only when the original completion time is verifiable.
- **Paper or omitted:** Paper earns zero digital credit and counts as a completed safety check. A dispatch job with no verified checklist stays in the denominator and goes to reconciliation.

## 3. What technicians see

Show the invitation on the job screen at the pre-start cue. Confirm immediately on verified completion. If an offline record needs verification, show “Saved on this device. Credit pending sync.”

- **Invitation:** “Before you start, open Safety Checklist for this job. Save it here so you can pull it up later without finding the paper copy. If the app fails, use the approved paper checklist. The pilot ends February 14.”
- **Confirmation:** “Pre-start safety checklist saved for job 1842. That’s 1 of your 2 digital checks this week.”
- **Progress:** “1 of 2 this week. On your next eligible job, open Safety Checklist before starting.”
- **Restart:** “New week. Start with the safety checklist on your next eligible job.”

Set the weekly goal at half the technician’s expected app-eligible jobs, rounded up and capped at three. Lower it if verified opportunities fall. One opportunity means a goal of one. With zero, show “No app-eligible jobs this week” and skip the goal.

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. Crews see their verified adoption rate against their own baseline, without names or rankings. Suppress figures for groups with fewer than 10 contributing technicians or where a person’s result could be inferred. Managers see aggregates. A designated steward can access individual pilot records for a correction, dispute or technician-requested coaching. Log the access, close it when the case ends and prohibit disciplinary reuse of pilot data. Agree the visibility, access and retention rules with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Reward

Private confirmation and progress toward the weekly goal are the reward. Shared crew progress shows whether the new workflow is taking hold. The pilot carries no prizes or individual rankings.

## 6. Operator card

The field operations lead owns the pilot. Dispatch supplies job IDs and original start records. The app supplies checklist timestamps, submissions and corrections. Before launch, verify those records can support every credit test above.

Each week, an independent safety lead reviews 20 randomly sampled jobs across pilot and comparison crews against existing safety records and checks required hazard dispositions. The safety lead pauses pilot messaging and progress reporting after any verified serious hazard bypass or if at least 2 of 20 sampled jobs lack a valid pre-start check. Required checks and fallbacks continue. Technicians can dispute credit privately through the steward using the job ID. Resolve within two business days. Budget three staff-hours per week for reconciliation, sampling and routine corrections.

## 7. End and decision

Use December 14–January 10 for baseline measurement, January 11–February 14 for the pilot, and February 15–March 21, 2027 for follow-up. Randomly assign comparable crews to the pilot or to the same app support and required safety reminders. At pilot end, remove the campaign invitations, weekly goals and crew display. Keep workflow fixes and checklist receipts.

Scale if the pilot crews’ follow-up gain exceeds the comparison crews’ gain by at least 10 percentage points, with no deterioration in sampled checklist quality or unresolved safety pause. Change and retest for a smaller positive gain with stable quality. Stop for no sustained gain or worse quality.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rushed checks:** Sampling checks for meaningful hazard dispositions.
- **Unequal access:** Paper remains available, and blocked jobs stay visible in reporting.
- **Surveillance concerns:** Union-agreed access limits and small-group suppression protect individual results.
