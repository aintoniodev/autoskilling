# S3 · Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

## 1. Diagnosis

Test whether nurses skip the kiosk because it’s unfamiliar, inaccessible, or too slow to use during care. Before launch, a nurse on each ward walks through one routine case and one blocked case, such as an urgent call or an unavailable kiosk. Launch only if nurses across shifts can reach the kiosk, save an entry in 15 seconds or less, and see how the ward uses the data to fix logging problems. If most opportunities lack a safe logging window, redesign the workflow first. The charge nurse records blockers during the shift. The kiosk owner responds by the next working day.

## 2. What earns credit

After completing hand hygiene at a hospital-defined moment, the nurse records it at the next safe opportunity within the same shift. One completed action earns at most one credit, even when it covers multiple indications. The kiosk confirms a save immediately. Only independently matched entries count toward the ward’s verified result. During urgent care or downtime, follow the hospital’s hand-hygiene procedure and skip the kiosk if a safe logging window never opens.

Before launch, test these cases against the kiosk’s event and edit history:

- **Routine** · A completed action and one matching entry earn one credit.
- **Duplicate** · A retry or second entry for the same action earns one credit total. An ambiguous match earns none pending review.
- **Reversal** · A withdrawn or invalidated entry loses credit. Recalculate totals after a correction.
- **Late or edited** · Use the original save time to enforce the shift cutoff. A same-shift entry can be corrected with an audit trail. Changing its timestamp cannot make it eligible.
- **Existing workflow** · Observed hand hygiene without a kiosk entry counts toward clinical compliance and zero toward kiosk adoption.
- **Omission** · An observed completed action on a supported shift with no entry stays in the adoption denominator. An observed missed action stays in the clinical compliance denominator.

## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation at the hand-hygiene station** · “After hand hygiene, log the moment at the kiosk when care allows. Your entry helps us see where logging fits into the shift and where it gets in the way. The pilot ends March 31.”
- **Confirmation** · “Saved. You can correct this entry from your private history.”
- **Personal progress** · “1 of your 2 practice entries saved this week. Log the next completed moment when care allows.” Start with two entries for nurses expecting at least two supported opportunities, one for nurses expecting one, and no goal for nurses expecting none. Extra entries earn no extra recognition.
- **Restart** · “Start with your next completed moment when care allows.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries and practice progress. Staff and managers see their ward’s verified adoption and observed compliance against its own baseline, with sample sizes and no names or shift-level breakdowns. Suppress results based on fewer than five contributing nurses or any breakdown that could identify someone. Only the designated data steward can access identifiable entries for a requested correction or dispute. Log access, close it when the case is resolved, and prohibit disciplinary reuse. Collect no patient identifiers.

## 5. Recognition

After the pilot, the nursing director sends one ward-wide thank-you to each pilot ward that meets the adoption and safety thresholds below. The note names the verified improvement and a workflow problem staff helped fix. Recognition gives staff credit for making the workflow usable. There are no individual prizes or volume targets.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner** · Infection Prevention lead. Budget 60 minutes a week for reconciliation and issue triage, plus existing monthly observer rounds. Confirm staffing, privacy approval, kiosk access, and reliable edit history before launch.

**Measurement** · During monthly audits, sample days, nights, and weekends using the same method on all three wards. Observers record opportunities and completed actions independently of the kiosk, then the data steward matches actions one-to-one using a temporary nurse code, time, and indication in approved audit windows. Adoption is matched entries divided by observed completed actions with kiosk access. Clinical compliance is observed completed actions divided by observed opportunities, including during downtime. Report downtime separately. Observation can change behaviour, so interpret results as sampled performance. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Correction and pause** · Nurses flag entries privately to the data steward. Infection Prevention pauses prompts and recognition on a ward after one confirmed care delay caused by logging or a five-percentage-point drop in monthly observed compliance versus baseline. Review the cause before restarting.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed 2027 schedule. Use January for a supported kiosk baseline on all three wards. Randomly select two wards for the programme from February 1 through March 31. The third gets the same kiosk training, support, and save confirmation. From April 1 through May 31, remove prompts, goals, and recognition. Keep kiosk fixes, private confirmations, and audits.

In June, scale if each pilot ward improves verified adoption by at least 10 percentage points more than the comparison ward during the pilot, retains at least half that advantage in follow-up, and stays below the safety pause threshold. Revise and retest if uptake fades or samples are too thin to judge. Stop if care delays recur after fixes. Three wards support a local decision.

## 8. Main risks

- **Entries outrun actual hygiene.** Keep rewards tied to observer-verified adoption and the compliance safeguard.
- **Logging competes with care.** Make deferral explicit and track access by shift.
- **Audit visibility distorts results or feels punitive.** Vary observation windows, publish ward aggregates, and restrict identifiable access.
