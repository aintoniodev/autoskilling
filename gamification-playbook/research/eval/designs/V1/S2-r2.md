## 1. Diagnosis

The median review response takes 2.6 days. That establishes a delay. The share answered within one working day and the reasons for the delay are still unknown. Engineers’ scepticism makes a prize or leaderboard a poor starting point.

During the baseline, the engineering lead and a reviewer walk through one recent PR that was ready to review and one that stalled. Check reviewer access, assignment, PR size, and whether engineers have time to review. Give reviewers a protected daily review slot and a backup route. If access or required context is missing, the reviewer flags the blocker and the lead clears it or routes it to the author the same working day. If most cases remain blocked or timely review requires recurring overtime, defer the pilot and fix the workflow.

## 2. What earns credit

The cue is a ready-for-review notification naming the primary reviewer. One private credit goes to the engineer who submits the PR’s **first substantive review within one working day**. A substantive review is an approval after checking the change and relevant tests, or a request for changes that explains the issue and what needs to happen next. A backup can earn the same credit by completing the review.

Start the clock at the first ready-for-review event. Count eight hours within the team’s published working schedule, excluding weekends and holidays. Assignments, reassignments and PR edits share the original deadline. The team metric is **PRs with a qualifying review by the deadline ÷ all in-scope PRs whose deadline has passed**. Declared test PRs and drafts are outside scope. A PR withdrawn before its deadline is excluded only if the author records a reason before any qualifying review. Track withdrawals and blockers alongside the result.

**Rule checks**

- **Normal case** — A timely, substantive review earns one credit and counts toward the team target.
- **Duplicate** — A second review, resubmission or reopened PR earns no additional credit.
- **Reversal** — A dismissed review loses provisional credit if it was invalid when submitted. A later code change leaves a valid earlier review intact.
- **Late or edited deadline** — A late review earns zero credit. Use the original deadline. The lead can correct a documented timestamp or calendar error in the audit log.
- **Old way** — An ordinary review submitted in the PR qualifies. A Slack acknowledgement alone leaves the PR unanswered.
- **Omission** — Reconcile the review queue against repository events. A missing assignment or notification stays in the denominator. If the timestamps cannot be reconstructed reliably, hold the affected results and fix the data before continuing.

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation, in the existing PR notification** — “You’re up to review #482 by Tuesday at 15:00. Use your review slot to check the change and submit your review here. If you’re tied up, ask for backup now so the author can plan their next step. Pilot ends 5 March.”
- **Confirmation** — “Your review of #482 was submitted on time. 2 of 3 toward your goal this week.”
- **Progress** — “Your goal is to answer four of every five review requests on time. This week, 2 of 3 toward your goal. Next up is #491, due Thursday at 11:00.” Calculate the weekly goal from assignments due that week, rounded up. With one assignment the goal is one. With none, show “No reviews due this week.”
- **Restart** — “New week. Start with #491, due Tuesday at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own counts. The team and managers see the weekly percentage against its baseline, with no names or reviewer breakdowns. Suppress small groups and any result that could identify an individual. The engineering lead can access individual records for a requested coaching session, correction or dispute. Log access, close it when the case is resolved, and exclude the records from performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Add-on

Use private confirmation and shared team progress as the incentive. Keep prizes, rankings and badges out of the pilot. The immediate benefit is predictable feedback for authors and a clear queue for reviewers.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead owns repository event reconciliation and private corrections via direct message. A rotating senior engineer independently audits up to 10 randomly sampled timely reviews weekly for evidence of meaningful review. Two failures in a weekly sample trigger a pause in credit and progress reporting while the lead checks the cause. Budget 60–90 minutes of combined staff time weekly, excluding blocker fixes. Assumptions include reliable repository history, a shared working calendar and approval for protected review time.

## 7. End and decision

**2027 schedule.** Baseline with review slots and backup routing from 11 January to 5 February. Add progress feedback from 8 February to 5 March. Keep review support and private confirmations through 2 April, with weekly goals and team progress displays retired on 5 March.

Keep the programme’s useful elements if at least 80% of PRs qualify in both the pilot and follow-up, both are at least 10 percentage points above baseline, and fewer than 10% of sampled reviews fail in each period. If gains fade or coverage is unreliable, change the workflow and retest. Stop the incentive if quality deteriorates or there is no lift. The same-team baseline supports a feasibility decision. It cannot establish causality.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rubber-stamp approvals.** Audit review quality independently and pause when it slips.
- **Avoidance of large or awkward PRs.** Keep unassigned and blocked PRs visible in the denominator.
- **Review load concentrates on fast responders.** Rotate backup duty and use retrospectives to check whether the review slot is sustainable.
