# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently have a digital checklist. That figure alone cannot tell us whether technicians lack access, find the app slower than the existing process, or simply miss the prompt.

Before launch, ride along with a technician on one supported job and one job with poor connectivity. Time both workflows, check for duplicate entry, and verify that dispatch can see a safety hold. The field operations lead owns blockers, with same-shift support and a fix or documented workaround within two business days. Keep the approved paper checklist available. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds material rework, fix the workflow before starting the pilot.

## 2. What earns credit

When a dispatched job reaches the pre-job safety check, the assigned technician earns **one credit per job** by submitting a complete digital checklist before work starts. A checklist that correctly puts the job on safety hold also earns credit, even if the job never starts. Follow the existing stop-work procedure whenever a hazard requires it.

Measure the share of all jobs reaching the safety check that earn verified digital credit. Reconcile against dispatch records, so paper fallbacks and missing checklists remain visible in the denominator. Report paper fallbacks, app blockers and missing records separately.

Test these cases before launch:

- **Normal job** — A complete checklist submitted before the recorded start earns one credit.
- **Duplicate** — A retry, second submission or reassignment cannot create a second credit.
- **Recall or correction** — A voided checklist loses provisional credit. A replacement qualifies only if completed before work starts. A valid safety hold keeps its credit.
- **Late or edited start** — A late submission earns no credit. Editing the scheduled time cannot make it timely.
- **Paper fallback** — Use the approved checklist if the app fails. Record it as a fallback with zero digital credit.
- **Omitted job** — A job with no checklist stays in the denominator with zero credit. A cancellation before the safety check is due is excluded.

**Launch assumptions:** Dispatch can provide stable job IDs and trustworthy start or hold records. Offline submissions have a verifiable completion time. Operations, safety and union representatives agree on the rules and data use before launch.

## 3. What technicians see

- **Invitation in the dispatch job card** — “Before starting this job, open the safety checklist. It saves your completed check against the job. If you find a hazard, follow the stop-work procedure. If the app is unavailable, use the approved paper checklist. The pilot ends April 11.”
- **Confirmation** — “Checklist saved for job 4821. You’ve completed 3 of your 4 available digital checklists this week.” For a hold, also show “Safety hold recorded. Follow the stop-work procedure.”
- **Personal progress** — “3 of 4 this week. Open the checklist on your next job before starting.” The goal is every job where the app is available. Someone with one opportunity sees a goal of one. Someone with none sees “No digital checklist opportunities this week.”
- **Restart** — “Your next job is a fresh start. Open the checklist before you begin.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress privately. Crews and managers see the crew’s verified rate against its own baseline, with no individual names. Suppress results for groups with fewer than 10 contributing technicians and any breakdown that could identify someone. A named data steward can access individual programme records for a correction, dispute or technician-requested coaching. Access is logged, time-limited and barred from disciplinary use.

## 5. Incentive

Use immediate confirmation, a reachable personal goal and visible crew improvement. Skip prizes and rankings. Credit for correctly stopping work gives technicians the same recognition for finding a hazard as for clearing a job to proceed.

## 6. Operator card

The field operations lead reconciles dispatch, app and paper records. An independent safety lead reviews 20 randomly selected jobs weekly, including digital checks, holds and fallbacks, against job records and technician accounts. **Pause prompts and progress reporting** if two sampled digital credits lack evidence of a timely, complete check, or immediately if a credible report links the pilot to pressure to start unsafe work. The safety lead owns the pause and restart. Approved safety procedures continue throughout. Technicians can flag a missing or wrong credit through dispatch. The data steward responds within two business days. Budget three staff-hours per week.

## 7. End and decision

Use February 1–28, 2027 as baseline. Run March 1–April 11, then follow through May 23. Randomly assign comparable crews to pilot or comparison. Both get the same app, training, support and checklist receipts. Retire campaign prompts, goals and crew displays on April 11. Keep receipts and workflow fixes.

Scale if the baseline-adjusted improvement over comparison crews is at least **10 percentage points during follow-up**, audit quality is at least as good as comparison, and no safety pause remains unresolved. Revise and retest if access or workflow issues explain a shortfall. Stop if the lift fades or safety quality worsens.

## 8. What could go wrong

- Technicians tick boxes under time pressure or avoid recording hazards.
- Crews with poor connectivity appear to underperform because digital access differs.
- Managers infer individual results from small groups, or participation falls when campaign feedback ends.
