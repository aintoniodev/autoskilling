## 1. Diagnosis

The median review response is 2.6 days. That establishes the delay. Before launch, the engineering lead checks recent requests and asks reviewers where time was lost. The working hypothesis is that requests get buried or sit without a clear owner.

Walk two cases with the team. For a ready PR with a clear owner and passing checks, follow the request from the normal review queue to the first useful response. For a PR whose reviewer lacks repository access, have the lead assign an authorized backup within four working hours. The original deadline stays in place. If most sampled requests are blocked, or reviewers lack time to do a proper review, fix access, routing or capacity before starting the pilot.

## 2. What earns credit

A ready PR entering the review queue starts one eight-working-hour clock, using the team’s published coverage hours and holiday calendar. Assign a primary reviewer and a backup. Either can earn the PR’s single credit by submitting a substantive review before the deadline. An approval must identify what was checked. A change request or blocking question must give the author a concrete next step. Late reviews still help the author and count as SLA misses.

Measure **the share of eligible PRs that receive a qualifying response within eight working hours**. Include unassigned and unanswered PRs. Exclude drafts, bot-only PRs and PRs genuinely withdrawn before the deadline. Report withdrawal counts alongside the rate.

Test the rule against PR event history before launch.

- **Normal** · A reviewer approves and records the check they ran in hour six. One credit.
- **Duplicate** · A second reviewer replies, or the author re-requests review. One credit total and one clock.
- **Reversal** · An approval is dismissed because it lacked a valid check. Void it and use the timestamp of the next qualifying review. A later code change leaves a valid earlier response intact.
- **Late or edited deadline** · A qualifying review in hour nine is a miss. Reassignment and due-date edits leave the original deadline in place.
- **Old way** · A chat reply can unblock the author. The PR review record supplies the response timestamp for credit.
- **Omitted** · A ready PR with no assignment or response remains in the denominator when its deadline passes.

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation in the existing review queue:** “PR #482 is ready for your review by Tuesday at 14:00. Give the author a decision or a concrete next step so they can keep moving. If you’re tied up, hand it to the backup now. This pilot ends February 26.”
- **Confirmation after submission:** “Review posted for #482 in 3 working hours. It counts toward your on-time goal.”
- **Private progress:** “2 of 3 due reviews answered on time this week. Aim for 4 in every 5 when you have enough volume. Next up is #491, due tomorrow at 11:00.”
- **Low volume and restart:** “No reviews due this week” for zero opportunities. With one to four, show counts without a weekly pass/fail. After a miss, show “Your next review is #491, due tomorrow at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress and the team’s weekly rate, eligible count and baseline. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress team weeks with fewer than five eligible PRs or fewer than five distinct reviewers. The pilot operator can access individual records for corrections, disputes or coaching requested by the engineer. Log the purpose, expire access when the case closes and exclude these records from performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Add-on

No prizes, badges or rankings. The incentive is immediate confirmation and visible progress on a shared target. That gives a team sceptical of gamification a short trial centred on the review queue it already uses.

## 6. Operator card

**Assumptions** · The engineering lead can appoint a backup and a rotating senior reviewer. The repository retains ready, assignment, submission and dismissal events. Verify those assumptions and reconcile the event history against the queue before launch.

**Owner and effort** · The engineering lead owns routing, corrections and the weekly tally. Budget 60 minutes a week across the lead and the rotating reviewer. Engineers can send a PR link privately to the lead for a correction within two working days.

**Quality gate** · Each week the rotating reviewer samples 10 credited PRs, or all if fewer, and checks whether the response supports its decision and addresses material issues. At two failures in a weekly sample, the lead pauses credit and progress reporting, corrects affected records and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Normal review work continues.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline January 4–29, 2027, using the same rules. Run February 1–26 and follow up March 1–26. End campaign reminders on February 26. Keep routing, backup coverage and private confirmation. Include late-window PRs once their deadlines pass.

Scale if both pilot and follow-up reach **80% on time and improve at least 15 percentage points over baseline**, with no unresolved quality pause. Stop if follow-up is at or below baseline or quality failures recur after a fix. Otherwise adjust routing or capacity and retest. The baseline comparison supports the team’s decision, with seasonal changes and workload shifts as possible explanations.

## 8. Most likely failure modes

- **Rubber-stamping for speed.** Sample review substance and pause credit when quality drops.
- **Easy PRs get picked first.** Count unassigned and omitted work, and inspect misses by PR size and dependency.
- **The lead’s attention drives a temporary lift.** Use the full follow-up window before scaling.
