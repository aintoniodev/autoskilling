## 1. Diagnosis

Coverage is 12% of resolved tickets. The cause is unknown. Before launch, the KB lead shadows two resolutions. For a routine password reset, the agent finds and links the approved article from the ticket. For an account-specific billing adjustment, the agent resolves the ticket through the normal workflow and records why the answer is unsuitable for reuse. Time the first path and ask why agents skip it today. Fix access or search friction first. Delay launch if most tickets are blocked or linking routinely adds more work than it saves. The KB lead triages blockers within two business days.

## 2. Credit rule

At resolution, the handling agent earns one credit per canonical ticket by linking a current, approved article that matches the fix, or by submitting a reusable article that is approved, published and linked within seven days. Resolve the customer’s issue on time either way.

Track **qualified coverage of all resolved tickets** against the 12% starting point, and **qualified coverage of eligible tickets** for the pilot decision. Eligibility uses predefined ticket types and auditable exceptions for non-reusable resolutions or tool outages. Missing KB activity alone never qualifies as an exception.

| Test case | Result |
|---|---|
| Matching article linked at resolution | One credit |
| Duplicate ticket or several article links | At most one credit for the canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopened or article recalled | Remove invalid credit. Restore it once if the corrected resolution qualifies. |
| Approval arrives late or a due date is edited | Use the original resolution timestamp and seven-day window. Late approval earns zero. |
| Agent resolves the ticket without using the KB | Zero credit. Eligible tickets stay in both denominators. |
| Ticket missing from the KB report | Reconcile against the ticket system. Include it in the denominator and correct credit from event history. |

## 3. What agents see

- **Invitation, in the resolution panel.** “Before you close, link the article that matches your fix so you can find it faster next time. If a reusable answer is missing, submit it for review and close the ticket as usual. The pilot ends March 7.”
- **Confirmation.** “Article linked. This ticket counts toward your weekly goal.” For a draft, show “Submitted for review. It counts once published and linked.”
- **Progress.** “2 of 4 eligible tickets covered this week. Aim for half. Check the KB on your next resolution.” With one opportunity, suggest trying it once. With zero, show “No eligible tickets yet.”
- **Restart.** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Each week stands on its own.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own counts. Staff and managers see organization-wide coverage against its baseline, with small or identifiable cells suppressed. The KB lead gets logged, time-limited individual access for requested coaching or corrections. Keep individual programme data out of compensation and performance reviews. The existing CSAT bonus stays on its current terms.

## 5. Add-on

Use private progress and team feedback as the incentive. Skip prizes because CSAT already carries financial stakes and the pilot needs to show whether the workflow earns repeat use on its own.

## 6. Operator card

The KB lead budgets **45 minutes a week** using existing ticket and KB reports. An independent support QA reviewer checks five credited tickets for article fit and three uncredited tickets for improper exclusions or missing records within the same budget. Two material errors in a weekly sample, or one customer-data exposure, trigger a pause in credit and prompts while the lead fixes the issue. Agents request corrections in the existing support channel. Before launch, confirm auditable event history and enough editorial capacity to review drafts within seven days.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 11–February 7, 2027** as baseline, **February 8–March 7** as the pilot and **March 8–April 4** as follow-up. End campaign prompts and weekly goals on March 7. Keep workflow fixes and immediate confirmation. Let the last cohort’s seven-day approval window close before scoring.

Scale if eligible coverage rises at least **15 percentage points** during the pilot and stays **10 points above baseline** in follow-up, with no unresolved quality pause and follow-up CSAT within **3 points** of baseline. Change the workflow and retest if adoption rises but fades. Stop the campaign if there is no meaningful lift or quality or CSAT deteriorates. Report the before-and-after result as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Risks

Agents may link generic articles to hit the goal, mark eligible tickets as exceptions to improve the rate, or keep customers waiting while drafting articles. Sample article fit and exclusions, and let agents close tickets while drafts await review.
