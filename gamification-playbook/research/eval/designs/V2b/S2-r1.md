## 1. Diagnosis

The reported median is 2.6 days. Before launch, the engineering lead walks two recent PRs with their reviewers. In the supported case, trace a ready PR with passing checks and an available reviewer from request to decision. In the blocked case, trace a PR routed to an absent reviewer or someone without access through reassignment. Check whether batching, interruptions, or review effort explains the delay. **Assumptions to verify:** requests sometimes go unnoticed, a daily review slot fits the team’s workload, and the repository preserves request and review history. The lead owns routing and access fixes by the next working day. If most cases are blocked or the slot proves impractical, fix the workflow before starting the pilot.

## 2. Credit rule

When a ready PR requests review, its designated reviewer earns one credit for submitting a formal approval after reviewing the diff, or requesting changes with a specific issue and actionable next step, within eight hours on the team’s working calendar. The first qualifying response earns the PR’s sole credit, including when a backup responds. Reassignment leaves the clock running. Authors keep their existing escalation route for urgent or blocked reviews.

The team measure is the percentage of eligible PRs whose deadline fell that week and that earned credit. A thank-you, an ETA, or a merge without a recorded qualifying review earns zero. Record genuine blockers present at intake separately and report their count.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Ready PR gets an actionable review in six working hours | One credit |
| Second request or second qualifying review on the same PR | One credit total |
| Review dismissed as invalid | Remove credit. A valid replacement before the original deadline restores it. |
| Deadline edited or response late | Original deadline applies. Late response earns zero. |
| Review answered only in chat | Zero. Submit the decision on the PR before the deadline to earn credit. |
| Eligible PR missing from the report | Restore it to the denominator and use repository history to determine credit. |

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation in the existing review notification:** “PR #482 is ready for you. Use today’s review slot to approve it or leave a specific change request by Tuesday at 14:00. A clear decision saves the author a follow-up. If you’re blocked, flag it here for the review lead. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation:** “Review submitted on #482 in six working hours. This PR counted toward your weekly goal.”
- **Private progress:** “3 of 4 timely reviews this week. Next due is #491 at 14:00.” Set the goal at 80% of each reviewer’s eligible volume, rounded up. With one or two opportunities, show the individual due times and outcomes. With zero, show “No reviews due this week.”
- **Restart:** “New week. Your next review is #491, due at 14:00.”

## 4. Visibility and incentive

Engineers see their own progress. The team and managers see the weekly team rate against its baseline and the blocked-case count, with no names or breakdowns. Suppress any result that could identify someone, including weeks with fewer than five reviewers. The lead can open an individual pilot record for requested coaching or a correction. Log the access, close it when the issue is resolved, and exclude pilot records from performance management.

The incentive is immediate confirmation and a reachable personal goal. There are no prizes, badges, or rankings. Give engineers a protected review slot so acting on the cue is feasible.

## 5. Operator card

The engineering lead runs the existing repository report and independently samples five credited reviews each week for evidence of a substantive decision. If two or more fail, pause goal messages and progress publication, correct the records, and resume after a fresh sample passes. Engineers can flag missing or incorrect credit privately to the lead, who resolves it within two working days. Budget **45–55 minutes per week**, including sampling and routine corrections. Launch depends on verifying that the report captures omitted PRs, original timestamps, and dismissed reviews.

## 6. End and decision

Use **January 11–February 5, 2027** as the baseline, with routing and review slots in place. Run the pilot **February 8–March 5**. End weekly goals and team progress posts on March 5. Keep routing, review slots, and submission confirmation. Measure the same outcome for four more weeks, **March 8–April 2**.

Keep the approach if the follow-up rate is **at least 80% and at least 15 percentage points above baseline**, with no quality pause during follow-up. Change the workflow if response times improve but miss either threshold. Retire the incentive if the rate returns to baseline or superficial reviews recur. This single-team comparison supports a local decision. It cannot isolate the effect of the incentive.

## 7. Three likely failure modes

- **Rubber-stamp approvals.** Sample review substance and pause the pilot if quality drops.
- **Hard PRs disappear from the denominator.** Reconcile against the full review-request history and report blocked cases separately.
- **The pilot feels like surveillance.** Keep personal progress private and make review time visible in the team’s workload planning.
