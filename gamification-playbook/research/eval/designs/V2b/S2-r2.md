## 1. Diagnosis

The median review response is 2.6 days. The cause is unknown. Before launch, the engineering lead and two reviewers walk one reviewable PR and one stalled PR from request to response. Check whether the reviewer had access, enough context, and time, and whether the request reached them. Test the working hypothesis that requests get buried and ownership is unclear. If most cases are blocked or reviewers lack capacity, fix that before launching. The engineering lead owns blockers and responds within one working day. Reviewers use the existing team channel to request a backup or flag missing context.

## 2. Credit rule

When a human-authored PR first becomes ready for review, request a reviewer through the existing repository workflow. Earn one credit for the first substantive human review within one working day. An approval identifies what was checked. A request for changes or clarification identifies a specific issue and what the author can do next.

Use the team’s published work calendar. A request received during working hours is due at the same time on the next working day. An out-of-hours request starts at the next opening. The backup takes over during reviewer leave. Reassignment keeps the original deadline.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Reviewer posts a substantive review before the deadline | One credit |
| Multiple reviewers respond, or the PR is reopened | One credit maximum per PR |
| Reviewer withdraws a qualifying review | Remove the credit unless another qualifying review met the original deadline |
| Someone edits the due date or reassigns the PR | Use the original deadline |
| Reviewer finds the PR through the usual queue | Same credit if the review qualifies |
| Ready PR has no reviewer assigned or gets no response | Count it as a miss once due |

The team metric is credited PRs divided by all eligible PRs due that week. Assume repository history can identify ready PRs independently of reviewer assignment and recover original timestamps and withdrawals. Verify those cases before launch.

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation on the review request** — “Can you give this PR a substantive first review by Tuesday at 14:00? A specific blocker is enough to help the author move forward. If you’re unavailable, ask the backup in the team channel. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation** — “Your review of #184 counted. You checked the migration and gave the author a concrete next step.”
- **Private progress** — “3 of your 4 assigned PRs received a qualifying response on time this week. Next up is #191, due Thursday at 11:00.” Aim for 80% of each person’s assigned volume. For zero to two opportunities, show upcoming reviews and confirmations without a weekly target.
- **Restart** — “Pick up your next review. #191 is due Thursday at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The team and managers see the team’s weekly rate against its baseline. Publish only team-level results with at least 10 eligible PRs from five authors. The engineering lead may inspect individual records for requested coaching or corrections. Log that access, close it when the case is resolved, and exclude it from performance reviews.

## 5. Incentive

Use prompt private confirmation and shared team progress. Engineers are skeptical of gamification, so keep both focused on getting authors an actionable response. Add no prizes, ranks, badges, or streaks.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead spends up to 45 minutes weekly using the existing repository report. A rotating engineer independently samples five credited reviews in about 10 minutes. Pause credit and team reporting if two sampled reviews lack substance or one bypasses a required safety check. The lead checks the scoring rule or workflow before resuming. Engineers can send the lead a PR link privately for correction within two working days.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed dates are for 2027. Establish routing and backup coverage before the January 11–February 5 baseline. Pilot February 8–March 5. Retire the weekly campaign updates on March 5, keep routing and private feedback, and measure through April 2. Continue the approach if both the pilot and follow-up reach 80% on time and beat baseline by at least 10 percentage points, with no quality pause. Adjust routing or workload and retest if improvement falls short. End programme reporting if follow-up returns to baseline or quality problems recur. With one team, treat the result as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- Reviewers rush approvals or manufacture blockers to meet the deadline.
- Reviewers pick easy PRs while complex reviews age.
- Engineers infer individual performance during quiet weeks and disengage.
