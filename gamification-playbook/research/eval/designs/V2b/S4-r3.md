## 1. Diagnosis

Agents link or write an article on 12% of resolved tickets today. That figure leaves three possible causes open: access or publication blockers, extra work at close, and low awareness. Before launch, the Support Ops lead walks two real cases with agents: a repeat issue with an approved answer and a case where an article is missing or access fails. Compare time and rework with the usual close. Support Ops triages blockers within two business days. Agents can always resolve the ticket and flag a KB gap. If most cases are blocked or the KB adds net work, fix the workflow before launching.

## 2. Credit rule

When a support agent resolves a ticket, they earn one credit if the ticket links to an approved article that accurately covers the resolution. A new article qualifies once it passes the existing editorial review and is linked to the ticket. Agents check for an existing answer first. One article can serve multiple tickets when it genuinely applies.

Measure **qualified tickets ÷ eligible resolved tickets**. Start with every resolved ticket. Support Ops alone can approve exclusions using predefined reasons such as sensitive one-off cases or broken access. Missing content stays eligible when agents can submit it for review. Report qualified coverage across **all** resolved tickets alongside the eligible rate so exclusions stay visible. Recalculate the 12% baseline under the pilot rules.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Accurate approved article linked at close | One credit |
| Duplicate link, repeated event, or multiple articles | One credit per ticket |
| Ticket reopens or article is withdrawn | Remove credit and recalculate after re-resolution or correction |
| Late link, approval, or edited timestamp | Use the original resolution week. Accept logged corrections and approvals within five business days |
| Resolved the usual way with no article | Zero credit. Eligible cases stay in the denominator |
| Ticket omitted from the KB report | Recover it from the ticket system’s resolution log and reconcile before reporting |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolve.** “Link the approved article so the next agent can reuse your answer. If it’s missing, submit the reusable steps for review. If you’re blocked, resolve the ticket and flag the gap. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation.** “Article linked. This ticket counts toward your goal.” For submissions: “Article sent for review. This ticket counts once the article is approved and linked.”
- **Private progress.** “2 of 3 this week. On your next eligible ticket, look for an approved answer before closing.” Set the weekly goal at half the agent’s recent eligible volume, rounded up and capped at five. Adjust downward for a lower-volume week. With one opportunity the goal is one. With zero, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart.** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Your goal resets each week.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own progress. The whole org, including managers, sees aggregate coverage against its baseline, the eligible ticket count, and the exclusion rate. Suppress any view with fewer than five contributing agents. Support Ops can open individual records for corrections, disputes, or requested coaching. Log access, close it when the case is resolved, and prohibit disciplinary use. Explain these rules at launch.

## 5. Incentive

Use immediate confirmation, a reachable private goal, and shared progress. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility intact. KB credit carries zero pay weight.

## 6. Operator card

**Support Ops lead, 45 minutes weekly.** Use the ticket resolution log, KB approval history, and existing CSAT report. An independent QA reviewer samples eight tickets per week, six credited and two uncredited or excluded, for article accuracy, applicability, sensitive content, and correct eligibility. Pause credit and prompts if two sampled credits fail, or immediately for any sensitive-data exposure. Support Ops fixes the issue with QA and resumes after a clean sample of eight. Agents submit corrections through the existing Support Ops queue.

## 7. End and decision

**Proposed dates:** baseline January 4–31, pilot February 1–28, follow-up March 1–28, 2027. End the rally prompts and weekly goal on February 28. Keep workflow fixes and immediate confirmation. Apply the same measurement rules throughout and allow five business days for late approvals before closing each period.

Keep the workflow and feedback if qualified adoption rises at least **10 percentage points** during the pilot and retains at least **75% of that lift** in follow-up, all-ticket coverage also rises, QA passes, and CSAT stays within **3 percentage points** of baseline. Redesign if adoption fades or exclusions rise. Stop the incentive if quality or CSAT deteriorates. The baseline comparison supports a rollout decision. It cannot establish causality.

## 8. Biggest risks

- Agents link generic articles or create duplicates to earn easy credit.
- Extra closeout work slows responses and puts the CSAT bonus under pressure.
- Approval delays or inconsistent exclusions disproportionately affect agents handling complex tickets.

**Launch assumptions:** Ticket and KB histories can be joined reliably, editorial review can meet the five-business-day window, and Support Ops can run the report and sample within the weekly budget.
