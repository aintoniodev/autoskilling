## 1. Diagnosis

Run a three-ward pilot to see whether nurses will use the kiosk when logging fits their workflow. Low awareness is a hypothesis. Before launch, the Infection Prevention lead walks through a routine bedside case and an urgent case with a nurse on each ward, including nights. Check that a routine entry takes under 15 seconds and that staff can skip logging whenever care takes priority or the kiosk is unavailable. The lead owns blockers and routes access or kiosk faults to IT by the next working day. Hold the pilot if most shifts lack a safe, practical logging opportunity. Confirm kiosk access, reporting and staff privacy approval before launch.

## 2. Credit rule

When a bedside nurse finishes hand hygiene at an eligible moment and care allows, they can record the moment in the kiosk. The first valid, saved entry earns one private progress credit per worked shift. Further entries earn zero extra credit. The programme measures **the share of staffed bedside nurse shifts with a valid entry**, including shifts affected by access problems. Infection Prevention measures **clinical compliance** separately through its existing observer audits, using hand hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. [WHO observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| First completed hand hygiene moment logged that shift | One credit |
| Double tap, duplicate or another genuine entry | Shift credit stays at one |
| Entry voided or corrected | Recalculate from valid entries. Keep correction history |
| Late entry or edited shift | Use the occurrence shift. Entries after that shift earn zero credit |
| Hand hygiene performed without using the kiosk | Counts toward clinical compliance if observed. Zero kiosk credit |
| Opportunity omitted | No kiosk credit for that opportunity. The audit records what the observer saw |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation** in the existing shift huddle: “After your next hand hygiene moment, log it when you’re free to step away. You’ll see it saved straight away. If care needs you or the kiosk is down, skip the log. The pilot ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation** at the kiosk: “Moment saved. You’ve logged one on this shift.”
- **Progress** on the private screen: “You’ve logged on 2 of your 3 bedside shifts this week. Try one on your next shift if care allows.” With one shift, the goal is one. With zero shifts, hide the goal.
- **Restart**: “Start with your next safe chance to log. There’s nothing to catch up on.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own progress. Staff and managers see ward-level logging uptake against that ward’s baseline. Pool or suppress results for groups with fewer than five nurses or where someone could be identified. Collect zero patient identifiers. Only the designated administrator can access individual records for a correction or dispute. Log and expire that access. Exclude pilot data from performance management and discipline.

## 5. Add-on

Use immediate confirmation and a reachable private goal as the incentive. Skip prizes and rankings. Extra entries carry zero reward.

## 6. Operator card

The Infection Prevention lead spends about 45 minutes weekly reviewing the existing roster and kiosk exports, checking voids and duplicates, and resolving corrections through the existing helpdesk. Keep monthly observer audits on a comparable mix of shifts and opportunities. Pause prompts immediately after a confirmed care delay or if a ward’s monthly observed compliance falls five percentage points below baseline. Investigate before resuming.

## 7. End and decision

Use January–February 2027 as the baseline with the kiosk available and routine support. Run the incentive March–April. Remove campaign prompts and goals on April 30. Keep the kiosk, save confirmation and workflow fixes, then measure through June 30.

Expand if May–June logging uptake is at least 15 percentage points above baseline on each ward, observed compliance stays within five points of baseline or improves, and there is no logging-related care delay. Revise and retest if uptake fades, access remains uneven or audit samples are too small to judge. Stop the incentive if it causes care delays or compliance declines persist. Treat the comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- Nurses might rush care to reach the kiosk. Make skipping explicit and pause after a confirmed delay.
- Staff might submit inaccurate entries or skip logging more often on busy shifts. Cap credit, allow corrections and track uptake across all bedside shifts.
- Staff may perform differently around observers. Keep audit methods comparable and interpret observed compliance in light of that effect. [Hospital observation study](https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/23/12/974)
