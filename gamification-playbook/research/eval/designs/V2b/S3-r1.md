# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

## 1. Diagnosis

The adoption barrier is unconfirmed. Before launch, walk one routine post-care moment and one blocked case, such as an urgent call or an occupied kiosk, with nurses on day and night shifts. Time the detour and entry. The ward charge nurse owns blocker reports and gets a workaround or repair ETA by the next shift. If most opportunities are blocked or logging interrupts care, fix the workflow before launching. Infection Prevention must approve kiosk placement and cleaning. [CDC hand-hygiene guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/)

## 2. Credit and measurement

After a required hand-hygiene action, the nurse logs the moment at the next safe chance. If care intervenes or the kiosk is unavailable, skip the entry. During the existing monthly audit, observers tally which completed actions they can also verify were logged. One observed action earns at most one credit, even when it meets multiple indications. [WHO observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

| Case | Credit |
|---|---|
| Observer sees hygiene and its matching entry | One |
| Duplicate entry or multiple indications for one action | One total |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Remove the original credit. Count a verified correction once. |
| Late or edited entry | Count only if the observer can still confirm the match. |
| Hygiene done, entry omitted or kiosk unavailable | Zero logging credit. Hygiene still counts in the compliance audit. |
| Entry with no matching observed action | Zero |

Report **verified logging coverage** as confirmed matches divided by observed completed actions, including actions during kiosk outages. Separately report **observed hygiene compliance** as observed actions divided by observed opportunities. Record blocked and unmatchable cases. Confirm before launch that observers can match entries without retaining identities and that corrections have a reliable history. If either check fails, use private feedback alone.

## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation at the kiosk:** “Clean your hands as usual. After care, tap the moment you just completed when it’s safe. Your entry saves in one step. The pilot ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation:** “Saved: after touching a patient.”
- **Private progress:** “You’ve logged a moment on 1 of your next 2 worked shifts. Try one on your next shift when it’s safe.”
- **Restart:** “Pick up with your next safe opportunity.” Shifts without a safe opportunity carry no target.

## 4. Visibility and recognition

Nurses see their own progress privately. Staff and managers see ward rates and sample sizes against their own baseline. Suppress groups where individuals could be inferred. Collect no patient identifiers. Restrict entry-level access to the designated corrections owner for logged, time-limited disputes or corrections. Programme data are excluded from discipline.

At pilot close, each ward that meets the pilot targets earns one specific thank-you at its huddle. Recognition is shared across shifts and carries no cash or individual ranking.

## 5. Operator card

The Infection Prevention lead owns the pilot. Use the kiosk export and existing monthly audits, sampling comparable shifts and keeping observers independent of ward scoring. Allow 45 minutes weekly for exceptions and reporting, plus 15 minutes per ward per monthly audit for matching. Nurses route corrections through the charge nurse. Pause recognition and prompts immediately after any credible care delay or contamination incident, or if a ward’s monthly observed compliance falls five percentage points below baseline. Review the cause before restarting.

## 6. End and decision

Run a quiet kiosk baseline in January–February 2027, the pilot in March–April, and follow-up in May–June. End recognition and prompts April 30. Keep private feedback and workflow fixes. Scale if each ward gains at least 10 percentage points in verified logging during the pilot, retains that gain in follow-up, and stays within the compliance guardrail. Set minimum audit counts before launch. Revise and retest if coverage or sampling falls short. Stop if safety problems persist. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 7. Main risks

Backfilled taps inflate apparent adoption. Kiosk detours disrupt care or contaminate clean hands. Observer presence and uneven shift coverage make a temporary lift look durable.
