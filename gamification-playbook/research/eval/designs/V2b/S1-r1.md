## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently get a digital checklist. Before launch, the field-safety lead and a union representative walk through one supported job and one job where the app cannot be used. Check access, offline use, and time and rework compared with paper. If the app adds friction, fix it before piloting. If most jobs are blocked, defer the pilot. Use the approved paper checklist before work whenever the app fails. The field-safety lead owns blockers and responds within one business day.

**Launch assumptions.** The union agrees to the pilot and data rules, paper is an approved fallback, and dispatch and app records can verify when work began.

## 2. What earns credit

When a technician opens their next job in dispatch, they earn one credit for submitting the complete digital safety checklist before work starts and recording the required response to any hazard. A properly documented safety stop earns credit even if work never starts. Unresolved hazards keep work paused.

Measure verified digital completions divided by all jobs that reach the point where a checklist is due. Report app-blocked jobs separately and keep them in the overall denominator so access problems stay visible.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete checklist before work | One credit |
| Duplicate submission or sync retry | One credit per job ID |
| Voided checklist | Remove credit. A valid replacement can qualify if submitted before work |
| Late submission or edited schedule | Use verified work-start history. Schedule edits do not change credit |
| Approved paper fallback | Record safety completion. Zero digital credit |
| Missing checklist or omitted job | Reconcile against dispatch. Keep the job in the denominator and route the gap for review |

## 3. What technicians see

- **Invitation** — “Before starting this job, open Safety Checklist in the app. It saves your answers with the job so you can find them later. If the app is unavailable, use the approved paper checklist before starting. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation** — “Checklist saved for job 4821. It counts toward your pilot goal.” If offline, show “Saved on this device. Credit will appear after sync and verification.”
- **Progress** — “1 of 2 checklists verified toward your goal this week. Use the app on your next supported job.” The weekly goal is up to two supported jobs. With one opportunity, the goal is one. With zero, display “No eligible jobs this week.”
- **Restart** — “Missed last week? Start with the checklist on your next supported job.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. Each crew and its manager see the crew’s verified rate against its own baseline, without names. Suppress results for groups with fewer than 10 active technicians or where a person’s result could be inferred. Only the field-safety lead can open individual pilot records for a correction, dispute, or technician-requested coaching. Log and time-limit access. Pilot records stay out of discipline and performance ratings.

## 5. Incentive

Give one crew-wide thank-you at close for meeting the adoption and quality thresholds. Name the practice the crew demonstrated, such as documenting a hazard and pausing work. This recognizes safe use without rewarding job volume. No cash, rankings, or individual awards.

## 6. Operator card

The field-safety lead uses existing dispatch and checklist exports, with a budget of **90 minutes per week** across 300 staff. Sample 12 jobs weekly across digital, paper, and missing records. Cross-check timing and hazard handling against existing job records and safety observations. If two records in a sample fail verification, or one shows work proceeding despite an unresolved stop condition, pause crew reporting and recognition, investigate, and correct affected credits. Technicians can request corrections through the existing safety channel, with a union representative if desired.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 4–31, 2027 as baseline. Pilot February 1–28. If comparable crews are available, assign them by crew to pilot or comparison, with the same app access and support in both groups. Close recognition March 1. Keep checklist confirmations and workflow fixes, then measure March 2–29 without pilot goals, crew progress posts, or recognition.

Scale if follow-up adoption is at least **10 percentage points above baseline**, and at least **5 points better in improvement than comparison crews** where available, with no unresolved quality pause. Change the workflow and retest if adoption improves but misses the threshold. Stop the incentive if the lift disappears or a safety or trust issue remains unresolved. A baseline-only result supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Main risks

- Technicians rush through checks to earn credit. Audit hazard handling as well as completion.
- Patchy coverage makes some crews look worse. Track blockers and protect the paper fallback.
- Crew reporting creates peer pressure or feels like surveillance. Suppress identifiable results and enforce the union-agreed access limits.
