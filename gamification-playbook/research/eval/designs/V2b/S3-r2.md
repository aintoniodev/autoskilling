## 1. Diagnosis

Test whether nurses can record hand hygiene without interrupting care. Before launch, the infection prevention lead walks two cases with a nurse on each ward: routine care with a working kiosk, and urgent care or a kiosk outage. Ask nurses whether the entry is quick enough and whether the feedback helps them spot missed documentation. The charge nurse routes access and kiosk issues to IT for same-shift triage. If most opportunities lack a safe, practical way to log, fix the workflow before launching. Confirm kiosk access, a correction route, and privacy approval during baseline.

## 2. What earns credit

Give the ward one credit when an independent observer sees a nurse perform hand hygiene at an eligible care moment and can verify a matching kiosk entry at the next safe chance. Eligibility is set before observing the outcome. One action earns one credit even if it covers several indications. The ward’s **verified logging rate** is credited actions divided by all eligible, observed actions performed, including omissions. Report kiosk outages and blocked opportunities alongside it. Keep the existing monthly **hand-hygiene compliance** measure based on actions performed divided by all observed opportunities. [WHO hand-hygiene technical reference manual](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241598606)

| Case | Credit |
|---|---|
| Observed action and matching entry | One |
| Duplicate entry or several indications for the same action | One total |
| Entry corrected or withdrawn | Use the corrected record. Remove credit if the match is invalid. |
| Late entry or edited timestamp | Count only if the observer can still verify the match. |
| Action documented through the approved fallback, or omitted from the kiosk | Zero kiosk credits. The observed action still counts toward hygiene compliance. |
| Entry without a verified action | Zero |

## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation.** “After hand hygiene, log the moment when patient care allows. Your ward’s feedback will show where recording is easy and where it needs fixing. The pilot ends 30 April.”
- **Confirmation.** “Entry saved. You can correct it here.”
- **Private goal.** “Start with one safe chance this shift. If none comes up, skip the goal.”
- **Restart.** “Missed a chance? Start with the next safe one.”

## 4. Visibility

Show confirmation privately and publish each ward’s verified rate against its own baseline, with the sample size. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress results that could identify a nurse. Keep names, patient identifiers, and individual histories out of programme reporting. Limit access to individual records to the designated correction lead for a specific dispute, log that access, and exclude programme records from performance reviews and discipline.

## 5. Incentive

Use immediate private confirmation and visible ward progress. Skip prizes, quotas, and rankings. Extra entries carry no advantage.

## 6. Operator card

The infection prevention lead uses the existing monthly audit and kiosk correction record. During scheduled rounds, trained observers verify entries only where they can do so without shadowing staff or collecting identifiers. Sample across wards and shifts using the same approach throughout. Budget 45 minutes a week for the lead, plus existing audit time. If reliable matching needs extra observation capacity, run a feasibility test first. Nurses flag corrections privately to the lead. Pause prompts and progress reports after any credible care-delay or privacy incident, or a monthly compliance drop of at least 5 percentage points on any ward. Investigate before restarting.

## 7. End and decision

Use January–February 2027 as the kiosk baseline, March–April for the pilot, and May–June for follow-up. End campaign prompts and ward progress reports on 30 April. Keep workflow fixes and private confirmation. Expand only if verified logging rises at least 10 percentage points during the pilot, retains at least 8 points in follow-up, and no ward meets a pause threshold. Revise and retest if gains fade or sampling is too sparse to judge. Stop if safety problems persist. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Logging displaces care.** Give staff an explicit fallback and pause on the first credible delay.
- **Entries overstate adoption.** Check observed work, deduplicate, and include omissions in the denominator.
- **Audit behaviour looks better than routine practice.** Keep sampling comparable across phases and avoid drawing conclusions from one shift or a small sample.
