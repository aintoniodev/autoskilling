## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs have a digital checklist. The reason for the gap is unknown. Before launch, the field safety lead and a union steward shadow two jobs from dispatch to work start. On a supported job, time the digital checklist against the paper process and check whether technicians enter the same information twice. On a job without signal or app access, verify that the technician can complete the approved fallback before starting. The field safety lead owns blockers and responds within one business day. Defer the pilot if most jobs are blocked or the digital workflow adds rework.

## 2. Credit rule

When a work order reaches the pre-job safety check, the assigned technician earns one credit by completing the applicable digital checklist before work starts, or by completing it and recording a safety hold. Required hazard responses must be present. An unresolved hazard requires a hold. Measure company adoption separately as the share of **all started jobs** with a qualified digital checklist completed before start. Report paper fallback and safety holds alongside it.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Valid checklist before start | One credit |
| Duplicate or resubmission | One credit per work order |
| Checklist voided or recalled | Remove credit unless a valid pre-start record remains |
| Late submission or edited due date | Use original completion and actual start times. Verified offline timestamps count. |
| Approved paper fallback | Record safety completion separately |
| Started job with no checklist | Keep it in the adoption denominator with zero credit |
| Completed checklist records a safety hold | One personal credit. Include in started-job adoption only if work later starts with a valid clearance. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation:** “Before you start this job, open Safety Checklist on the work order. It saves your check with the job so you can find it later. If the app is unavailable, use the approved paper checklist before starting. If you find an unresolved hazard, stop and follow the safety procedure. The pilot ends March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Checklist saved for job 1842 before work started.” For a hold, show “Checklist saved. Safety hold recorded.”
- **Progress:** “3 of 4 app-ready jobs checked digitally this week. Open the checklist on your next job.” The goal is each app-ready job, including weeks with just one. Show “No app-ready jobs this week” at zero.
- **Restart:** “Start fresh on your next job. Open the checklist before work begins.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. Crews and managers see weekly adoption against the crew’s own baseline, with no names. Suppress crew results with fewer than 10 contributing technicians and any breakdown that could identify someone. Only the designated safety lead can access individual programme records for a quality review, requested coaching or a correction. Log access, limit it to the case and prohibit disciplinary reuse of programme data. Agree these terms with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Add-on

Use private feedback and crew progress. Keep prizes and rankings out of a process where reporting a hazard and stopping work must always be safe choices.

## 6. Operator card

The field safety lead joins dispatch work orders and independently recorded start times to checklist history. Verify offline timestamps, voids and missing records before launch. Each week, sample 10 started jobs, including digital completions, fallbacks and omissions where present. Cross-check records with dispatch or technician confirmation. Pause programme feedback for one confirmed start without a required safety check or two falsely credited jobs in a sample. Follow the existing safety procedure and correct the records before resuming. Technicians can request corrections through the lead or their steward. Budget 90 minutes a week using existing reports. Launch assumes reliable records, approved fallback and union agreement.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 11–February 7, 2027 as baseline, February 8–March 7 as pilot and March 8–April 4 as follow-up. End goals and crew displays on March 7. Keep checklist receipts, training and workflow fixes. If enough similar crews are available, randomly assign within matched pairs to the programme or the same rollout support without programme feedback. Otherwise use the baseline and treat the result as feasibility. Scale if follow-up adoption is at least 55%, improves at least 10 percentage points more than comparison crews or 10 points over baseline, and has no unresolved pause trigger. Revise if gains fade or access gaps explain misses. Stop if unsafe starts or false credits recur after correction.

## 8. Risks

- Technicians rush checks or hide hazards to keep their numbers up.
- Poor connectivity or unreliable start times distort who gets credit.
- Managers infer individual performance from small crews, undermining trust and reporting.
