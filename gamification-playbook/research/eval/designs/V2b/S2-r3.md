## 1. Diagnosis

The median review wait is 2.6 days. Before launch, the engineering lead walks two recent PRs with their reviewers. For a ready PR with a clear owner and passing required checks, find out whether the reviewer saw the request and had time to act. For a PR blocked by failing checks or missing context, confirm who can unblock it and how the author gets help. **Working assumption:** ready reviews get buried in the queue. If most cases are blocked or reviewers lack capacity, defer the pilot and fix routing, workload, or readiness first. The engineering lead triages blockers that working day and routes urgent reviews to the existing backup reviewer.

## 2. Credit rule

One credit per PR goes to the first peer who submits a qualified review within eight team working hours of the PR becoming ready for review with required checks passing. A qualified review is an approval that identifies something checked or a change request with a concrete next step. An acknowledgement earns no credit. The team metric is the percentage of eligible PRs that receive that review on time. Assume the Git host records the required history and the team has an agreed working calendar. Verify both before launch.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Ready PR gets a qualified review at hour six | One credit |
| Two reviewers respond, or the PR is reopened | At most one credit per PR |
| Review is withdrawn or found invalid | Remove provisional credit. A valid replacement counts only within the original window. |
| Review comes at hour nine, or someone edits the due date or reassigns the PR | Late. Use the original clock. |
| Feedback happens in chat or on a call | Counts when the reviewer records the qualified review on the PR within the window. |
| Ready PR gets no reviewer or no answer | Stays in the denominator and counts as late. |
| PR is blocked before it becomes ready, or author withdraws it before the deadline | Exclude and report the reason in aggregate. A withdrawal after the deadline leaves the result in place. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation in the existing review notification:** “This PR is ready for you. Review it by [time] so the author knows whether they can merge or what to change. If you’re booked, route it to [backup]. The pilot ends on 5 February.”
- **Confirmation:** “Review submitted on time. The author has your decision.”
- **Private Friday progress:** “4 of 5 eligible reviews answered within one working day. Your target is 4 of 5. Next up is [PR], due [time].” Set the target at 80% of each reviewer’s eligible assignments, rounded up. With fewer than three, show deadlines and completed reviews only. With zero, show “No reviews due.”
- **Restart:** “Your next review is [PR], due [time]. If you can’t take it, route it to [backup].”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The team and managers see one weekly aggregate against the team’s baseline, with eligible and blocked counts. Publish a weekly rate only when it covers at least five reviewers and ten PRs. The engineering lead can open individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log the purpose and close access when resolved. Exclude pilot metrics from performance reviews and discipline.

## 5. Add-on

Use private confirmation and team progress as the incentive. Skip prizes, rankings, and badges. The pitch to sceptical engineers is a quicker answer for the author and a clear way to hand off when busy.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead runs the existing Git report and samples five credited reviews weekly. A peer who did not author or review those PRs checks whether the recorded decision shows a real check or gives an actionable change. Budget **45 minutes total per week** across both people. If two of five fail, or one sampled approval misses a material defect, the lead pauses pilot reminders and progress reporting, corrects the affected credits, and investigates before resuming. Engineers can send the lead a PR link privately for corrections.

## 7. End and decision

Run **11 January–5 February 2027**. Use the four working weeks from **16 November–11 December 2026** as the predeclared baseline, applying the same eligibility and calendar rules. End pilot reminders and shared progress on 5 February. Keep routing fixes and private confirmation. Measure eligible PRs through **5 March** with the same quality sample. With one team, treat results as feasibility evidence.

Expand to another team if at least 80% of eligible PRs are answered on time during both windows, the follow-up is at least 15 percentage points above baseline, and follow-up triggers no quality pause. If speed improves but misses those thresholds, change routing or capacity before retrying. Stop the programme if follow-up returns to baseline or quality problems recur.

## 8. Risks

- Reviewers rubber-stamp to beat the clock. Sample the substance of approvals.
- Ready PRs disappear from the queue or stay marked blocked. Include unassigned PRs and inspect changes in the blocked count.
- Reminders shift work into bursts or the lift fades. Check the full four-week follow-up before expanding.
