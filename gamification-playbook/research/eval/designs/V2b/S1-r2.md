## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs reportedly get a digital checklist. Before launch, the field safety lead and a union steward walk two jobs with technicians, one where the app works and one where it fails or is skipped. Compare time, duplicate entry and rework against the current checklist. Use the programme where the app works and saves technicians effort. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds work, fix that first. Confirm paid time for the checklist, the approved fallback and the data rules with the union before launch. Dispatch handles immediate fallback questions and routes app blockers to the mobile app owner for a response within one business day.

## 2. Credit rule

When a dispatched job reaches its pre-start safety check, the assigned technician earns one credit per job ID for submitting a complete digital checklist before work starts and following the existing escalation procedure for any hazards found. Finding a hazard never costs credit. Use submission history and the existing job-start record to verify order, including approved offline submissions if their original timestamps can be verified.

Measure verified digital completions divided by all jobs started. Paper, blocked and missing cases stay in the denominator and appear separately in the team report.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Complete digital checklist before start | One credit |
| Duplicate submission | One credit total |
| Submission voided or found invalid | Correct the tally and tell the technician |
| Late submission or edited start time | Use the original history and any independently verified correction |
| Approved paper fallback | Record fallback use, with zero digital credit |
| Started job missing from app | Recover it from dispatch records and count it in the denominator |
| Job cancelled or stopped before work begins | Track safety stops separately from the adoption rate |

## 3. Worker copy

- **At the job cue** “Before you start, open this job’s safety checklist. Submit it here to save the check directly to the job record. If the app is unavailable, use the approved paper checklist. Follow the usual stop-work procedure if you find a hazard. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **After verification** “Pre-start check verified for this job. You’ve reached 2 of 3 for your weekly milestone.” While waiting for sync or verification, show “Saved. Verification pending.”
- **Progress** “Your next milestone check is on your next supported job. Complete the safety check on every job.” Size the milestone to up to three supported jobs per week. Lower it for technicians with one or two opportunities. Show “No supported jobs this week” at zero.
- **After a miss** “Start with your next supported job. Your previous checks still count in your history.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. Teams and managers see verified team adoption against the team’s baseline, plus aggregate fallback and missing-check counts. Suppress small groups or breakdowns that could identify someone. Limit individual access to named reviewers for corrections, disputes or technician-requested coaching. Log it, expire it after resolution and prohibit reuse of programme data for discipline.

## 5. Add-on

Use private milestones and shared team progress. Award no cash, prizes or ranks. The incentive is immediate confirmation and a reachable milestone, with no benefit from rushing a check or suppressing a hazard.

## 6. Operator card

The field safety lead runs the pilot in about 90 minutes per week using existing dispatch and app reports. An independent safety reviewer samples 10 jobs weekly across digital, fallback and missing cases, checking actual start sequence and whether recorded hazards were handled correctly. Pause programme scoring if one confirmed unsafe start was linked to the incentive, or if two of ten sampled jobs have a materially incomplete or late check. Keep the safety procedure active, investigate with the union steward and resume after correcting the cause. Technicians can dispute a tally through dispatch or their steward. Verify reliable job IDs and timestamp history before activating scoring.

## 7. End and decision

Use January 4–31, 2027 as baseline, February 1–28 as the pilot and March 1–28 as follow-up. Where several comparable crews exist, assign crews to either the programme or the same app support and submission confirmation throughout. Otherwise, use the declared baseline and treat the result as feasibility evidence.

End milestones and campaign reporting on February 28. Keep workflow fixes and private submission confirmation. On March 31, scale if adoption rises at least 15 percentage points during the pilot and retains at least 10 points during follow-up, after accounting for comparison crew changes where available, with no unresolved quality pause. Change the workflow and retest if gains fade or blockers dominate. Stop the incentive if it drives unsafe starts or produces no sustained improvement after retesting.

## 8. Risks

- **Rushed checks or hidden hazards.** Give credit regardless of hazard findings and independently sample quality.
- **Unequal connectivity, app access or job volume.** Keep a safe fallback, report blocked cases and size milestones to opportunity.
- **Distrust or a short-lived lift.** Agree visibility with the union before launch and measure behaviour for four weeks after campaign scoring ends.
