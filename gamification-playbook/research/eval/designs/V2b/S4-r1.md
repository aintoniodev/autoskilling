## 1. Diagnosis

The reported 12% adoption rate leaves three possibilities open: agents lack access or usable articles, documentation adds too much work at close, or agents miss the cue. Before launch, the KB lead shadows two live tickets end to end: one with a matching article and one where an article is missing or publishing is blocked. Compare close time and rework with the normal path, and check the baseline for how often blockers occur. The KB lead owns fixes and responds within two business days. Agents can resolve tickets normally and flag a blocker. Fix material extra work before launch and defer the pilot if most cases are blocked.

## 2. Credit rule

When an agent resolves a ticket, award **one credit per ticket ID** for linking an approved article that supports the actual resolution or linking a new article they wrote once it passes the existing publication review. Allow two business days after resolution for approval and linking. Credit the resolving agent. Existing articles can earn credit on multiple tickets when they genuinely apply.

Measure verified coverage as credited tickets divided by **all resolved support tickets**, including omissions and blocked cases. Exclude spam, test and merged duplicate records. Report blockers separately.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Relevant approved article linked, or new article published and linked on time | One credit |
| Multiple links or repeated close events | One credit maximum |
| Ticket reopened or article withdrawn | Reverse pending or recorded credit. Recheck at final resolution. |
| Link added late or close date edited | Use the recorded resolution event. Edits cannot extend the window. |
| Resolved the old way, draft only, or no KB activity | Zero credit. Ticket stays in the denominator. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution:** “Link the article you used so the next agent can reuse the fix. If it’s missing, capture the fix for KB review. Resolve the ticket as usual if you’re blocked. The pilot ends March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked and verified. This ticket counts toward your weekly goal.”
- **Progress:** “2 of 3 this week. On your next resolution, link the article that matches the fix.”
- **Restart:** “Start with your next resolved ticket. Link the fix or flag what’s missing.”

Start with up to three credits weekly, capped at each agent’s recent weekly ticket volume and adjusted down for leave or fewer opportunities. With zero opportunities, show “No eligible tickets this week.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own tickets and progress. Everyone, including managers, sees weekly team coverage against its own baseline, with no names. Suppress breakdowns that could identify individuals. Log and time-limit individual access for agent-requested coaching, corrections or disputes. Restrict programme data to those purposes and pilot evaluation.

## 5. Add-on

Use private goals and shared team progress as the incentive. The existing CSAT bonus follows its current terms. Agents can close a ticket while KB work is pending.

## 6. Operator card

**Assumptions:** Ticket and KB logs capture close, reopen, link, approval and withdrawal history. An existing editor can review new articles. Confirm both before launch.

The KB lead joins the existing reports weekly and independently checks five randomly selected credited tickets for resolution fit, accuracy and customer data. If two fail, or any exposes customer data, the KB lead pauses credit and prompts, corrects affected records and resumes after the cause is fixed. Agents request corrections through the existing KB queue. Budget **45 minutes weekly** for pilot administration, plus existing editorial review.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 11–February 7, 2027** as baseline, **February 8–March 7** as the pilot and **March 8–April 4** as follow-up. End weekly goals and team progress posts on March 7. Keep workflow fixes and private confirmation.

Scale if pilot coverage reaches **30% and gains at least 15 percentage points** over the verified baseline, follow-up retains **80% of the gain**, quality clears the pause rule and CSAT falls by less than **3 percentage points** versus baseline. Review CSAT response counts and ticket mix before deciding. Change the workflow and retest if uptake fades or blockers dominate. Stop if quality or CSAT crosses its threshold and the cause persists. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Risks

- Agents attach loosely related articles to hit the goal.
- Writing or waiting for review slows resolutions and puts CSAT bonuses under pressure.
- Coverage falls when weekly goals end because agents still spend more time documenting than they save through reuse.
