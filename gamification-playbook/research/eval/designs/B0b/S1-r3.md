# Pre-job safety checklist incentive

Run an eight-week pilot. Every technician covered by the pilot earns **one hour of paid personal time** if the company reaches 70% verified pre-job checklist completion in weeks 3–4, and **a second hour** if it reaches 85% in weeks 7–8. Technicians earn the hours regardless of their own completion rate, shift, or job volume. Agree the terms with union representatives before launch, including how earned time is scheduled and how disputed records are corrected.

## What earns credit

A checklist counts when the technician completes the job-specific checks before work begins and records the outcome. A valid decision to stop or escalate because of a hazard earns the same credit as a decision to proceed. Provide paid time for the check and include it in dispatch planning. Give technicians an offline option and an approved fallback for device failures or outages. A contemporaneous fallback record earns credit once verified.

## How it’s measured

Use dispatch records to count all jobs that reached the point where a pre-job check was required, including jobs stopped at that point. Exclude jobs cancelled before then and reconcile exclusions against dispatch records each week. Divide qualifying checks by eligible jobs across each two-week reward window. Record app completion time on the device so an offline check can sync later and still count. Use the dispatch work-start record to check timing for jobs that proceed, and the stop or escalation record for jobs that halt. Give technicians five working days after each window to sync offline records or request corrections before locking the result.

Each week, safety staff audit 30 randomly sampled credited jobs across shifts, job types, and recording methods. They check timing, whether the answers fit the job, and whether flagged hazards were acted on. Each reward window requires at least 90% of audited records to pass. Publish the sample size alongside the result. Use cases where timestamps disagree to investigate the workflow before treating them as individual failures.

## What people see

The app shows technicians whether each of their own jobs is recorded, awaiting sync or verification, or needs a correction. A weekly update shows everyone the company-wide completion rate, eligible job count, audit result, and progress toward the next reward. It also shows which recurring problems dispatch, safety, or IT is fixing. Agree access and retention with the union. Keep individual records private and pilot data out of performance ratings and disciplinary decisions.

## How it runs and ends

In weeks 1–2, confirm the roughly 35% baseline against the dispatch denominator and fix setup, offline sync, and scheduling problems. Publish the reward dates and rules at launch. After week 8, close the correction window, announce the results, and credit earned time in the next payroll cycle. The rewards expire on schedule. Keep paid checklist time, the fallback, and aggregate reporting, then review completion and audit quality with the union 30 days later. If completion slips, use technician feedback and job records to identify what made the routine harder to sustain.

## What could go wrong

- **Rushed checks or backdating.** A completion target can reward box-ticking. Audit job-specific answers and timing, and keep unusually fast submissions as prompts for review.
- **Pressure to clear a job or blame coworkers.** Give stopped jobs equal credit, keep individual scores private, and let technicians raise pressure concerns directly with safety staff or a union representative.
- **Unequal access.** Poor signal, shared devices, and tight routes can make some shifts miss credit. Review those patterns weekly, verify fallback records, and assign recurring scheduling or equipment fixes to dispatch or IT.
- **A disputed payout.** Publish the denominator, eligibility rules, and audit criteria before launch. Have safety and union representatives jointly resolve disputed exclusions and systemic recording issues before finalizing each window.
