## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward reliable logging as a ward team.** Each ward can earn a one-time €300 budget for a staff-chosen workplace improvement. Recognize all qualifying wards at their handovers so every shift is included. Fund sanitizer, dispensers, and kiosk repairs through normal hospital budgets. Award no individual prizes or points per entry.

**Make the expected action clear.** Nurses log completed hand-hygiene moments at the next safe break in care. A badge tap opens the kiosk, and the nurse selects the moment and approximate time. The kiosk confirms the entry and lets the nurse correct it. Collect no patient identifiers. In an emergency, care takes priority and the nurse can skip the entry.

### How a ward qualifies

- **Adoption.** In each of the final two pilot months, at least 80% of eligible nursing shifts on the ward have a kiosk entry. Use the staffing roster as the denominator, including agency and float nurses assigned to the ward. Publish eligibility rules before launch and show kiosk outages alongside the result. One entry establishes that the kiosk was used on a shift. It says nothing about how many moments were captured.
- **Logging accuracy and coverage.** During the existing monthly audits, infection prevention samples completed hand-hygiene actions and checks whether the corresponding entries appear by the end of the shift. Use a restricted reconciliation record with a temporary staff code, approximate time, and moment type. Also check sampled kiosk entries from those same observation windows for a corresponding observed action when the observer had a clear view. Set a pilot target of at least 80% matched in each direction across the final two months, with at least 30 assessable records in each direction per ward. Show the sample sizes and hold the award if more observations are needed.
- **Clinical safeguard.** Keep the monthly audit rate independent of kiosk data. Calculate it as observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities, using the hospital’s existing definitions. Use brief sessions at unannounced times across day, night, and weekend shifts, with a comparable sampling mix before and during the pilot. A drop of five percentage points or more from baseline triggers an infection-prevention review before any award. Show the underlying counts so a small sample can be interpreted appropriately. [WHO hand-hygiene observation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

### What staff see

The kiosk shows whether an entry was saved and how to correct it. A staff-only ward board shows weekly shift adoption and kiosk uptime, plus monthly audit compliance and logging match rates with their denominators. The charge nurse uses handover to share one barrier staff raised and who is fixing it. Individual logging history is available to the nurse. Restrict identifiable reconciliation data to infection prevention and delete it after verification. The board carries no individual scores or ward rankings.

### Timing and exit

Use one baseline month to train staff, test the kiosk on every shift, and establish comparable audit coverage. Run the incentive for three months and announce the end date at launch. Month one is for feedback and workflow fixes. Months two and three determine eligibility. Close out at the final audit and issue each earned award once. Continue monthly audits and check kiosk use in the first month after rewards end. Keep routine logging if it stays usable and sufficiently complete without the reward. If it falls away, investigate the workflow before expanding to other wards.

### What could go wrong

- **Token entries, duplicates, or invented logs.** Shift adoption alone can be gamed. Use the two-way audit match, review unusual batches privately, and investigate before crediting disputed entries.
- **Audit-day behavior or uneven sampling.** Rotate observation times, calibrate observers, and report sample sizes. Treat the audit as a sample of practice.
- **Queues, outages, or unequal access.** Test badge access for temporary staff and check kiosk placement with night-shift nurses. Record downtime centrally. If lost access makes a ward’s result unreliable, defer its eligibility decision until access is restored and there is enough usable data.
- **Logging interrupts care or creates a shared-touch hazard.** Place the kiosk away from the point of care, assign cleaning responsibility, and give staff a direct route to report delays. Pause logging on an affected ward if it interferes with care.
