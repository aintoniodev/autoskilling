# Eight-week knowledge-base adoption programme

## What earns the reward

The 60-person support team earns one hour of paid learning time per agent if, in each of the final two weeks, it reaches **90% knowledge-base coverage** and **at least 90% of audited links pass review**. Schedule the hour during normal shifts over the following month, with coverage arranged for every shift. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged.

At resolution, agents link the article that explains the fix. If no suitable article exists, they submit a draft or link a pending draft that covers the same issue. A draft earns credit once a reviewer approves it. Reusing an article and contributing a new one earn the same credit.

## How it is measured

- **Coverage** is the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article link or approved draft. Count each ticket once. If a ticket reopens, assess it against the final resolution. Credit approved drafts to the week the ticket was resolved, with a two-business-day review window after week close.
- **Exceptions** cover spam, duplicate tickets merged into a parent, and cases where documenting even a sanitized resolution would expose restricted information. Agents select a reason at close. Missing articles stay in the denominator, and invalid exceptions go back into it.
- **Quality** is checked weekly on 30 randomly sampled credited tickets, spread across queues and shifts, and 10 sampled exceptions, or all exceptions if there are fewer than 10. A link passes if the article matches the actual resolution, gives another agent enough detail to apply it, and contains no restricted information. Review all new drafts before publication.

## What people see

The ticket close screen shows **Link article**, **Submit or link draft**, and **Record exception**. Agents can close the ticket while a draft is under review. Each agent sees their own tickets with missing links, draft decisions, and audit feedback. The team sees weekly coverage, the quality audit result with its sample size, exception rates, and common article gaps. Managers use ticket examples in private coaching.

## How it runs and ends

- **Week 1.** Measure the prior four weeks under the programme’s rules so the reported 12% is comparable. Set up the close workflow, calibrate reviewers on real tickets, and schedule daily review coverage.
- **Weeks 2–6.** Run the workflow on every queue. Give agents time on shift to write and review articles. Once a week, use the most common gaps to choose which articles to improve.
- **Weeks 7–8.** Score the two qualifying weeks. Close scoring after the final review window, announce the outcome, and award the learning hour if earned. The incentive expires either way. Keep the close workflow in place and check coverage and quality four weeks later to see whether the routine has stuck.

## What could go wrong

- **Agents attach generic articles or overuse exceptions.** Failed links lose credit. If exception use doubles from baseline, audit the affected queue’s exceptions before certifying the result.
- **Drafts pile up or customers wait longer.** Give a rotating reviewer protected time and a two-business-day turnaround. Assign overdue drafts to the knowledge-base lead. Keep ticket closure independent of draft approval.
- **The new step hurts support quality.** Compare weekly CSAT, reopen rate, and median resolution time against the four-week baseline by queue. If CSAT falls 3 percentage points, reopen rate rises 2 points, or median resolution time rises 15% for two consecutive weeks, pause scoring in the affected queue and review a sample of tickets. If the workflow caused the decline, fix it before resuming. Both qualifying weeks must include all queues, so a pause can leave the reward unearned at week eight.
