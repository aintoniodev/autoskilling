## Before every job

Run a 12-week programme to raise verified pre-job checklist completion from about 35% to 90%. Give technicians paid time to complete the checklist at the job site. Agree the reward, measurement rules, and data access with union representatives before launch.

### What earns the reward

If the company reaches 90% verified completion across the final four weeks, every field technician gets one hour of paid release time, scheduled during a regular shift within the following month. Technicians who were on leave or had few assigned jobs share in the reward. There are no individual targets or crew prizes.

A checklist counts when a technician completes it before work begins. A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a stop, escalation, or reschedule earns the same credit as one that clears the job to proceed. Reported hazards, near misses, and injuries never reduce the reward.

### How it’s measured

Use dispatch records to count jobs where on-site work began or a technician reached the site and stopped for safety. Exclude jobs cancelled before arrival and duplicate dispatch records. Match one completed checklist to each job using its dispatch ID. For jobs that proceed, compare the checklist timestamp with the recorded work start. For jobs stopped on site, use the completed checklist and linked stop record. A checklist completed offline counts using its original timestamp once it syncs.

Keep missing, late, and unverified records visible as separate categories. Agree a paper fallback for device failures and a documented exception process for urgent callouts under the existing safety procedure. Report both separately, with union and safety representatives reviewing exclusions before the final result. Take a small random sample each week and check whether the answers reflect site conditions. Give technicians a private way to correct mismatched jobs and timestamps.

### What people see

In the app, show the current job’s checklist status, whether it has synced, and the action to take if a hazard is found. Each technician can see their own job records and flag an error. Share one company-wide update each week with the completion rate, job count, exception count, and fixes underway. Keep names and crew rankings out of the update. Use individual records for verification and support under the agreed access rules. Keep incentive records out of disciplinary and performance scoring.

### How it runs and ends

Use weeks 1–2 to verify the baseline against dispatch records, train technicians on paid time, and test offline completion and hazard escalation. Launch the reward in week 3. Fix app and dispatch problems during weeks 3–8, then use weeks 9–12 to determine whether the target was met. Allow one week after the close for offline syncs, corrections, and joint sign-off.

Publish the result and release any earned time. End the incentive on schedule even if the target is missed. Keep the checklist and paid completion time in the normal workflow. Check completion four weeks later to see whether it holds after the reward ends.

### What could go wrong

- **People tap through or log a later start time.** Compare a random sample with site conditions and dispatch records. Review repeated discrepancies before signing off on the result.
- **Technicians feel pressure to clear a job.** Make the stop action easy to find. Review credible reports of pressure promptly with union and safety representatives, and pause the incentive if it is compromising stop-work decisions.
- **Dispatch schedules leave no time for the check.** Review missed checks alongside scheduling and sync failures each week. Adjust the schedule or fix the app where those problems recur.
- **Completion rises while safety practice stays the same.** Review whether hazards found in sampled checklists led to appropriate action. Use those findings alongside the completion rate when deciding what to improve after the programme.
