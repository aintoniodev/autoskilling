## Three-ward hand-hygiene logging pilot

**Reward.** Recognize each ward that reliably logs hand hygiene while maintaining observed compliance. Every qualifying ward earns recognition at the nursing huddle and a €250 budget for a staff-chosen ward improvement, with input from day, evening, and night shifts. Each ward can earn the award once. There are no individual prizes, rankings, or rewards for the number of entries.

**What nurses do.** Clean hands at the point of care, then log the completed action at the next safe break in care. The kiosk uses a badge tap and a quick selection of the applicable hand-hygiene moment or moments. One action gets one entry, even when it covers multiple moments. The kiosk records ward, time, and moment without patient identifiers. Urgent care takes priority over logging.

**How wards qualify.** Infection Prevention measures two rates in the monthly audit.

- **Logging capture** is the percentage of observed, correctly performed hand-hygiene actions with a matching kiosk entry within 30 minutes. Observers record the action time and moment using a temporary staff code that Infection Prevention can match privately to kiosk records. Each entry can match only one action. Confirmed kiosk outages and observer-documented urgent care that prevents timely logging are excluded from this measure and reported separately.
- **Observed compliance** is the percentage of all observed hand-hygiene opportunities where nurses performed hand hygiene. This includes opportunities during kiosk outages and actions that were never logged. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

A ward qualifies if it captures **at least 80% in both of the final two monthly audits**, and its observed compliance pooled across those two months is **no more than five percentage points below its own baseline**. Use trained observers, unannounced sessions across all shifts and weekends, and comparable sampling across months. Aim for at least 60 opportunities and 40 eligible performed actions per ward per month. If either minimum is missed, add sessions within that month. If the sample stays too small, report insufficient evidence and withhold the award decision.

**What people see.** The kiosk confirms a successful save and provides a way to report a fault. At monthly huddles, staff see their own ward’s two rates, the underlying counts, the baseline, and one practical fix agreed with the ward lead. Individual matching records are restricted to Infection Prevention and deleted after the pilot review. Ward managers receive aggregate results.

**Timing and exit.** Run one baseline month with the kiosk live, brief training on every shift, and no rewards. Then run three incentive months. Use the first for feedback and workflow fixes and the final two for qualification. Announce the end date at launch, issue awards after the final audit, and retire the incentive. Keep monthly observation and recheck logging capture 30 and 90 days later. If capture drops, review kiosk access, workflow, and staff feedback before deciding whether to retain or revise the logging process.

**What could go wrong.**

- **Duplicate or fabricated entries.** One-to-one matching prevents extra taps from earning credit. Infection Prevention reviews unusual entry bursts and resolves discrepancies before scoring.
- **Better numbers only while observers are present.** Vary observation times and keep the audit method consistent across baseline and pilot. Report the figures as sampled performance.
- **Logging delays care or favors quieter shifts.** Place kiosks on normal staff routes, review capture and outages by shift, and fix access problems. Pause the incentive on any ward where logging interferes with care.
- **Team pressure discourages honest reporting.** Treat missed entries as workflow feedback. Give staff a private channel to report pressure or faults, and keep individual results out of performance reviews.
