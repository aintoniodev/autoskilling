## Eight-week knowledge-base incentive programme

### What earns the reward

Every resolved ticket gets a knowledge-base disposition. Agents link an existing article that supports the resolution, link a draft when the resolution is reusable and no suitable article exists, or record a reason from a short list of exceptions, such as spam, duplicates, or account-specific work with no reusable guidance. The ticket form searches the knowledge base and suggests articles. Agents can close a ticket with a draft attached, and a rotating reviewer checks drafts within two working days.

The 60-person support org earns **two hours of paid, agent-chosen learning time per person** if it meets the final two-week targets. Schedule the time across shifts with queue coverage. Give opt-in recognition in the weekly huddle for specific contributions, such as an article another agent used to resolve a ticket or a correction that fixed misleading guidance. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and eligibility in place.

### How it is measured

Use the prior four weeks to establish baselines for article coverage, CSAT, and seven-day reopen rate. The reported starting coverage is 12%. Check that the historical calculation uses the same denominator before using it to report improvement.

- **Article coverage** is the share of unique resolved tickets with a linked published article or a linked draft that passes review by the end of the scoring window. Keep exceptions in the denominator and show them separately. Count a reopened ticket once, using its final resolution.
- **Disposition completion** is the share of resolved tickets with an article link, a submitted draft, or a documented exception. Show pending and rejected drafts separately so agents can act on them.
- **Quality** comes from a weekly random review of 30 linked tickets and up to 15 exceptions, spread across queues and shifts. Reviewers check that the article actually supports the resolution, is accurate, and contains no customer-sensitive details. They check that each sampled exception is justified. Use the same rubric across teams.

The reward requires **at least 70% article coverage and 95% disposition completion in each of weeks 7 and 8**, plus **at least 90% of sampled links and 90% of sampled exceptions passing review** across those two weeks. Compared with baseline, CSAT must fall by no more than 2 percentage points and the seven-day reopen rate must rise by no more than 1 point. Show the number of CSAT responses and review changes in queue mix before interpreting a breach. If the CSAT sample is too sparse to judge, extend the measurement window by up to two weeks without changing the targets.

### What people see

At close, agents see the article search, draft option, and exception menu. Each agent gets a private view of their missing dispositions, pending drafts, and reviewer feedback. The shared weekly dashboard shows org-wide coverage against target, completion, exception mix, quality results, CSAT with response count, and reopen rate. Managers use queue-level breakdowns to find missing articles, search problems, or review bottlenecks. Skip individual rankings and article-count targets.

### How it runs and ends

Use week 1 to demo the workflow and give agents scheduled time to try it. In weeks 2–6, publish results weekly and use huddles to fix the most common gaps. Score weeks 7–8 and announce the result once the final seven-day reopen window closes. Award the learning time if the gates pass. Then retire the incentive and keep the ticket workflow, review rota, and monthly coverage and quality checks. Check coverage 30 days later to see whether the behavior held.

### What could go wrong

- **Agents attach a generic article or overuse exceptions.** Reviewers give ticket-specific feedback, and managers investigate spikes in use of a single article or exception reason.
- **Agents create duplicates or put customer details in drafts.** Show related articles before drafting and send drafts through review before publication. Credit approved updates through the same workflow as new articles.
- **Documentation slows support or puts the CSAT bonus under pressure.** Give agents scheduled time to write longer articles. If CSAT or reopens breach the thresholds during the rollout, inspect the affected queues that week and fix the workflow before pushing for more coverage.
- **Queues have unequal opportunities to contribute.** Use a shared target and compare exception patterns by queue. Treat a queue with many one-off cases differently in coaching from a queue missing articles for recurring issues.
