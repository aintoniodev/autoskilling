## Eight-week knowledge-base adoption programme

**Reward useful knowledge capture.** On every resolved ticket, the agent records one of three outcomes in the help desk: link an article that answers the issue, submit a new article or correction for review, or select an approved exception. A new article or correction earns credit once a reviewer accepts it. If several tickets need the same article, agents can attach them to one draft and receive credit when it passes review. Give agents scheduled time to write and review articles during the shift. Keep the existing CSAT bonus terms in place.

**Use one shared reward.** If the team reaches 85% valid coverage in each of the final two weeks and at least 90% of audited links pass across those weeks, everyone earns two hours of paid time for a learning activity of their choice. Schedule it within the following month, with staggered coverage. Use 40% by week 2 and 65% by week 4 as coaching milestones. Managers can recognise a particularly useful article in the weekly huddle by showing the problem it solves.

## Measurement

- **Count unique resolved tickets.** Valid coverage is the share of eligible resolved tickets with a relevant article link or an accepted draft or correction. A ticket earns credit once, regardless of how many articles are linked or how many times it is closed. A reopened ticket is assessed after its final resolution. Keep draft reviews to two business days and attribute accepted drafts to the ticket’s resolution week.
- **Track exceptions separately.** Allow exceptions for cases such as spam, duplicate contacts consolidated into a parent ticket, and account-specific requests with no reusable guidance. Audit exception use and report coverage across **all** resolved tickets alongside eligible coverage. Use the two weeks before launch to recalculate the reported 12% baseline under the same rules.
- **Check whether the link actually helps.** Each week, randomly sample 30 linked tickets across queues and 10 exceptions. A link passes if the article applies to the issue, is accurate, and is usable by its intended audience. Review every new draft before publication for accuracy, duplication, and sensitive information. Remove invalid credits and expand the audit in any queue with repeated failures before certifying the result.
- **Watch service quality weekly.** Compare CSAT, seven-day reopen rate, and median resolution time with the pre-launch baseline by queue. Show CSAT response counts. If a queue’s reopen rate rises by more than 2 percentage points or median resolution time rises by more than 15% for two consecutive weeks, investigate ticket mix and writing workload. Hold reward certification if the investigation finds the programme caused a deterioration, including a sustained CSAT decline.

## What people see

At resolution, agents see the article search, draft option, and exception menu. Privately, they see their own unlinked tickets, pending reviews, and audit feedback with a way to correct mistakes. The team sees a weekly dashboard with coverage, audit pass rate, exception rate, review backlog, and service metrics. Managers use individual data for coaching. Public recognition highlights useful articles and their authors.

## How it ends

Close the reward window at week 8. Seven days later, settle reopened tickets, complete pending reviews and audits, and publish the result. Retire the reward and weekly milestones. Keep the resolution workflow, assign a named knowledge-base owner, and run a monthly quality sample. Recheck coverage four weeks after the reward ends to see whether the habit holds.

## What could go wrong

- **Agents paste generic links or overuse exceptions.** Audit relevance and exception reasons, correct the counts, and coach repeated patterns.
- **Agents delay closures or rush customers to protect their numbers.** Monitor resolution time, reopens, and CSAT together. Give agents protected writing time and clear the review backlog when it builds.
- **Some queues have fewer reusable issues or more writing work.** Compare queue-level coverage and exception patterns before coaching. Rotate review duty and give queues with recurring knowledge gaps extra authoring time.
- **Duplicate or sensitive articles pile up.** Search before drafting, let agents attach tickets to a shared draft, and require review before publication.
