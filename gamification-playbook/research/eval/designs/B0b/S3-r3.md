## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward.** Give each ward that meets the pilot target a $300 team fund for a staff-chosen break-room improvement and a thank-you at handover on every shift. All three wards can earn it. The target is to accurately log at least 80% of the completed hand-hygiene actions sampled in **both** of the final two monthly audits. Set the target and announce the end date before launch. Each verified action counts once, even when it covers several hand-hygiene moments.

**Measurement.** Nurses badge in, select the applicable moment or moments, and confirm the entry at the next safe pause. If entry is delayed, they can record the approximate time of the action before the end of the shift. The kiosk collects no patient identifiers. Urgent care takes priority, and nurses can flag kiosk downtime or access problems at handover.

- **Logging accuracy** is the number of observed completed actions with a matching kiosk entry divided by the number of completed actions observed during sampled windows when the kiosk was available. Infection prevention matches entries one-to-one by temporary staff code, approximate time and moment. Aim for at least 50 observed completed actions per ward per month for the reward decision, with coverage across days, nights and weekends. If a ward falls short, schedule a make-up observation before closing that month’s results.
- **Clinical compliance** is the number of hand-hygiene actions observers see performed divided by all opportunities they observe. Trained observers follow the same protocol before and during the pilot, vary observation times, and complete their counts before seeing kiosk data. Compare each ward with its own pre-pilot audit results. Infection prevention investigates declines in compliance or reports of delayed care before approving the award. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

**What staff see.** The kiosk confirms each submission and allows corrections. At weekly handover, staff see the share of staffed nursing shifts on their ward with any kiosk use, along with access issues and fixes. After each monthly audit, they see the ward’s verified logging rate and observed compliance side by side, with the numerator and denominator for each. Individual scores and ward rankings stay off the display. Access to the temporary staff-code mapping is limited to the audit team, which deletes it after the results are verified.

**Timing and exit.** Run the pilot for three months. Use the first month to train every shift, measure initial uptake and fix kiosk placement or access. Score the second and third months, then issue the one-time awards after the final audit. Turn off reward tracking. Keep the kiosk if it fits the workflow and the audit shows credible logging across shifts. Continue routine monthly compliance audits and check kiosk use four weeks after the award to see whether the habit persists.

**What could go wrong.** Duplicate or invented entries can inflate apparent uptake. Review unusual bursts and unmatched entries in windows where the nurse was continuously observed before certifying results. Staff may log more when observers are present, so check whether ordinary shifts use the kiosk and treat the verified rate as a sample. A poorly placed or unreliable kiosk can disadvantage busy or overnight shifts. Review missed access by shift, fix the cause, and repeat affected observations. If staff report pressure to enter false data or delay care, pause scoring on that ward while infection prevention investigates.
