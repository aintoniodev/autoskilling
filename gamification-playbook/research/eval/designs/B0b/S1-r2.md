## Before every job

Run an eight-week pilot for all 300 technicians. The goal is to raise verified pre-job checklist completion from roughly 35% to at least 85% in the final two weeks. Before launch, agree on the reward, paid work time, data access, and appeals with union representatives. Give technicians a paid practice session and build checklist time into dispatch schedules.

### What earns the reward

Technicians earn a one-time $50 bonus, or a locally agreed equivalent, by completing a valid checklist before at least 90% of their eligible job starts during the pilot. The bonus is the same regardless of job volume. For technicians with fewer than 10 eligible jobs, apply the same threshold to their actual opportunities. Technicians with no eligible jobs receive a fresh eight-week window when they return to field work.

A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a pause, escalation, or cancellation earns credit. Follow the existing stop-work procedure. Injury rates, the number of hazards reported, and time spent on the checklist have no effect on the reward.

### How completion is measured

Use dispatch records to identify jobs requiring a checklist, including jobs paused or canceled after the technician arrives. Match each job to its checklist using the job ID and compare the checklist completion time with the existing job-start record. For a job stopped before it starts, use the documented stop-work record. A valid checklist has all required responses and records any required escalation.

The app records completion offline and syncs later. If the app or device fails, technicians use the existing safety checklist and log the outage. Verified fallback checklists count toward the bonus. Report digital and fallback completion separately so fallback use stays visible. Log canceled dispatches before arrival and other exclusions with a reason, and have a joint union-management group review them weekly.

Each week, safety staff sample completed records across crews, shifts, and job types. Check that records match field conditions and that any hazards requiring escalation were handled. Let technicians explain discrepancies and correct record errors before deciding eligibility.

### What people see

The app gives each technician a private view of jobs counted, jobs completed on time, pending syncs or exceptions, and whether they’re on track for the bonus. Send one reminder at the point in the workflow when the checklist is due. Publish weekly company-wide completion, fallback use, and audit findings in aggregate. Limit individual records to the technician and the staff who need them to resolve exceptions or administer the pilot. Pilot scores stay out of performance reviews and disciplinary decisions.

### How it ends

Use the four weeks before launch as the baseline and recalculate it with the same eligibility rules wherever records allow. At week eight, close scoring, give technicians five working days to challenge their records, and pay confirmed bonuses in the next practicable pay cycle. Retire the bonus and progress display. Keep the pre-job reminder, paid checklist time, and offline fallback.

Four weeks later, review whether completion held without the bonus. The joint group reviews completion, audit findings, time pressure reported by technicians, and whether hazards are being escalated. Share the results and which workflow problems the company will fix.

### What could go wrong

- **Technicians tap through or log the job start late.** Audit checklist content against field conditions and investigate timestamp discrepancies. If the records cannot establish when work began, resolve the measurement issue before using them to decide payouts.
- **The bonus pressures people to clear a job.** Give full credit for a documented stop. If workers report pressure to proceed or skip steps, suspend scoring while the joint group investigates.
- **Dispatch schedules or device failures make the target unfair.** Review missed jobs by shift, route, and device. Correct verified record errors before payout and fix recurring scheduling or equipment problems during the pilot.
- **Completion drops when the money ends.** Use the four-week follow-up to identify which crews or job types lost ground and address the barriers in their workflow.
