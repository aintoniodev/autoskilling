## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward resolved tickets with a useful article.** Give each support team a four-week window to reach 50% coverage across its final two weeks. If the team reaches 50% and passes the quality check, everyone on the team gets one extra paid hour off. Announce the reward at kickoff and have the support lead recognise the team at its regular huddle. Let people book the hour across shifts with queue coverage. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules in place.

**Give agents time to make the workflow work.** In the first two weeks, ask agents from different shifts to walk through recent tickets and show where finding, linking or writing an article slows them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected work time to practise on real tickets. Fix the biggest blockers and share what changed at the next huddle. Before rewards start, put article search in the ticket resolution flow and offer a prefilled draft for missing answers. Let agents skip an irrelevant suggestion and log a knowledge gap when an article cannot safely be written.

**Count each ticket once.** Use existing support and knowledge-base records. The rule agents hear is **“A resolved ticket counts once when it links to an approved article that explains or supports the resolution.”** A new article counts once it is approved and linked. A good existing article earns the same credit. Divide qualifying tickets by all tickets resolved in the window so the result stays comparable with the current 12%. A ticket closed through the old workflow or with a logged knowledge gap stays in the denominator. Duplicate links add no credit. Reopening reverses the count until the ticket is resolved again, at which point it can count once. Before launch, the support operations lead checks these cases, plus a late approval and an irrelevant link, against actual records.

**Show agents their own progress.** Confirm credit in the ticket when it qualifies or when a pending article is approved. Give agents a private view of their credited tickets and a personal goal of one qualifying resolution for every two tickets they close. Progress accumulates across the four weeks, so an absence costs no streak and small caseloads mean fewer tickets to cover. Post each team’s percentage and distance to 50% where the team already checks its stats. Managers see team totals only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews.

**Use the existing QA pass.** Each week, QA checks 10 credited tickets per active wave for relevance, accuracy and customer data in articles. If two or more fail, the support lead pauses rewards for that wave until the cause is fixed and the next sample passes. A customer-data exposure triggers an immediate pause and follows the existing incident process. Support operations corrects wrong counts, tells the affected agent privately and updates the team total. Review team CSAT in the existing huddle. If it falls five percentage points below its pre-pilot baseline for two consecutive weeks with at least 30 responses across those weeks, pause rewards for that team and use QA to check for rushed resolutions. Reusing the QA pass, reports and huddle keeps weekly programme administration under an hour.

**Stagger the reward and decide after it ends.** Give all teams the workflow fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Start half the teams in weeks 3–6 and half in weeks 5–8, balancing the waves by ticket mix and baseline coverage. Compare coverage in weeks 3–4 to see what the reward adds while both waves have the same workflow. End prizes on schedule and leave article search and drafting in the resolution flow. For each wave, measure qualifying coverage over weeks 3–4 after prizes end. Use the combined, ticket-weighted result to decide what happens next. **At 40% or above**, keep the workflow and feedback as standard. **At 25–39%**, fix the most common remaining barrier and recheck. **Below 25%**, retire the rally and revisit the workflow with agents.

The main failure modes are generic links, duplicate articles, rushed closes and pressure to link something on tickets with no safe reusable answer. Equal credit for reuse, approval for new articles, the QA pause and the skip option address those risks. The post-reward check shows how much of the lift lasts once the prize is gone.
