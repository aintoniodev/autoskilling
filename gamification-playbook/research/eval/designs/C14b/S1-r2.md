## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Before launch.** Ask the union to nominate technicians from different shifts and routes for a paid walkthrough. Have them try the checklist on a routine job, at a site with poor signal, and on a job they need to stop. Fix the friction they find, give everyone paid practice time, and tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Agree with the union on the counting rule, reward, data access, and appeal process before announcing the pilot.

**What earns credit.** “One credit per job when you finish the digital checklist before work starts and record the required action for any hazard you find.” A checklist that leads to a stop-work decision earns the same credit as one that clears a job to proceed. The team earns a 30-minute paid break for every member, scheduled in rotation, when it reaches 80% over two consecutive weeks with the quality gate open. Name the reward at launch and have the field lead thank the crew in person for a specific safety practice. The safety procedure applies on every job throughout the pilot.

**How it’s counted.** Use the existing job ID, checklist completion time, and recorded work-start or safety-hold event. The denominator is dispatched jobs that reach the pre-work safety check, including jobs stopped for a hazard. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. A duplicate earns one credit, a voided checklist loses its credit until a valid replacement qualifies, and a paper fallback follows the existing safety procedure and stays visible in the denominator. Accept offline completions when the app preserves the original completion time on sync. Before launch, run these cases through the report alongside a late checklist, a hazard stop, and a job completed the old way. Verify that the existing start event reflects when work begins. Hold the reward launch if the records cannot establish the order reliably.

**What people see.** After submitting, technicians see credit confirmed or pending sync. Their private view shows qualifying jobs out of their own eligible jobs, with an 80% target sized to their caseload. A week off costs them nothing and their next eligible job counts as usual. Post team progress in the existing safety huddle or crew channel. Managers see team totals only, grouped where needed to protect privacy. Pilot data stays out of performance reviews. The pilot coordinator corrects disputed counts from the existing job record, with a union steward available to help raise a dispute.

**Timing and ending.** Give all crews the fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2 and measure their updated baseline against the reported 35%. Start four-week reward windows in weeks 3 and 5 across comparable crews, with the start order agreed with the union. Compare the groups in weeks 3–4 to see what the reward adds. Before each window closes, make the checklist the first screen when opening an eligible job, with job details prefilled. Four weeks after each crew’s reward ends, use the same measure to decide whether to keep the rollout as designed. At 80% or above, keep the workflow. At 60–79%, fix the remaining friction and recheck. Below 60%, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians. The pre-job safety requirement continues throughout.

**What could go wrong.** The reward could encourage rushed answers, backdating, or pressure to clear an unsafe job. Use the existing weekly safety review to sample 10 checklist records across crews, including any available stop-work cases. If two of 10 have material omissions, or any reviewed job started with an unresolved critical hazard, the safety lead pauses reward accrual and addresses the issue with the union steward before restarting. Review fallback rates by crew to spot connectivity or device problems early. Give one coordinator the weekly report and corrections, and use the existing huddle for quality review so pilot administration stays under an hour a week.
