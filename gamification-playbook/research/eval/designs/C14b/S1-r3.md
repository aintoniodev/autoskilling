## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Reward crews for completing one accurate digital safety checklist before each job. A checklist that identifies a hazard and puts the job on hold earns the same credit as one that clears the job to start. Each crew that reaches **75% of eligible jobs over its four-week reward window** earns one hour of paid time off per crew member. Announce the reward at launch, recognise crews at their existing safety huddle, and let members schedule the hour around shift coverage.

### Before launch

Ask technicians from different shifts and routes, union representatives, dispatch, and safety staff to walk through a real job. Give technicians paid time to practise. Fix problems they identify, such as poor signal, repeat data entry, or a checklist that takes too long to find, and show crews what changed. Agree with union representatives on the reward, eligibility, data access, and count corrections before announcing the pilot.

Put the checklist first on the job screen and prefill job details. Leave every safety answer for the technician to verify. Keep the approved fallback available when the app fails and preserve the technician’s authority to stop the job.

### What counts

Tell crews the rule in one sentence. **A job counts once when its signed digital checklist is recorded before work starts, or when the checklist documents the hazard that put the job on hold.**

Match the checklist to the existing job ID and use existing job-start and safety-hold records. Assign one checklist owner on multi-technician jobs so the crew earns one credit. Count jobs that start and jobs put on a documented safety hold. Exclude jobs cancelled before either event. A correctly completed paper fallback satisfies the safety procedure and stays in the adoption denominator. Exclude a verified app outage from reward scoring, using existing incident records, and show how many jobs were excluded. Use offline digital submissions only when the app already preserves a reliable completion timestamp.

Before launch, test a duplicate, a voided checklist, a late submission, a safety hold, and a paper fallback against real job records. A duplicate adds zero, a void removes the credit, and a late submission earns zero. The pilot coordinator resolves count disputes privately and corrects the tally before awarding paid time.

### What people see

After submission, show the technician which job was recorded and whether it qualified, or that it is pending sync. Each technician can see their own eligible jobs and completions, with a personal goal of three in four. A missed shift or week carries no penalty. Show each crew its running total and the 75% goal at the existing safety huddle. Managers receive crew totals only, with small groups combined where a total would identify an individual. Pilot data stays out of performance reviews. Publish no individual rankings.

### Timing and ending

Give all crews the fixes, checklist default, and feedback first. Measure two weeks of adoption under those conditions, then start the four-week reward window for half the crews. Start the other half two weeks later, balancing the groups by job volume and shift mix. Compare adoption during those two weeks to estimate what the reward adds. Keep the checklist default and feedback after each crew’s reward window closes.

Four weeks after the final reward window, review the **share of eligible jobs with a qualifying digital checklist**, including fallback jobs. At **75% or above**, keep the workflow as configured. At **60–74%**, fix the most common remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **60%**, retire the incentive design and review how the app fits field work. The pre-job safety requirement applies throughout.

### What could go wrong

- **Technicians rush through answers or feel pressure to start an unsafe job.** Use the existing weekly safety review to sample ten credited jobs across crews. Two materially inaccurate checklists in a sample, or one confirmed case of reward-related pressure to bypass a safety hold, pauses rewards. The safety lead decides when to resume after reviewing the cause with the union representative and affected crew.
- **Connectivity or dispatch records distort the tally.** Review mismatches privately and check outage exclusions by route. Do a dry run before launch. If the existing records cannot establish that the checklist came first, fix that gap before attaching paid time.
- **The reward creates peer pressure or extra administration.** Keep progress at crew level, protect absences, and handle corrections privately. Use existing reports and the existing safety review so one coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
