# One working day for a first code review

**Make time for reviews.** In weeks 1–2, ask engineers from both review groups to walk through a few PRs that took longer than a day. Use their findings to fix routing, unclear ownership and oversized PRs. Give the on-duty reviewer a protected 30-minute block each working day and name a backup for absences. Put ready PRs oldest first in the review queue engineers already use. Share one concrete fix in the team channel so engineers can see what changed before any reward starts.

**Count a useful first review.** A PR earns credit once when a teammate submits a substantive review within eight working hours of the PR becoming ready. An approval after checking the change, a request for changes with a concrete next step, or a review that identifies a specific blocker all qualify. Use the existing Git host’s ready and submitted-review events. Publish the working-hours calendar for each review group, including holidays. The clock starts even if routing fails, and reassignment, duplicate reviews and reopening the same PR earn no new credit. A withdrawn review loses credit unless another qualifying review met the original deadline. Feedback given live or in chat counts when the reviewer submits a short summary on the PR within the deadline. Test these cases against recent PRs before launch.

**Measure the share answered on time.** The denominator is all eligible human-authored PRs whose review deadline has passed, including those still waiting or closed after the deadline. Exclude drafts that never became ready, bot PRs and PRs genuinely withdrawn before the deadline. The first two weeks establish the on-time baseline under this definition. The current median of 2.6 days gives context, but the on-time share is the number that drives the decision.

**Give engineers feedback and a shared reward.** After a qualifying review, send the reviewer a private confirmation through the existing review notification channel. Their private view shows the reviews they picked up and the share answered on time, with an 80% goal scaled to their actual volume across the reward window. A missed day or week leaves the next PR as a fresh chance. The team channel shows weekly on-time counts and percentages, and managers receive the same totals. Programme data stays out of performance reviews. Announce at launch that each group earns a company-paid team lunch if it answers at least 80% of eligible PRs on time across its four-week window and clears the quality check. The engineering lead hosts the lunch and thanks the group for specific improvements.

**Stagger the four-week reward windows.** Both groups get the workflow fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. One group starts rewards in week 3 and the other in week 5. Use existing review groups or rotations so work can stay in its usual queue. Compare the groups’ change from baseline in weeks 3–4 for an early read on what the reward adds, allowing for differences in PR volume and complexity. By week 8, make the oldest-first queue and backup routing the default and keep review time on the rota. Four weeks after the last reward window, measure the on-time share again. Keep the workflow if it holds at 80% or better, adjust it if it improves on baseline but falls below 80%, and retire the reward programme if it returns to baseline or worse.

**Watch for rubber stamps, cherry-picking and bad counts.** In the existing weekly engineering meeting, sample five credited reviews across both groups, including any that look unusually quick. Check that each review engaged with the change and gave the author a clear disposition or next step. If two of five fail, the engineering lead pauses reward accrual for the affected group and resumes it after a passing sample. Easy PRs can still crowd out difficult ones, so use the same meeting to look at the oldest overdue PRs and assign help. Engineers can flag a scoring error privately to the rota owner, who checks the Git events and corrects the total. Budget 45 minutes a week for the rota owner to sample reviews, resolve corrections and post team progress.
