## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward.** Each ward can earn a hospital-funded meal on every shift, with break coverage and an in-person thank-you from the nursing director. Announce the reward at launch and confirm with day and night nurses that they’d value it. The rule staff hear is “Your ward earns the meal if it reaches its verified kiosk logging goal over two monthly audits and maintains hand-hygiene compliance.”

**Make logging easy first.** Give a few nurses from each shift protected time to try the kiosk during a normal round. Fix placement, queues, sign-in friction and cleaning arrangements before the reward starts. At the next huddle, show what changed because of their feedback. Make the ward prefilled and the logging screen the default. Nurses clean their hands at the required moment, then log at the next safe break in care. During an outage, use the approved fallback and resume kiosk use at the next eligible moment.

**Measure real work.** During the existing monthly audit, observers use a small preset sample of rounds where they can see both the hand-hygiene action and whether it was logged by the end of the round. They tally at ward level only. The **verified capture rate** is the share of correctly performed, sampled moments with one confirmed kiosk entry. A duplicate counts once, a void counts zero, and a fallback entry counts as completed hygiene with no kiosk credit. Keep ambiguous observations out of the rate and report how many were excluded. Before launch, test the tally on a duplicate, a void, an old-method entry and an urgent-care interruption. If observers cannot verify the link within the existing audit without identifying staff or disrupting care, redesign the logging flow before offering a prize.

Set each ward’s target from its first month with the improved kiosk. Aim for a **10-percentage-point increase, capped at 90%**. Wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. Pool the two reward audits and use a pilot minimum of 30 verifiable moments spanning day and night shifts. If the sample falls short, defer the decision to the next routine audit. Infection Prevention pauses the reward if routinely audited hand-hygiene compliance drops **5 percentage points or more** from the ward’s pre-reward baseline or if logging delays care. The ward lead takes tally disputes at the existing huddle, the audit lead corrects them, and Infection Prevention decides when to resume.

**What staff see.** The kiosk confirms each saved entry immediately and offers an undo. At the existing huddle, show the ward’s verified capture rate, sample size, target and latest hand-hygiene audit result. Managers see the same ward totals. The programme collects no new staff identifiers and supplies no data for performance reviews. A missed shift or week carries no penalty. The next eligible moment is a fresh chance to log.

**Run and end.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback in month 1. Draw the start order and run two reward months per ward, starting in months 2, 3 and 4. Use wards still waiting to start as a rough check on what the reward adds. Give each qualifying ward its meal after its reward window and end prizes on schedule. Keep the kiosk defaults and routine feedback. Check verified capture again at the first two monthly audits after prizes stop. Keep the workflow if the pooled rate meets the ward target and clears the safety guardrail. If it stays above baseline but misses target, fix the largest remaining barrier. If it returns to baseline or falls below it, end the kiosk logging push and review whether the workflow merits further investment. Budget under an hour a week for the pilot owner outside existing audits and huddles.

**Watch for gaming and blind spots.** Bulk entries, duplicates and logging without cleaning earn no credit. Keep audit windows unannounced, vary the sampled shifts, and check whether exclusions cluster around busy rounds or certain shifts. Visible observers can change behaviour, and three wards provide only a rough comparison. Treat the results as evidence about the sampled workflow.
