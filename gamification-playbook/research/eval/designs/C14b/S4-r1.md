## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward a useful article on a resolved ticket.** Use one rule at launch: “A resolved support ticket earns one credit when it links to a relevant, approved article within two business days of resolution, whether you reused it or wrote it.” If an article already answers the question, link it. If there’s a gap, use the existing article review process. Resolve the customer’s issue as soon as it’s ready and add the approved link afterward if needed.

**Fix the workflow first.** In weeks 1–2, ask agents across shifts to show where finding, linking, or publishing an article slows them down. Give each agent 30 minutes of paid queue coverage to practice on real cases. Fix the main blockers, show agents what changed, and start the pilot once they can reliably add a link during normal ticket work. Give all teams the fixes and feedback at the same time.

**Measure with existing ticket records.** Divide credited tickets by all distinct resolved support tickets. Exclude merged duplicates, spam, and test tickets using existing dispositions. Every other resolved ticket stays in the denominator, including tickets resolved through the old workflow. Multiple links earn one credit. If a ticket reopens or its link is removed, reverse the credit and reassess after final resolution. Before launch, have Support Operations check these cases against the existing records and recalculate the reported 12% baseline using the pilot rule. Support Operations owns count corrections through the existing support channel.

**Make progress visible and the reward reachable.** Use the existing ticket confirmation to show agents when a link qualifies. Give each agent a private weekly goal of linking articles on half their resolved tickets, rounded up. Each week is a fresh start. Post team totals and the team’s percentage in the existing team channel. Managers get team totals only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews and leave the CSAT bonus calculation as it is.

At launch, announce the team reward: **one hour of paid time off per team member** if the team reaches 60% over any two consecutive pilot weeks with quality checks passing. Include agents who were on leave or had low ticket volume. Have the support director recognize the team at its regular huddle and arrange staggered time off with queue coverage.

**Catch shortcuts and customer harm.** In the existing weekly QA pass, review 10 randomly selected credited tickets across active teams, with each team represented. Check whether the article actually addresses the issue, is accurate, and contains no customer-specific information. Also spot-check two uncredited tickets for missed links or misuse of exclusions. If two or more credited tickets fail, pause reward qualification for the affected teams. The support lead decides when to resume after Support Operations corrects the counts and the next sample passes. Use the existing CSAT report as a separate guardrail. If an active team’s CSAT falls at least five percentage points below its pre-pilot baseline for two consecutive weeks, with at least 20 responses in each week, pause that team’s reward qualification and review the cases with QA. Check specifically for premature closes and delays while agents wait for article approval. Fold the sampling and score update into existing reviews so one owner can administer the pilot in under an hour a week.

**End rewards after four weeks per team.** Start half the existing teams in week 3 and the rest in week 5. Compare the teams’ change in linked-ticket rate during weeks 3–4, when both groups have the workflow fixes and one group has the reward. Before the first cohort’s prizes end, make article suggestions and the link field the default in the resolve screen for everyone, with a skip option for cases without a suitable article. Keep that workflow after prizes end in weeks 6 and 8.

Four weeks after each cohort finishes, measure its credited-ticket rate using the same rule. Use the combined rate across both cohorts to decide whether to **keep** the default workflow as configured at 60% or higher, **change** it and retest at 35–59%, or **drop** the incentive design below 35%. Resolve any outstanding QA or CSAT breach before declaring the pilot successful. Paid rewards expire on schedule in all three cases.
