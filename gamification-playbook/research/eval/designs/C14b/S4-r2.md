## Knowledge-base adoption rally

**Start by making the work easier.** Give a small group of agents across shifts and queues paid time to resolve real tickets with the knowledge-base tool. Ask where search, permissions, or writing an article slows them down or risks a poor customer experience. Fix the biggest blockers before the rally, show agents what changed in their regular huddle, and give everyone protected practice time. Put article search and linking in the ticket resolution panel, with a prefilled draft when an article is missing. Keep an exception path for cases where linking or writing an article would be inappropriate.

**Reward one finished ticket.** Publish this rule at launch: “A resolved ticket earns one credit when it links to a relevant, usable knowledge-base article, including one you wrote, and stays resolved for seven days.” A published article can help on many tickets, but each ticket ID counts once. Drafts, generic links, duplicate links, and old-workflow closures without a valid link earn zero. A reopen removes pending credit. A later qualifying closure can earn it once. Use the existing ticket status, article link, and publication records.

**Give people a reachable goal and name the reward.** Each agent’s private goal is to qualify on half of their own eligible resolved tickets during their team’s four-week window, so the target scales with their workload. A missed week costs no streak. Announce at launch that everyone on a team earns **one hour of paid time off** if the team reaches 50% qualifying resolutions over its reward window and clears the quality check. The support lead announces the result at the regular huddle and schedules the time off with coverage. Keep the existing CSAT bonus intact.

**Show progress where people already work.** At resolution, confirm which article was linked and show that the ticket is pending credit. Agents see their own confirmed count, eligible ticket count, and goal in the support tool. The team sees its aggregate rate and distance to the milestone in the existing huddle dashboard. Managers see team totals only. Rally data stays out of performance reviews. The support operations lead corrects disputed counts from the existing ticket record and tells the agent what changed.

**Run it in staggered waves.** In weeks 1–2, give everyone the workflow fixes, default resolution panel, practice, and feedback. Randomly assign existing teams to reward windows in weeks 3–6 or 5–8, balancing queue mix where possible. Compare the change in qualifying resolution rates during weeks 3–4, when only the first wave has a reward. End each team’s window after four weeks, confirm awards after the seven-day reopen period, and keep the resolution panel and feedback in place.

**Check for gaming and customer harm.** Before launch, test the count on a duplicate, a reopen, an old-workflow closure, and a privacy exception. Exclude only agreed categories such as spam, merged duplicates, and documented privacy exceptions. Keep ordinary eligible closures without an article in the denominator and watch for increases in exception use. In the existing QA pass, sample 10 credited tickets each week across active teams for relevance, accuracy, and customer-data leakage. If fewer than 8 pass, the support lead pauses award confirmation and fixes the cause with QA before resuming. Review team CSAT and reopen rates in the same meeting for signs that agents are rushing closures or prioritizing easy tickets. Use the existing dashboard and QA slot to keep administration under an hour a week.

**Decide four weeks after the rewards end.** Use the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article as the decision metric. Recalculate the current 12% baseline using the same eligibility rule before launch. At 50% or higher, keep the workflow and feedback as standard practice. Above baseline but below 50%, revisit the barriers agents report and adjust the workflow. At or below baseline, retire the rally and investigate what still makes the old path easier. In all three cases, the paid reward ends on schedule.
