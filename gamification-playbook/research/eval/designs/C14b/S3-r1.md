## Three-ward hand hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene verified in its existing monthly audits.** Give each ward that reaches its milestone a hospital-funded meal with the nursing director, offered across shifts with paid break coverage. Announce the reward and target at launch. Set each ward’s target five percentage points above its pre-pilot audit rate, capped at 90%. Wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. Use the combined results from the ward’s two reward-period audits, dividing compliant opportunities by all observed opportunities. Every ward can earn the meal.

**Make the kiosk easy to use before rewards start.** Ask nurses from day, night, and weekend shifts to try it during paid time. Fix problems with access, placement, and duplicate submissions. Report back at the ward huddle on what changed. Make the entry screen the kiosk’s default, prefill the ward, and let nurses correct a mistaken entry. The launch instruction is simple. “Clean your hands at the point of care, then log the moment once when it’s safe to do so.” During urgent care or an outage, nurses follow the usual hand hygiene procedure and skip the kiosk if needed.

**Measure kiosk uptake separately.** Use existing save records and the staffing rota to calculate the percentage of staffed ward shifts with at least one saved entry. This measures how widely the kiosk is used across shifts. Keep monthly observer audits as the measure of sampled hand hygiene compliance. Kiosk entries carry no points, and neither measure establishes how many moments were logged correctly across all patient care. Keep audit sampling comparable across periods and show the number of observed opportunities alongside the rate. [WHO guidance on hand hygiene observation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Give immediate feedback and show ward progress.** After each entry, show “Saved” and an undo option. At the existing huddle, show the ward’s kiosk shift coverage, the latest audit rate and sample size, and progress toward the meal. Managers see ward totals only. Use existing ward and shift fields, with no new nurse or patient identifiers, public rankings, or use in performance reviews. A missed entry or missed week carries no penalty.

**Run rewards for eight weeks per ward.** Give all three wards four weeks with the fixed kiosk and feedback first. Draw the order and start rewards four weeks apart, aligned to the monthly audit cycle. Compare each ward with its own baseline and use wards still waiting for rewards as a rough comparison. End each ward’s reward after its second monthly audit. Keep the kiosk default and feedback in place, then check shift coverage four weeks later. Set the decision rule at launch. Keep the workflow if coverage is at least 15 percentage points above baseline or reaches 90%, whichever comes first. For a baseline already above 90%, use the baseline as the target. If coverage improves but misses the target, fix the remaining friction. If it returns to baseline or worse, drop the kiosk campaign and review whether the logging workflow is worth keeping.

**Catch failures in the existing audit review.** Before launch, test a retried save, an undone entry, hand hygiene performed without a kiosk entry, and an outage. A retried save counts once, an undone entry is removed, and an observed compliant action counts toward the audit even if it was never logged. Each month, the infection prevention lead checks a small sample of existing kiosk records for retries and corrections. If observed compliance falls five percentage points below the ward’s baseline, pause its reward and review care interruptions, supply access, and audit sampling. The infection prevention lead decides when to resume and corrects audit totals. The kiosk owner corrects reporting errors. Keep the coordinator’s work under an hour a week by using the existing huddle and audit review.
