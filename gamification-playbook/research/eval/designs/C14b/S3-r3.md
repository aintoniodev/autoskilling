## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Set up the workflow.** In the first four weeks, ask nurses on day and night shifts to try the kiosk during paid time. Use existing safety huddles to find and fix bottlenecks such as queues, awkward placement, or slow sign-in. Post what changed in response. The rule for staff is simple. **Clean your hands when care calls for it, then log the completed action once at the next safe opportunity.** If care is urgent or the kiosk is down, carry on with care and skip the entry. Before launch, walk through a double tap, a corrected entry, an outage, and one action that covers two indications.

**Reward verified use.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, with equivalent provision for every shift, when at least 70% of completed hand-hygiene actions in its routine audit sample are logged accurately. Announce the meal at launch and have the nursing director deliver it in person and thank the team for a specific improvement. Every shift shares in the reward. An absent nurse or a missed entry can pick up with the next eligible action.

**Measure adoption alongside safety.** During the existing monthly audit, observers tally whether a completed action they already observe is followed by one correct kiosk entry, where both can be verified from the usual observation position. Keep only aggregate ward tallies for the programme. A duplicate adds zero credit, a voided entry earns zero, and one action covering two indications counts once. Record cases the observer cannot verify as unknown and report their share. Use comparable day and night audit coverage, with at least 30 assessable completed actions per ward and no more than 20% unknown before awarding a meal. If coverage falls short, carry the milestone to the next routine audit.

Infection Prevention continues to measure compliance as **observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities**. It pauses a ward’s reward if compliance falls 5 percentage points or more below that ward’s pre-pilot rate, or immediately if kiosk use delays care or interferes with hand hygiene. Infection Prevention decides when to resume. Nurses raise counting errors through the existing huddle, and the kiosk owner corrects them before the next progress update. **Show staff what they need.** The kiosk immediately confirms the moment saved and offers a correction. In the existing huddle, each ward sees its adoption rate, audit sample size, safety result, and progress toward the meal. Managers receive the same ward totals. Keep individual activity out of manager dashboards and performance reviews.

**Run it and end it.** Give all three wards the kiosk fixes and feedback during the four-week baseline. Start eight-week reward windows in weeks 5, 9, and 13, with ward order drawn by lot. Compare each ward with its own baseline and, where windows overlap, with wards still receiving feedback alone. Before each reward ends, make the kiosk open directly to the moment selector with the ward prefilled. At the first routine audit roughly four weeks after rewards stop, use the same adoption measure to decide what to keep. With the safety and sample rules met, **keep** the workflow at 70% or higher, **change** it at 50–69% and recheck at the next audit, and **drop** the kiosk logging requirement below 50%.

**Watch for gaming and workload.** Raw kiosk totals can rise through duplicates, backfilling, or logging that happens mainly when observers are present. Keep audit timing independent of the reward and check whether gains persist after the meal. Use the existing huddle to catch queues and shift access problems early. Cap programme administration at 45 minutes a week using the existing audit report and huddle.
