## One working day for code review

Give every ready pull request a useful first review within one working day. The current median is 2.6 days. Before launch, ask five engineers across time zones and experience levels what slows reviews down. Use their feedback to fix routing and coverage, then tell the team what changed. Make a rotating reviewer and backup the default, put the oldest ready PRs first in the existing queue, and give reviewers a protected 30-minute block each workday. Use paid work time for a short walkthrough of what a useful review looks like.

### What earns the reward

Tell the team at launch: “If at least 80% of your rotation’s ready PRs get a useful first review within one working day during its three-week window, everyone in the rotation earns two hours of paid time off.” Confirm coverage and approval before announcing it. The engineering lead thanks each qualifying rotation at its next team meeting and confirms how to book the time. Each rotation can earn the reward independently.

Use existing PR events. Start the clock when a PR first becomes ready for review and route it immediately. One working day means eight hours on the rotation’s agreed coverage calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. Stop the clock at the first submitted review from an eligible engineer other than the author. A useful review is a considered approval, a request for changes that explains what to fix, or a submitted review with a specific question the author can act on. The metric is the **percentage of ready PRs that receive a qualifying review within one working day**. Assign each PR to a window by its ready timestamp and wait for its deadline to pass before closing the count.

Count each PR once. An emoji, a “looking now” comment, or a bot response earns no credit. Reassigning, reopening, or briefly moving a PR back to draft does not restart the clock. An unreviewed PR that closes still counts in the denominator. A useful review from someone outside the rotation counts toward the rotation that owned the queue when the PR became ready.

### What engineers see

Send the reviewer private confirmation through the existing PR notification channel when a review counts. Show each reviewer their own on-time count out of the PRs they covered, with a personal goal of four in five. On a low-volume week, the next assigned PR is the goal. A missed week has no penalty.

Post the whole team’s weekly percentage and each rotation’s milestone progress in the engineering channel. Managers receive the same aggregates, with no individual drill-down. Keep programme counts out of performance reviews.

### Timing and ending

Use four weeks of historical PR data to establish the baseline percentage. Give both rotations the routing fixes, protected time, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Randomly choose which rotation starts its reward window in weeks 3–5. The other runs in weeks 5–7. Compare the rotations during weeks 3–4 and check each against its own pre-reward rate. With 25 engineers, use that comparison as a directional signal.

End rewards after week 7. Keep routing, protected time, and feedback in place. Measure the same percentage over the next three weeks. At **80% or above**, keep the workflow as the default. At **65–79%**, change the biggest remaining bottleneck and measure again. **Below 65%**, retire the incentive design and revisit coverage and workload with the team.

### What could go wrong

Rushed approvals can buy speed at the cost of quality. The pilot owner samples five credited reviews each week using the existing PR record. If two fail the useful-review rule, the engineering lead pauses reward accrual, fixes the cause with reviewers, and resumes after a follow-up sample clears the threshold. The pilot owner also handles private count corrections from the PR history. Before launch, test a duplicate review, a revoked approval, a reopened PR, and a review handled outside the rotation.

Uneven workload can make the milestone feel arbitrary. Use the backup during absences and spikes, and have the lead check queue age at the regular team meeting so large PRs get attention too. Automate the tally from existing events and cap the weekly sample, corrections, and update at 45 minutes.
