## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward a ward for reliably logging hand hygiene that observers independently verify.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, with equivalent options for every shift. Announce it at launch. When a ward earns it, the nursing director delivers it at shift huddles and thanks staff for a specific improvement they made. Every ward can earn the meal, and every nurse on that ward shares it.

**Make the kiosk easy to use first.** Give day- and night-shift nurses paid time to try it during a walkthrough. Ask where logging interrupts care, whether the kiosk is within easy reach and how nurses would correct a mistaken entry. Fix access, placement and slow entry before starting the reward. Post the fixes on the existing huddle board. Nurses perform hand hygiene when required and log it at the next safe point in their workflow. During urgent care or a kiosk outage, care comes first.

**Use the existing monthly audits.** For each completed hand-hygiene opportunity in the routine sample, the observer checks whether they can directly verify one corresponding kiosk entry. Record only ward-level counts. The adoption measure is **verified kiosk entries ÷ observed completed hand-hygiene opportunities**. Each opportunity counts once. Duplicate and corrected entries count at most once, voided entries count zero, and an action recorded only through the old workflow counts toward clinical compliance and remains in the adoption denominator. Ambiguous matches earn no credit. Before launch, test those cases plus an outage. If observers cannot verify entries without adding staff identifiers or tracking individual movements, skip the contingent reward and proceed with workflow fixes and feedback.

**Set the milestone and the safety gate before launch.** A ward earns the meal at **80% verified logging** across its two reward-period audits, with at least **30 observed completed opportunities** in total. Infection Prevention pauses the award if audited hand-hygiene compliance falls **5 percentage points or more below the ward’s pre-pilot baseline**, or below the hospital’s existing minimum. Compliance uses all observed opportunities as its denominator. If the sample is too small, close the pilot without an award decision for that ward. Infection Prevention owns the decision and corrects audit tally errors. The kiosk lead fixes entry and counting problems. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

**Show staff the information they can act on.** The kiosk immediately confirms a saved entry and offers an undo. The huddle board shows the ward’s latest verified rate, sample size and progress toward the meal. Managers see ward totals only. Keep names, patient identifiers, individual rankings and performance-review use out of the programme. Personal feedback is the save confirmation, so staff can resume with the next opportunity after any absence.

**Stagger the start and end on schedule.** Give all three wards the fixes, practice time and feedback for one audit cycle. Then start each ward’s eight-week reward period one monthly audit cycle apart. Compare each ward with its own baseline and look for changes when rewards start. Three wards provide a practical comparison with limited ability to isolate the reward’s effect. Before each ward’s reward period ends, make the kiosk the first option in the routine logging workflow and keep the fallback available for outages. Four weeks after the reward ends, use the next routine audit to check the same verified logging rate. Keep the workflow at **80% or higher** with the safety gate met. Below 80%, fix the barriers nurses report and recheck once. If verification remains impractical or the kiosk repeatedly disrupts care, drop the kiosk incentive. Budget the coordinator up to one hour a week, using existing audits and huddles.

**Watch for gaming and uneven access.** Extra taps earn nothing. Keep audit times unannounced and sample a comparable mix of shifts so a busy night shift or good performance during a visible audit does not drive the result. Review a small sample of matches for duplicates and corrections. If nurses report rushing, peer pressure or delayed care, Infection Prevention pauses the reward and checks the workflow with the affected shift.
