## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward rule.** A ward earns a hospital-funded meal, served at paid huddles across all shifts, when at least 80% of its sampled, completed hand-hygiene actions have one accurate kiosk entry and its audited hand-hygiene compliance stays within 5 percentage points of baseline. Every ward can earn the meal once. Announce it at launch and have the nursing director present it with specific thanks for a change the ward made to improve the workflow.

**Fix the workflow first.** Give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts protected time to try the kiosk during normal work. Ask where logging interrupts care, creates a queue or duplicates documentation. Fix those issues before starting rewards, then share what changed in the shift huddle. If the kiosk still pulls nurses away from care, delay the incentive. During urgent care or a kiosk outage, follow usual clinical practice and skip the kiosk entry.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** During routine observation on each ward, the auditor keeps the usual count of hand-hygiene actions and opportunities. Where the auditor can also verify the corresponding kiosk entry during the same session, they tally whether the entry matches the completed action. Keep only ward-level tallies, with no nurse or patient identifiers in the programme data. The adoption measure is the share of completed actions in those sessions with one verified, accurate entry. An action done without a kiosk entry stays in the denominator. A duplicate counts once, a corrected entry counts once, and a voided entry earns zero. A kiosk tap alone earns zero. Before launch, have auditors and nurses walk through each of those cases and an outage. If auditors cannot verify entries within their existing rounds without following staff or adding identifiable tracking, hold the reward and fix the workflow. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**What staff see.** The kiosk confirms the moment saved and lets the nurse correct it. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s latest verified logging rate, sample size, milestone and fixes made from staff feedback. Managers see the same ward totals. There are no individual scores, rankings, streaks or performance-review uses. A missed entry or shift has no carryover penalty.

**Timing and decision.** Give all three wards the fixes, practice and feedback for one monthly audit cycle before rewards begin. Start the eight-week reward window on wards A, B and C four weeks apart so each gets two monthly audits and the hospital can compare wards with and without the reward during the rollout. Use at least 30 sampled completed actions across each ward’s two audits to decide the milestone. Before each window closes, set the kiosk to open directly to the logging screen with the ward prefilled. Four weeks after rewards end, check the same adoption measure again. With at least 30 sampled actions, keep the workflow at 80% or higher, revise it at 60–79%, and drop mandatory kiosk logging below 60%. If the sample is too small, use the next routine audit before deciding. These are pilot thresholds.

**What could go wrong.** Staff may log for points, rush care or log more only while observers are present. Infection Prevention should spread routine sampling across shifts and pause the reward if an audit shows a compliance drop greater than 5 percentage points from baseline or staff report that logging delays care. Infection Prevention owns the pause decision and corrects disputed audit tallies. The ward kiosk lead fixes workflow or count errors and reports back at huddle. Treat the three-ward rollout as a practical comparison because observation can itself change behaviour. One coordinator updates the existing huddle boards and handles exceptions in under an hour a week using the routine audit totals.
