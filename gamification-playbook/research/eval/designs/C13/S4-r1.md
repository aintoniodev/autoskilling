## Knowledge-base adoption programme

**Start with two weeks of paid practice.** Have six agents across shifts walk through recent tickets with the support lead and knowledge-base owner. Give every agent 30 minutes on shift to practise. Fix the biggest barriers they find, such as poor search, missing permissions, or slow article approval. Show the team what changed and make article search and linking the first option in the resolution screen. Keep an exception path for sensitive or genuinely one-off tickets so agents can close them promptly. The existing CSAT bonus continues on its current terms.

**Reward one completed ticket with useful knowledge attached.** At launch, announce the rule in one sentence: “If your team closes 40% of its tickets with a relevant approved article attached over four weeks, everyone on the team earns one hour of paid time off.” The support lead thanks the team in person and schedules the hour with shift coverage. The 40% milestone is a proposed first step from 12% toward the expectation of using the knowledge base on every applicable ticket.

**Measure it from existing ticket and article records.** The denominator is all unique resolved support tickets, including exceptions, so exception use cannot inflate the rate. The numerator is tickets with a relevant approved article linked in the resolution record. An agent who writes the article can close the ticket with the draft attached. It counts once the knowledge-base owner approves it, provided that happens within seven days of closure. One ticket earns one credit, even with several links. A reopened ticket loses its provisional credit and can earn it again at final resolution. An old-workflow closure without a qualifying link stays in the denominator. Test those cases and a wrong-article link before launch. Use the existing helpdesk and article records to calculate credit.

**Give agents immediate, private feedback.** On closure, show the agent whether the ticket counted or the article is awaiting approval. Show each agent their own share and a personal goal of roughly four qualifying tickets per ten they resolve, rounded to a whole ticket and scaled to their actual volume. Personal goals guide practice. Everyone shares the team reward, including agents who missed a week. Post the team percentage and distance to the milestone in the existing support channel each week. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews.

**Run four reward weeks per team, staggered across three groups.** Use existing teams or shift groups and assign them to start in weeks 3, 5, and 7. Everyone gets the workflow fixes and private feedback from week 1. Compare team coverage during the stagger to see whether rewards add anything beyond those changes. Keep the article-first resolution screen and feedback after rewards end. Use the same coverage measure for the decision, averaged across weeks 3 and 4 after each team’s reward ends. At **35% or higher**, keep the workflow and retire the prize. At **20–34%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. **Below 20%**, drop the campaign and revisit whether the tool fits the work.

**Catch shortcuts and customer harm early.** Add a relevance check to 10 tickets in the existing weekly QA sample, spread across active teams. Check that each sampled article actually supports the resolution and that any customer-facing link was safe to share. Two irrelevant or incorrect links in the sample pause reward awards. A sensitive disclosure or missed closure SLA caused by waiting for article approval also triggers a pause. The support lead decides when to resume and watches existing CSAT and closure-time reports for deterioration. Agents can flag a wrong count privately in the existing support channel’s escalation route. The support lead corrects the tally before awards, and the knowledge-base owner fixes article and approval issues. Budget under an hour a week for the sample, tally, and team update.
