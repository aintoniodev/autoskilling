# Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot for three wards

**Reward observed hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, with equivalent provision for night and weekend staff, by meeting its target across two routine monthly audits. Announce the meal at launch. The ward lead delivers it in person and thanks the team for a specific improvement. All staff on the ward share the reward, including staff who were on leave or worked fewer shifts.

**Use the audit to determine what counts.** Divide correctly performed hand-hygiene opportunities by all eligible opportunities in the existing observer audit. Pool the counts across two audits, with at least 30 opportunities in total. Set each ward’s target at five percentage points above its two-audit baseline, capped at 95%. A ward already at or above 95% aims to stay at or above 95%. Each opportunity counts once under the hospital’s existing audit rules, whether it was entered in the kiosk or missed there. Kiosk entries earn no reward credit.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Before launch, ask nurses from day and night shifts on each ward to try the kiosk during paid time. Fix the friction they identify, such as slow sign-in, poor placement or repeated ward selection, and report the fixes back. Give nurses a specific confirmation after saving an entry, with a way to correct it. Their next opportunity to use the kiosk is the next eligible case. There are no quotas or streaks. Track whether the kiosk is being used across shifts using aggregate ward and time data already available, and ask staff what gets in the way. A shift with an entry shows some use, so treat it as a rollout signal only.

**Show progress where staff already look.** After each audit, post the ward’s compliance percentage, sample size and target at the usual huddle or on the ward board. Keep individual entries private, give managers ward totals, and exclude pilot data from performance reviews. Use no named leaderboard or cross-ward ranking.

**Run the reward for two monthly audit cycles per ward.** Give all three wards the kiosk fixes and feedback first. Start the reward one audit cycle apart, so each ward gets the same two-cycle window and the hospital can see whether compliance shifts when the reward starts. With three wards, treat that comparison as directional. Before each ward’s reward ends, make the kiosk open directly to the ward’s logging screen and leave that setup in place afterward.

**Use audited compliance to decide what stays.** Check the next two routine monthly audits after rewards end, using the same pooled measure and minimum sample. If the ward still meets target, keep the kiosk setup and routine feedback. If compliance is above baseline but below target, troubleshoot with nurses and check the next audit. If it falls to or below baseline without meeting target, drop the reward model and review whether the kiosk is helping the ward. A small sample means the decision waits for the next routine audit.

**Rehearse failures before launch.** A duplicate or corrected kiosk entry leaves reward credit unchanged. Correct hand hygiene during a kiosk outage still counts in the audit. If staff report that logging delays care, fix the workflow before continuing the rollout on that ward. If a monthly audit with at least 15 opportunities falls five percentage points or more below baseline, pause the ward’s reward. The infection-prevention lead decides when it resumes, checks for changes in audit coverage, and corrects any disputed audit count. The pilot coordinator updates totals and follows up on issues within one hour a week, using the existing audits and huddles.
