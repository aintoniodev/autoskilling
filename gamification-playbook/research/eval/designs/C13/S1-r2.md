# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Goal:** Raise the share of jobs with a verified digital safety checklist completed before work starts from about 35% to at least 80%, and sustain it four weeks after rewards end.

### Before launch

Bring union reps, technicians from different shifts and routes, dispatch, and Safety together. Give technicians paid time to try the app on real jobs, including weak-signal sites and jobs with more than one technician. Fix the biggest obstacles before the reward starts and tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Agree with union reps on the counting rule, data access, corrections, and how crews on every shift can take the reward.

### What earns credit

Tell crews: “Your team gets one credit when the app verifies that the required checklist for a dispatched job was completed before work began.”

- Use the existing dispatch job ID and an independently recorded start event from the current workflow. Count once per job, including multi-technician jobs. A checklist counts if it documents a hazard and the crew follows the existing stop-work procedure, even if the job never starts.
- The denominator is dispatched jobs that reach the point where a pre-job checklist is required, including jobs handled on paper. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. Confirm the reported 35% using the same definition before comparing results.
- Test duplicate submissions, voided checklists, late submissions, paper fallback, and jobs stopped for a hazard. An offline submission can count once it syncs if the existing records establish the order of events. Where that order cannot be verified, keep the job in the denominator, show the entry as unverified, and fix verification before putting any affected crew into the reward pilot. Use existing records without adding GPS or technician tracking.

### What crews see and earn

After each submission, the app privately shows **Counted**, **Pending sync**, or the specific reason it did not count. Each technician sees their own eligible jobs and a personal target sized to their workload. A missed job or week leaves the next job available to count.

Post each crew’s weekly percentage in its existing briefing channel. Managers see crew totals only, with small groups combined where a total could identify someone. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews. The pilot owner handles private correction requests, and the app administrator fixes verified counting errors in the source records.

Announce the reward at launch: **Each crew that reaches 80% over its four-week reward window earns one additional paid hour off per crew member.** Set up shift coverage in advance so everyone can take it. The field-service lead announces the milestone in person and thanks the crew for a specific improvement it made.

### Timing and decision

Give every crew two weeks with the fixes, paid practice, and feedback before rewards begin. Pair crews with similar baseline rates and job volumes, then randomly assign one from each pair to weeks 1–4 and the other to weeks 5–8. Compare the two groups during weeks 1–4 to estimate what the reward adds. Before each crew’s reward ends, make the checklist the first screen on opening a job and prefill known job details. Keep the private confirmation and crew feedback.

Four weeks after each crew’s reward ends, use the companywide, job-weighted verified completion rate from those follow-up weeks as the decision measure. **Keep** the workflow as standard at 80% or above, **change and retest** at 60–79%, and **drop the incentive design** below 60%. Existing safety procedures apply throughout.

### What could go wrong

Crews may rush a checklist, submit it after starting, or feel pressure to avoid reporting a hazard. Safety samples 10 credited checklists per week through its existing quality review, with names removed where feasible. Agree on the quality criteria before launch. The Safety lead pauses rewards if two of the 10 have material omissions or contradictions, or if any credible report indicates pressure to bypass a check or suppress a hazard. Safety and the pilot owner investigate and correct the cause before rewards resume. Crews follow the existing fallback during app outages and still get credit for verifiable offline work. Automate the tally and use the existing quality review so one pilot owner can run the programme in under an hour a week.
