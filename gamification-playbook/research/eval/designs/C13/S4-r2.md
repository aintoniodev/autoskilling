## 1. Fix the workflow first

The starting article-link rate is 12%. Give agents 30 minutes of protected time to try the workflow on real tickets. Ask agents across shifts and queues where search, permissions or article approval slow them down. Fix the biggest blockers, show agents what changed, and put suggested articles and a one-click attach action in the resolution screen. Give agents a way to flag a knowledge gap from the same screen. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula intact.

## 2. Reward useful knowledge on resolved tickets

Tell agents at launch: **“If your team reaches 60% of eligible resolutions during its four-week window and passes the quality checks, everyone on the team earns two hours of paid time off.”** Validate that agents want the reward and reserve coverage before announcing it. The support lead will award it at a team huddle, with equivalent scheduling options across shifts.

Count one credit per ticket when the support system records a resolution and a relevant, published article is linked. A new article earns the same credit once it passes the existing publishing review and is linked to the ticket. Run these cases through the rule before launch:

- Repeated links, duplicate tickets and merged tickets earn one credit per underlying case. A reopened ticket loses provisional credit and can earn it again when resolved.
- A ticket resolved through the old workflow stays in the denominator. It earns credit if it has a qualifying article link in the system.
- A missing article stays in the denominator. Exclude only pre-agreed categories identifiable from existing records, such as spam and test tickets. Report exclusions alongside the rate so changes are visible.

## 3. Give agents feedback without exposing individual activity

On resolution, show the agent which article counted or what is pending. Each agent sees their own tally and a target scaled to their eligible ticket volume. A missed week carries no penalty. Post team progress in the existing shift channel once a week. Managers see team totals only, and programme data stays out of performance reviews. The programme owner handles disputed counts privately and corrects them before the milestone is settled.

## 4. Stagger the reward and measure what sticks

Give all teams the workflow fixes and private feedback, then collect two weeks of baseline data. Randomly assign existing teams to two start dates two weeks apart. Each team gets four reward weeks. Compare the teams’ change from baseline during the two weeks when only the first group can earn the reward.

Keep article suggestions in the resolution screen after the reward ends. Four weeks after the last team finishes, use the org-wide share of eligible resolutions with a qualifying article to decide what to do next. **At 60% or above**, keep the workflow as standard practice. **At 30–59%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. **Below 30%**, retire the contest and redesign the workflow with agents. Any unresolved quality issue takes precedence over the adoption result.

## 5. Catch gaming and service problems

The support QA lead reviews 12 randomly sampled credited tickets each week across teams using existing QA access. Check whether the article supports the resolution. If three or more fail, pause reward awards, fix the scoring or workflow, and require a passing sample before resuming. Use existing team CSAT and reopen reports to spot possible rushed closures or slower service. Watch for rising exclusion rates and duplicate articles. Automate the counts and cap the weekly review at one hour.
