## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Prepare with technicians and the union.** Have union stewards and technicians from different shifts, job types and coverage areas walk through real jobs on paid time. Agree on the reward, counting rules, data access and dispute process before announcing the pilot. Fix the friction they find. Prefill job details, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job, and protect time in the schedule to do it. Test the offline workflow on actual routes and tell crews which problems they raised and what changed. Give every crew the fixes, paid practice and private feedback before collecting a two-week baseline. Use the reported 35% as a starting estimate.

**Reward a completed pre-job safety check.** The launch rule is “One check counts per job visit when the app records a complete checklist before work starts, including when the checklist puts the job on a safety hold.” Count required answers and any required hazard disposition using the existing safety workflow. A genuine stop-work gets the same credit as a cleared job. Hazard, near-miss and injury reports carry no reward penalty, and technicians retain their stop-work authority. [OSHA guidance on safety incentives and reporting](https://www.osha.gov/recordkeeping/modernization-guidance)

Use the existing dispatch visit ID, checklist record and independently recorded work-start or safety-hold event. The denominator is every dispatched visit that reaches the pre-job check, including visits delayed or abandoned for safety. Exclude visits cancelled before that point. A duplicate earns one credit, a voided submission loses credit unless a valid replacement was completed on time, and a late backfill earns zero. Count offline completions after sync if the existing records verify the original timing. Where timing cannot be verified, use the approved safety fallback and keep the visit in the denominator as a fallback. Test these cases before launch. If existing records cannot verify timing or crews lack reliable access, fix that before starting prizes.

**Make progress visible and the reward reachable.** Announce one reward at launch. Each crew that reaches its milestone earns a company-paid meal on paid time, with the operations lead thanking the crew in person for a specific example of good safety practice. Start with a milestone 15 percentage points above the crew’s post-fix baseline, capped at 90%. Crews already above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. Everyone on an earning crew shares the reward, including colleagues who were on leave.

After each submission, show the technician whether the visit counted or is pending sync, and why. Give each technician a private target based on their expected job volume and show weekly crew progress in the existing safety huddle. Managers see crew totals only. The programme has no named rankings or individual reports for performance reviews. A missed week creates no reset. The next eligible visit still counts.

**Run two waves and end on schedule.** Pair comparable crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly assign one in each pair to start two weeks earlier. Each crew has four reward weeks and can earn the meal once. Compare changes in completion rates while the later crews still have the same app and feedback without prizes. End the prizes after each crew’s fourth week and keep the checklist as the first step.

Use the valid digital pre-job completion rate as the decision metric. Four weeks after each crew’s prizes end, compare the latest two weeks with its post-fix baseline. For crews below 90% at baseline, retain the rollout as designed if the rate is at least 10 points higher or has reached 90%. For crews already at 90% or higher, use maintenance of baseline. If the rate improved but missed the target, fix the remaining workflow friction. If it returned to baseline or fell, retire the incentive. Use the staggered comparison to judge whether the prize added anything worth using in a future rollout.

**Catch shortcuts and pressure early.** In under an hour a week, the safety coordinator spot-checks 10 visits against existing records for late entry, generic hazard responses and missing hold actions. Two failed checks in the sample, one missed critical control, or a credible report of pressure to rush or hide a hazard pauses new awards. The safety lead and union steward decide when to resume. Technicians can raise disputed counts privately through either of them, and the coordinator corrects verified errors and updates the crew total. If outages or device access put a crew at a disadvantage, pause its reward window while operations fixes access.
