## Safe Start

Run a four-week team incentive to make the digital safety checklist part of starting a job. About 35% of jobs currently have a completed digital checklist. Use the first two weeks to verify that baseline against the pre-start rule below and fix the problems technicians identify.

### Before launch

Meet with union stewards and a small group of technicians from different shifts and routes on paid time. Walk through a routine job, a job with no signal, and a job where a hazard stops work. Fix the biggest blockers and tell crews what changed. Give every shift paid practice time. Agree with the union on the reward, who can see the counts, and how technicians can get a count corrected.

### What earns credit

**One credit per job when the app accepts a complete safety checklist before the recorded job start or a documented safety hold.** Required hazard responses must be complete. A technician who identifies a hazard and stops the job can earn the same credit as one who clears it to proceed.

Use the app’s existing validation and timestamps, matched by job ID to the existing dispatch start or safety-hold record. Use the original completion time for offline submissions if the app already records it reliably. If either system lacks a reliable timestamp, fix that before attaching a reward. Use existing records and collect no new location or activity data.

The team rate is credited jobs divided by jobs that started or reached a documented safety hold. A duplicate earns one credit total. A voided checklist loses its credit. A checklist completed after starting earns zero. A routine job completed using paper stays in the denominator. Jobs cancelled before the safety check was due are excluded. Keep the approved paper fallback available during outages and show outage volume alongside the team rate so everyone can see its effect.

### What technicians see and earn

After submission, the app confirms the job and its status, such as “Pre-start checklist counted” or “Saved offline. Credit pending sync.” Only the technician sees their own progress. The suggested personal goal is four of their next five eligible jobs, at their own pace. Leave, a quiet route, or a missed job never resets progress.

Post the weekly team percentage in the existing crew briefing. Managers see team totals only. Pool small crews where a total could reveal someone’s result. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews and discipline.

Announce the reward at launch. If a crew reaches **80% across its four reward weeks**, every crew member gets a company-paid meal on paid time, with equivalent sessions for each shift. The service leader hosts it in person and thanks the crew for specific safe-start practices. Take-home eligibility is the same for technicians on leave or on low-volume routes.

### Timing and decision

Give all crews the fixes and private confirmation in weeks 1–2. Make the checklist the first action when a technician opens a dispatched job, with job details prefilled and hazard answers left blank. Keep that default after the programme ends.

Randomly assign comparable crews to start rewards in week 3 or week 5. Each crew gets four reward weeks. Compare their rates in weeks 3–4, when both groups have the workflow fixes and only the early group has the reward. Stop earning rewards at the end of each crew’s window.

Use the **pre-start completion rate four weeks after each crew’s reward ends** to decide what stays. At **75% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback as standard. At **60–74%**, fix the remaining friction and remeasure. Below **60%**, drop the incentive format and revisit the field workflow with technicians and stewards. Keep the safety requirement and approved fallback in every case.

### What could go wrong

- **Rushed or copied checks.** The safety lead reviews five credited jobs a week using existing safety records. Pause rewards if two of the latest 20 sampled jobs have material defects, such as an unaddressed hazard, or immediately if a check falsely clears a serious hazard. The safety lead decides when rewards resume. The dispatch systems owner fixes wrong counts and confirms the correction privately with the technician.
- **Pressure to start or hide a hazard.** Make the stop-work credit explicit in the launch briefing. Review the duplicate, void, paper, offline, and safety-hold cases with union stewards before launch so crews can see how each will count.
- **The lift disappears.** Use the post-reward rate to decide whether the routine stuck. Keep weekly administration under an hour with the existing dispatch report, five quality checks, and one crew update.
