### Reward

Give everyone on a team **one hour of paid time off** when the team reaches **60% of resolved tickets with a qualifying knowledge-base article over its four-week reward window** and passes the quality check. Announce the reward and the dates at launch. Have the support lead recognize each qualifying team in its regular huddle and schedule the time off with coverage across shifts. Keep the existing CSAT bonus formula and payout schedule.

Tell agents the counting rule in one sentence. “A resolved ticket earns one credit when its resolution record links to an approved article that answers the issue, including a new article once it’s approved.”

### Count and verify

Use the existing ticket resolution record and the knowledge base’s publication status. Divide credited resolutions by all distinct resolved tickets. Merged duplicates count under the surviving ticket. If a ticket reopens, reverse the credit until its final resolution. Multiple links earn one credit. An article linked to a resolution handled through the old workflow earns the same credit if it meets the rule. Drafts can earn credit back to the original resolution date if approved within five business days after the reward window ends. Use existing approved internal articles for sensitive cases. Where there’s no safe article to link, let the agent resolve the ticket and leave it in the denominator.

Before launch, run the count against a merged duplicate, a reopened ticket, a draft awaiting approval, and a ticket handled through the old workflow. The support operations lead owns the count and corrects verified errors before rewards are awarded.

### What agents see

Show private confirmation in the ticket when credit lands, with the article title and an explanation if credit is pending or reversed. Give each agent a private weekly target based on expected ticket volume, such as 6 of 10 or 24 of 40. A missed week has no carryover penalty. Post the team’s percentage and progress toward 60% in its regular support channel. Give managers team totals only, with no individual drill-down, named rankings, or use in performance reviews.

### Timing and handoff

- **Weeks 1–2.** Use the reported 12% as a starting point and verify it with the counting rule above. Give a few agents across shifts paid time to walk through closures. Fix the main blockers they identify, such as slow article search or an approval backlog, and tell the team what changed. Give everyone private feedback and put article suggestions first in the resolution flow, with a clear way to finish the ticket when none fits.
- **Weeks 3–9.** Randomly assign existing teams to two start dates three weeks apart, balancing ticket volume and baseline adoption where possible. Each team gets four reward weeks. During the stagger, compare changes from baseline between the groups to estimate what the reward adds to the workflow fixes and feedback.
- **Four weeks after each group’s reward ends.** Measure the qualifying resolution rate over the prior two weeks. Set the decision rule before launch. At **50% or higher**, keep the default and feedback as routine practice. At **30–49%**, fix the most common remaining barrier and measure again. Below **30%**, retire the reward programme and revisit the workflow with agents. Paid rewards end on schedule.

### What could go wrong

Each week, use the existing support QA process to sample eight credited tickets across teams with active rewards. Check that the article was relevant, accurate, and safe for its intended audience. If **two or more of eight fail**, the support operations lead pauses awards for the affected team, corrects the count, and assigns the fix to the knowledge-base owner. A confirmed disclosure of customer data triggers an immediate pause and the existing incident process. Resume after the fix and a fresh sample passes. Review team-level CSAT and reopen rates through the existing dashboard for signs that agents are rushing closure or sending articles in place of an answer. Keep the weekly count, sample, and team update within one hour for one owner.
