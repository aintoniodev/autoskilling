## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward one useful article per resolved ticket.** Credit a ticket when the agent links an approved article that helps resolve the issue, or writes an article that passes the existing publication review and is linked back to the ticket. Linking an existing article and writing a new one earn the same credit. Use the ticket system’s existing resolution and article records. Logins, searches, drafts and extra links earn no credit.

**Make it fit the support queue.** Before launch, spend two weeks with agents from different shifts and queues finding what makes article search or writing slow. Give agents 30 minutes of protected, paid time each week to learn the tool and document recurring fixes, with queue coverage. Fix the biggest blockers, publish what changed, and put article search first in the close flow for everyone before rewards begin. Keep an easy bypass for cases where an article would be inappropriate, such as sensitive or genuinely one-off issues. Keep the existing CSAT bonus and its rules in place.

**Count each ticket once.** Use unique resolved ticket IDs. An existing article should be linked by close. A new article can earn credit if it’s submitted by close and approved within seven days. Show credit as pending during that window. Duplicate links earn one credit, a removed link loses credit, and a reopened ticket returns to pending until it’s resolved again. A ticket resolved the old way stays in the denominator and earns zero credit. Exclude only predefined, auditable categories, such as spam, merged duplicates and sensitive cases, using existing ticket fields. If an exception can’t be verified from those fields, keep it in the denominator. Recalculate the reported 12% under this rule before launch.

**Set a reachable goal and name the reward at launch.** Anyone who reaches 30% of their own eligible tickets during their four-week window earns one hour of paid time off. The goal is sized to actual ticket volume, with no minimum ticket count, weekly quota or streak. Agents who handle zero eligible tickets because of leave can take the next equivalent window when they return. Leads hand over the award in a brief one-to-one and schedule it with coverage. Agents get immediate, private confirmation in the ticket workflow and can see their own count and pending credits. Post team-level progress in the existing support channel. Managers get aggregate results only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews. The coordinator tells each earner privately, and scheduling receives only the award entitlement.

**Stagger the rewards and close the pilot.** Pair existing teams by baseline adoption and ticket mix, then randomly assign one team in each pair to start two weeks later. Everyone gets the fixes, paid learning time and feedback from day one. Compare adoption during the first two reward weeks, when the later teams have the same support and no active reward yet. Each team gets four reward weeks. Allow seven days to settle pending credits, then close the awards. Keep article search in the close flow, and measure adoption again over weeks three and four after each team’s rewards end.

**Decide in advance what earns a permanent place.** Use the post-reward share of eligible resolved tickets with a useful article, estimated from recorded coverage and the quality sample. Keep the workflow if that share is at least 25% and at least 10 percentage points above the recalculated baseline. If it improves but misses either target, fix the main barrier and retest the workflow. If it returns to baseline or falls below it, drop the incentive and revisit how knowledge is captured. The prize ends on schedule in every case.

**Watch for bad links, rushed closes and a growing review queue.** The coordinator reviews 10 randomly sampled credited tickets per week in the existing QA process and tracks team CSAT, reopen rate and overdue article reviews. If fewer than 8 of 10 links are accurate, relevant and safe to reuse, pause new awards for the affected team while the support lead fixes the cause. Investigate a five-point CSAT drop or a three-point reopen-rate rise over two consecutive weeks against the pre-pilot baseline before resuming or expanding rewards. Agents can challenge a count privately with the coordinator, who checks the ticket record and corrects it. The support lead owns pause and restart decisions. Use the existing QA slot and an automated team rollup to keep administration under an hour a week.
