## One working day for a useful code review

Run a six-week pilot for the 25 engineers. Reward a timely first review that helps the author move the PR forward. Leave the existing approval and merge requirements in place.

### What earns credit

**Each ready PR counts once when a teammate submits a useful review within eight team working hours.** A useful review is an approval that briefly says what was checked, a specific change request, or a concrete question that identifies what the author needs to clarify before review can proceed.

Use the PR system’s existing ready-for-review and submitted-review timestamps. Apply published team working hours and holidays. Drafts and bot-authored PRs are outside the pilot. Acknowledgments and automated comments earn no credit. New commits, reassignment, and reopening preserve the original clock and credit. A PR withdrawn by its author before the deadline and before any review is excluded. The pilot owner checks withdrawals for patterns. A late or unanswered PR counts as a miss once its deadline passes.

### What engineers see and earn

In week 1, ask engineers across time zones and experience levels to identify why reviews wait. Give them paid time to try the revised workflow. Set up a rotating primary reviewer and backup, protect a daily review block, and route ready PRs to the rotation automatically. Tell the team what changed in response to their feedback.

A qualifying review gets private confirmation in the existing workflow. Each engineer sees their own on-time reviews out of the eligible PRs they covered, with a personal target of four in five over the pilot. Their goal scales with their review volume. After an absence or a miss, the next PR counts as usual. The existing team channel shows the combined weekly on-time percentage and PR count. Managers receive those same aggregates, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews.

At launch, name the shared reward. **Each rotation that answers at least 80% of eligible PRs on time during its two reward weeks earns a 90-minute paid Friday finish**, scheduled in turns so review coverage continues. The engineering lead thanks the rotation in person and confirms the time off. Eligibility is shared across the rotation, including people on leave.

### Timing and decision

- **Weeks 1–2** — Give everyone the workflow fixes, protected time, and private feedback. Recalculate the baseline using the pilot rules and report it alongside the current 2.6-day median. Before launch, test the count against a Friday handoff, a duplicate review, a reopened PR, a withdrawal, and an out-of-band review. Ask reviewers to record substantive feedback on the PR so it can count.
- **Weeks 3–6** — Pair engineers into two rotations with comparable review volume and coverage. Randomly choose which rotation has its reward window in weeks 3–4. The other gets weeks 5–6. During weeks 3–4, compare each rotation’s change from its own baseline for an indication of what the reward added. At the end of week 6, the prize ends and automatic routing to the primary and backup stays on.
- **Weeks 7–10** — Use the percentage of eligible PRs receiving a useful review within one working day to decide what to keep. At **80% or above**, keep the workflow. At **65–79%**, adjust coverage or routing and measure again. **Below 65%**, retire the incentive programme and revisit why reviews are waiting. Use the full four weeks after prizes end for the decision.

### What could go wrong

A reviewer might rush an approval or ask a token question to earn credit. Each week, a rotating engineer samples five credited reviews against the launch rule. **Two or more that fail the rule pause the reward.** The engineering lead decides when to resume. The pilot owner corrects counts before awarding time off and handles private disputes using the PR record.

Large PRs and difficult cases can still bottleneck. The reviewer can identify a concrete issue within one day and continue the review afterward. The backup covers overload and absences. Keep administration under an hour a week for one owner by automating the count and spending the weekly check on the five-review sample, withdrawals, and any disputes.
