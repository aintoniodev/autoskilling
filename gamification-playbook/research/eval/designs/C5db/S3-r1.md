# Three-ward hand hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene observed in the existing monthly audit.** Each ward can earn a catered meal, available across all shifts, and a specific thank-you from the chief nursing officer at handover. Announce the reward and the ward’s target at launch. A ward earns it if its pooled audit score over two reward months reaches 90%, or improves by at least five percentage points from baseline. For wards starting at or above 90%, the target is to maintain their baseline. Kiosk entries carry no points, quotas, or prizes. A nurse who misses a shift or forgets an entry can resume with the next eligible moment.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Before launch, walk through the workflow with day, night, and float nurses from each ward. Give them 15 minutes of paid practice and fix kiosk placement, queues, and slow screens before starting. Prefill the ward and shift and put the entry screen first. Nurses log a completed hand hygiene action when care permits. One action earns one entry even if it covers several indications. During urgent care or an outage, skip the entry and resume at the next eligible moment. At handover, tell staff which problems they reported and what changed.

**Use the audit and kiosk for different decisions.** Infection Prevention calculates observed compliance as completed hand hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. Use two comparable monthly audits for baseline. Before launch, Infection Prevention sets a minimum sample and shift mix for judging the award, then uses the same observation protocol across wards and phases. Vary observation times within the existing audit schedule. Pool the counts across both months before calculating the score. An undersampled ward’s decision stays pending until the next adequate routine audit. Report the result as *observed compliance*, since staff may change their behaviour when an observer is present. [WHO hand hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Show staff what they need.** The kiosk confirms “Saved” and offers an immediate undo. Store ward, shift, and moment category without staff or patient identifiers. At handover, show the ward’s latest audit result, sample size, target, and kiosk fixes. Staff and managers see ward-level summaries only. Keep the data out of individual performance reviews and skip ward rankings. The kiosk owner handles entry and outage issues. Infection Prevention resolves disputed audit counts and makes the award decision.

**Run eight reward weeks per ward, then stop.** Give all wards four weeks with the fixed kiosk and feedback before prizes begin. Randomly assign reward starts at weeks 5, 9, and 13 so the wards still waiting give a rough comparison for what recognition adds. Keep the prefilled kiosk and feedback after prizes end. At four and eight weeks after each ward finishes, check the share of staffed ward-shifts with at least one plausible kiosk entry. This measures how widely the workflow is used across shifts. At week eight, keep the workflow at 80% or above, revise it at 60–79%, and retire it below 60%, subject to the safety rule below. With three wards, treat comparisons as directional.

**Catch failures early.** Before launch, test a double tap, an undone entry, a missed entry during urgent care, and an outage. Suppress double submissions, remove undone entries, and leave missing entries missing. The ward improvement lead spends up to 45 minutes a week checking for bursts, outages, and staff-reported friction. If a kiosk interaction delays care, suspend logging on that ward immediately. If an adequately sampled monthly audit falls five percentage points or more below baseline, Infection Prevention pauses the reward decision and investigates before restarting. A rise in kiosk entries never overrides either trigger.
