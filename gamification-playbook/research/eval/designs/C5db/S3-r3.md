## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for audited hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one covered, paid 30-minute break per nurse, including nights, weekends, and part-time staff. The nursing director announces the reward at launch, thanks the ward at handover when it earns it, and funds staggered cover so everyone can take the break without reducing bedside staffing. The launch rule is simple. “Your ward earns a covered paid break when it meets its hand-hygiene goal across two monthly audits.” Kiosk entries carry zero reward value.

**Make logging fit the shift.** In the first two weeks, give nurses from each ward and shift ten paid minutes to try the kiosk and identify where it adds walking, queues, or risk. Infection Prevention and Control signs off on placement and cleaning. Fix reported problems before starting rewards. Set the kiosk to open directly to a short form with the ward prefilled and an immediate “Saved” confirmation with an undo option. Use the kiosk after hand hygiene when care permits. If it is busy or offline, continue care and resume at the next opportunity. Share what staff reported and what changed at handover.

**Use the existing monthly audit to decide the reward.** Divide correctly performed hand-hygiene actions by the opportunities observers saw for nurses on that ward. Count each observed opportunity once, including opportunities where the nurse missed hand hygiene. A correctly performed action counts even if the kiosk was unavailable or the nurse skipped logging. Pool the counts from the two reward months. Set each ward’s target at five percentage points above its two-month baseline, capped at 95%. A ward already at or above 95% aims to maintain its baseline. Infection Prevention and Control sets the minimum sample and shift coverage before launch, using the existing audit protocol. If either month falls short, hold the decision until the next adequate audit. Keep the mix of shifts and observation windows comparable, and rotate sessions within the existing audit schedule. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

**Show staff their ward’s progress.** At the usual ward huddle and on the staff board, show the latest audit rate, sample size, ward goal, and reward status. Track kiosk uptake separately as the share of staffed ward shifts with at least one saved, non-voided entry, using existing kiosk timestamps and ward rosters. Show only the monthly ward aggregate. This measures how widely the kiosk is used across shifts. An extra entry on the same shift leaves that measure unchanged, and an undone entry drops out. Managers receive ward totals. The pilot uses no individual scores, patient identifiers, rankings, or data for performance reviews.

**Stagger the start and set the end.** Give all wards the fixes, paid practice, and feedback in months 1 and 2. Draw the start order for two-month reward windows beginning in months 3, 4, and 5. Compare changes while some wards are still receiving feedback alone. With three wards, treat that comparison as directional. End each reward window after its second scheduled audit, even if a payout decision is pending. Keep the simplified kiosk and monthly feedback in place. Recheck the same audit rate one and two months after rewards end. Keep the routine if the pooled follow-up meets the ward’s goal. Revise the workflow if it falls short but stays at or above baseline. If it falls below baseline, drop the incentive design and have Infection Prevention and Control review the workflow. Use the kiosk uptake trend to identify where logging still needs work.

**Act on problems early.** Duplicate and batch entries cannot unlock a reward. Observer effects and uneven shift coverage can still skew the result. If any valid monthly audit drops five percentage points below baseline, or staff report that the kiosk delayed care, Infection Prevention and Control pauses the reward decision and investigates. The ward educator takes count disputes privately. The audit lead corrects the source tally and republishes the ward total. The educator runs the pilot from existing reports and huddles, with a target of under one hour a week outside routine audits.
