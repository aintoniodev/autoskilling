## Six-week knowledge-base adoption programme

**Reward a resolved ticket with a useful article.** The launch rule is “Earn one credit when you resolve a ticket with an existing article or a published article you wrote that explains the actual fix.” Earn one credit per ticket, even if it has several links. Linking a good article and writing a needed one earn the same credit. For cases with no reusable solution or where documenting the fix would expose customer information, agents resolve the ticket normally. The existing CSAT bonus stays in place.

**Make it easy during the shift.** In the first two weeks, give six agents across shifts and queues 30 minutes of paid time to try the workflow and identify what slows them down. Fix the main blockers before rewards start, such as poor search, publishing permissions or slow approval. Tell the team what changed because of their feedback. Give every agent protected practice time and a paid block for article writing when needed. Put article search and linking on the resolution screen, with a prefilled draft for knowledge gaps. Let agents close a ticket while an article is awaiting publication. The credit arrives when the article is published and linked.

**Use existing ticket and article records.** Measure the share of unique resolved tickets with a linked, published article. Keep all resolved tickets in the denominator, including tickets handled through the old workflow. Attribute credit to the resolving agent, and confirm it after the ticket has stayed resolved for seven days. If it reopens during that window or the link is removed, withdraw the pending credit. A later qualifying resolution can earn the ticket’s single credit. Before launch, have the support operations lead test duplicate links, reopenings, removed links, late publication and tickets closed through the old workflow. Use only article relationships the systems already record.

**Show agents their own progress.** When a link qualifies, show a private confirmation on the ticket with the article title and pending credit. Each agent sees their credited tickets and a goal of qualifying on 40% of their resolved tickets over their four reward weeks, rounded up. Calculate it from actual volume so leave and part-time schedules don’t create a catch-up quota. There are no weekly streaks. Announce the reward at launch. Anyone who reaches their goal earns 45 minutes of paid time off, with coverage arranged by the team lead and the reward handed over in person. Post the team’s aggregate progress in its usual weekly channel. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews.

**Stagger rewards and set an end date.** Give everyone the fixes and private feedback for two weeks. Then start four reward weeks for half the existing teams, with the other half starting two weeks later. Balance the groups by queue mix and baseline link rate. During the overlap, compare each group’s change from its own baseline to estimate what the reward adds. Allow one final week for pending credits to settle. Keep the article workflow on the resolution screen after prizes end. Four weeks later, use the same org-wide qualifying ticket rate to decide what to do with that workflow. At **40% or higher**, keep it as the default. At **20–39%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck. Below **20%**, remove the resolution prompt and rethink the workflow. Verify the current 12% baseline with the same counting rule before launch.

**Watch for irrelevant links, rushed service and uneven opportunity.** Each week, the support operations lead samples eight credited tickets across queues and checks that the article matches the fix, is current and contains no customer data. If two or more fail, or any exposes customer data, the lead pauses new reward credits, corrects counts privately and fixes the cause before resuming. Use the existing CSAT and reopen reports to check for deterioration by queue, taking response volume and ticket mix into account. If payouts cluster in queues with the easiest cases, review article coverage and publishing delays before running another round. The weekly sample, scorecard and routine corrections should fit within one hour.
