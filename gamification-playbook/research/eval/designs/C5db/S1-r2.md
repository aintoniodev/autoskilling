# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward one completed safety check per job.** The launch rule is “Your team earns a paid crew meal when 70% of its jobs have a completed app checklist before work starts during its four-week reward window.” The company pays for the meal and the time, with equivalent arrangements for technicians on different shifts or routes. The safety lead thanks each crew in person for a specific example of good safety practice. A checklist that identifies a hazard earns the same credit as one that clears the job when the crew follows the required stop or escalation procedure.

**Make it workable before launch.** The safety lead and a union steward walk through the app with technicians from different shifts, including crews with poor coverage. Give technicians paid time to practice and include the check in scheduled job time. Fix the friction they find, such as repeated job details or unreliable offline saves, and tell crews what changed. Agree with the steward on the counting rule, reward, data access and correction process. Keep the approved paper fallback available whenever the app is unusable. If the safety check cannot be completed, work waits.

**Count jobs using existing records.** Match the app’s completed checklist to its dispatch job ID and compare the recorded completion time with the existing job-start time. Count each job once, including jobs with multiple technicians or repeat submissions. The denominator is all jobs that started plus jobs formally stopped at the pre-job safety check, using existing dispatch records. For a stopped job, the checklist must be completed by the recorded stop time. Exclude cancellations before the crew reaches the site. A voided checklist loses credit unless a valid replacement was completed in time. Approved paper checks and late app checks stay in the denominator and receive no digital credit. Show paper fallback separately in team totals so outages are visible. Accept late syncs when the app preserves a reliable offline completion time. Confirm these rules against real dispatch records before launch. Resolve any missing or unreliable timestamps before tying a reward to them.

**Give technicians immediate feedback and a way to fix mistakes.** After submission, show “Checklist complete for job 123” or “Saved offline, credit pending sync.” Each technician can see their own credited jobs and an optional goal to complete the next four out of five assigned jobs, carrying the goal across weeks. A missed week costs nothing. Post each crew’s aggregate rate and progress toward the meal in its existing dispatch channel. Managers see crew totals for the programme. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews, and there is no named ranking. Technicians can raise a disputed count privately with the coordinator, who checks the source records, fixes the tally and replies within two working days.

**Run four reward weeks per crew, then remove the reward.** Give every crew the workflow fixes and feedback together. Measure two weeks with those changes in place, then start comparable crews in two waves three weeks apart. Use the overlap to estimate what the reward adds beyond the fixes and feedback. Before each crew’s reward ends, make the prefilled checklist the first action when opening an assigned job. Keep that default and the completion receipt. Retire the reward tally once corrections close.

**Agree on the decision before launch.** Use one measure four weeks after each crew’s reward ends, the share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-job app checklist during those four weeks. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow. At **50% to below 70%**, fix the remaining barriers and recheck. Below **50%**, drop the incentive and investigate the workflow. The safety requirement stays in force throughout.

**Watch for rushed checks, misleading counts and crew pressure.** Use eight jobs from existing safety spot checks each week to look for copied answers, checks completed after work began and missed hazard escalations. Two materially invalid checks in eight, or one missed critical escalation, pauses reward decisions. The safety lead makes that call with the union steward and corrects the cause before resuming. Keep credited work on record during a pause. If an outage makes the milestone unfair, extend the affected crew’s window by the time lost. Limit the coordinator’s weekly work to the tally, sample results and exceptions so it fits within an hour.
