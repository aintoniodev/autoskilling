# One working day for a code review

Run a six-week pilot for the 25-engineer team. Call it the **one-working-day review pilot**. The goal is for at least 80% of review-ready PRs to get a decision within one working day. The launch message names the reward up front: “If we hit 80% and pass the quality check during our two reward weeks, everyone in the group gets a paid half-day off.”

## Fix the queue before offering the reward

Use the first two weeks to give everyone a shared review queue, put the oldest ready PRs first, and assign a rotating primary reviewer and backup. Protect 30 minutes a day for the person on rotation and reduce their sprint commitments accordingly. Route reviews that need specialist knowledge to an available code owner.

Ask a handful of engineers who regularly submit and review PRs where reviews stall and what makes them risky to pick up. Use paid team time for the discussion. Publish the fixes in the channel the team already uses, with a short note connecting each fix to the problem engineers raised.

## What counts

**A PR counts once when a human reviewer approves it or requests changes with an actionable reason within eight working hours of the PR first becoming ready.**

Use existing PR timestamps and review events. A PR opened ready starts the clock at opening. A draft starts when it first becomes ready. Count hours during the team’s published working window, excluding weekends and team holidays. A Friday PR marked ready one hour before close is due seven working hours into Monday, assuming Monday is a working day.

The weekly rate is the share of eligible PRs first marked ready that week that received a qualifying review on time. Calculate it once every PR in that week has reached its deadline. Include overdue PRs and PRs switched back to draft after the clock started. Count the first qualifying decision only. A bot approval, a “looking now” comment, or an approval withdrawn before the deadline earns no credit. A substantive request for changes counts even if the PR never merges.

Before launch, have the rotation coordinator test the rule on real examples, including a duplicate approval, a withdrawn approval, a Friday handoff, and a decision made in chat. Ask reviewers to record chat decisions on the PR so the author and the queue have the same record. The coordinator handles count disputes privately and corrects the weekly tally.

## What engineers see

After a qualifying review, send the reviewer a private confirmation through the existing PR integration, for example, “PR #418 received a decision in 5 working hours.” During the pilot, each reviewer can see their own on-time count and the number of PRs they were assigned while on duty. Use four out of every five as the personal target across the pilot. Someone assigned two PRs sees progress against those two opportunities. A missed week carries no penalty.

Post the team’s weekly percentage and numerator and denominator in its usual channel. Managers receive only team totals. Keep individual figures out of performance reviews. The reward is shared, including with engineers who had no review assignments. The engineering lead announces it in the team meeting and protects the time off in the sprint plan.

## Timing, quality, and the decision

Give everyone the queue fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. If the team already has two distinct review groups with little overlap, draw lots to determine which group gets the reward window in weeks 3–4 and which gets it in weeks 5–6. Compare each group with its own queue-only period. If the same engineers cover both queues, run one team-wide reward window in weeks 3–4. Use the queue-only weeks as a comparison and treat the difference as directional.

Each reward week, the rotation coordinator samples five credited PRs, or all of them if there are fewer. Check whether the reviewer’s decision was defensible given the diff, the checks, and the context available at the time. If two sampled decisions in a week miss a material issue or give the author no actionable path forward, the engineering lead pauses the award and the coordinator fixes the cause before rewards resume. Pay particular attention to rushed approvals, easy PRs being picked ahead of tricky ones, and large PRs with no available owner. Use the oldest-first queue and the backup reviewer to address those routing problems. Keep reporting, sampling, and corrections within one coordinator hour a week.

Before rewards end, make the ready queue, routing, and backup coverage the default. Use one number to decide what happens next: **the percentage of eligible PRs reviewed within one working day across the four weeks after prizes stop**. At 80% or above, keep the review workflow and retire the reward. At 65–79%, change the workflow where delays cluster and recheck four weeks later. Below 65%, drop the incentive programme and revisit review capacity and ownership.
