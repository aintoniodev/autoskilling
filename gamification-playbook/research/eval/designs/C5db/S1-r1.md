# Pre-job checklist pilot

**Goal.** Raise digital checklist completion from about 35% to at least 75% of eligible jobs four weeks after rewards end, while protecting technicians’ ability to stop work and report hazards.

### Before launch

The safety lead and union steward run two paid, 30-minute sessions with technicians from different shifts, job types and coverage areas. Walk through a real job from dispatch to setup. Fix the barriers technicians identify, such as poor reception, duplicate entry or schedules that leave no time for the check. Give every crew paid practice time and tell them which changes came from their feedback.

Make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens an assigned job. Prefill job details and leave safety answers blank. Test offline saving and later sync. Agree with the union on the counting rule, reward, access to data and appeals before launch. Keep the approved paper fallback available when the app fails. Protect stop-work and reporting rights throughout the pilot. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management%2Fworker-participation)

### What earns credit

**One completed digital safety checklist per eligible job, saved before work starts or when the technician puts the job on a safety hold before starting.** A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a hold earns the same credit as one that clears the job to proceed.

Use existing job IDs, checklist records and dispatch start or hold records. The denominator is jobs that started or reached a documented pre-start safety hold. Exclude jobs cancelled before either event. Count a shared job once, for the assigned crew. An offline submission earns credit after sync if the existing record establishes when it was completed. A paper fallback still satisfies the safety procedure and stays in the denominator, with confirmed outages shown separately so the team can see where the app is failing. Validate the timestamps in the pilot before tying a reward to them.

Before launch, test a duplicate submission, a voided checklist, a late entry, an offline sync, a paper fallback and a hazard hold. Duplicates count once, voids remove credit, and late entries earn zero.

### What people see and earn

After saving, the technician sees **“Pre-job checklist recorded”** or **“Saved on device. Credit pending sync.”** Their private view shows completed checks out of eligible jobs and a personal target of seven per ten over the pilot, scaled to actual job volume. A missed shift or week carries no penalty. The next job is a fresh chance.

Crews see their weekly aggregate in the existing safety huddle. Managers see crew totals with small groups combined where needed to protect privacy. Individual incentive data stays out of manager dashboards, discipline and performance reviews. Technicians can flag a wrong count through the steward. The safety lead decides the appeal with the steward and the dispatch administrator corrects the record.

Announce the reward at kickoff. Every crew that reaches **70% over its four reward weeks** earns a company-paid meal during a regular paid safety huddle, with equivalent arrangements across shifts. The operations lead attends and thanks the crew for a specific improvement. Injury and near-miss counts play no part in eligibility.

### Timing and decision

Give everyone the fixes, app default and feedback for two weeks. Then randomly assign comparable crews to start their four-week reward window two weeks apart. Compare early and later crews during those two weeks to see what the meal adds after the same workflow improvements. Close each crew’s reward window on schedule and check its completion rate again four weeks later.

Use the four-week **post-reward digital completion rate** to decide. At **75% or above**, keep the workflow and close the programme. At **60–74%**, fix the largest remaining barrier and recheck. Below **60%**, drop the incentive design and rework the digital rollout with technicians and the union. The pre-job safety procedure continues at every threshold.

### What could go wrong

Rushed checks, backdating and pressure to clear a hazardous job are the main risks. The safety lead samples ten records a week through the existing safety review and checks timing, job-specific answers and follow-through on flagged hazards. Pause rewards if fewer than nine pass, or immediately if there is credible pressure to start unsafely or suppress a report. The safety lead and steward agree on the fix and verify it before rewards resume. Use an automated weekly tally and the existing huddle to keep routine administration under an hour.
