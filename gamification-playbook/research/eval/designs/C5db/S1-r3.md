## Safe starts pilot

Raise the share of eligible jobs with a valid digital safety checklist completed before work starts from about 35% to 75%, and test whether that level holds after the reward ends.

**Fix the workflow first.** Spend two weeks with union stewards and a paid group of technicians from different shifts and coverage areas. Walk through a normal job, a no-signal job, and a job stopped for a hazard. Fix the problems they find, such as slow sign-in, missing job details, or offline saves, and show crews what changed. Agree with the union on paid checklist time, the counting rule, access to data, and the correction process. Verify the 35% baseline using the same rule the pilot will use.

**Reward one safe start per job.** Tell crews at launch: “If your crew completes valid digital checklists before work on at least 75% of eligible jobs during its four-week pilot, everyone on the crew earns one hour of paid release time.” Confirm scheduling and coverage before announcing the reward, and have the field lead recognize the crew at a regular huddle. A checklist that leads to a safety stop earns the same credit as one that clears the job. A missed job or week leaves all previous credit intact.

**Measure it with existing records.** Use the dispatch job ID, the app’s checklist completion record, and the existing work-start or safety-stop record. Count each job once when its completed checklist was saved before work started, or recorded a stop before work began. Accept late syncs when the original offline save time establishes the order. Voided checklists lose credit, and a correction submitted after work starts cannot restore it. Include eligible jobs completed with the approved paper fallback in the denominator, with a separate fallback count so recurring app failures are visible. Exclude jobs cancelled before the pre-job assessment was due. Test these cases with crews before launch. If existing records cannot establish the sequence reliably, fix the workflow before starting the reward.

**Show technicians their own progress.** After saving, the app confirms the job and its status, including whether it is awaiting sync. Each technician sees their own eligible jobs and credited checklists, with a personal target scaled to their job volume. The regular crew huddle shows the crew’s aggregate progress toward 75%. Managers see crew totals only. Programme counts stay out of performance reviews and discipline. Technicians can flag a wrong count to the pilot coordinator, who checks the source record and corrects it before the reward is finalized.

**Run four reward weeks per crew.** Give every crew the fixes, private feedback, and a prefilled checklist as the first step in the normal job screen. Keep the approved fallback available. Start crews in three waves, two weeks apart, with the order drawn among comparable crews where scheduling allows. Compare crews earning the reward with crews still waiting during the same weeks to estimate what the reward adds. After each crew’s four weeks, deliver any earned time and keep the app workflow and feedback in place. Use the valid pre-job digital completion rate in weeks three and four after rewards end as the decision measure. At 75% or above, keep the workflow as standard. From 60% to 74%, fix the main barrier and remeasure. Below 60%, retire the reward design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards.

**Watch for rushed checks, delayed start logging, and pressure to proceed.** Each week, an existing safety reviewer checks 10 credited jobs across crews against the work records and checks whether the answers fit the job. The safety lead pauses reward decisions if two or more fail that check, or immediately on a credible report that someone was pressured to start unsafe work. The coordinator and union steward review the cause and agree on the correction before rewards resume. Automate the counts and cap routine administration, including the sample and corrections, at one hour a week.
