# Knowledge-base adoption programme

**Reward.** Each agent who uses a relevant knowledge-base article on at least 30% of their eligible resolved tickets during their four-week reward window earns 30 minutes of paid flex time. The goal scales to the agent’s actual ticket volume. An agent with 20 eligible tickets needs six qualifying tickets. Absences carry no penalty, and progress resumes on the next case. The existing CSAT bonus stays separate.

**Fix the workflow first.** In the first two weeks, sit with agents from different queues and shifts to see where finding, linking or writing an article slows them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected practice time. Put article search and linking in the ticket resolution screen, and give agents a quick way to start a draft from the ticket. For gaps that need editorial review, assign a reviewer and let agents close the customer’s ticket as soon as the issue is resolved. Show agents what changed in response to their feedback. Start rewards once agents can complete the workflow within their normal paid time and the existing ticket and article records can verify it.

**Count completed work.** Tell agents the rule in one sentence: “A resolved ticket counts once when it has a relevant published article linked, including an article you wrote for that ticket and linked before closing it that is approved within seven days.” Use existing ticket IDs, resolution history, linked article IDs and article status. Credit the resolving agent. Count every resolved ticket in the denominator except where policy prohibits using an article, using existing ticket categories to apply those exclusions consistently. Check how that denominator compares with the reported 12% baseline before launch.

Before launch, test a duplicate link, a reopened ticket and a ticket closed through the old workflow. A duplicate earns one credit. A reopened ticket is assessed on its final resolution and can earn one credit across the programme. An eligible ticket closed without an article remains in the denominator. Let the final week’s tickets settle for seven days before confirming awards. The support operations lead handles disputed counts and corrects reporting errors.

**Show progress where agents work.** After resolution, show the agent which article counted or that their draft is awaiting approval. In the same tool, show their private qualifying count, eligible total and progress toward 30%. Post each team’s percentage weekly in its existing team channel. Managers get team totals only, with no individual drill-down. Keep programme data out of performance reviews.

**Run it and end it.** Give all teams the fixes and private feedback first. Start the four-week reward windows in two cohorts, two weeks apart, so the later cohort offers a short comparison under the improved workflow with feedback alone. Before each window ends, make article search the first option in the resolution screen and keep linking and drafting available there. Four weeks after the last reward window, measure the percentage of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article across the organisation. Set the decision before launch: **keep** the workflow and feedback as designed at 30% or above, **change** them at 20% to below 30%, and **drop** the incentive design and revisit the workflow below 20%. Rewards end on schedule in all cases.

**Watch for shortcuts and customer impact.** Each week, the support operations lead samples eight credited tickets across participating teams and checks whether the article helped resolve the issue and was safe to publish. If two or more fail, the lead pauses awards, corrects affected counts and resumes once a fresh sample passes. Use the existing service review to watch for slower resolution, more reopens or falling CSAT. Remind agents to close resolved tickets promptly and give them protected time for article work. Review the automated totals, sample and corrections in under an hour a week.
