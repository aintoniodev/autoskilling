# Three-ward hand-hygiene logging pilot

**Reward the ward for accurately logging hand hygiene that took place.** Each ward earns one hospital-funded team meal, with equivalent options for day, night and weekend staff, if at least 80% of the hand-hygiene actions sampled in its monthly audit have a matching kiosk entry. Use the same threshold for all three wards. Every shift shares in the reward, including staff who were off during the audit.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Before launch, walk through the workflow with day- and night-shift nurses from each ward. Give them 10 minutes of paid, covered time to try it. Put the kiosk on an existing walking route, open it directly to the logging screen, prefill the ward, and make undo easy. Fix any placement, queue or access problem before starting rewards. Post the fixes in the shift huddle. Nurses clean their hands at the point of care and log when care safely allows. During an emergency or outbreak surge, patient care takes priority and the reward window can be extended.

**Measure a sample through the existing monthly audit.** Infection Prevention selects a small sample of observed, completed hand-hygiene actions across day, night and weekend shifts. Use the kiosk’s existing ward, moment type and event-time fields to match those actions one-to-one within an agreed time window. Validate the window with nurses before launch. Test a duplicate, an undone entry, two similar actions close together and an action that was never logged. A duplicate can count once, an undone entry is removed, and an unlogged action is a miss. Exclude ambiguous matches and report how many were excluded. Require at least 20 clearly assessable actions per ward before awarding the meal. If the audit falls short, carry the decision to the next audit.

Keep the monthly clinical compliance measure based on observers’ recording of correctly performed actions divided by observed opportunities. Kiosk entries are self-reports. If observed compliance drops by five percentage points or more from baseline, Infection Prevention pauses the reward and reviews the audit mix, supplies and logging burden before deciding whether to resume. The kiosk administrator corrects disputed counts, and Infection Prevention resolves any audit disputes. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Give feedback where nurses already look.** After each entry, show the moment saved and an Undo button. In the existing shift huddle, share the ward’s latest audited result as a numerator and denominator, its progress toward the meal, and one fix made in response to staff feedback. Managers see ward totals only. Keep names and patient identifiers out of the pilot reporting, with no individual rankings, quotas, streaks or use in performance reviews. After an absence or a missed entry, resume with the next safe opportunity.

**Run four monthly audit cycles, then check retention.** In month one, all wards get the workflow fixes and feedback, and Infection Prevention records a baseline. Randomize the ward order and offer the meal incentive for one audit cycle per ward, starting in months two, three and four. Compare each ward with its baseline and with wards still waiting for their incentive. Keep the direct-to-log screen and save confirmation in place after rewards end. At each ward’s first audit at least four weeks after its reward ends, use its audited logging rate to decide the kiosk’s future. Keep the workflow at 80% or above, revise and retest at 60–79%, and drop routine kiosk logging below 60%. Continue standard hand-hygiene audits throughout.

**Watch for gaming, audit bias and extra work.** A burst of entries or a queue at the kiosk gets a workflow check and earns no extra credit. Keep audit timing unannounced and shift coverage comparable. If the existing kiosk fields cannot support reliable matching or the sample needs new tracking of individual nurses, defer the logging reward until the measurement works with existing records. One Infection Prevention coordinator runs the pilot in under an hour a week using the existing audits, kiosk export and huddles.
