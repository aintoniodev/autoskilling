## Reward a safety checklist completed before the job starts

Run a 12-week pilot. Use **the percentage of eligible jobs with a complete digital checklist submitted before work starts** to decide whether to keep the rollout. Check the reported 35% baseline against that definition before launch.

### Weeks 1–2 · Fix the workflow with technicians

Give a small group of technicians from different shifts and routes, including union stewards, paid time to try the app on real jobs. Find out where it costs them time or creates pressure to start work early. Fix the biggest barriers first, especially poor signal, repeated data entry, and schedules that leave no time for the check. Tell crews what changed because of their feedback.

Agree with union representatives on the counting rule, reward, data access, and correction process before announcing the pilot. Give everyone paid practice time and build checklist time into the job schedule.

### What earns credit

Tell technicians: **“You get one credit per job when you complete the app checklist before work starts, including when it tells you to stop and escalate a hazard.”**

Match the checklist’s existing submission record to the job ID and the existing dispatch record of work starting. For jobs stopped over a hazard, use the existing stop-work record to verify the checklist was completed first. Confirm that these records reflect the actual order of events before tying them to rewards. Deduplicate by job ID, remove credit if a submission is voided, and use the original completion time when an offline checklist syncs. Test those cases, plus a late submission, a paper fallback, and a hazard stop, before launch.

Keep the approved safety fallback available. A paper checklist gets recorded as a fallback and stays in the adoption denominator for eligible jobs. Exclude jobs cancelled before any safety check was needed. If existing records can’t reliably establish what happened first, delay rewards until the workflow produces trustworthy records.

### What technicians see and earn

Show a private confirmation in the app after each checklist: **“Job 4821 checklist saved before start. Credit earned.”** If it’s offline, show that it’s saved and credit is pending sync. Technicians see their own credited jobs and progress toward a goal based on their job volume: **four out of every five eligible jobs during their four-week reward window**, with a smaller target for low-volume routes. A missed day or week leaves the next job available to earn credit.

Anyone who meets the goal earns **30 minutes of paid release time**, scheduled on shift. Keep that time out of production targets. Post team adoption percentages weekly in the existing crew briefing, combining small crews where a total could identify an individual. Managers see aggregate results only. Keep credit records out of performance reviews and discipline. A named programme coordinator handles private count disputes and corrects errors before rewards are issued.

### Timing and decision

Give every crew the workflow fixes and feedback in week 1. Start four weeks of rewards for half the crews in week 3 and for the other half in week 5. Pair crews with similar baseline adoption and job mix, then draw the start order within each pair. Compare the two groups in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds.

Before the first reward window ends, make the checklist the first screen when a technician opens a job. Pre-fill job details and leave safety answers blank. Rewards end for everyone after week 8. Measure adoption again in weeks 11–12. **Keep** the checklist default and feedback at 75% or higher. **Change** the workflow and retest at 50–74%. **Drop** the incentive approach below 50% and work with technicians and union stewards on the remaining barriers. The safety check remains required throughout.

### What could go wrong

Technicians may rush answers, record the start late to earn credit, or feel pressure to proceed after flagging a hazard. Each week, the safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected credited jobs using existing records. **Pause rewards if two or more of the 10 have a material defect**, such as an unsupported pre-start time or a hazard recorded without the required escalation. The safety lead decides when to resume after reviewing the fix with a union steward. The coordinator uses the same weekly report for counts, exceptions, and crew totals so routine administration fits within an hour.
