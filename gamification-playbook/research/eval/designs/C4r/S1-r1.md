## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Make the checklist easy to complete on site.** Before launch, have the safety lead and union steward work with technicians from different shifts, including crews with poor reception. Give them paid time to walk through a job, a job they have to stop, and a job with no signal. Fix the blockers they find, such as sign-in delays or missing job details, and post what changed in the existing crew briefing. Open the checklist from the first screen of the assigned job, prefill job details, and give technicians paid time to complete it before work starts. Agree on the reward, data access, and dispute process with union representatives before launch.

**Reward a completed safety check, including a decision to stop.** Give technicians one credit per attended job when the app records a completed checklist before work starts or, if the check stops the job, before the job is placed on hold. A checklist that identifies a hazard earns the same credit as one that clears the job to proceed. Use the existing job ID, checklist completion time, and job status history. Use the original completion time for offline checklists if the app already records it. If the app is unavailable, use the existing safety fallback and show documented outages separately so they don’t count against technicians’ reward eligibility. Before launch, test the rule against a duplicate, a reopened job, a late upload, a hazard stop, and a checklist completed on paper.

**Give technicians private feedback and a reachable milestone.** After each checklist, confirm that it was saved and credited, or show that it’s awaiting sync. Each technician sees their own progress toward completing digital checks on 75% of their eligible jobs during their four-week reward window. Set the goal as a share of actual jobs so it adjusts to workload, shift patterns, and leave. Missing a week doesn’t reset progress. Technicians who reach the goal earn one paid hour off, scheduled with coverage. Show the crew’s weekly completion rate in the existing briefing. Managers see team totals only, with small crews combined where a total could identify someone. Keep the pilot data out of performance reviews and individual rankings. A designated app administrator investigates private count disputes and corrects records, with the union steward available to help technicians raise them.

**Stagger the reward and keep the better workflow.** Give all crews the app fixes and private feedback for two weeks. Then assign comparable crews to two waves. Wave A earns rewards in weeks 3–6 and Wave B in weeks 5–8. Compare their completion rates in weeks 3–4, when both have the same workflow and only Wave A has the reward. Confirm before rewards end that the checklist opens by default for all crews, then keep that default and the completion feedback. Measure the same outcome four weeks after each wave’s rewards end. Use one decision metric agreed before launch, the share of all attended jobs with a timely digital checklist, including jobs stopped for safety. Documented outage jobs stay in this metric so it reflects real-world reliability. Keep the workflow as designed at 75% or higher after rewards end, revise it at 50–74%, and retire the incentive approach below 50% while retaining the required safety check.

**Watch for rushed checks and pressure to proceed.** The safety lead reviews 10 existing records each week, including hazard stops and late uploads when available. Pause reward accrual if two or more sampled checks were materially incomplete or recorded after work began, or if a report of pressure to skip a check or proceed despite a hazard is substantiated. Preserve credits already earned. The safety lead investigates with the union steward and resumes accrual after the cause is addressed. Use one weekly export for team reporting, the sample, and count corrections. Budget 45 minutes a week for routine administration.
