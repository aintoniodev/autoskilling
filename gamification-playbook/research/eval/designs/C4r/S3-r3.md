# Hand hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene verified by its existing monthly audit.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, available across all shifts, if its audit meets the target for its four-week reward window. Set the target at five percentage points above the ward’s pre-reward baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to stay at or above 95%. Each ward earns the reward independently.

The rule staff see is **“Clean your hands at the required moment, log it once when patient care allows, and your ward earns a meal if it meets its observed hand hygiene target.”** Kiosk submissions track adoption. Each additional submission earns zero points.

### Make logging fit the shift

Before launch, the ward educator spends protected time with nurses from day and night shifts on all three wards. Have them try logging during a normal round and identify where the kiosk is out of the way, unavailable, or slow. Fix the main barrier before starting rewards and post a specific update where staff already look, such as “You reported that the kiosk was too far from the bays. We moved it to the exit you already use.” Give nurses paid time during a huddle to practise.

Make the ward’s log screen the kiosk’s opening screen. After a successful entry, show “Hand hygiene moment saved” and offer a way to correct an accidental entry if the kiosk supports it. The ward’s existing huddle board shows its monthly observed compliance, its target, and its weekly kiosk log rate. Staff see immediate confirmation of their own entry. Managers get ward totals, with no individual reports or use in performance reviews. A missed shift or week carries no penalty.

### Measurement and safeguards

- **Hand hygiene.** Use the existing monthly audit’s compliant moments divided by its observed opportunities. Keep its observation method and schedule. Show the denominator alongside the percentage. An audit that falls short of the hospital’s usual coverage requirements leaves the reward decision pending until Infection Prevention obtains a usable result.
- **Kiosk adoption.** Each week, divide saved, non-voided kiosk records by occupied bed-days using existing ward totals. Count a retried save with the same record ID once. Before launch, test a double submission, a correction, and a moment completed while the kiosk is unavailable. That last moment can still count in the observer audit if seen. Kiosk counts measure recorded activity and cannot establish compliance.
- **Quality stop.** Pause rewards on a ward if observed compliance falls five percentage points or more below its pre-reward baseline. The Infection Prevention lead decides whether to restart after reviewing the audit and checking for workflow problems. The kiosk administrator corrects record-count errors. The ward educator reviews a small sample of existing records weekly for retries and voids, without collecting staff identities. If counts jump while observed compliance stays flat, check for duplicate entries, changes in occupancy, or changes in logging before interpreting the increase.

### Timing and ending

Give all three wards the fixes and feedback in weeks 1–4. Record each ward’s baseline log rate and monthly audit result during that period. Start the reward in weeks 5, 9, and 13, one ward at a time, with each four-week window aligned to its usual audit. Compare changes with wards still waiting for their reward window. With three wards and monthly audits, treat the comparison as directional.

Four weeks after each ward’s reward ends, compare its **kiosk logs per occupied bed-day** with its baseline. Keep the kiosk as the default if the rate is at least 10% above baseline and the quality stop has not triggered. If it is above baseline but short of 10%, fix the remaining workflow barrier and recheck once. If it is at or below baseline, drop the incentive and revisit whether the kiosk fits the work. If a ward starts at zero, agree on an absolute log-rate target with its nurses before the reward begins.

The main failure modes are logging interrupting patient care, inflated counts, and audit swings from small samples or changes in who observers see. Tell nurses to put care first and skip the kiosk when it would interrupt care. Review audit coverage across shifts before acting on a swing. After setup, the ward educator can update the board, sample records, and route exceptions in under an hour a week.
