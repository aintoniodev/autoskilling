### Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Make logging fit the shift.** Before launch, walk through the workflow with day- and night-shift nurses from each ward. Give every nurse 10 minutes of protected paid practice. Put the kiosk on an existing staff route, make “Log a moment” the first screen, and prefill the ward and shift. Time the workflow with nurses and fix any detour or queue before starting rewards. Post the fixes in the existing huddle notes. Nurses clean their hands and attend to patients first, then log at the next safe opportunity. During downtime, use the ward’s existing fallback and resume kiosk logging when it returns.

**Reward observed hand hygiene.** One rule for staff: “Your ward earns a catered break for every shift if its final monthly audit meets the ward’s hand-hygiene target.” Provide break coverage across all shifts. Infection Prevention sets the target before launch using the two preceding monthly audits: five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%, with wards already at 95% or above aiming to maintain their baseline. The audit rate is the number of observed opportunities in which hand hygiene was performed divided by all observed opportunities. Each opportunity counts once. A correctly performed action counts even if it was recorded through the fallback or never entered in the kiosk. Kiosk entries carry no prize value. Self-reports can overstate compliance, and people may change their behaviour when they see an observer. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Show feedback where nurses already look.** After each save, the kiosk confirms the moment type and time and offers Undo. A missed entry or shift resets nothing. Weekly huddles show the ward’s share of staffed shift blocks with at least one usable kiosk entry. The monthly audit update shows the ward’s compliance rate, opportunity count, and reward target. Use existing kiosk records and staffing totals to calculate uptake. Managers see ward aggregates only, with no named leaderboard, individual drill-down, or use in performance reviews. Capture no patient identifiers.

**Stagger the reward and remove it on schedule.** Give all three wards the improved kiosk, paid practice, and feedback for one full month. Then start two-month reward windows one month apart, in a randomly drawn ward order. The stagger gives each ward a turn and helps show whether uptake changes when the reward starts. Keep the kiosk as the default logging route after the prize ends. Four weeks after each ward’s window closes, use the previous four weeks’ share of staffed shift blocks with a usable kiosk entry to decide the rollout: **80% or more**, keep the workflow; **50–79%**, fix the main barrier and recheck in four weeks; **below 50%**, drop the kiosk as the default and restore the prior logging route. This measures routine use across shifts. It cannot establish logging completeness.

**Catch errors and unintended effects.** Before launch, test a duplicate submission, an undone entry, and a fallback entry. Count a retry once, exclude undone entries, and keep fallback entries out of kiosk uptake. Each week, the pilot lead samples five saved records per ward for duplicates, invalid fields, and undo handling. If more than 10% fail, pause that ward’s uptake reporting until the kiosk administrator fixes and reruns the count. Infection Prevention pauses reward decisions if audited compliance falls more than five percentage points below baseline or the audit misses its usual sampling minimum. Keep audit timing and shift coverage comparable, and investigate rising kiosk use alongside falling audit compliance. Assign the pilot lead 45 minutes weekly for the sample, huddle update, and exception review. The existing audit team continues its monthly schedule.
