# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward a completed pre-job check.** A technician earns one credit when the app records a complete safety checklist for an assigned job before the existing job-start record. Count each job once. A checklist that identifies a hazard and leads to a documented stop-work decision also earns credit, using the existing stop-work record. The rule technicians see is “Complete the digital safety check before starting the job, or record a hazard that stops the job, and earn one credit.”

Before launch, work with union representatives and technicians from several crews, including those who work offline. Give them paid time to try the checklist on real routes. Fix the slow or unreliable steps and post what changed in the existing crew briefing. Agree with the union on the reward, paid training time, data access and correction process. Keep the approved fallback available whenever the app fails or using it would be unsafe.

## Measurement and feedback

- **Use existing records.** Match checklist and dispatch records by job ID. Test duplicates, voided checklists, rescheduled work, stop-work decisions, paper fallback and late offline sync before launch. Credit valid offline completions when they sync if the app already records a reliable completion time. If it cannot establish the order of events, fix that before rewarding offline work. Exclude jobs cancelled before a technician carries out the check. Keep jobs completed with the approved fallback in the adoption denominator and track the reasons in aggregate.
- **Give immediate, private feedback.** After submission, show “Pre-job check recorded” and the technician’s progress toward their milestone. Show “Saved offline” when credit is pending. In the existing weekly crew briefing, show the team’s percentage of eligible jobs with a timely digital check. Roll small crews into a larger group where totals would identify individuals.
- **Offer a modest, reachable reward.** Award one 30-minute paid release period per technician during their four-week reward window. Set the milestone before launch using typical assigned job volume, for example 8 checks for someone expected to have 12 eligible jobs, or 2 for someone expected to have 3. Allow adjustments for leave and reassignment. Credits accumulate across the window, so a missed week carries no penalty. Schedule earned time through the normal roster process.
- **Limit access.** Technicians see their own counts. Managers see aggregate adoption only. Use the existing payroll or roster process to fulfil the reward without sharing checklist counts. Keep programme data out of performance reviews and discipline. A designated programme owner handles disputed counts privately and corrects credit before the next weekly update.

## Timing and decision

Use two weeks of existing records to confirm the roughly 35% baseline. Give every crew the workflow fixes, paid practice and feedback at the same time. After two weeks with those changes in place, start the four-week reward window for half the crews and start the other half two weeks later. Where scheduling permits, assign start dates by crew and balance the groups by baseline adoption and job mix. Compare the groups during the two weeks when only the first group has rewards.

Before either group’s prizes end, make the checklist the first action when opening a job and prefill job details. Technicians still make each safety judgment themselves. Keep the private confirmation and team reporting after rewards end.

Use one decision metric: **the percentage of eligible jobs with a valid digital check recorded before work began or a documented stop-work decision**. Measure it across all crews in weeks three and four after the final reward ends. Agree the thresholds up front. At **75% or more**, keep the workflow and feedback. At **50–74%**, fix the main remaining barrier and recheck. Below **50%**, end the incentive programme and revisit how the app fits field work with technicians and the union. The safety procedure continues throughout.

## What could go wrong

- **Rushed or backfilled checks.** The safety lead reviews 10 randomly selected credited jobs each week against existing records. Pause new reward credits if two or more checks are materially incomplete or recorded after work began. Investigate and correct the rule or workflow before restarting. Address any immediate hazard through the existing safety process.
- **Pressure to proceed or avoid reporting problems.** Stop-work decisions earn credit, crews have no shared prize, and technicians can flag pressure or counting errors privately through the union representative or programme owner.
- **Unequal access to credits.** Check aggregate fallback rates by crew and review disputed counts for recurring connectivity, assignment or device problems. Fix the underlying issue and restore eligible credits when existing records support them. Automate the weekly totals and reserve up to one hour a week for sampling, corrections and the crew update.
