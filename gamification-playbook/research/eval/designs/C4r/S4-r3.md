# Knowledge base adoption programme

**Make the article step easy first.** Ask six agents across shifts to walk through recent tickets, including cases where searching took too long, the right article was missing, or the ticket contained sensitive information. Give all 60 agents 30 minutes of protected paid practice. Put article search and suggested matches in the ticket resolution panel, with a shortcut to draft an internal article. Assign a knowledge-base editor to review drafts within one business day. Let agents close tickets while drafts are pending. Post the fixes in the support team channel and show which agent feedback prompted them.

## What earns credit

**A resolved ticket earns one credit when it has a relevant, published knowledge-base article linked to it, including a new article that passes editorial review and is published within seven days of resolution.**

Use the ticketing system’s resolution records and the knowledge base’s existing link and publication records. The weekly rate is credited tickets divided by all unique tickets resolved that week. Verify before launch that the existing records can join by ticket ID. Give immediate private confirmation when a link is recorded, show drafts as pending, and finalize credit seven days after resolution.

Test the rule on awkward cases before launch. Two links on one ticket earn one credit. A reopened ticket loses provisional credit and can earn it once at its next resolution. An old-way closure stays in the denominator. A generic article added to fill the field fails the quality check. Agents can skip the link when no safe, relevant article exists and close the ticket on time. New articles must omit customer-identifying details and follow existing access rules.

## What agents see and earn

Each agent sees their own credited tickets, pending drafts, and personal progress toward **one in two of their resolved tickets**. The goal scales with ticket volume. Progress accumulates through the reward window, so a missed week simply means picking up with the next case.

Post team totals and progress toward **50%** in the support channel each week. If a team reaches 50% across its four-week reward window and clears the quality checks, everyone on the team earns one paid hour of their choice, scheduled around coverage. Managers see team totals only. Programme data stays out of performance reviews, and the existing CSAT bonus is calculated independently.

## Timing and ending

Give everyone the fixes and private feedback in weeks 1–2, and measure the rate after those changes. Group existing teams into three waves and draw the start order. Start four-week reward windows in weeks 3, 5, and 7. Compare rewarded teams with teams still receiving feedback alone during the same weeks to see what the paid hour adds.

Before each window ends, make article suggestions the default view in the resolution panel and keep the draft shortcut available. Rewards expire after four weeks. Four weeks after each team’s reward window ends, check its credited-ticket rate again. Decide on the workflow using that rate across all 60 agents, weighted by ticket volume. At **50% or higher**, keep it as the standard. At **30% to under 50%**, fix the most common barrier and remeasure. **Below 30%**, retire the rollout approach and redesign the article step. Keep tracking progress toward article coverage on every resolved ticket.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or low-quality articles.** Each week, the programme owner samples 12 credited tickets across teams and checks whether the article is accurate, relevant to the resolution, and safe to share with its intended audience. If fewer than 11 pass, the owner pauses reward payouts, corrects invalid credits, and works with the knowledge-base editor to fix the cause. Payouts resume after a passing weekly sample. Agents can flag a wrong count privately to the owner, who checks the records and corrects the tally.
- **Slower service or pressure on CSAT.** Check team CSAT and median resolution time against the two-week baseline and teams awaiting rewards. A five-point CSAT drop with at least 30 responses, or a 10% increase in median resolution time for two consecutive weeks, pauses the affected team’s reward window while the owner checks ticket mix and workflow delays. Agents should resolve the customer’s issue first.
- **Frequent ticket types crowd out harder work.** Keep every resolved ticket in the denominator and include at least a few newly written articles in the weekly sample when available. Use the existing report to pull counts and the sample so one owner can run the tally, review, and team update in under an hour a week.
