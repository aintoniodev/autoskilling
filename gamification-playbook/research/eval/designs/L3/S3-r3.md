# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**1. Diagnosis.** Check access and workload before offering a reward. The Infection Prevention lead walks one routine case with a nurse from hand hygiene through kiosk confirmation, then walks a blocked case such as an urgent call or unavailable kiosk. Time the detour and test it with day- and night-shift nurses. If logging interrupts care or most opportunities lack a safe logging window, fix access first. Nursing Operations owns kiosk fixes and responds within two working days. Assume the hospital approves anonymous audit matching and funds the reward.

**2. Credit rule.** After performing hand hygiene at a required moment, the nurse logs it at the first safe break in care. One ward credit requires one compliant action independently observed in the monthly audit and one valid kiosk entry the auditor can confidently match to that event. Measure **verified logging coverage** as credited events divided by all observed compliant actions in the sampled windows. Separately, measure **observed hand-hygiene compliance** as compliant actions divided by all observed opportunities. Keep missed actions in that denominator. [WHO hand-hygiene observation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

| Test case | Result |
|---|---|
| Compliant action with a matching entry | One credit |
| Repeat entry for the same action | One credit total |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Recheck the match and remove credit if invalid |
| Delayed entry | Credit only when the auditor can still verify the match |
| Hand hygiene performed without the kiosk | Counts toward compliance and stays in the logging denominator |
| Required action omitted, with or without an entry | Miss in the compliance audit and zero credit |

**3. Worker copy.**

- **Invitation at the shift huddle:** “After cleaning your hands at a required moment, log it at your first safe break. One accurate entry helps us see where the kiosk fits into care. If care needs you or the kiosk is down, carry on with care. The reward period ends March 31.”
- **Kiosk confirmation:** “Entry saved.”
- **Private prompt:** “This shift, start with one safe chance to log. Already logged one? Keep logging when care allows. If no safe chance comes up, start on your next shift.”
- **Restart:** “Missed a log? Start with your next safe opportunity. Skip catch-up entries you can’t verify.”

**4. Visibility.** Show each ward its verified coverage, baseline and target on the existing huddle board. Pool sparse results across audit rounds before sharing. Nurses see their own confirmation at the kiosk. Managers receive ward totals. The Infection Prevention lead alone accesses temporary matching records for verification or corrections. Log that access and delete matching records after reconciliation. Collect no patient identifiers and prohibit disciplinary use.

**5. Reward.** Each ward that reaches **80% verified logging coverage and improves by 10 percentage points over baseline, or reaches 95% coverage**, earns a modest hospital-funded meal available across shifts, pending the compliance check. Test the reward with nurses before launch. Share kiosk fixes at huddles as they ship.

**6. Operator card.** The Infection Prevention lead reconciles kiosk timestamps and moment categories with temporary observer event IDs. Sample across day, night and weekend shifts using the existing monthly rounds. Target at least 30 observed opportunities per ward per month and validate match reliability before attaching stakes. Nurses report errors or delays privately to the lead. Budget 30 minutes weekly plus 60 minutes per monthly reconciliation, alongside existing rounds. Pause a ward’s reward if audited compliance drops **5 percentage points below baseline** or logging delays care. The lead investigates before releasing any reward.

**7. End and decision.** Run a supported kiosk baseline in January 2027, offer the reward February 1 through March 31, then measure April and May with feedback and fixes still in place. Use the same shift mix across phases. Extend to other wards only if at least two wards reach the milestone and retain at least half their coverage gain through May, with no unresolved safety pause. Revise and retest if coverage fades or matching is unreliable. Stop the incentive if care delays recur. Treat this baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

**8. Main risks.** Nurses may rush or defer care to reach the kiosk. Duplicate or invented entries may inflate apparent adoption. Visible observers may temporarily improve behaviour, so report the result as coverage and compliance **during sampled audits**.
