# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

### 1. Diagnosis

**Working assumption:** nurses will use the kiosk if it replaces a documentation step or makes it easier. Before launch, the Infection Prevention lead walks through one routine case and one urgent-care or kiosk-outage case with nurses on each ward. Confirm the kiosk saves time and is reachable without delaying care. The ward charge nurse owns workarounds that shift. The kiosk team triages faults within one working day. If most cases are blocked or logging adds work without a clear benefit, fix the workflow before offering a reward.

### 2. What earns credit

After performing hand hygiene at a required moment, the nurse logs it once at the next safe pause. In the monthly audit sample, one credit requires an observer to see the hygiene action and unambiguously match it to a valid kiosk entry. Adoption is **matched entries ÷ all observed completed hygiene actions in the pilot scope**. Clinical compliance is **observed hygiene actions ÷ all observed required opportunities**. Track both by ward. [WHO observation method](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

| Case | Credit |
|---|---|
| Observed action and valid entry | One |
| Duplicate or retry | One total |
| Entry withdrawn or action recorded in error | Zero after correction |
| Late entry or edited timestamp | One only if within the same shift and the original event can be verified |
| Hygiene done and documented via downtime procedure | Zero kiosk credit. Count the hygiene action and flag the outage |
| Hygiene done, entry omitted | Zero. Keep the action in the adoption denominator |
| Entry without observed hygiene | Zero |

### 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation at the kiosk:** “After cleaning your hands, log the moment once when care allows. This replaces the pilot paper tally. If the kiosk is down, use the usual downtime process. Rewards end 30 June.”
- **Confirmation:** “Entry saved.”
- **Private progress:** “2 of your 3 planned logs this week. Log your next eligible moment when care allows.” Set the practice goal from the nurse’s expected opportunities. One opportunity means a goal of one. Zero means no goal.
- **Restart:** “Start again with your next eligible moment.”

### 4. Visibility

Show personal progress only in the nurse’s private kiosk session. Post each ward’s verified progress against its own baseline in the existing huddle report. Managers see aggregates. Suppress identifiable small groups. Individual access is limited to requested corrections or disputes, logged and time-limited. Pilot data stays out of performance reviews and discipline.

### 5. Reward

Test a hospital-funded team meal with nurses from all shifts, then offer it to each ward that meets the adoption target and passes the safety check. Make it available across shifts. Post “you reported it, we fixed it” updates in the huddle.

### 6. Operator card

The Infection Prevention lead reconciles kiosk event and edit history with the existing monthly audit, using short, unannounced samples across shifts. Assume approved, reliable matching without patient identifiers and capacity within the existing audit. If matching or sample coverage fails, withhold the award decision. Nurses report corrections privately to the lead. Budget **45 minutes a week** beyond existing audits. Pause rewards if any ward’s observed compliance drops **5 percentage points** below baseline or a report links logging to delayed care. The lead investigates before restarting.

### 7. End and decision

**2027:** January–March baseline with kiosk support, April–June rewards, July–September follow-up with fixes and feedback retained. Each ward’s target is **10 percentage points above baseline, capped at 90%**. Wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain baseline. Expand only if all three wards meet their targets during rewards and follow-up and pass the safety check. If adoption fades, revise the workflow before retesting. Stop if care delays persist. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

### 8. Main risks

Logging delays care. Duplicate or invented entries inflate activity. Observation changes behaviour or misses busy shifts. Review these alongside the monthly results before releasing rewards.
