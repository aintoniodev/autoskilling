## 1. Diagnosis

**Test whether the kiosk is worth using before offering a reward.** On each ward, walk through a routine care transition and a blocked case, such as urgent care with a queue at the kiosk. Ask nurses on day and night shifts whether logging is quick enough and whether seeing where documentation falls short would help. Infection Prevention must approve kiosk placement and cleaning. The pilot lead triages access, queue and equipment issues by the next working day and posts fixes in the ward huddle. If most cases lack a safe, quick logging path, defer incentives. Nurses perform hand hygiene at the required moment and skip kiosk entry whenever care takes priority.

## 2. Credit rule

When a nurse completes hand hygiene at an indicated moment, they log it once at the next safe break in care. During the existing monthly audits, observers independently record opportunities and whether hand hygiene occurred. A ward earns **one verified credit** for each observed, completed action with one unambiguously matched kiosk entry. The reward metric is **verified credits ÷ observed completed actions**. The safety metric is **observed completed actions ÷ all observed opportunities**. One action can cover multiple simultaneous indications. [WHO technical reference manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Completed and correctly logged | One credit |
| Duplicate tap or multiple indications for one action | One credit total |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Recalculate from the correction history |
| Late entry or edited time | Credit only if the original audit trail still supports a unique match |
| Hand hygiene done, kiosk skipped | Counts toward compliance and remains in the logging denominator |
| Missed hand hygiene or entry without a verified action | Zero credit. An observed miss stays in the compliance denominator. |

## 3. What nurses see

- **Invitation at shift handover near the kiosk.** “After your next hand-hygiene moment, log it once when care allows. Your ward’s audit summary will show where the kiosk is easy to use and where we need to fix it. The reward period ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation on the kiosk.** “Entry saved.”
- **Private progress.** “1 of 1 for today’s practice goal. Keep logging when care allows.” The practice goal starts at the first safe opportunity. Shifts without one have no target.
- **Restart.** “Missed a shift? Start with your next safe opportunity.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own progress privately. Ward huddles show each ward’s audited logging rate against its own baseline. Managers receive the same aggregates. Suppress results that could identify a nurse. Only the reconciliation lead may access temporary individual matches for verification or a requested correction. Log access, delete the match file 30 days after results are finalized and prohibit disciplinary use. Collect no patient identifiers.

## 5. Reward

Each ward can earn a hospital-funded team meal available across shifts if it improves verified logging by **10 percentage points**, capped at a **95% target**, and passes the safety check. A ward starting at 95% or higher must maintain its baseline. Test the reward with day and night nurses before launch. Pay out after April’s audit review.

## 6. Operator card

The Infection Prevention lead owns the pilot and budgets **45 minutes weekly on average**, using existing monthly observer rounds and kiosk exports. Confirm during baseline that timestamps, a temporary nurse code and edit history allow one-to-one matching without extra bedside steps. If linkage is unreliable, defer rewards. Nurses can request a private correction from the lead. Pause a ward’s rewards immediately after a confirmed kiosk-related delay to care or hand hygiene, or if observed compliance drops **5 percentage points or more** from baseline. Resume only after the cause is addressed and a repeat audit clears the safety threshold.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January–February 2027** for baseline with kiosk support, **March–April** for rewards and **May–June** for follow-up with the same support and private feedback. Use comparable shift coverage and at least 30 observed completed actions per ward per phase. Expand to another ward only if all three meet their logging target during rewards and sustain it afterward with compliance within five points of baseline and no unresolved safety issues. Revise and retest if gains fade or samples are too small. Stop incentives if safety problems persist. This comparison tests feasibility and persistence. Causal attribution would require a stronger comparison.

## 8. Likely failure modes

- Kiosk queues or poorly placed terminals interrupt care.
- Duplicate, backfilled or audit-only logging inflates apparent adoption.
- Staff perceive individual matching as surveillance and stop reporting problems.
