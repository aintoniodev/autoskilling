## 1. Diagnosis

The team’s median review response is 2.6 days. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the review lead walks one routine PR and one delayed PR with the engineers involved. Check whether the reviewer got the request, had access and enough context, and had time set aside. If missing access or context blocks the review, flag it in the PR and ask the lead to unblock or reassign it that working day. If workload or blockers explain most delays, fix those first. Run the pilot if ready PRs still sit unanswered.

## 2. Credit rule

When a human-authored PR is marked ready, the author requests a reviewer through the existing workflow. The first non-author reviewer to submit a substantive review within eight team working hours earns one credit for the PR. A substantive review records approval with what was checked, or identifies a specific issue or question the author can act on.

The clock starts when the PR is ready in a pilot repository and uses the published team calendar. Reassignment keeps the original deadline. The team metric is the percentage of eligible ready PRs with a qualifying response by that deadline. Include unanswered and unassigned PRs in the denominator once due.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Substantive review within eight working hours | One credit |
| Second review or duplicate event | Original credit only |
| Review dismissed because it was invalid | Remove credit and recalculate from the next valid review, if any |
| Review arrives late or someone edits the due date | No credit. Use the original event timestamp and calendar |
| Answer given in chat or in person | Credit only if the substantive review is recorded on the PR by the deadline |
| Ready PR missing from the report | Restore it from repository history. If unanswered when due, count it as a miss |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation** — “You’ve been asked to review #482. Please approve it with a note on what you checked, or leave feedback the author can act on, by Tuesday at 14:00. A clear answer gets the PR moving and saves you a follow-up ping. If you’re blocked, flag it on the PR. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation** — “Your review of #482 counted. You answered in 5 working hours.”
- **Progress** — “2 of 3 toward your goal this week. Review #491 is due tomorrow.” Set the private goal at 75% of assigned PRs due that week, rounded up. With one or two, just show the next review. With zero, show “No reviews due.”
- **Restart** — “New week. Your next review is #491, due tomorrow.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress privately. The existing team channel shows the weekly verified rate against baseline, without names or breakdowns, and suppresses it when fewer than five reviewers contributed. Managers see the same aggregate. Individual access for corrections, disputes, or requested coaching is logged, expires when resolved, and is excluded from performance and disciplinary use.

## 5. Motivator

Test the idea with a few engineers before launch. Offer the whole team one paid hour of protected focus time, with flexible scheduling, if it reaches **75% on time and improves by at least 15 percentage points over baseline**, subject to the quality check. Use the weekly update to show which review blockers the lead fixed.

## 6. Operator card

The review lead owns the pilot. Assume repository history supports ready, request, review, and dismissal events, and existing notifications can deliver private feedback. Verify both and secure approval for the reward before launch. Weekly, the lead reconciles the report against ready PRs and samples five reviews they did not write for actionable feedback or evidence of what was checked. Allow 50 minutes weekly. Engineers DM the lead with the PR link for corrections. Two deficient reviews in five, or one missed issue with serious consequences, pauses reward eligibility pending investigation and correction.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline **Jan 11–Feb 5, 2027**. Pilot **Feb 8–Mar 5**. Follow-up **Mar 8–Apr 2**, with workflow fixes and private feedback continuing. Use the same reporting rule and quality sample throughout. Award the milestone once after the final pilot check. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence. Scale if both periods hit the milestone and pass quality checks. Change the design if quality holds but speed misses or fades. Stop if quality failures recur after correction.

## 8. Risks

Shallow approvals earn credit. Hard PRs get left unassigned. Team pressure turns private progress into an informal ranking.
