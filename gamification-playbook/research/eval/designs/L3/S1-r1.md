## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently get a digital checklist. The reason is unknown. Before launch, the safety lead and union steward walk one routine job and one job with poor signal or device trouble. Time both routes and ask technicians what makes them skip the app. Dispatch protects paid time for the check. The mobile-app owner responds to blockers within one business day and posts fixes in the crew briefing. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds rework, fix the workflow before offering rewards. Agree on the pilot and data rules with union representatives.

## 2. Credit rule

A technician earns one credit per dispatched visit by completing all applicable items in the app before the recorded work start, or by completing the check and documenting a safety stop when work cannot begin. Both outcomes count equally. Measure digital adoption as credited visits divided by all dispatched visits requiring a check, including outages and omissions. Exclude cancellations before assessment. Track approved fallback checks separately to verify overall safety-check coverage.

| Test case | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete check before work, or document a hazard and stop | One credit |
| Duplicate, retry, or second submission | One credit per visit |
| Check voided or corrected | Remove pending credit if invalid. Preserve the original record and audit the correction. |
| Late entry or edited schedule | Use original completion and actual start or stop records. Offline sync can count with a trustworthy completion time. |
| Approved fallback during an outage | Counts toward safety coverage. No digital credit. |
| Missing check | Stays in the denominator. No credit. |

**Assumptions** Dispatch has stable visit IDs, independently recorded start or stop times, and auditable history. If those records cannot verify timing or omissions, defer rewards.

## 3. What technicians see

- **At arrival in the dispatch app** “Open this job’s safety check before starting. Your answers save to the job record so you can skip re-entry. If the app is down, use the approved fallback. If it’s unsafe, stop and report it. Pilot rewards end February 28.”
- **Confirmation** “Pre-job check saved. One visit recorded, pending verification.”
- **Private progress** “2 of your next 3 checks complete. Open the checklist at your next arrival.” Set the goal using each technician’s planned volume, including one check for a one-job week. With zero jobs, show “No eligible visits this week.”
- **After a miss** “Start fresh on your next visit. Your completed checks still count.”

## 4. Visibility

Post each crew’s weekly verified rate against its own baseline in the existing crew briefing. Suppress groups with fewer than 10 technicians or where someone could be identified. Managers see aggregates. A designated reviewer may access individual records for corrections or disputes using logged, time-limited access. Pilot data stays out of discipline and performance ratings. [Electronic monitoring meta-analysis, grade A](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/peps.12514)

## 5. Reward

Test a **20-minute paid break, scheduled by each technician**, with a few technicians and the union. Everyone in a pilot crew earns it if verified adoption reaches 60% over four weeks and passes the quality gate. Ordinary pay and reporting rights remain protected. Hazard, near-miss, and injury reports never reduce the reward. [OSHA worker participation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/safeandsound/safety-and-health-programs/worker-participation)

## 6. Operator card

Allow the safety lead about two hours weekly. Join dispatch and app records and independently review 10 randomly selected visits across pilot crews against work records and technician follow-up. Pause rewards for any critical safety miss, suspected reporting pressure, or two failed checks in a weekly sample. The safety lead and steward review and resolve issues before resuming. Technicians can dispute a record privately through either person.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline January 4–31, 2027. Run February 1–28. Track March 1–28 without rewards, keeping fixes and private feedback. If enough comparable crews exist, randomly assign crews to the reward or to the same support without the reward. Otherwise, treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

Scale if pilot adoption reaches 60%, follow-up reaches 50% and stays at least 10 percentage points above the comparison or baseline, overall safety-check coverage holds, and no quality or reporting issue remains open. Revise if adoption fades or access remains uneven. Stop incentives if quality deteriorates or reporting is pressured.

## 8. Main risks

Rushed or backfilled checks distort completion. Poor connectivity puts some crews at a disadvantage. Crew rewards can create pressure to clear unsafe jobs or stay quiet about hazards.
