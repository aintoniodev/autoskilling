## 1. Diagnosis

Today, agents link or write an article on 12% of resolved tickets. Check why before offering a reward. Walk one ticket with a reusable answer from resolution through article linking, and one that requires a new article from drafting through approval. Time both paths and ask six agents where they get stuck. The support operations lead owns access and workflow fixes within two business days. Assume agents can link from the ticket and a knowledge reviewer can approve articles within two business days. Verify both before launch. If most cases are blocked or the workflow adds substantial rework, fix it first.

## 2. Credit rule

At ticket resolution, the resolving agent earns **one credit per ticket** for linking an approved article that supports the actual answer, or for linking a reusable article they wrote that is approved within seven days. An internal link counts when the article is for staff only. For sensitive or one-off cases with no reusable guidance, resolve normally and record the reason. The adoption rate is credited tickets divided by **all** resolved tickets. Report exclusions separately so they stay visible.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Relevant approved article linked at resolution | One credit |
| Duplicate links or merge | At most one credit for the canonical ticket |
| Reopened ticket or recalled article within seven days | Remove credit. Recheck at final resolution and count the ticket once. |
| Late link or edited resolution date | Use the recorded resolution event. Only pending article approval gets the seven-day window. |
| Resolved the old way or link omitted | Zero credit. Ticket stays in the denominator. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **At resolution:** “Link the article that supports your answer so the next agent can find it. If the answer is reusable and there’s no article, draft one from the ticket. Resolve the ticket as usual. The team reward runs through February 28.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked to ticket #4821. One credit pending the seven-day check.” For a draft, show “Awaiting approval.”
- **Progress:** “3 of 5 verified this week. On your next eligible ticket, check for an article before closing.” Set goals at roughly one in three expected eligible tickets, based on each agent’s recent volume. With fewer than three expected, show the next eligible case as the goal. With zero actual opportunities, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart:** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Your previous credits still count.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own progress. The existing support channel shows the whole team’s verified rate against baseline each week. Managers see aggregates. The support operations lead logs individual access for corrections, disputes, or agent-requested coaching and closes it when resolved. Suppress breakdowns that could identify someone. Progress stays out of performance reviews and CSAT pay decisions.

## 5. Reward

If the team reaches **30% verified adoption** and clears the quality checks, everyone gets 30 minutes of paid time back, scheduled with queue coverage. Test the reward with the six agents before launch. Post blocker fixes alongside weekly progress. The existing CSAT bonus continues under its current terms.

## 6. Operator card

The support operations lead spends **45 minutes weekly** reconciling the ticket-resolution export with article links and approval history, reviewing corrections through the existing help channel, and spot-checking with a knowledge reviewer. The reviewer samples six credited and two uncredited tickets weekly. Two unsuitable credits or one sensitive-data exposure pauses reward verification. The lead corrects affected credits and resumes after the cause is fixed and a fresh sample passes. Confirm the exports capture omissions and reversals before attaching the reward.

## 7. End and decision

Apply the same rule to the **January 4–31, 2027** baseline. Run **February 1–28** and follow up **March 1–28** with workflow fixes and private feedback retained. Settle February rewards after the final seven-day check. Continue the workflow without prizes if adoption reached 30% during the programme, stays at or above 25% in follow-up, and neither window shows a CSAT drop greater than three percentage points against baseline. Review response counts before interpreting CSAT. Change the workflow and retest if adoption fades. Stop incentives if quality or CSAT deteriorates. The baseline comparison estimates feasibility.

## 8. Risks

- **Irrelevant links or thin articles** inflate adoption. The reviewer checks whether the content supports the answer.
- **Slower resolutions threaten CSAT and bonus earnings.** Agents can resolve promptly while article approval is pending.
- **Adoption falls after paid time is awarded.** The four-week follow-up determines whether the workflow is worth keeping.
