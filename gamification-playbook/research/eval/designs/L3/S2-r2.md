## 1. Diagnosis

The team’s median review response is 2.6 days. Check whether the delay comes from unclear ownership, overloaded reviewers, or cases that genuinely need specialist input. Before launch, walk one routine PR and one blocked PR from request to response with the engineers involved. The engineering lead owns routing and access fixes and assigns a backup within one working day. Authors keep their usual escalation route for urgent work. If most requests lack a reviewer with enough capacity, fix coverage before launching.

## 2. Credit rule

When an author marks a PR ready and requests review from the team, the duty reviewer owns the first response. A submitted approval or a review with concrete changes or questions earns one credit per PR if posted within one working day on the team’s published working calendar. Acknowledgments and draft comments earn zero. Reassignment keeps the original clock. The team metric is credited PRs divided by all eligible PRs whose deadlines fell in the measurement window.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Timely approval or actionable review | One credit |
| Second reviewer, repeated request, or reopened PR | One credit maximum, original clock |
| Review withdrawn as invalid | Remove credit unless a qualifying replacement met the original deadline |
| Late review or edited due date | Zero, using the original request timestamp |
| Review through the usual workflow | Counts if the qualifying response is recorded on the PR in time |
| Unassigned, unanswered, or closed after request | Stays in the denominator at zero unless already credited |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation in the existing review notification** “PR #482 is ready for your review. By Tuesday at 2 p.m., post an approval or explain what needs to change so the author can keep moving. If you’re tied up, hand it to the backup today. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation** “Review submitted on PR #482 before the deadline. It counts toward this week’s goal.”
- **Private progress** “2 of 3 reviews due this week answered on time. Next up is PR #491.” The weekly goal is to answer at least 80% of assigned requests on time when four or more are due. With one to three, show the count and next deadline. With zero, show “No reviews due.”
- **Restart** “Your next review is PR #497, due Wednesday at noon. If you need coverage, hand it to the backup today.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel shows the weekly verified rate against baseline without names and suppresses weeks with fewer than five distinct reviewers. Managers see the same aggregate. The lead may access individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log that access, remove it when the case closes, and prohibit disciplinary use.

## 5. Reward

If the team reaches **80% on time across the pilot** and passes the quality check, all 25 engineers get one protected paid hour to use as they choose. Confirm the reward with a few skeptical engineers and get sponsor approval before launch. The lead reserves coverage and shares which routing or access issues the team reported and fixed.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead spends **about 50 minutes a week** using the PR event report and review queue. Before launch, verify original timestamps, withdrawals, and omitted requests. A lead who did not submit the sampled reviews checks five randomly selected credited PRs each week for rubber-stamp approvals or missed material issues. At **two material failures in five**, the lead pauses reward verification and fixes the cause before resuming. Engineers can privately send a PR link to the lead for correction within two working days. Reliable event history and paid time approval are launch assumptions.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed 2027 dates are **January 11–February 5** for baseline, **February 8–March 5** for the pilot, and **March 8–April 9** for follow-up. Verify and award the paid hour once after the pilot. Keep routing fixes and private feedback through follow-up. Use the same metric and quality sample throughout.

Keep the workflow if both pilot and follow-up reach 80% and exceed baseline by at least 15 percentage points, with no unresolved quality pause. If the pilot meets the target and follow-up falls short, fix the remaining workflow friction without renewing the prize. If the pilot misses the target or quality problems persist, retire the incentive. The baseline comparison tests feasibility.

## 8. Risks

- Reviewers rush approvals to beat the clock, letting material issues through.
- Easy PRs get attention first while complex work waits or authors delay requesting review.
- Engineers read private tracking as performance scoring and lose trust.
