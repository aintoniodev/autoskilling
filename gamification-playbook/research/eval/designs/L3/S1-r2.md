## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs have a digital checklist. The reason is unknown. Before launch, the field safety lead and a union steward walk two real jobs with technicians, one where the app works and one where it is unavailable or impractical. Compare the digital and approved fallback workflows for time, duplicate entry and handling a failed safety check. Show technicians any fixes before launch. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds work without a clear benefit to technicians, defer the pilot. The field safety lead owns blockers, acknowledges them by the next working day and publishes the fix or workaround.

## 2. What earns credit

When a work order reaches the existing pre-job safety step, the assigned technician earns one credit by completing the required digital checklist before starting work, or by completing it and following the approved stop-work procedure when a hazard prevents the start. A failed safety item can earn credit when handled correctly. Use the approved fallback if the app fails. If neither route is available, hold the job and call dispatch.

The team score is credited work orders divided by all dispatched work orders that reached the safety step, including jobs held for hazards, jobs completed on paper and jobs with missing checklists. Use dispatch records to establish the denominator independently of the app. Verify completion against the actual start or documented safety hold. For offline submissions, use a trustworthy device completion timestamp. Confirm reliable records with dispatch before attaching rewards.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Checklist completed before the actual start | One credit |
| Duplicate submission or reassignment | One maximum per work order |
| Submission recalled or invalidated | Remove pending credit and review the correction |
| Scheduled time edited or checklist completed after work began | Use actual start history. Late completion earns zero |
| App unavailable and approved paper checklist used | Zero digital credit. Record the blocker |
| Checklist omitted or job never reached the safety step | An omitted checklist counts as zero. A job canceled before the safety step is excluded |

## 3. What technicians see

Place the invitation in the existing dispatch workflow at the pre-job cue.

- **Invitation:** “Open the safety checklist before you start. Your answers stay with the work order, so they’re there if dispatch needs them. If the app fails, use the approved fallback. The team reward runs through March 21.”
- **Confirmation:** “Checklist saved for job 4821. Credit pending verification.”
- **Progress:** “3 of your 4 checklists verified this week. Complete the checklist before your next job.” Set the personal goal from actual opportunities, including weeks with one job. With zero jobs, show “No eligible jobs this week.”
- **Restart:** “Start with your next job. Earlier credits still count toward the team milestone.”

## 4. Visibility and reward

Technicians see their own progress. Post each participating crew’s verified rate against its own baseline in the existing crew channel. Suppress groups or breakdowns that could identify individuals. Managers see aggregates. Give the named corrections reviewer logged, time-limited individual access for disputes or corrections. Agree with union representatives before launch that pilot progress data stays out of performance and disciplinary files.

Offer one company-funded meal during paid work time, available across shifts, if the crew improves by 20 percentage points or reaches 90%. Include crew members with few or zero jobs. Test the reward with technicians and agree on release terms with union representatives before launch. Release it after quality review.

## 5. Operator card

The field safety lead reconciles existing dispatch and app exports and samples 10 credited work orders weekly across shifts, reviewing actual start timing and whether flagged hazards were handled correctly. Budget 90 minutes a week. If two sampled credits are invalid, or one job started despite an unresolved stop-work item, the lead pauses reward accrual, alerts the union liaison and follows the existing safety process. Technicians can request corrections privately through the lead or their steward.

## 6. End and decision

**Proposed 2027 dates:** baseline January 11–February 7, pilot February 8–March 21, and reward-free follow-up March 22–May 2. Keep workflow fixes and private feedback. If comparable crews are available, randomly assign one crew per matched pair to receive the reward and shared progress. Both crews receive the same training, fixes and private feedback.

Scale if adoption rises at least 15 percentage points above baseline during the pilot, retains at least 10 points in follow-up, and clears the quality checks. With comparison crews, require those gains above their change as well. With baseline alone, treat the outcome as feasibility and extend in stages. Revise if documented workflow blockers explain a miss. Stop the incentive if gains fade below threshold or safety failures recur.

## 7. Main risks

- **Rushed checks or backfilling.** Verify timing and hazard handling before releasing rewards.
- **Pressure on crews with poor connectivity.** Publish blocker fixes and protect use of the approved fallback.
- **Surveillance concerns.** Agree on access and use with the union and keep individual progress private.
