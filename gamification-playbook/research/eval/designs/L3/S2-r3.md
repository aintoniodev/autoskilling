## 1. Diagnosis

The reported median review turnaround is 2.6 days. Before launch, the engineering lead walks through one ready PR and one stalled PR with their reviewers. Check routing, access, CI, workload and the time it takes to resume interrupted work. If most requests lack a reachable reviewer or a reviewable change, fix that before launching. The lead owns routing and access fixes by the next working day. The programme assumes an existing PR queue, usable review history and approval for paid team time.

## 2. Credit rule

When a ready PR enters the existing review queue, the assigned engineer or rota backup earns one credit for submitting a substantive review within eight staffed hours. An approval cites what was checked. A change request identifies a concrete issue and what needs to change. Authors use the backup route when the assignee is unavailable.

The clock starts when the ready PR is first requested during staffed hours, or at the next opening if requested after hours. Use the published team calendar. The team metric is the percentage of eligible PRs whose first qualifying review met that deadline. Count PRs in the period their deadline falls in.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Ready PR gets a substantive review on time | One credit and one on-time PR |
| Reassignment, repeat request or second reviewer | Original clock and one credit maximum |
| Review withdrawn or found to be superficial | Use the next valid review’s timestamp, if any |
| Late review or edited due date | Keep the original deadline and record a miss |
| Review discussed in chat or live | Credit once the substantive decision is recorded on the PR, using that timestamp |
| Ready PR omitted from the queue report | Restore it from request history and score against the original deadline |
| PR withdrawn before deadline or genuinely unreviewable | Log the reason, verify it independently and report exclusions weekly |

If timestamps or omissions cannot be checked reliably, run the pilot without a reward.

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation:** “PR #214 is ready for your review by 15:00 tomorrow. A clear decision gives the author their next step. If you’re tied up, hand it to the rota backup today. The team reward window ends 5 March.”
- **Confirmation:** “Your review of #214 counted. You gave the author a decision before the deadline.”
- **Progress:** “You reviewed 2 of your 3 assigned PRs on time this week. Next up is #219, due tomorrow at 11:00.” The personal goal is to cover the requests you actually receive. With zero requests, show “No reviews assigned this week.”
- **Restart:** “Your next review is #219, due tomorrow at 11:00. If you’re at capacity, hand it to the backup now.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own counts privately. Post the team’s weekly rate, denominator and baseline in the existing engineering channel, with routing fixes made that week. Combine weeks when fewer than five reviewers contributed. Managers see aggregates. The programme owner may inspect individual records for a correction, dispute or requested coaching. Log access, close it when resolved and exclude programme records from performance management.

## 5. Reward

If the team reaches **80% on time** across the pilot and passes the quality check, give everyone 30 minutes of paid time back by cancelling or shortening an existing team meeting. Test that reward and the 80% milestone with a few engineers before launch. Use the existing queue and channel.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead runs the existing PR activity report and reconciles it against ready review requests. Budget **45 minutes weekly**, including a rotating engineer’s independent check of five credited reviews for specific, credible review evidence. If two of five fail, the lead pauses reward qualification, corrects the counts and resumes after a clean sample. Engineers can send the lead a PR link privately for correction.

## 7. End and decision

Use **11 January–5 February 2027** as the baseline, **8 February–5 March** as the reward period and **8 March–2 April** as the reward-free follow-up. Keep routing fixes and private feedback. Keep the workflow if follow-up is at least **80% on time and 10 percentage points above baseline**, with no unresolved quality pause. Change it if turnaround improves but misses either threshold. End the programme if turnaround fails to improve or quality failures recur. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rubber-stamping** earns credit unless the sample catches it.
- **Queue avoidance** hides delays if engineers stop requesting reviews or mark reviewable PRs as blocked.
- **Interruptions and a temporary lift** can make review faster during the pilot while slowing the rest of engineering work. Check the rota burden weekly and use follow-up to test whether the gains hold.
