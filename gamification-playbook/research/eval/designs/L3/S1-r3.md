### 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently get a digital checklist. Before launch, have a technician and union steward walk two real jobs with the field safety lead. On a supported job, time the app and the current checklist, and check whether the app saves re-entry. On a job with no signal or a failed login, verify the approved paper fallback and how dispatch records it. The safety lead owns blockers and gives dispatch a fix or workaround within one business day. Launch only if most jobs support the app and technicians can name a practical benefit. Budget paid time for setup and fixes.

### 2. Credit rule

When a job reaches its pre-start safety check, the assigned lead technician earns one credit for completing the job-specific digital checklist before work begins or before recording a safety hold. Required checks and any hazards or holds must be recorded. Dispatch timestamps establish the sequence. Follow the existing stop-work procedure for unresolved hazards.

Team adoption is verified credits divided by app-supported jobs that reached the safety check, including omissions and voluntary paper use. Report verified outages and fallback use separately, alongside checklist completion by either method across all jobs.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete before start, or record a safety hold | One credit |
| Duplicate submission | One credit for the job |
| Checklist voided or recalled as invalid | Remove pending credit before reward sign-off |
| Late submission or edited schedule | Use original start or hold history. Accept late sync with trustworthy pre-start evidence |
| Paper checklist | Safe fallback, zero digital credit. Exclude from the adoption denominator only for a verified access failure or outage |
| Checklist omitted | Zero credit. Keep the supported job in the denominator |

### 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation.** “Before you start, open Safety Check from this job. The app saves the checklist to the job record. If it’s unavailable, use the approved paper checklist and tell dispatch. The pilot ends March 14.”
- **Confirmation.** “Safety checklist saved for job 4821. Your safety hold is recorded.” Show the hold sentence when applicable. Offline, show “Saved on this device. Credit pending sync.”
- **Progress.** “2 of 3 this week. Open Safety Check on your next supported job.” Set the goal to roughly half each technician’s expected supported jobs, rounded up. With zero opportunities, show “No supported jobs this week.”
- **Restart.** “Your next supported job is a fresh start. Open Safety Check before work begins.”

### 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing dispatch briefing shows each pilot team’s verified rate against its own baseline, without names. Suppress breakdowns that could identify a technician. Managers see aggregates. A designated reviewer can access individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log and time-limit access and prohibit using pilot progress for discipline. Agree on these terms with union representatives before launch.

### 5. Reward

Offer everyone on a pilot team **30 minutes of paid release time** if the team reaches 55% verified adoption over six weeks and clears the quality review. Include staff who had no supported jobs. Confirm the reward with technicians and the union before launch, and use the dispatch briefing to show which reported blockers were fixed.

### 6. Operator card

The field safety lead reconciles app and dispatch records weekly, samples 20 jobs across digital and paper checklists, and checks job-specific hazards and timing against work records. Pause rewards for any missed critical hazard or two materially inaccurate checklists in a sample. Keep safety checks running. Technicians can request corrections through dispatch or their steward. Budget three staff hours weekly and verify timestamp history before attaching rewards.

### 7. End and decision

Proposed dates are January 4–31, 2027 for baseline, February 1–March 14 for rewards, and March 15–April 25 for follow-up. Keep app support and private feedback running. If enough comparable depots participate, randomly assign some to receive the same support without rewards or public progress. Otherwise use the baseline to assess feasibility. Scale if follow-up adoption stays at least 15 percentage points above baseline, exceeds the comparison group’s improvement where available, and all-job checklist completion holds or improves. Revise if adoption fades. Stop the incentive if safety quality worsens or the records cannot support reliable credit.

### 8. Risks

Technicians could rush through hazards to earn credit. Poor connectivity could leave some routes with fewer chances. Team reporting could feel like individual surveillance, especially on small crews.
