## 1. Diagnosis

The 25-engineer team’s median review response is 2.6 days. That establishes the delay. The cause is still unknown. Before launch, the engineering lead walks through one routine PR and one stalled PR with their authors and reviewers. Check whether the request reached an available reviewer, the PR was ready, and the reviewer had time and context to act.

If missed requests are common, put a review link in the channel engineers already use. If capacity or access is the main blocker, fix it before launching. The engineering lead owns blockers and routes uncovered requests to a backup within two staffed hours. Reviewers who are unavailable can hand off in the same channel.

## 2. Credit rule

**Worker rule.** Give a substantive review on a teammate’s ready PR within one working day, and the team earns a paid hour off when 80% of eligible PRs get one.

Count one credit per human-authored PR in the team’s repositories. Start the clock at the first review request while the PR is ready, using eight staffed hours on the agreed team calendar. A qualifying response is a submitted review that records what was checked and either approves the change, requests specific changes, or raises a concrete technical question the author must resolve. The team metric is credited PRs divided by all eligible PRs whose deadlines fall in the measurement window.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal | A reviewer submits a substantive review by the deadline. One credit. |
| Duplicate | Multiple reviewers, repeated submissions, or a reopened PR still earn at most one credit. |
| Reversal | A withdrawn or invalidated review loses credit unless another qualifying review met the original deadline. |
| Late or edited | Late reviews earn no credit. Reassignment, a draft toggle, or an edited due date leaves the original deadline in place. |
| Existing workflow | A qualifying review submitted directly in GitHub counts. A chat acknowledgment earns no credit. |
| Omitted | A ready PR with no review request enters the denominator at its ready timestamp and appears in the routing queue. A genuine cancellation before the deadline is excluded with a recorded reason. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation, on the review request.** “PR #482 is ready for review. Please submit your review by 3 p.m. tomorrow so the author can move it forward. If you’re unavailable, hand it to the backup in #reviews. The pilot ends February 26.”
- **Confirmation, after submission.** “Your review on #482 counted toward this week’s team goal.”
- **Private progress.** “You’ve answered 3 of your 4 review requests on time this week. #491 is next, due tomorrow at 11 a.m.” The personal goal uses actual assigned opportunities. With one request, show just that request and deadline. With zero, show “No reviews assigned this week.”
- **Restart.** “Your next review is #491, due tomorrow at 11 a.m. Hand it off in #reviews if you need cover.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress privately. The existing team channel shows the full team’s weekly verified rate against its January baseline and the 80% milestone, with no names or breakdowns. Suppress updates based on fewer than 10 eligible PRs and pool them into the next update. Managers see the same aggregate. The engineering lead may inspect individual records for requested coaching or corrections, with access logged and closed when the case is resolved. Pilot data stays out of performance and disciplinary decisions.

## 5. Motivator

If the team clears 80% across the pilot and passes the quality gate, everyone gets one paid hour off, staggered around coverage. Test the reward and wording with three engineers before launch, including a skeptic. The team channel also reports specific friction fixes the engineering lead has funded or shipped.

## 6. Operator card

Use GitHub ready, request, review, dismissal, and close events. Verify historical coverage before attaching the reward. Each week, a rotating senior engineer who did not author or review the sampled PRs audits eight randomly selected credited reviews, or all credits if fewer. Check whether the review demonstrates a real check and whether its feedback is technically sound. Two failures in a weekly sample or one serious defect clearly missed by a credited review triggers a pause. The engineering lead pauses reward progress, investigates, and resumes after correcting the cause and rechecking affected credits. Engineers dispute a count by sending the lead the PR link privately. Budget about one hour per week. **Assumed** GitHub event access, backup coverage, and approval for paid time.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline January 4–29, 2027. Pilot February 1–26. End reward accrual February 26, verify the final sample, and grant earned time. Keep routing fixes and private feedback through the March 1–26 reward-free follow-up.

Use the same eligibility and calendar throughout. Extend to another team as a controlled trial if both pilot and follow-up reach 80%, follow-up is at least 15 percentage points above baseline, and no quality pause remains unresolved. If the gain fades, revise routing or coverage before retrying. Stop incentives if the pilot rate fails to improve or quality problems recur. The baseline comparison supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Risks

- Rubber-stamp approvals earn speed credit while defects slip through.
- Authors avoid the clock by leaving ready work in draft or moving reviews to chat.
- Review load concentrates on responsive engineers, then turnaround slips when the reward ends.
