## 1. Diagnosis

**Test whether kiosk logging is worth the time.** The hospital already audits hand hygiene monthly. Before launch, Infection Prevention must name a decision the kiosk data will inform and show nurses how it will be used. The reason nurses skip logging is unconfirmed.

The nurse educator walks a routine case from hand hygiene at the point of care to a kiosk entry at the next safe pause, then walks an urgent case with an unavailable kiosk. In the urgent case, continue care and follow normal hand-hygiene practice. The nurse educator owns workflow fixes and routes kiosk faults to IT for next-business-day triage. Give the pilot a small budget for fixes. Hold launch if most cases are blocked, logging interrupts care, or the kiosk data has no clear use.

## 2. Credit rule

A nurse performs hand hygiene at an indicated moment, then records it at the next safe pause. **One correctly matched entry per independently observed action** counts toward the ward reward. The reward metric is verified entries divided by observed completed actions with a safe logging opportunity. Report the share excluded for access or care constraints. Measure clinical compliance separately as observed actions divided by **all** observed hand-hygiene opportunities. [WHO hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

| Case | Reward credit |
|---|---|
| Completed action, correct entry at next safe pause | One when independently matched |
| Duplicate or several indications for one action | One total |
| Entry voided or corrected | Recheck the match before payout |
| Late entry or edited time | Count only if the original timestamp and audit trail establish the match within the same shift |
| Hygiene done, kiosk skipped | Zero credit. Counts as compliant in the clinical audit |
| Action omitted or entry unsupported | Zero credit. An observed omission counts against clinical compliance |
| Kiosk down or care takes priority | Exclude from the logging denominator and retain in the clinical audit |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at the staff handoff board** “Clean your hands as usual. At your next safe pause, log the moment at the kiosk so we can see where logging fits into care. The ward earns a catered break if at least 80% of sampled, safely loggable actions are recorded and the safety check passes. Rewards end March 31.”
- **Confirmation** “Saved. You can return to care.”
- **Private progress** “1 of 2 moments you chose to log this shift saved. Log the next one when care allows.” Offer one or two as a voluntary goal. With no safe opportunity, show no goal.
- **Restart** “Start with your next safe opportunity.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own receipts and optional goal. The staff board shows each ward its verified monthly logging rate against its own baseline, plus fixes made from staff reports. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress breakdowns that could identify someone. A designated data steward can open an individual record for a correction or dispute using logged, time-limited access. Prohibit disciplinary reuse and patient identifiers.

## 5. Motivator

Fund one catered break per qualifying ward, with equivalent access across shifts and protected coverage. Check the reward and the 80% milestone with day, night and weekend nurses before launch. Pay once after the final safety review. Post concrete fixes on the handoff board as they ship.

## 6. Operator card

- **Owner and effort** Infection Prevention leads verification and can pause the programme. Budget two staff-hours per ward per week beyond routine audit time for matching, corrections and feedback. Confirm capacity before launch.
- **Data and quality check** Match timestamped kiosk entries to trained observers’ records using a temporary nurse code, indication and time window. Sample across shifts using the same method in every phase. Observers record actions and omissions without prompting entries. Confirm that the kiosk supports deduplication and traceable edits before attaching a reward.
- **Pause and correction** Pause rewards and investigate if observed compliance drops at least 5 percentage points from baseline in any ward, more than 10% of reviewed entries in observable windows lack a defensible match, or staff report any care delay linked to the kiosk. Nurses can request a correction privately through the data steward. Resume after Infection Prevention verifies the fix.

## 7. End and decision plan

**Proposed dates, 2027.** January is a no-reward baseline with the same kiosk support. Rewards run February 1–March 31. Keep workflow fixes and private feedback through April–May without rewards. Aim for at least 60 observed opportunities per ward per month. Extend measurement if coverage is thin.

Expand only if each ward sustains verified logging at 80% or above and at least 10 percentage points above January across follow-up, unless it already started at 80% or above, in which case require maintenance of baseline. Also require no unresolved safety trigger. Change the workflow and retest if logging fades or exclusions cluster by shift. Stop the incentive if care delays recur or verification remains unreliable. Treat the baseline comparison as a feasibility result.

## 8. Main risks

- **Care gets interrupted.** Log only at a safe pause. A reported delay triggers review.
- **The reward drives false entries or audit-only behaviour.** Reward independently matched work and sample across shifts. Observer presence can still inflate measured compliance. [Multicentre observation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111925/)
- **Staff experience monitoring as surveillance.** Keep progress private, restrict identifiable access and suppress small groups.
