## 1. Diagnosis

Run an eight-week team incentive for **accurate kiosk logging after hand hygiene**. The hospital already audits hand hygiene monthly. Kiosk adoption, access on all shifts, and the value of logging to nurses are unknown.

Before launch, walk two cases with day- and night-shift nurses. In a routine case, the nurse performs hand hygiene at the clinical cue and logs it at the first safe opportunity. Time the entry and ask what feedback would make it worth recording. In a blocked case, such as urgent care or a kiosk outage, the nurse continues care, skips the entry if needed, and flags the blocker at handover. The ward nurse lead owns the issue and routes it to kiosk support by the next working day. Hold the incentive if most cases are blocked or logging routinely interrupts care. Use the incentive where the workflow is usable and nurses have yet to try it.

## 2. Credit rule

A nurse earns **one credit for the ward** when a kiosk entry made during the same shift matches one independently observed, completed hand-hygiene opportunity. One action covering several indications still earns one credit. The ward score is **matched entries ÷ observed completed opportunities** during sampled windows when logging was feasible. Monthly clinical compliance remains **observed hand-hygiene actions ÷ all observed opportunities**, including opportunities where hygiene was missed. [WHO observation manual](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/hand-hygiene/monitoring/9789241598606_eng.pdf)

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Observed action with one accurate same-shift entry | One credit |
| Duplicate entry | One credit total |
| Entry withdrawn or observer corrects the record | Remove the credit and update the denominator if the observation changed |
| Next-shift entry or edited timestamp | Zero credit. Use the original submission time |
| Hygiene performed, kiosk skipped or old method used | Zero credit. Keep the opportunity in the denominator if logging was feasible |
| Hygiene missed, even if logged | Zero credit. Include the missed opportunity in clinical compliance |
| Kiosk outage or care prevents safe entry | Exclude from the logging score under predeclared criteria and record the exclusion |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at the kiosk:** “After hand hygiene, log the moment when care allows. You’ll see that it saved, and if our ward logs more of the moments verified in routine checks by 2 May, we’ll earn a ward meal across shifts.”
- **Confirmation:** “Saved for this shift. You can correct this entry here.”
- **Private progress:** “1 of 3 practice entries this week. Log the next moment when care allows.” Offer a one-entry goal for low-volume weeks and show “No opportunities this week” when applicable. Practice goals carry no reward.
- **Restart:** “Start with your next safe opportunity.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s verified logging rate, baseline, and target once there is enough data. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress small or identifiable groups. The designated audit analyst has logged, time-limited access for matching and disputes. Disclose the pilot’s data use before launch and prohibit reuse for discipline.

## 5. Motivator

Each ward can earn a hospital-funded meal with equivalent access across shifts by closing **25% of its baseline gap to full logging** and passing the safety check. A 60% baseline gives a 70% target. Test the reward with nurses from different shifts before announcing it. Use the huddle to report which kiosk problems the hospital has fixed.

## 6. Operator card

- **Owner and data:** Infection Prevention owns the pilot. Use kiosk timestamps and correction history plus an observer record made before reviewing the kiosk entries. Assume privacy approval and a tested match using a temporary shift code, approximate event time, and indication, with zero patient identifiers. If matching is ambiguous, withhold credit. Launch with stakes only after the dry run shows reliable matching.
- **Quality and pause:** Keep the monthly clinical audit. Add short observation windows across day, night, and weekend shifts for the logging measure. Infection Prevention freezes reward scoring and investigates if a ward’s monthly clinical compliance falls **5 percentage points below baseline**, or immediately after any credible report that logging delayed care. Nurses can request corrections privately through the ward lead. Budget **about one hour per ward per week** beyond the existing audits, subject to the dry run.

## 7. End and decision

**Proposed dates for 2027:** Baseline with kiosk support and no reward, **11 January–7 March**. Incentive, **8 March–2 May**. Follow-up with the fixes and private feedback retained, **3 May–27 June**.

Compare each ward with its own baseline using the same shift mix. Require at least **100 observed completed opportunities per ward per phase** before deciding. Award the meal after verifying the incentive results. Expand only if at least two wards reach their targets and retain at least half their gains through follow-up, with no unresolved safety pause. Revise if coverage is thin or gains fade. Stop the incentive if logging interferes with care or clinical compliance remains below the safety threshold. Treat the before-and-after results as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Logging displaces care.** Protect the safe-entry window and pause on credible reports of delay.
- **Duplicate or invented entries inflate apparent adoption.** Base rewards on one-to-one observer matches.
- **Audit windows overstate adoption or feel like surveillance.** Sample across shifts and limit who can see or retain matching records.
