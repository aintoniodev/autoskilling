## 1. Diagnosis

Article coverage is 12%. That figure alone cannot tell us whether agents overlook the tool, struggle to find an article, or lose time writing one. Before launch, shadow two real tickets from resolution through closure. On a routine ticket, check whether the agent can find and link an accurate article without leaving the ticket. On a ticket with no suitable article, check whether the agent can submit a reusable article for review and close the ticket promptly. These are proposed walkthroughs. The Support Ops lead owns access and workflow fixes, with a two-business-day response target. If most tickets are blocked or the supported workflow adds material effort, fix the workflow before launching.

## 2. Credit rule

When an agent resolves a ticket, it earns one credit if it links to an approved article that explains the actual resolution. A new article submitted from the ticket earns credit once the KB editor approves and links it. Credit belongs to the ticket’s original resolution week. The fallback is to close the ticket normally and flag the KB gap. Assume ticket and KB history can verify attribution, timestamps, approvals and edits. Run the cases below against real records before attaching rewards.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Resolved ticket with a relevant approved article | One credit |
| Second link, repeat save or duplicate ticket | One credit per canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopened or article withdrawn | Remove provisional credit. Restore it only after the resolution and article qualify again. |
| Article submitted on time and approved by the weekly cutoff | Credit the original week. A changed ticket due date has no effect. |
| Eligible ticket resolved through the old workflow | In the denominator, zero credit |
| Ticket missing from the programme feed | Reconcile against the source queue, restore it to the denominator and award credit if history verifies it. Hold rewards if reconciliation fails. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation in the resolution screen.** “Before you close, link the article that explains the fix so the next agent can reuse it. If one is missing, submit it for review and close as usual. Each verified ticket counts toward an extra paid hour for everyone on the pilot team when we reach 25% coverage. The pilot ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation.** “Article verified for this ticket. You’re at 2 of 4 this week.” For a submission, show “Article sent for review” immediately, then confirm when it qualifies.
- **Progress.** “2 of 4 this week. On your next eligible ticket, link the article that explains the fix.” Set the goal at one in four expected eligible tickets, rounded up and adjusted for leave and queue assignment. Show one as the goal for one to three opportunities. Show “No eligible tickets this week” at zero.
- **Restart.** “New week. Start with your next eligible ticket.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own progress. Post the pilot team’s verified coverage against its baseline weekly in the existing support channel. Managers see aggregates. Support Ops can grant logged, time-limited individual access for a correction, dispute or agent-requested coaching. Prohibit disciplinary reuse. Suppress breakdowns covering fewer than five agents or otherwise exposing someone’s activity.

## 5. Motivator

Give every pilot-team member one additional paid hour off, with queue coverage arranged, if verified coverage reaches 25% across the four weeks and passes the quality check. Test the reward and wording with agents across shifts. Assume approval for coverage and funding separate from the existing CSAT bonus. Post the KB fixes agents report in the same channel as team progress.

## 6. Operator card

Support Ops reconciles the ticket log against KB approval history. Coverage is verified credits divided by distinct eligible resolved tickets. Predeclare exclusions for spam, merged duplicates and cases where policy bars KB documentation. Report all-ticket coverage and exclusions alongside the pilot metric.

An independent QA lead samples 20 credited tickets across shifts each week for relevance, accuracy and safe content. At three failures in a sample or one privacy incident, pause awards, correct records and review a fresh sample before resuming. Resolve disputes through the existing Support Ops channel within two business days. Estimate two hours weekly for reconciliation and QA, plus article review. Lock weekly results after two business days for review and corrections.

## 7. End and decision plan

Proposed dates are January 4–31, 2027 for baseline, February 1–28 for the pilot and March 1–28 for follow-up. Randomly assign comparable intact teams to the pilot or comparison after matching on queue mix and baseline coverage. Give both groups the same workflow fixes and private feedback. End rewards and team progress posts February 28. Keep measuring both groups.

Scale if the pilot improves at least 10 percentage points more than the comparison during the pilot and retains an eight-point advantage in improvement during follow-up. Require QA clearance and no deterioration versus comparison greater than three points in CSAT or two points in reopen rate. Check CSAT response counts before deciding. Revise if results are inconclusive or the lift fades. Stop if quality or customer outcomes breach those limits and the problem persists after correction.

## 8. Risks

- **Irrelevant links inflate coverage.** Independently check relevance and correct credits.
- **The CSAT bonus encourages agents to skip harder cases or delay closure.** Keep the normal closure path and check closure times and exclusions by queue.
- **The lift fades when the paid hour disappears.** Use the four-week follow-up to decide whether to scale.
