## 1. Diagnosis

Agents link or write an article on 12% of resolved tickets. That figure measures adoption. Before launch, the Support Ops lead and two agents walk a routine ticket with a good existing article and a ticket with a permissions or publishing blocker from assignment to resolution. Time both paths and ask agents where using the knowledge base adds effort or puts CSAT at risk. Fund protected writing time and assign the Support Ops lead to triage blockers within one business day and post fixes in the support channel.

**Launch assumption:** Most tickets can be handled with a relevant link or a short, reusable article during paid work. Fix recurring access or workflow problems before launch. Agents can always resolve the customer’s issue through the existing workflow and flag a blocker.

## 2. Credit rule

When a resolving agent closes a ticket, they earn **one credit per canonical ticket** if it has a relevant, accurate link to a published article or to a new approved article they wrote that captures the fix without customer data. Capture the link at resolution and verify it after seven days. Attribute credit to the resolving agent. Calculate the CSAT bonus under its existing rules.

The team measure is credited tickets divided by **all unique resolved tickets** in the cohort, including old-workflow, blocked and omitted cases. Track blocker reasons separately so the sponsor can see where access or coverage limits adoption.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal resolution with a usable article | One credit after verification. |
| Duplicate link, merged ticket or repeated close | Count once under the canonical ticket ID. |
| Ticket reopens or the article is recalled during verification | Remove pending credit. A later qualified resolution can earn the single credit. |
| Late link or edited due date | Use the recorded close and link timestamps. A due-date edit has no effect. Ops can correct a proven logging error. |
| Ticket resolved through the old workflow | Count the resolution in the denominator. |
| Agent skips a ticket in the programme | Reconcile against the ticket system’s resolution log and count the omitted resolution. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution:** “Closing this ticket? Link the article that explains the fix, or publish a short one so the next agent can reuse it. Each verified ticket counts toward a team lunch if we reach 30% by March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Article linked to ticket #4821. It’s pending verification.”
- **Progress:** “3 of your 5 tickets verified this week. On your next resolution, link the article that explains the fix.”
- **Restart:** “Start with your next resolved ticket. Your earlier verified work still counts toward the team goal.”

Set private weekly goals using each agent’s recent ticket volume. For someone with one or two expected opportunities, suggest one qualified ticket. Show “No resolved tickets yet” for someone with zero and skip the reminder.

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own ticket-level progress. The support channel shows the pilot cohort’s verified rate against its baseline, without names. Managers get the same aggregate view. Suppress breakdowns with fewer than five agents or where a person can be inferred. A designated manager can request time-limited, logged access to an individual record for agent-requested coaching or a correction or dispute. Exclude programme data from disciplinary use. The monitoring evidence supports limiting individual visibility (Grade A, Ravid et al.).

## 5. Motivator

Fund a team lunch during paid time if the pilot cohort reaches **30% verified adoption** and passes the quality gate. Include everyone in the cohort regardless of ticket volume. Test the lunch and milestone with a few agents across shifts before launch. Post one weekly progress update alongside a specific blocker the team reported and Ops fixed.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner:** Support Ops lead. **Budget:** Two hours a week across Ops and QA. **Data:** Ticket resolution, merge and reopen history plus article link, approval and recall history. Reliable timestamps, sufficient QA capacity and lunch funding are launch assumptions.

Each week an independent QA reviewer randomly samples 20 credited tickets, or all if fewer, checking relevance, accuracy and customer data. At **three failures in a weekly sample or one customer-data exposure**, the Support Ops lead pauses reward qualification, fixes the cause and resumes after a passing review. Agents can send a ticket ID privately to Ops. Ops checks the history and answers within two business days. Pay the reward after final verification and QA.

## 7. End and decision plan

**Baseline:** January 11–February 7, 2027. **Pilot:** February 8–March 7. **Reward-free follow-up:** March 8–April 4. Assuming comparable pods, match them by baseline adoption and volume, then randomly assign one per pair to the programme. Both groups get the same workflow fixes, writing time and private feedback. After March 7, end the lunch incentive and keep measuring both groups.

Scale if pilot adoption reaches 30%, the follow-up gain over baseline exceeds the comparison group’s gain by at least **10 percentage points**, QA passes, and CSAT falls by fewer than **3 percentage points** relative to the comparison group’s change. Change and retest if quality and CSAT hold but adoption misses either threshold. Stop the incentive if QA stays below standard after a fix or CSAT breaches the guardrail. With only a baseline comparison, treat results as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Risks

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles** inflate adoption. Audit the usefulness of the linked article.
- **Writing time competes with fast resolution and CSAT pay.** Protect writing time and keep the existing resolution path available.
- **Adoption drops after lunch.** Base the scale decision on the four reward-free weeks.
