# Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

## 1. Diagnosis

The hospital has a new kiosk and monthly observer audits. The adoption barrier is unknown. Before launch, shadow nurses on day and night shifts through one routine case, from hand hygiene at the point of care to a saved entry at the next safe break, and one blocked case during an urgent transfer or kiosk outage. Time the entry and ask nurses where it interrupts care. If logging is quick and useful but unfamiliar, run the pilot. If access, workload or kiosk placement blocks most cases, defer rewards and fix the workflow. The nursing informatics lead acknowledges blockers within one working day and owns resolution. During urgent care or downtime, follow the usual clinical protocol and resume logging at the next safe opportunity.

**Assumptions to confirm** — Infection Prevention approves kiosk placement and cleaning, Nursing approves the pooled reward, and the kiosk supports reliable event matching with minimal staff data. The proposed dates and thresholds below are pilot settings.

## 2. Credit rule

One credit for a nurse’s independently observed, correctly performed hand-hygiene action that matches one accurate kiosk entry saved by shift end. The cue is the next safe pause after a standard hand-hygiene moment. Multiple indications for the same opportunity earn one credit. The reward metric is **verified entries ÷ all sampled correctly performed actions**, including actions during kiosk downtime. Clinical compliance is tracked independently as **correct actions ÷ all observed opportunities**. [WHO observation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

| Case | Credit |
|---|---|
| Correct action with a matching entry by shift end | One |
| Duplicate entry or several indications for one action | One total |
| Entry voided or corrected to an unmatched action | Remove credit before payout |
| Late entry or edited timestamp | Use original save time and recorded shift end |
| Correct hygiene with paper or verbal documentation only | Zero kiosk credit, compliant in the audit |
| Observed action omitted from kiosk, including during downtime | Zero kiosk credit, retained in denominator |
| Kiosk entry for a missed action or ambiguous match | Zero |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at the next safe charting pause** — “After cleaning your hands at the point of care, log the moment at your next safe pause so you can check it saved without re-entering it. If our three wards reach [target]% verified logging by February 28, everyone on the pilot nursing teams earns a €10 meal voucher.”
- **Confirmation** — “Saved for this shift. You can check or correct it in My entries.”
- **Private progress** — “You’ve logged on 2 of your 3 planned practice shifts. On your next shift, log each moment when care allows.” Nurses choose up to three scheduled shifts. A shift without a safe opportunity can be skipped. Staff with no eligible shifts have no practice target.
- **Restart** — “Missed a shift? Pick up with your next hand-hygiene moment when care allows.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own records and practice progress. The existing staff huddle board shows one pooled, verified rate for the three wards against their own baseline and target. Publish only with at least 30 sampled actions from 10 nurses. Managers receive the same aggregates. A designated Infection Prevention reviewer gets logged, time-limited access for verification, corrections and disputes. Programme records are excluded from individual performance reviews and discipline. Explain the sampling and access rules at launch.

## 5. Motivator

Test the pooled €10 voucher with day- and night-shift nurses before launch. Everyone on the pilot nursing teams qualifies, including staff with few sampled opportunities. Pay once after Infection Prevention clears the results. Post one concrete kiosk fix at huddles each week when a fix ships.

## 6. Operator card

Infection Prevention runs two rotating 20-minute observation windows per ward weekly, covering days, nights and weekends across each phase. Observers record opportunities and actions before viewing logs. Match using a temporary staff code, time and moment type. Collect zero patient identifiers. Use the existing monthly observer audit as an additional clinical check. If matching is unreliable, run without rewards.

Pause rewards and investigate any reported care delay linked to the kiosk, a five-percentage-point compliance drop on any ward versus baseline with at least 30 observed opportunities, or more than 5% duplicate or contradictory entries among at least 30 sampled entries. Nurses request corrections privately through Infection Prevention. Budget about four staff-hours weekly for observation, reconciliation and huddle updates. [Hand-hygiene monitoring practice recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)

## 7. End and decision

Use January 4–31, 2027 for supported baseline logging, February 1–28 for the reward, and March 1–28 for reward-free follow-up. Keep fixes and private feedback throughout. Set the pooled milestone at baseline plus 15 percentage points, capped at 95%. If baseline already reaches 95%, skip the reward.

Expand only if the milestone is met during the reward phase and again in follow-up, each ward contributes at least 50 observed opportunities per phase, and all safety issues are resolved. Change and retest if logging fades or sampling is too sparse. Stop the incentive if care delays or lower compliance persist. This pre/post pilot measures feasibility and persistence. Causal attribution remains uncertain.

## 8. Main risks

- Nurses delay care or clean at the kiosk to make logging easier.
- Duplicate entries or extra effort during observation inflate apparent adoption.
- Staff perceive surveillance, underreport problems, or stop logging after payout.
