### 1. Diagnosis

The reported 35% digital completion rate leaves two questions open: how many technicians already complete a paper check, and what keeps them from using the app? Before launch, the Field Operations Lead and a union-nominated technician walk two real jobs from dispatch through the safety decision. On a supported job, compare time and rework with paper. On a blocked job, test a common failure such as poor signal and confirm the approved fallback works. Validate the 35% against dispatch records.

Fund device, login and offline fixes before offering a reward. Give technicians a dispatch help route, with a same-shift response and a fix or workaround within two working days. Proceed if the app is at least as practical as paper on supported jobs and lack of trial is a material barrier. Defer if most jobs are blocked. Assumptions to verify with the union and safety team include paid time for checks, a usable fallback, and reliable job and checklist history.

### 2. Credit rule

When a technician reaches the pre-job safety assessment, one dispatch job earns one credit for a complete, valid digital checklist submitted before work starts. A completed check that correctly puts the job on safety hold also earns credit. The team rate is credited jobs divided by app-supported dispatch jobs that reached assessment, including supported jobs completed on paper or with no recorded check. A separate all-jobs view tracks paper checks, missing checks and app blockers. Technicians follow the approved fallback whenever the app fails and start work only after the required safety checks and clearance.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Complete digital check before work, or valid safety hold | One credit |
| Two submissions, sync retry or technician handoff | One credit per dispatch job ID |
| Checklist voided or recalled as invalid | Remove provisional credit unless a valid pre-start record exists |
| Late submission or dispatch due-date edit | Use original completion and actual start records. A due-date edit changes neither. Offline sync counts if original timing is trustworthy. |
| Approved paper fallback | Record the safety check separately. An app-supported job stays in the denominator. |
| Omitted check or missing timing | Zero credit. Keep the supported job in the denominator and route it for review. |

### 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation in dispatch at the job cue:** “Before you start, open the safety checklist to see the required checks and record hazards in one place. If the app fails, use the approved paper checklist. Each verified digital pre-job check counts once, and if our depot reaches its target by 28 February, everyone on the pilot crew gets 30 minutes of paid time back.”
- **Confirmation:** “Safety checklist saved before work started. This job counts once, pending verification.” For a hold: “Safety hold recorded. Keep the job on hold until it’s cleared.”
- **Private progress:** “You’ve completed 2 of your first 3 app-supported checks this week. Open the checklist on your next job.” With one or two opportunities, size the milestone to those jobs. With zero, show “No app-supported jobs this week.” The safety check still applies to every job.
- **Restart:** “Start with your next job. Your previous checks still count toward the team total.”

### 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing depot huddle or dispatch channel shows the depot’s verified rate against its own baseline and target, with no individual names or rankings. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress any breakdown that could identify someone. Named access is restricted to a designated reviewer for corrections, disputes or requested coaching, with logged, time-limited access and no disciplinary reuse. Agree access and retention with union representatives before launch.

### 5. Motivator

Offer 30 minutes of paid time back to the whole pilot crew at each depot that improves by 15 percentage points over its baseline and passes the quality gate. Include low-volume staff and staff with no eligible jobs. Test the reward and target with technicians and union representatives before launch. Share one concrete fix at each huddle when available, such as “You reported lost drafts. The app now restores them.”

### 6. Operator card

Field Operations reconciles dispatch jobs, start records, checklist audit history and verified outage records. Technicians can request corrections through dispatch or their union representative. Resolve them within two working days and before reward release.

An independent Safety Lead reviews 20 randomly selected digital checks weekly across pilot depots against job details and hold records. Pause reward reporting and release for any credible missed critical hazard or two materially deficient checks in a weekly sample. Safety investigates, corrects records and clears the restart. Continue normal safety checks throughout. Budget two staff-hours weekly for reconciliation and review, plus investigations.

### 7. End and decision

Proposed 2027 dates are 4–31 January for baseline, 1–28 February for the pilot and 1–28 March for reward-free follow-up. Keep app fixes and private feedback. Assign comparable depots to the programme or to the same app support and private feedback alone. Release earned rewards after February verification.

Scale if improvement over comparison is at least 10 percentage points during the pilot and 5 during follow-up, with no quality pause or increase in missing safety checks. Revise and retest if uptake rises but fades. Stop for unresolved safety or trust problems, or no improvement. Without a credible comparator, report feasibility only.

### 8. Risks

- **Box-ticking or rushing past hazards.** Quality review and credit for proper holds protect the safety decision.
- **Outages disadvantage crews.** Verify exclusions independently and keep fallback use visible.
- **Technicians feel monitored.** Agree limits with the union before launch and enforce them through access logs and aggregate reporting.
