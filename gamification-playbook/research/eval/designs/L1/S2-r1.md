## 1. Diagnosis

The team’s median review response is 2.6 days against a one-working-day goal. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the engineering lead walks two actual PRs with their authors and reviewers. For a routine PR, trace the time from ready to first substantive review and check whether a clear assignment and a review slot would have helped. For a delayed PR, find where it waited and why.

**Working assumption:** Some reviews wait because ownership is unclear or review time gets displaced. Start a rotating review owner and protect review time during baseline measurement. The engineering lead resolves routing and access blockers within one working day and assigns a backup when needed. If most PRs still lack access, qualified coverage, or review capacity, defer the incentive and fix those blockers first.

## 2. Credit rule

When a human-authored product PR first becomes ready for review, the assigned reviewer or backup has **eight working hours** to submit a substantive first review. Use the team’s agreed working calendar and working hours. An approval identifies what was checked. A request for changes identifies an actionable issue. Each eligible PR contributes once to the denominator, and a qualifying on-time review earns one credit and contributes once to the numerator. The first-response measure ends at the review, even if changes are needed before merge.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Reviewer checks the changed path and approves within eight working hours | One credit |
| Two reviewers respond, or the first edits their review | One credit at most |
| The qualifying review is withdrawn because it was invalid | Remove the credit. Another valid review earns it only if submitted by the original deadline. |
| Review is late, the due date is edited, or the PR returns to draft | Original clock stands. A late review gets zero credit. |
| Reviewer answers in chat or acknowledges receipt | Zero credit until a qualifying review is submitted on the PR |
| Ready PR is unassigned or missing from the report | Reconcile it into the denominator at its original timestamp. Withhold reward settlement if the history is unresolved. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation, on assignment:** “Review [PR] by [time] so the author can keep moving. If you’re blocked, hand it to [backup]. Through 2 April, each PR answered with a substantive review within one working day counts once toward 80% and a paid hour off for everyone.”
- **Confirmation:** “Your review on [PR] counted. You answered within one working day.”
- **Private progress:** “3 of your 4 reviews due this week were answered on time. [PR] is next, due Thursday at 14:00.” The personal target is 80% of assigned opportunities. With one or two, show the cases and deadlines without a target. With zero, send no progress reminder.
- **Restart:** “Start with [PR], due [time]. [Backup] can take it if you’re tied up.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The team and managers see the verified team rate alongside its baseline in the existing engineering channel, with no names or rankings. Suppress any weekly figure based on fewer than five PRs and roll it into the next update. Individual programme records are available to the operator for corrections or requested coaching. Log access, expire it when the case closes, and prohibit disciplinary use.

## 5. Motivator

Offer **one paid hour off for all 25 engineers** if the team reaches 80% over the pilot and clears the quality check. Let people schedule it around coverage. Before launch, confirm with a few engineers that the hour is worth earning and with the sponsor that it can be protected. Post routing and tooling fixes in the engineering channel as they land.

## 6. Operator card

- **Data:** Repository events for readiness, assignment, review submission and dismissal, plus the team calendar. Reconcile eligible PRs weekly against the repository list. Assume reliable event history and sponsor approval. Resolve both before attaching a reward.
- **Quality:** Each week, a rotating senior engineer who neither authored nor reviewed the sampled PRs audits 10 randomly selected timely reviews, or all if fewer. They check that the review engages with the actual change. Two superficial reviews in a weekly sample, or one missed release-blocking issue that the reviewer claimed to have checked, triggers an engineering-lead pause on reward settlement. Fix the cause and pass a fresh sample before resuming.
- **Corrections:** Engineers send the PR link privately to the engineering lead, who resolves disputes within two working days. Budget 60 minutes per week across reconciliation and audit.

## 7. End and decision

**Baseline:** 11 January–19 February 2027 with routing, protected review time, and private feedback. **Pilot:** 22 February–2 April. Verify and settle the reward the following week. **Follow-up:** 12 April–21 May with the same workflow and private feedback, after reward settlement.

Continue the workflow if pilot on-time response reaches 80%, improves by at least 15 percentage points over baseline, passes the quality gate, and stays within five points of the pilot rate during follow-up. If the lift fades, end the reward and address the remaining workflow friction. If quality deteriorates or adoption stalls, stop the incentive. The before-and-after comparison supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamp approvals.** Audit substance and pause settlement when quality slips.
- **Cherry-picking easy PRs.** Keep unassigned and complex PRs in the denominator and have the rotation route them.
- **Peer pressure or after-hours work.** Use aggregate progress, working-hour deadlines, and backup coverage.
