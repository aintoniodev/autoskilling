## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently have a digital checklist completed before work starts. The reason for the gap is unknown. Before launch, the field operations lead and a union steward ride along on two jobs. On a supported job, they follow the dispatch alert through checklist completion and compare time and rework with the current process. On a blocked job, they verify the approved fallback and how the technician reports the issue. Check access, signal, device availability and whether the app duplicates paperwork.

Field Operations owns blockers, acknowledges them by the next business day and posts fixes in the dispatch channel. If most jobs are blocked or the app adds work without a clear benefit to technicians, fix the workflow before launching rewards. The design assumes an approved fallback and a reliable record of when work starts. Confirm both with Safety and the union before launch.

## 2. Credit rule

When a technician opens their next dispatched job, the assigned checklist owner earns one credit for submitting a complete, job-linked digital safety checklist before work starts. A checklist that identifies a hazard counts once verified. Reported hazards and stop-work decisions do not reduce credit.

Measure verified credits divided by all jobs that actually started, including jobs that used the approved fallback and jobs with missing checklists. Build the denominator from dispatch and reconcile it with job-start records. Count cancelled jobs that never started separately.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete checklist submitted before work starts | One credit |
| Duplicate submission or reassignment | One credit per job, attributed to the technician who completed the valid checklist |
| Checklist voided or recalled | Remove provisional credit unless another valid pre-start submission exists |
| Late submission or edited appointment time | Use the audited actual start. A schedule edit cannot make a late checklist eligible |
| App unavailable and approved fallback used | Record fallback separately. Job stays in the denominator |
| Checklist omitted | Job stays in the denominator with zero credit |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at dispatch.** “Open the safety checklist for this job before you start so the checks and any hazards are recorded on the job. If the app is unavailable, use the usual approved backup.”
- **Confirmation.** “Checklist saved before work started. This job counts toward your weekly goal.” If the record is awaiting sync, show “Saved on this device. Credit will show after sync.”
- **Progress.** “2 of your next 3 supported jobs checked this week. Open the checklist on your next job.” The personal goal covers up to three supported jobs each week. With one or two, the goal shrinks to match. With zero, show “No supported jobs this week.”
- **Restart.** “New week. Open the checklist on your next job.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing dispatch channel shows pooled pilot progress against its baseline each week, with no names, rankings or depot breakdowns. Suppress any update that covers fewer than 10 technicians or lets readers infer someone’s result from prior updates. Managers see aggregates. The programme administrator may access an individual record for a correction, dispute or technician-requested coaching. Log that access, close it when the case is resolved and prohibit disciplinary use of programme records.

## 5. Motivator

If the pilot group reaches **55% verified pre-start completion across the six weeks** and passes the quality check, everyone in that group gets **30 minutes of paid release time**, scheduled around field coverage. Confirm the reward and scheduling with the union and test whether technicians value it before launch.

**Launch line.** “Each job with a digital checklist completed before work starts counts, and if our group reaches 55% by March 14, everyone earns 30 minutes of paid time back.”

## 6. Operator card

Use dispatch history, app audit events and trusted job-start timestamps. Verify that offline submissions retain auditable timestamps before attaching rewards. Each week, a Safety reviewer independent of the reward draws 20 credited jobs at random, or all if fewer, and checks timing and job-specific completeness against service records. If two sampled jobs fail either check or the review identifies a serious safety lapse, the Safety lead pauses reward scoring, investigates with the union steward and resumes only after the cause is fixed and affected credits are reconciled. Technicians can dispute a credit privately through the dispatch help channel. Field Operations responds within two business days. Budget two staff hours a week for review and administration.

## 7. End and decision plan

Measure baseline **January 4–31, 2027**. Pair comparable crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly assign one crew per pair to the incentive. Both groups get the same training, fixes and private feedback. Run rewards **February 1–March 14** and measure both groups through **April 25**, six weeks after rewards end.

Scale if the pilot reaches 55%, retains a baseline-adjusted advantage of at least 10 percentage points during follow-up and passes the quality review. Change and retest if completion improves but misses either threshold. Stop the incentive if completion fails to improve or quality problems recur. Keep the workflow fixes and private feedback.

## 8. Risks

- **Rushed checklists.** Review job-specific entries and investigate discrepancies before paying the reward.
- **Pressure to start unsafe jobs.** Credit valid hazard reports and preserve stop-work authority.
- **Unequal access.** Track fallback by job type so weak coverage or device shortages surface even when the pooled rate rises.
