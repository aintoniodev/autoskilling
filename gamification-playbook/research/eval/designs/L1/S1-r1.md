## 1. Diagnosis

Only about 35% of technicians currently complete the checklist in the app. Before launch, verify that figure against actual job starts and have a technician and union representative walk two jobs with the field safety lead. On a supported job, open the checklist from dispatch, record a hazard and its resolution, and confirm it saves before work starts. On a blocked job, try the same flow at a site with no signal and walk the approved fallback. Ask technicians on several shifts whether the app saves time, adds duplicate entry, or is easy to miss.

**Assumption to test:** Enough jobs support the app, and opening the checklist from dispatch makes the safety check easier. The field operations lead owns a funded fix queue and responds within one working day. Fix duplicate entry or other recurring friction before launch. If most jobs are blocked, defer the incentive.

## 2. Credit rule

When a technician reaches the pre-job safety check on their assigned dispatch job, completing and saving the required digital checklist before work starts earns one credit per job. Hazard reporting earns the same credit as a clear checklist. Follow the existing safety procedure before starting, including the approved fallback if the app fails. A safety hold carries no penalty.

Measure verified digital completions divided by **all started jobs that require a checklist**, using dispatch records reconciled against actual starts. Keep approved fallback jobs in the denominator and report their frequency separately. Jobs that never start are excluded.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Valid checklist saved before work starts | One credit |
| Retry, duplicate submission, or reassignment | At most one credit against the original job ID |
| Submission recalled or invalidated | Remove provisional credit unless a valid pre-start record remains |
| Job runs late or its scheduled time changes | Compare the original save time with actual start. A schedule edit cannot turn a late checklist into a timely one. |
| Approved paper fallback | Counts as a started job and is recorded as a fallback |
| Checklist missing or job omitted from the app | Count the job from dispatch and start records. Zero credit. |

Attach the reward only after verifying that dispatch and app records can support these checks, including reliable save times for offline submissions.

## 3. What technicians see

- **Invitation in the dispatch job:** “Open the safety checklist here before starting. It’s linked to this job, so you can skip re-entering the job number. Each job with a verified pre-start digital checklist counts toward a paid team break if we reach 55% by March 7.”
- **Confirmation:** “Checklist saved for job 4821 before work started. This is your third digital check this week.”
- **Private progress:** “3 of 4 this week. Open the checklist from your next job.” Set goals at roughly half each technician’s expected eligible volume, with a goal of one for weeks with one or two jobs. Show “No eligible jobs this week” when there are none. Personal goals carry no stakes.
- **Restart:** “Your next job is a fresh start. Open the checklist before you begin.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. Pilot crews see one pooled weekly rate alongside their own pre-pilot baseline in the existing crew briefing. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress any breakdown with fewer than ten technicians or where subtraction could identify someone. A designated steward can open individual pilot records for requested coaching, corrections, or disputes. Log each access, close access when the case closes, and prohibit disciplinary reuse of pilot records. Agree these terms with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Motivator

Test a **30-minute paid team break** with technicians across shifts and get union and scheduling agreement before announcing it. Everyone in the pilot crews earns it if their pooled eight-week rate reaches 55% and passes the quality gate. Offer equivalent paid slots across shifts and arrange coverage. Post fixes in the crew briefing when technicians’ reports lead to changes.

## 6. Operator card

- **Owner and effort:** Field operations lead, with a union-designated steward and a safety reviewer outside crew management. Budget three staff-hours weekly across reconciliation, review, and corrections.
- **Quality gate:** Each week, the safety reviewer randomly samples 20 credited jobs and checks timing, site-specific answers, and hazard handling against existing job records and routine safety observations. If at least two have material defects, or one shows work began despite an unresolved stop-work hazard, the field safety lead pauses reward accrual and payout, investigates, and resumes only after correction and a passing fresh sample. Safety procedures continue throughout.
- **Corrections:** Technicians can raise mismatches privately with the steward, who responds within two working days and records any adjustment before payout.

## 7. End and decision

**Proposed dates:** January 11–March 7, 2027, with follow-up March 8–May 2. Where scheduling permits, randomly assign comparable crews to the incentive or to the same app, fixes, and private feedback without the team reward. At close, verify and settle the break, retire the reward messages, and keep the fixes and private feedback.

Scale if the pilot reaches 55%, improves at least 10 percentage points more than comparison crews relative to baseline, and retains a five-point advantage during follow-up with no unresolved quality failures. If lift fades, revise and retest. Stop if quality problems persist or there is no comparative lift. With only a baseline comparison, treat the run as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- Technicians rush checks or hide hazards to help the team reach 55%.
- Poor connectivity makes some routes carry more of the burden.
- Workers read the dashboard as surveillance, or adoption falls once the paid break is earned.
