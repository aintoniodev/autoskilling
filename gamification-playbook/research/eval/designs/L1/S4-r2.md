## 1. Diagnosis

**12% of resolved tickets currently get an article.** Before launch, the Support Ops lead shadows agents through two real cases. On a routine password-reset ticket, check whether the agent can find and link the approved article from the resolution screen and reuse it in the reply. On a ticket containing customer-specific information with no safe reusable guidance, have the agent resolve it normally and record the exception privately.

Check why agents skip the step. If search, permissions, or publishing block it, Support Ops owns the fix and responds within one business day. If linking or drafting adds more work than it saves, fix the workflow first. Launch only if most cases support a useful article and some agents are skipping a path that already works. **Assumptions to verify** include ticket history, article approvals, comparable support pods, and approval for paid time.

## 2. Credit rule

When an agent resolves a ticket, they earn **one credit per ticket** for linking a relevant, approved article or writing a reusable article that the KB editor approves and links within seven days. Resolve the customer’s issue on the usual schedule. If no safe article fits, close normally and flag the gap.

The team rate is verified credits divided by **all unique resolved tickets** in the period. Report blocked and exception volumes alongside it so changes in ticket mix are visible. Attribute credit to the resolving agent and original resolution week.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Relevant approved article linked | One credit |
| Two links, repeat events, or duplicate tickets merged | One credit per canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopened or article withdrawn before verification | Hold credit until the ticket is resolved and the link qualifies |
| Due date edited or article approved after seven days | Due-date edits have no effect. Late approval earns no pilot credit |
| Agent resolves through the usual path without an article | Zero credit. Ticket stays in the denominator |
| Ticket missing from the programme feed | Reconcile against the resolution log. Hold the tally if history is incomplete |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution** “Link a relevant article so you can reuse the answer, or draft one if the answer is missing. Each verified ticket counts once. If we reach 30% by February 28, everyone in the pilot gets 30 minutes of paid time.”
- **Confirmation** “Article linked to ticket #4821. It’s in your progress.” For drafts, show “Draft sent to the KB editor. We’ll confirm when it’s approved and linked.”
- **Private progress** “2 of your 3 this week. On your next eligible ticket, search for an article before writing the answer.” Set weekly goals at roughly one in three expected eligible tickets, using each agent’s recent volume. With one or two opportunities, the goal is one. With zero, show “No eligible tickets yet.”
- **Restart** “Start with your next eligible ticket. Your earlier credits still count toward the team goal.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own ticket-level progress. Post the pilot team’s verified rate against its own baseline in the existing support channel each week, with no names. Managers see aggregates. Suppress breakdowns with fewer than 10 contributing agents or where a person could be inferred. Only designated Support Ops staff can open individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log the access, close it when the case ends, and exclude programme records from discipline and compensation decisions.

## 5. Motivator

If the pilot reaches 30% and passes the quality gate, everyone in the pilot gets **30 minutes of paid time**, scheduled around queue coverage. Test whether agents value it before launch. Fund KB editor time and post the workflow fixes agents asked for in the same channel. Keep the existing CSAT bonus terms.

## 6. Operator card

Support Ops reconciles ticket resolution and reopen events with link history and KB approvals. An independent QA reviewer samples 20 credited tickets and 10 uncredited tickets weekly, or all if fewer, to check relevance, accuracy, privacy, and missed credits. The Support Ops lead pauses reward accrual and updates if more than 10% of sampled credits fail or any article exposes customer data. Correct affected records and resume after QA confirms the fix. Agents dispute records privately with Support Ops, which replies within two business days. Budget three hours a week for reconciliation and QA, plus drafting and editorial time.

## 7. End and decision

**2027 baseline:** January 4–31. **Pilot:** February 1–28. Randomly assign comparable pods to the incentive or to the same workflow fixes, prompts, and private feedback without the reward or public progress post. Verify February credits and confirm the reward by March 7. Keep the workflow fixes and private feedback. Measure four reward-free weeks from March 8 through April 4.

Scale only if the pilot reaches 30%, retains at least a 10-percentage-point improvement over its baseline after rewards, and improves at least five points more than the comparison pods over the same follow-up. Require the quality gate to pass and no decline in CSAT greater than two points relative to comparison. Check survey response rates before interpreting CSAT. Revise and retest if results are mixed or too sparse. Stop if quality or customer experience worsens. If pod comparison falls through, use the baseline for feasibility only.

## 8. Risks

- Agents link loosely related articles or write near-duplicates to earn credit. QA checks whether the content actually helps resolve the ticket.
- Agents delay closure or avoid difficult cases to protect their CSAT bonus. Keep the normal closure process and review resolution times and case mix by pod.
- Agents drop the habit once paid time ends. Make the scale decision after the four reward-free weeks.
