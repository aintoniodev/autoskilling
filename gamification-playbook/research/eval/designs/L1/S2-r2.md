## 1. Diagnosis

The current median is 2.6 days. That shows a delay. **The working diagnosis is that review requests get missed or lose out to other work.** Confirm it with two engineers before launch. Walk one review-ready PR from request to response and check whether the owner saw it and had time to review. Walk one blocked PR, such as one with an inaccessible test environment, and check what the reviewer could safely do next.

The engineering lead owns blockers and provides a fix or fallback by the next working day. If the walks show that reviews routinely need more time than engineers have, protect review time first. If most requests are blocked, defer the incentive. The design assumes access to PR event history, a team working-hours calendar and approval for paid time.

## 2. Credit rule

When a ready product PR enters the existing review queue, the on-duty engineer owns its first review. **One PR earns one credit when a requested reviewer submits a substantive review within eight scheduled team working hours of the first ready review request.** A qualifying review approves with a brief note on what was checked, requests a specific change, or identifies a concrete blocker and next step. If safe review is impossible, flag the blocker and route it to the engineering lead. The lead verifies any exclusion.

The team metric is credited PRs divided by all eligible PRs whose deadlines fell in the reporting window. Use one published team calendar for nights, weekends and holidays.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Review submitted on time with a specific finding | One credit |
| Second review, reassignment or reopened request | Same PR and original clock, with one credit maximum |
| Review dismissed or withdrawn before verification | Remove credit unless a qualifying replacement met the original deadline |
| Review late or displayed due date edited | Use the original event and team calendar. Late work stays in the denominator |
| Review given in chat or a call | Counts if the reviewer records the substantive outcome on the PR before the deadline |
| Ready PR omitted from the queue or never answered | Catch it in the PR reconciliation and include it as a miss |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation** “You’re up to review #482. Open it from the usual review queue and leave a decision with what you checked by Tuesday at 14:00, so the author can move forward without chasing. If you’re blocked, flag it for the engineering lead.”
- **Confirmation** “Review submitted on #482 at 11:20. It’s on time and recorded for verification.”
- **Progress** “4 of 5 reviews due to you this week were answered on time. Your next review is #491, due Thursday at 12:00.” Aim for four in five. With one to four opportunities, show the count and the next due review. With zero, show “No reviews due this week.”
- **Restart** “Last week is closed. Start with #491, due Thursday at 12:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel shows the verified weekly rate against baseline, with no names. Suppress a result if fewer than five reviewers contributed or the breakdown could identify anyone. Managers receive the same aggregate. A designated operator can access individual records for a correction, dispute or requested coaching. Log access, close it with the case and prohibit disciplinary reuse.

## 5. Reward

**Answer four in five eligible PRs on time during the pilot and everyone on the team gets one paid hour back.** Award it once after quality verification, including to engineers who had no review opportunities. Confirm with a few engineers beforehand that an hour they can actually schedule is worth earning. The lead posts the team result and the review friction they fixed in the usual channel.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead owns the pilot and names a rotating peer to verify results. Budget 45 minutes a week, assuming automated counts from PR events. Reconcile against all ready product PRs. A peer who did not perform the original reviews samples 10 credited PRs each week, or all if fewer, and checks whether the review reflects the diff and supports its disposition. If two sampled reviews fail, the lead pauses the reward and investigates before resuming. Engineers can privately send a PR link to the operator for correction within two working days. If event history cannot support the table tests, run without stakes.

## 7. End and decision

Use **11 January–7 February 2027** as the baseline with the same routing, blocker support and private feedback. Run the reward **8 February–7 March**, then withdraw it and measure through **4 April** with support and feedback continuing. Publish the 7 March end date at launch.

If baseline is already at 80%, skip the reward. Extend the workflow to another team if both pilot and follow-up reach 80%, follow-up is at least 10 percentage points above baseline, and no quality pause remains unresolved. If the lift fades, end the incentive and fix the remaining friction. If quality deteriorates or there is no lift, stop. Treat the baseline comparison as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rubber-stamping** to beat the deadline. Peer sampling can pause the reward.
- **Hard PRs silently pushed out.** Reconcile the queue and require verified exclusions.
- **Resentment or a short-lived lift.** Test the reward with engineers, keep individual progress private and measure four weeks after payout eligibility ends.
