# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Give agents from each queue paid time to walk through recent tickets, identify where search and article writing slow them down, and confirm that an hour of paid time off is a reward they want. Give all teams the fixes before the pilot. Put article suggestions in the ticket close form, let agents flag a gap without delaying the customer’s resolution, and roster paid writing time with queue coverage. At launch, show agents what changed because of their feedback and use the same days to establish a post-fix baseline. The current 12% is the starting article-link or write rate.

**Count one disposition per resolved ticket.** Tell agents the rule in one sentence. “A resolved ticket counts once when you attach a relevant approved article, attach a new article after it passes the usual review, or record a valid reason why no article is needed.” Use the ticket system’s existing resolution event and ticket ID. A reopened ticket still earns at most one credit, and a pending article earns credit when it is approved and attached. An account-specific billing correction can qualify as “not needed.” A missing article for a recurring issue is a knowledge gap. Add both checks to the existing ticket QA sample. The support operations lead corrects disputed counts in the ticket record.

**Offer one team reward.** Announce at launch that every member of a team that reaches 60% correct dispositions over its four reward weeks earns one hour of paid time off. The support director thanks the team in person and confirms that agents can book the hour on paid shifts with coverage. There is no individual eligibility cutoff, so leave, low ticket volume, or a bad week does not cost someone the reward. Use the next resolved ticket as the next opportunity to contribute.

**Show progress where agents already work.** The close form gives immediate, specific feedback, such as “Article linked. This ticket counts” or “Article awaiting approval.” Each agent sees only their own count and a weekly target based on their expected volume and case mix, prorated for scheduled hours. For example, an agent expecting 20 resolutions, 15 in repeat-issue queues and five in specialist queues, might see a target of 12, based on targets of 70% and 30% for those queues. Team progress appears in the existing support dashboard. Managers see team totals only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews. Existing CSAT bonus rules still apply. Schedule writing time so agents can resolve customer issues promptly and protect the bonus they already depend on.

**Run four reward weeks per team, with a staggered start.** Pair teams by queue mix and baseline rate and randomly start one team in each pair two weeks later. Both groups have the same workflow fixes throughout. Compare their first two calendar weeks to estimate what the reward adds. After each team’s fourth week, hand over any earned reward and leave the close-form suggestions, disposition options, and paid writing time in place. Keep ticket closure available while an article is pending.

**Decide four weeks after the last reward ends.** Use the share of distinct resolved tickets with a correct disposition as the decision metric. Take the recorded share and adjust it using the proportion of dispositions that pass the existing QA sample. Keep the workflow if the post-reward rate is at least 60% and at least 10 percentage points above the post-fix baseline. Change it if the rate improves but misses either threshold. Drop the incentive if the rate returns to baseline or falls below it. The staggered comparison also tells you whether a reward is worth using in a future rollout.

**Watch for irrelevant links, excessive “not needed” claims, and rushed service.** The support operations lead spends up to 45 minutes a week reviewing the team dashboard and existing QA and CSAT reports. Pause a team’s reward if more than 10% of sampled credited tickets have an irrelevant link or an invalid exception over two weeks, or if its two-week CSAT falls five percentage points below its pre-launch baseline with enough survey responses to meet the existing reporting minimum. Fix the cause before restarting. Credit that already passed QA carries forward.
