## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Agree the rules with union stewards and technicians from different shifts and routes. Use paid ride-alongs or walkthroughs to find what makes the app slower or harder to use than the current process. Fix issues such as sign-in, poor signal and duplicate entry before the pilot. Show crews what changed based on their feedback and give everyone paid practice time. Put the checklist first on the job screen, prefill job details and let technicians complete it offline. Keep the approved paper fallback available and follow the existing stop-work procedure whenever a job is unsafe.

**What earns credit.** A job counts once when its required digital safety checklist is completed before work starts, or when it records a valid safety hold before any work begins. A valid hold earns the same credit as a job cleared to start. Apply the existing safety procedure before resuming a held job. Logins, repeat submissions and checklists completed after work begins earn no credit.

**How it’s measured.** Use the checklist record, job ID and existing job-start record. For offline submissions, use the time captured on the device and update the count when it syncs. The denominator is all dispatched jobs that reached the point where a safety assessment was due, including jobs put on hold. Exclude jobs cancelled before an assessment was due. Paper checklists remain valid under the safety procedure and stay in the denominator so outages and app friction show up in the adoption rate. The pilot coordinator fixes disputed counts through the existing dispatch support channel.

**What people see and earn.** Confirm each submission in the app and show whether it has synced. Technicians see their own credited jobs and a private goal based on their expected eligible workload and case mix. A missed week simply means picking up with the next job. Crews see one team progress figure in their usual safety huddle. Managers see team totals, with small crews combined where needed for privacy. Agree with the union that pilot data stays out of performance reviews and discipline.

Announce one reward at launch. Every member of a team that reaches 60% on-time digital completion over its four reward weeks earns one hour of paid time off, scheduled through the normal process. Confirm with technicians and the union before launch that the reward is wanted and the target is reachable across routes. Recognize the team at its next safety huddle without naming individual results. Include team members who were on leave.

**How it runs and ends.** Give every crew the fixes and the new app default first. Measure two weeks with those changes in place, then randomly assign comparable crews to start their four reward weeks immediately or two weeks later. Compare the groups during the two weeks when only the first group can earn the reward. End each group’s reward on schedule and leave the checklist first in the app. Use one number to decide what to do next: the percentage of eligible jobs with an on-time digital checklist or valid digital hold during weeks three and four after rewards end. Set the decision rule before launch. At 70% or higher, keep the workflow as standard. At 50–69%, fix the remaining friction and recheck. Below 50%, retire the incentive and revisit the workflow with technicians and the union.

**What could go wrong.** The milestone can encourage rushed checks, inaccurate start times or pressure to clear an unsafe job. Use the existing safety audit to check completion quality and timestamp discrepancies. Pause rewards if the share of audited checklists meeting the existing quality standard drops by five percentage points from the two-week baseline, or immediately if an audit finds a missed critical hazard or evidence of pressure to bypass a hold. Review any pause with the union and safety lead. Have one coordinator spend up to an hour a week reviewing the team report, correcting counts and checking audit results.
