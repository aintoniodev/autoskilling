## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward a correct knowledge decision on a resolved ticket.** At launch, announce one reward: an hour of paid time off for agents who handle at least 80% of their resolved tickets correctly during their four-week window. A ticket counts once when the agent links an article that supports the resolution, publishes a reusable article for a gap, or records a valid reason no article is needed. Merge duplicate tickets under the original. A reopened ticket can earn credit once, after its final resolution. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged. Give agents a rolling four-week goal based on their own caseload so a missed shift or week never resets progress.

**Make the workflow easier before launch.** Ask agents across queues which searches fail, which cases warrant an article, and whether paid time off is a reward they can realistically use. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected practice time. Put suggested articles and a one-click link in the resolution screen, prefill article drafts with non-sensitive ticket details, and keep a visible “No article needed” option with a short reason. Show agents which changes came from their feedback. Schedule earned time off around queue coverage.

**Use the resolution record.** Read the link, published article ID, or exception reason from the ticket system. Use the existing support QA review to spot-check relevance, usefulness, and valid exceptions. Deduct credit for irrelevant links, duplicate or thin articles, and unsupported exceptions. Add an exception ticket to the existing review sample when there is one. The support ops lead corrects disputed counts and runs the pilot from one report in under an hour a week. Pause new reward accrual if two consecutive weekly QA samples find incorrect knowledge decisions on more than 10% of reviewed tickets, or if rolling two-week CSAT falls five percentage points below the pre-pilot baseline with at least 30 survey responses. Review the cause before restarting.

**Show agents their own progress.** After each resolution, confirm the recorded action and show progress toward the 80% goal. Put a single team completion rate in the existing support huddle or dashboard. Managers see team totals only. Individual incentive data stays out of performance reviews. The support ops lead confirms earned time off privately.

**Stagger the start and set the end date.** Give all teams the workflow fixes in weeks 1–2. Randomly choose half the teams to earn rewards in weeks 3–6. The other half earns rewards in weeks 5–8. Give both groups four weeks and compare their results in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds. Stop rewards after week 8 and leave the resolution workflow in place.

**Decide in week 12.** Use one decision metric: the percentage of tickets that warranted knowledge reuse or capture and received a relevant link or a reusable new article. Estimate it through the existing QA sample, using the same criteria at baseline and follow-up. The reported 12% is a starting reference because its denominator may include tickets that never needed an article. Keep the workflow as configured if the week 12 rate is at least 20 percentage points above the comparable baseline, revise it if the gain is 5 to under 20 points, and drop the pilot configuration if the gain is under 5 points.

**Watch for shortcuts.** Agents may link the first search result, publish near-duplicates, overuse exceptions, or delay closing tickets to protect CSAT. Include links, new articles, and exceptions in QA, and watch ticket age and reopen rates in the existing queue report. If legitimate article writing is slowing replies, give agents protected time to publish before resuming rewards.
