Call it **One Working Day for Code Review**. Give the 25 engineers one promise to make to each other: every review-ready PR gets a substantive first response within one working day.

**Make reviews easier to pick up.** Before launch, give five engineers from different parts of the team 30 minutes of paid work time to identify what stalls reviews and confirm they’d value the proposed reward. Use the existing PR queue to assign one reviewer and a backup, put the oldest requests first, and reserve review time in the workday. Route specialist changes to a qualified reviewer. Give everyone paid time to try the workflow on a real PR, then post the changes made in response to their feedback.

**Count one response per PR.** “A PR counts once when an assigned reviewer approves it after reviewing the change, requests specific changes, or documents a concrete blocker the author can act on, within one working day of the PR becoming ready for review.” Use the PR system’s ready timestamp and review record. One working day means eight hours on the team’s published coverage calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. Reassignment and reopening keep the original clock. Acknowledgments, bot activity, and author replies earn no credit. A documented review exemption counts only when existing repository policy explicitly permits it.

**Give the team one reward.** Announce at launch that each review group earns a paid half-day off for every member if at least **80% of eligible PRs receive a qualifying response within one working day** during its four reward weeks. The engineering lead announces the result at an existing team meeting and arranges staggered time off so review coverage holds. The milestone has no individual cutoff. After a missed week or an absence, the next assigned review is a fresh chance to contribute.

**Show progress where engineers already work.** Send the reviewer a private confirmation in the existing PR notification channel, such as “Review counted for PR #142 in 5 working hours.” Give each engineer a private weekly goal based on their expected review volume and case mix, such as two on-time responses in a week with two complex assignments. Pin one aggregate view in the team channel showing the number on time, the number due, and progress toward 80%. Managers see group totals only. Keep individual counts out of performance reviews. The review rota owner corrects disputed counts from the PR record and spends no more than an hour a week on the programme.

**Run it for six weeks and check what lasts.** Recalculate the prior four weeks using the same on-time measure, since the current 2.6-day median cannot establish the baseline percentage. Give both groups the workflow fixes on day one. Use two existing review groups if available, or form two groups with comparable volume and specialist coverage. Randomly choose one to earn the reward in weeks 1–4 and the other in weeks 3–6. Use the first two weeks to estimate what the reward adds and record cross-group coverage when interpreting the difference. Count each eligible PR against the group responsible when it became ready. Include PRs whose deadline has passed without a reply in the denominator, and let the final deadlines mature before settling rewards. End prizes after week 6 and keep assignment, backup coverage, and the oldest-first queue as defaults. Four weeks later, use the on-time percentage to decide: **keep** the workflow at 80% or better, **change** the review routing or capacity if it improved from baseline but fell short of 80%, or **drop** the added workflow if it returned to baseline or worse.

**Pause for quality.** The quickest way to earn credit may become a shallow approval or a vague blocker. Use the team’s existing review-quality checks or routine defect triage. If they identify two PRs within a rolling two-week window that needed rework because the first review missed a required check, pause reward accrual and fix the cause. Watch for easy PRs jumping the queue and specialist work piling up. The rota owner uses the same queue to move coverage before deadlines expire.
