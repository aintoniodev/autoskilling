## Knowledge-base adoption rally

**Reward one useful knowledge-base decision per resolved ticket.** The rule agents hear at launch is “You earn one credit when you resolve a ticket with a relevant article linked, a new article approved, or a valid reason that no article is needed.” A valid exception covers cases such as duplicates or issues with no reusable guidance. A missing article is a reason to write one when the answer would help someone else. Count each ticket ID once, even if it reopens or has several links. Credit a new article back to the ticket when it passes the existing publishing review.

**Fix the workflow before launch.** Ask agents from each queue what makes finding, linking or writing an article slow, and confirm they want the proposed reward. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice. Put article suggestions and a prefilled draft action in the ticket resolution screen, with a short reason field for exceptions. Share the fixes in the support channel so agents can see what changed based on their feedback.

**Make the reward visible.** Announce at launch that everyone on a team that reaches its four-week target earns one hour of paid time off. Set each team’s target at 30% of eligible resolutions with a useful article, up from the reported 12% link-or-write rate, and check that it is reachable against the prelaunch baseline. Agents get a private confirmation at resolution or article approval and a personal goal sized to their ticket volume and case mix. The support channel shows one team-level progress figure. Managers see team totals only, individual data stays out of performance reviews, and an absence leaves agents free to pick up with the next ticket. The support lead presents the reward in the team huddle and schedules the hour around coverage.

**Use the ticket system and existing QA.** The ticket’s resolution record supplies the count. The existing QA sample checks whether links answer the question, new articles are accurate and reusable, and exceptions are valid. Valid exceptions earn completion credit and are excluded from the team’s article-rate denominator. The support lead corrects disputed credits and spends up to an hour a week reviewing the team view, disputes and QA results. Pause reward qualification if more than 10% of the week’s sampled links, articles or exceptions fail QA, or if team CSAT falls five percentage points below its prelaunch baseline once it meets the existing minimum survey count. Run the rally reward outside the CSAT bonus formula.

**Run the rally for six weeks, with four reward weeks per team.** Give all teams the workflow improvements first and measure a two-week baseline under the new workflow. Randomly assign half the teams to start earning rewards in week one and half in week three. Compare the groups during the first two weeks to estimate what the reward adds. Close each team’s reward window after four weeks and keep the resolution workflow in place.

**Decide whether to keep, change or drop the rally.** Use one decision metric, the share of eligible resolved tickets with a relevant linked or approved new article, estimated from the existing QA sample. Measure it again in the four weeks after the last rewards end. Keep the rally as an option for future rollouts if it adds at least five percentage points during the staggered start and the post-reward rate stays at or above 30%. Change the design if the reward adds lift but adoption fades, or if the sample is too small to judge. Drop the reward if the measured lift is under five points with adequate data.

**Watch for rushed closures, irrelevant links, duplicate articles and inflated exceptions.** QA checks those failure modes using the same ticket sample. If drafting starts to delay replies, use protected time for reusable articles and adjust the workflow before restarting rewards.
