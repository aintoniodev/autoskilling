**Reward observed hand hygiene.** Launch with one rule: “Each ward earns a hospital-paid meal when its routine hand-hygiene audit reaches the ward’s published target.” Count each opportunity the observer records as compliant once, using the hospital’s existing audit criteria and denominator. Kiosk entries carry no points. Observers continue their usual rounds and give the programme lead ward totals only. Set each ward’s target before launch at five percentage points above its baseline from the last two monthly audits, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Deliver the meal in person, with paid coverage so night and weekend staff can take part.

**Make logging easy before launch.** Give nurses from all three wards, including night and weekend staff, paid time to try the kiosk. Ask where logging interrupts care, whether the kiosk is in the right place, and whether the meal is worth earning. Fix the barriers they identify and tell each ward what changed. Put the log action first, prefill the ward and shift where the system can do so reliably, and show a private “Saved” confirmation after each entry. Nurses log when care allows. If the kiosk is down or a nurse cannot reliably recall a moment, they leave it unrecorded.

**Show ward progress.** Use the existing ward huddle board for the latest audited compliance rate, the target, and the date of the next audit. Nurses can see their own submissions privately. Managers get ward totals only, with no individual drill-down or use in performance reviews. There are no rankings or streaks. Name one infection-prevention lead to update the board, correct disputed counts, and handle exceptions in under an hour a week.

**Run it for eight weeks.** Give all three wards the kiosk fixes and training together. Randomly choose one ward to start the meal offer in week one and start the other two in week three. Each ward can earn the meal once, based on its first full routine monthly audit after its offer starts. Use the two-week stagger to look for a change in kiosk uptake when the offer begins. With three wards and monthly audits, treat the comparison as a signal. End the offer in week eight and keep the kiosk shortcut in place.

**Decide whether to keep the workflow.** Before launch, use **the percentage of eligible nurse shifts with at least one saved kiosk entry** as the adoption measure. Calculate it from aggregate shift and kiosk counts, counting each shift once and excluding shifts when the kiosk was unavailable. Set a pilot target of 80% per ward and check it again four weeks after the offer ends. Keep the workflow if wards sustain the target and audited compliance holds. If uptake falls short, fix the documented workflow barriers and recheck. Drop the kiosk requirement if it continues to add work with little sustained use. Keep routine audits throughout.

**Pause if care or audit quality suffers.** The lead pauses the offer on an affected ward if audited compliance drops five percentage points below baseline, nurses report that logging delayed care, or audit coverage falls below the hospital’s usual minimum. Check for missing shifts or skewed observation coverage before awarding a meal. A shift with one entry shows that someone used the kiosk. The monthly audit remains the measure of hand-hygiene compliance.
