# Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Give technicians from different shifts, routes, and coverage areas paid time to try the app with their union reps. Ask where it slows them down, what makes it hard to record a safety hold, and whether they’d value 30 minutes of paid time off as the reward. Before launch, test offline completion and sync in the field, prefill job details, and make the checklist the first step when technicians open a job. Show crews what changed based on their feedback. Keep the approved fallback available when the app fails.

**Use one rule.** “Before starting each job visit, submit one accurate digital safety checklist for the visit. If it’s unsafe to start, record the reason and put the job on hold. Either outcome counts once.”

**Measure the share of eligible visits with a valid pre-job checklist.** Use dispatch’s visit ID and the app’s submission record. Include visits that went ahead and visits held for safety. Exclude visits canceled before the technician arrived. For jobs that proceed, compare the checklist completion time with the existing work-start record. For holds, use the checklist and the recorded hold. Offline entries retain their original completion time and count after syncing. An approved fallback protects the technician and gets recorded as an app failure if the checklist couldn’t be completed digitally. Keep those visits in the denominator so outages show up in the result. The pilot owner resolves missing or disputed credit through the existing support channel.

**Give crews one reachable milestone.** Announce at launch that everyone on a crew earns 30 minutes of paid time off if the crew reaches 70% across its six reward weeks. Agree on the reward and coverage with union reps before announcing it. Confirm each credited visit in the app, with a pending-sync status when offline. Show technicians their own progress against a private goal based on their assigned visits and case mix. Show managers and crews only crew totals in the existing weekly briefing. Combine small groups where totals could identify an individual. Keep incentive data out of performance reviews and give managers no individual drill-down. A missed week leaves the next visit available to count. At the end of each crew’s window, the supervisor announces the earned time at a huddle and schedules it on paid shifts.

**Stagger the launch and end it on schedule.** Give everyone the workflow fixes and paid practice first. Record two weeks of adoption with the improved app, then pair comparable crews by volume and adoption and randomly start one crew in each pair two weeks earlier. Each crew gets six reward weeks. Compare the change in valid completion rates during the two weeks when only the early crews can earn the reward. Check the same rate four weeks after each crew’s reward ends. Set the decision rule up front. If the post-reward rate is at least 70%, keep the checklist as the default and retire the reward. Below 70%, revise the workflow if missed visits cluster around a fixable barrier. Consider another time-limited reward test only if the staggered launch showed at least a 10 percentage point lift. Otherwise drop the incentive and continue the standard safety process.

**Watch for rushed checks and pressure to start unsafe jobs.** Use existing safety spot checks to catch copied answers, implausibly fast completions, and holds that were skipped or cleared to maintain progress. Pause rewards immediately if a check finds work started despite a required hold. Also pause if the share of audited checklists with a material error rises five percentage points above the pre-reward baseline. The safety lead and union rep review the cause before rewards resume. Keep the weekly administration with one pilot owner, using the existing dispatch report and safety review.
