## One working day for a useful code review

**Fix the queue first.** In week 1, ask five engineers across time zones and on-call rotations which reviews get stuck, what makes them hard to pick up, and whether two hours of paid time off is a reward they’d want. Give everyone a protected 30-minute walkthrough during work hours. For the first two weeks, give ready PRs a named reviewer and backup, protect a daily review slot, and put the oldest ready requests first in the queue engineers already use. Post the fixes in the team channel and credit the people who raised them. Start the reward only once the team has reliable review coverage.

**Count one useful answer per PR.** The rule at launch is **“A ready PR counts once when a requested reviewer submits a substantive review within eight working hours.”** Use the existing ready-for-review, reviewer-request, and submitted-review events. Start the clock when the PR is both ready and assigned, using the assigned review group’s published working hours and holiday calendar. A reasoned approval, actionable request for changes, or legitimate hold with a specific blocker and next step counts. A bot response or “I’ll look later” earns no credit. Reassignment and re-requests leave the original clock running. Calculate the rate over all eligible PRs whose deadline has passed, including unanswered ones. The review lead corrects missed or misclassified events through a private message.

**Give people feedback and one shared reward.** Each qualifying review triggers a private confirmation with the elapsed working time. Engineers see their own progress toward a weekly goal sized to their assigned volume, availability, and case mix. A missed week has no penalty. Post one team view in the existing engineering channel with the on-time rate and eligible PR count. Managers get that same team view. Keep individual figures out of performance reviews and skip rankings. Announce the reward at launch: everyone in a review group that reaches **80% on time across its four reward weeks** gets **two hours of paid time off**, scheduled with coverage. The engineering lead announces the award in the regular team meeting and thanks reviewers for specific helpful work.

**Run it for eight weeks, then check whether it stuck.** Use two stable review groups with comparable PR volume, case mix, and working-hour coverage. Give both the queue fixes in weeks 1–2. Randomly choose one group to start earning in weeks 3–6 and the other in weeks 5–8. Compare their on-time rates in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds, treating the result as directional with only two groups. Make oldest-first ordering and backup routing the default before either group finishes. In week 12, use the on-time rate over the four prize-free weeks to decide: **80% or higher** keeps the workflow, **60% to under 80%** changes routing or coverage, and **under 60%** drops the setup and triggers a fresh look at where reviews wait. The prize ends after week 8.

**Watch for rushed approvals, easy PRs getting picked first, and work spilling past hours.** Use the team’s normal maintainer follow-ups and incident reviews to catch shallow approvals. Pause reward accrual for an affected group if two reviews credited in a rolling two-week window are flagged through those channels for missing a required check. The review lead clears the issue with maintainers before restarting. Keep older and complex PRs visible at the top of the queue, and have the backup take over when the assigned reviewer is out. Automate the count and weekly post so one review lead can handle corrections and quality flags in under an hour a week.
