## One-working-day code reviews

Run a six-week pilot. Give each review request a clear owner and give reviewers protected time to answer. Offer one team reward for getting useful answers back within a working day.

**Fix the queue first.** In week 1, ask a few authors and reviewers what makes reviews wait and whether a paid team lunch is worth earning. Use their feedback to fix routing, interruptions, or oversized PRs before the reward starts. Give the rotating reviewer a protected 30-minute block each workday and name a backup for absences. Make the review queue the default view for whoever is on rotation. Tell the team what changed based on their feedback.

**Count one outcome per PR.** “A PR counts when it receives its first substantive review within eight working hours of being marked ready and requesting review.” An approval after review, a change request with a concrete next step, or a documented hold with a reason and next step qualifies. Acknowledgments and emoji reactions do not. Use timestamps from the existing PR system and the team’s working calendar, excluding weekends and holidays. Reassignments and re-requests keep the original clock. Include overdue unanswered PRs in the denominator so leaving them open cannot improve the score.

**Give people feedback where they already work.** After a qualifying review, send the reviewer a private confirmation with the elapsed working time. Let each engineer see their own results and choose a reachable goal for their review load, such as answering their next two assigned small PRs on time. Post one weekly update in the engineering channel showing the team’s on-time percentage, the number of eligible PRs, and progress toward 80%. The pilot gives managers aggregate results only and stays out of performance reviews. Anyone who misses a week picks up with their next assigned PR. The engineering lead fixes disputed counts privately and owns the pilot in under an hour a week.

**Announce the reward and its end at launch.** Put the queue fixes in place for everyone in week 1. Split the review rotation into two groups with a similar mix of reviewers and PRs. Start the reward in week 2 for one group and week 4 for the other, with three reward weeks each. If a group answers at least 80% of its eligible PRs on time during its window, the engineering lead hosts a paid lunch during working hours for the whole group. Use weeks 2 and 3 to see whether the reward adds anything once both groups have the queue fixes. With 25 engineers, treat the comparison as directional.

**Watch for rushed reviews and cherry-picking.** Keep ownership on the rotation so reviewers cannot improve the score by picking easy PRs. During the existing engineering retro, spot-check five counted reviews across simple and complex PRs. If two lack evidence of a substantive review, pause reward accrual, correct the counts, and address the cause before resuming. Keep normal merge checks in force. If complex PRs still wait, use the retro to adjust routing or protected review time.

**Decide four weeks after prizes stop.** Leave the queue default and review rotation in place through the follow-up. Use the percentage of eligible PRs answered within one working day over those four weeks as the decision metric. At **80% or higher**, keep the workflow. At **60–79%**, adjust routing or capacity and recheck in four weeks. Below **60%**, retire the pilot setup and revisit the bottleneck with the team. The prizes end on schedule in every case.
