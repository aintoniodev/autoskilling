## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Set up with technicians and union stewards.** Use a paid session with technicians from different shifts, job types and coverage areas to walk through the checklist and confirm the reward. Before launch, fix slow loading, missing job details and offline access. Give everyone paid practice time and publish what changed in response to their feedback. Put the checklist first on the assigned job screen, prefill known job details, and keep hazard answers blank for the technician to complete.

**Launch rule.** “Your crew earns one hour of paid time off per technician when it completes the app safety checklist before work starts on at least 75% of eligible jobs during its four-week reward window. A properly recorded safety hold counts.”

**Count one completion per job.** Use dispatch records for the denominator and match the app’s checklist completion time to the existing job-start record. For a safety hold, match it to the recorded hold and verify that work stayed paused until clearance. Use the time the checklist was saved on the device for offline submissions, and reconcile them after sync. Exclude jobs cancelled before the technician begins the on-site assessment. If the app fails, use the approved paper procedure before starting work. Centrally verified outages are excluded from reward scoring and remain in the digital adoption measure. Supervisors continue to enforce the safety procedure on every job.

**Show technicians their own progress and the crew’s total.** After saving, show “Checklist recorded,” “Saved offline, sync pending,” or “Safety hold recorded,” as applicable. Give each technician a private count and a weekly goal based on the eligible jobs they actually handle, with the next job always a fresh chance to contribute. Show crew progress toward 75% in the existing weekly safety huddle. Managers see crew totals only. Agree with the union on who can reconcile disputed records and how long pilot data is retained. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews and discipline.

**Run the rewards in two waves.** Give every crew the app fixes and practice time, then measure a common two-week baseline. Pair crews with similar job mix and coverage, then randomly assign one crew in each pair to start in week 1 and the other in week 3. Give each crew four reward weeks. Compare the change in digital completion during the first two weeks, when only the early crews have rewards. At the next huddle after a crew reaches the milestone, the operations lead confirms the paid hour in person. Technicians book it with their supervisor on paid time, with coverage arranged.

**Use one number for the decision.** Measure the share of eligible jobs with a timely digital checklist or qualifying digital safety hold, including jobs that needed the paper fallback in the denominator. Recheck it for each crew in the fourth week after its rewards end. At **80% or more**, keep the app workflow and end the prize. At **60–79%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **60%**, retire this incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The pre-job safety requirement continues throughout.

**Watch for rushed answers, backdated records and pressure to clear holds.** The safety lead uses existing audits to check checklist accuracy and keeps control of the hold procedure. Pause rewards if the audit pass rate drops five percentage points below baseline or if a confirmed case of backdating or pressure to bypass a hold occurs. Assign one safety coordinator up to an hour a week to reconcile exceptions, correct disputed counts privately and post the crew totals.
