### Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Make logging fit the shift.** Give nurses from day, night and weekend shifts 15 minutes of paid time to try the kiosk and identify where logging adds walking, queues or interruptions. Use their feedback to place kiosks on the existing workflow, open directly to a one-tap entry and confirm each save with “Hand-hygiene moment recorded.” Post the fixes in the shift huddle. Start the reward only once staff can log safely during routine care.

**Reward one verified outcome.** Announce at launch: “Your ward earns a catered meal across all shifts when at least 80% of sampled hand-hygiene moments that nurses complete and can safely log are verified in the kiosk, with hand-hygiene compliance holding steady.” Check that the target is reachable against each ward’s baseline and agree any adjustment before announcing it. The nursing director hosts the meal and thanks the ward in person at shift handovers.

**Use the monthly audit.** In a sample of existing observation sessions spread across shifts, the observer adds one tick when they see a completed hand-hygiene action and its corresponding kiosk confirmation. Credit each opportunity once. A repeat tap adds nothing. For urgent care or kiosk downtime, the observer marks logging excused and excludes the opportunity from the logging rate. The usual infection-prevention audit still measures hand-hygiene actions against all observed opportunities. Before launch, agree a minimum sample and check that observers can verify entries without interrupting care. **Show ward progress in the usual huddle.** Share the ward’s verified percentage, sample size and target on its existing huddle board. Nurses get immediate confirmation at the kiosk. Managers get ward totals only. Collect no staff or patient identifiers for the pilot, and keep the data out of performance reviews. The infection-prevention lead handles disputed counts and updates the board within an hour a week outside routine audit work. A missed shift leaves the next eligible moment as a fresh chance to contribute.

**Run one short cycle.** Give all wards the workflow fixes before the baseline audit. Randomly select one ward to start the reward two weeks before the other two, with the next monthly audit inside that two-week gap. Use that comparison as an early signal, since three wards give a limited estimate of the reward’s effect. End rewards for all three wards at the following monthly audit and keep the one-tap workflow in place. At the next reward-free audit, keep the workflow if the verified logging rate still meets the agreed target and compliance is steady. If logging improves over baseline but misses the target, fix the remaining workflow friction. If it returns to baseline or worse, drop the kiosk rollout and continue routine audits.

**Pause for harm or unreliable counts.** Pause a ward’s reward if audited hand-hygiene compliance falls five percentage points below baseline or if staff report that logging delayed care. Review high rates of excused entries, duplicate taps or gains seen only during observation before awarding the meal. If the sample is too small, hold the decision for the next routine audit.
