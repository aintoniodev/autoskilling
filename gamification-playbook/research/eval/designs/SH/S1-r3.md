## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

The goal is to raise verified digital checklist completion before job start from about 35% to at least 80%, and sustain it after rewards end. The existing safety procedure applies to every job throughout the pilot.

### Fix the workflow first

In the first two weeks, give technicians from different shifts and coverage areas paid time to test the app with union stewards, dispatch and safety. Fix the biggest barriers they find, such as poor offline support, missing job details or pressure to start immediately. Share what changed in the regular crew briefing. Before rewards begin, make the prefilled checklist the first action on the mobile job card and agree with union representatives on the counting rule, reward and data access.

### Reward and measurement

**Count one checklist per job when a technician submits it in the app before work starts.** Use the existing dispatch job ID and job-start record. The denominator is all started jobs that require a checklist, including those completed on paper or where the app failed. Exclude cancellations before work begins. Use the original submission time for offline records if the app preserves it reliably. Verify that the existing start record reflects when work begins before tying rewards to it. If it cannot establish the order reliably, fix the workflow before launching rewards.

A checklist that identifies a hazard qualifies once the hazard is resolved and the job safely starts. If the job stays on hold or is cancelled, it stays outside the adoption rate. Downtime and paper fallback count as gaps in digital adoption and carry no individual penalty. Before launch, have technicians and stewards walk through a duplicate, a late submission, an offline sync, an invalidated submission, a paper fallback and a stop-work case.

Each team that reaches **75% during its four-week reward window** earns one hour of paid time per technician, scheduled around coverage. Every team can earn it. Give each technician a private goal based on their expected job volume, with progress based on jobs they actually start. A missed week or an absence leaves the next eligible job available.

### What people see

The app immediately confirms that the checklist was saved, flags a pending offline sync and confirms when the job counts. Technicians see their own progress. Managers and crews see team totals in the existing weekly briefing, with small groups combined where a total would identify an individual. There is no named leaderboard, manager drill-down or use of pilot data in performance reviews. Technicians can bring a disputed count to the pilot coordinator, who checks the source record and corrects the tally.

### Timing and ending

After the two-week setup, pair comparable teams by job mix and baseline adoption. Randomly assign one from each pair to rewards in weeks 3–6 and the other to weeks 7–10. All teams get the workflow fixes and feedback from week 3. Compare the groups in weeks 3–6 to estimate what the reward adds. At the end of each team’s window, award any earned paid time and end the reward. Keep the checklist first on the job card.

Use the **verified pre-start completion rate across all teams in weeks 11–14** to decide what happens next. At 80% or higher, keep the default workflow and end the campaign. At 60–79%, fix the most common remaining barrier and remeasure. Below 60%, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards before trying another campaign.

### What could go wrong

- **People rush, backfill or tick through the checklist.** Deduplicate by job ID and give late or invalidated submissions no credit. Each week, the pilot coordinator checks eight records drawn across teams, including flagged exceptions, against existing safety and dispatch records. Two material quality failures in the sample, or one verified case of work starting with an unresolved critical hazard, pause reward accrual. The safety lead decides when to resume after reviewing the cause with a union steward. The coordinator corrects affected counts.
- **Poor coverage or job mix makes the target unfair.** Review digital failures by crew and job type using existing records. If a verified outage materially affects a team’s opportunity to earn the reward, repeat the affected reward period once service is restored. Keep those jobs in the adoption measure so the failure stays visible.
- **Crew pressure discourages hazard reporting.** Reinforce stop-work authority at launch and in briefings. Give technicians a private route through their steward to flag pressure or disputed counts.

Use one existing weekly briefing and one coordinator, with a target of under an hour a week for tallying, sampling and routine corrections.
