## Knowledge base adoption pilot

**Reward one useful knowledge contribution per resolved ticket.** The rule agents see is “Link an approved article that helps resolve the ticket, or submit a new article that gets approved and linked to it. Each ticket counts once.”

Before launch, ask six agents across shifts and queues to walk through a recent ticket. Give each agent 30 minutes of paid practice time. Fix the biggest friction they identify, such as poor search results or a slow approval queue, and tell the team what changed. Give a rotating knowledge-base reviewer protected time to handle drafts so agents can resolve tickets while articles are under review.

**Count from the existing ticket and article records.** The denominator is unique resolved tickets, excluding spam and duplicate tickets already marked as such. A ticket handled the old way stays in the denominator. Multiple links earn one credit. If a ticket reopens or its qualifying link is removed, reverse the credit and reassess it on final resolution. Attribute an approved draft to the ticket’s resolution date. Allow five business days after each reward window for reviews and corrections, and give the reviewer that same deadline to resolve disputes. Before launch, replay a duplicate, a reopened ticket, an old-way resolution, and a late approval against the rule.

**Show progress where agents work.** Confirm credit in the ticket when a link qualifies. Show drafts as pending and confirm credit on approval. Each agent sees their own count and a personal target of one qualifying contribution for every two tickets they resolve. A missed week carries no penalty. Post team totals weekly in the existing support channel. Managers see team totals only, and pilot counts stay out of performance reviews. Each team earns a meal worth up to $20 per person when it reaches 50% coverage over its four-week reward window and clears the quality checks. Fund it separately from the existing CSAT bonus.

**Run staggered four-week reward windows.** Give all teams two weeks with the workflow fixes and progress feedback before rewards start. Randomly assign existing teams to start in weeks 3, 5, or 7. Compare teams earning rewards with teams still waiting during the same weeks, using each team’s two-week starting rate alongside the org’s current 12%. By the end of week 10, make article search the first step in the resolution panel and prefill it with the ticket topic. Keep resolution available for cases where linking is inappropriate. Rewards end after week 10, with four more weeks of measurement.

**Catch the ways this can go wrong.** Agents could attach generic articles, split tickets, rush closure, or avoid difficult cases to protect CSAT. The pilot owner reviews eight randomly sampled credited tickets each week across teams. Pause reward payouts if fewer than seven of eight articles materially helped resolve the issue, or if pooled CSAT falls five percentage points below the pre-reward baseline over two weeks with at least 30 survey responses. Review reopen rates and survey response volume alongside CSAT. Treat a smaller response sample as inconclusive. The pilot owner decides when to resume payouts after the knowledge-base reviewer fixes bad links or credits and the relevant quality measure recovers. Budget under an hour a week for pilot reporting, sampling, and disputes.

**Decide whether the habit stuck.** Use the org-wide share of resolved tickets with a qualifying contribution in the four prize-free weeks. At 50% or higher with quality checks clear, keep the workflow and feedback. At 30–49%, fix the largest remaining friction and remeasure. Below 30%, retire the incentive and revisit the workflow before trying another reward. An unresolved quality pause sends the pilot to workflow review at any adoption rate.
