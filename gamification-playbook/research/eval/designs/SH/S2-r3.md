# One working day for code review

Give authors a substantive first review within one working day. Offer the 25 engineers a modest team reward for meeting that service standard while keeping review quality steady.

### Fix the queue first

Ask five engineers, including frequent authors and reviewers, to walk through delayed PRs. Use the first two weeks to fix the bottlenecks they identify. Set up a rotating primary reviewer and backup for each existing ownership group, protect 30 minutes of review time each working day, and route ready PRs to the rotation through the existing review-request workflow. Put unreviewed PRs oldest first in the queue engineers already use. Give both groups these changes on day one and post a short summary of what engineers raised and what changed.

### What earns the reward

**Each ready PR counts once when a reviewer submits an approval, a request for changes, or a review that identifies a concrete blocker and the next action within one working day.**

Use the existing PR and submitted-review timestamps. The clock starts when a human-authored PR first becomes ready for review. One working day means eight hours on the owning group’s published working calendar, excluding weekends and team holidays. A PR ready at 3 p.m. is due at 3 p.m. the next working day on a 9–5 calendar. The backup covers individual absences.

Before launch, walk through a duplicate review, a reopened PR, a return to draft, and feedback given in chat. Duplicate reviews add nothing, and reopening or toggling draft leaves the original clock intact. Feedback given elsewhere counts once the reviewer records the decision or blocker in a submitted PR review. Exclude genuine withdrawals before the deadline and check those exclusions in the weekly sample.

Each ownership group earns a team lunch or equivalent meal credit once it reaches **80% on time over two consecutive weeks combined**, with at least 10 eligible PRs. If volume is lower, use the full four-week reward window. Pending PRs enter the denominator when their deadline passes. A late PR remains late, and the next PR is a fresh chance to contribute.

### What engineers see

Use the existing PR notification to tell the author and reviewer when the first review landed and whether it met the deadline. Each reviewer sees their own assigned queue and due times. Their practical goal is to clear reviews due during their rotation or hand them to the backup before they’re due. Post the group’s weekly result as a count and percentage in its usual engineering channel. Managers receive group totals. Keep individual figures out of rankings and performance reviews.

### Timing and ending

Recalculate the previous four weeks using the same clock and eligibility rules. Keep the reported 2.6-day median as context and use **the percentage of eligible PRs with a substantive first review within one working day** as the decision metric.

After two weeks of shared queue fixes and feedback, run the reward for four weeks in one ownership group. Start the other group two weeks later, giving both the same four-week window. Compare their results during the stagger to see whether the reward appears to add anything beyond the queue changes. Keep the rotation, protected time and oldest-first queue as defaults when rewards end.

Four weeks after the last reward window, use the same measure across the team. **Keep** the operating changes at 80% or higher. **Change** the routing or coverage if the rate is below 80% but above baseline. **Drop** the incentive programme if the rate is at or below baseline and revisit what holds up reviews.

### What could go wrong

- **Rubber-stamp approvals or vague blockers.** The programme owner samples five counted reviews a week, including at least one approval and one blocker when available. A review fails if it has no actionable substance where needed or misses a material issue apparent in the diff. Pause the reward if two of five fail. The engineering lead decides when to resume after the cause is addressed.
- **Easy PRs get attention while complex ones wait.** Check the oldest overdue PRs in the weekly pass. The primary reviewer can bring in a specialist, and the owning group keeps responsibility for the deadline.
- **Wrong counts or resentment about tracking.** Engineers can flag a count privately with the PR link. The programme owner corrects the aggregate and explains the decision to the person who raised it. Use existing PR events and reserve 45 minutes a week for the sample, corrections and group update.
