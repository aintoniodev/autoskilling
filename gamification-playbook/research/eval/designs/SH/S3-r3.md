## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for better hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one modest reward during its four-week incentive window: a hospital-funded meal with equivalent options for day, night and weekend staff. The ward earns it if its monthly observer audit reaches its pre-agreed target. Set the target at five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. Every ward can earn the reward independently. Kiosk entries earn zero points.

**Make the kiosk worth using.** Before launch, give nurses from each ward and shift paid time to try the workflow and identify where it adds trips, queues or interruptions. Fix the problems they raise and post the fixes at shift huddles. Make the kiosk open directly to a one-tap entry screen with the ward prefilled, an immediate “Entry saved” confirmation and a short undo option. Log only after hand hygiene and when patient care permits. If the kiosk is unavailable or care takes priority, skip the entry. Keep the ward out of the reward phase if logging still routinely interrupts care.

**Measure practice and adoption separately.** Infection Prevention uses the existing monthly audit to calculate the percentage of observed nursing opportunities with hand hygiene performed. Count each opportunity once, even when several indications apply. Establish each ward’s baseline from its last three comparable audits. Agree on minimum sample size and shift coverage before launch, and use comparable sampling across the pilot. An undersampled audit leaves the reward pending until the next valid audit. Use weekly ward-level kiosk totals and brief huddle feedback to spot adoption problems. Kiosk totals cannot establish how many opportunities occurred or how many were logged. [WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

**Show teams what they can act on.** At the usual ward huddle or staff board, share the latest audit result with its sample size, the ward’s target and one workflow fix or follow-up. Keep kiosk entries free of staff and patient identifiers. Managers see ward totals. Exclude pilot data from performance reviews. Give staff a named ward contact for reporting problems privately. Missed entries and absences carry no penalty.

**Run for five months.** In month one, give all three wards the workflow fixes, paid practice and audit feedback. Randomly assign the wards to incentive windows in months two, three and four, one ward per month. Keep feedback running throughout. The stagger gives an early read on whether the reward adds anything beyond the fixes, though three wards and monthly sampling limit what it can establish. In month five, check the last ward at least four weeks after its reward window and review the earlier wards’ follow-up audits. Use **observed compliance against each ward’s target** to decide what to keep. If the gains hold, keep the kiosk workflow and audit feedback and let the prize expire. If gains fade, revise the workflow based on what nurses report. If safe logging still adds work without helping the ward act on a problem, drop the kiosk step and continue the audits.

**Handle failure before launch.** Walk through a duplicate tap, an undone entry, a kiosk outage and correctly performed hand hygiene with no kiosk entry. Only the observed opportunity affects the audit score. To reduce the risk of staff improving mainly when watched, sample across shifts and vary observation times within the existing protocol. Infection Prevention pauses rewards and investigates if a valid audit falls five percentage points or more below baseline or a nurse reports a care delay linked to logging. Infection Prevention resolves audit disputes, and the ward contact routes kiosk fixes to the system owner. Use existing huddles and audit reports to keep routine administration under an hour a week.
