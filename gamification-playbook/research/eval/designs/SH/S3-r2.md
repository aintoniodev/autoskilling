### Reward

Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, with equivalent options for night and weekend staff. The rule nurses hear is **“Your ward earns a meal when its monthly hand-hygiene audit improves by five percentage points from baseline or reaches 95%.”** Wards earn the reward independently. Everyone on a qualifying ward is included, even if they missed a week or never used the kiosk.

The reward follows hand hygiene performed during patient care. A kiosk entry is a self-report and earns zero points. Nurses get an immediate **“Entry saved”** confirmation and an Undo button. This gives them a reason to trust the kiosk without giving them a reason to inflate its counts.

### Setup and measurement

Start with four weeks of kiosk use and feedback on all three wards. Ask nurses from day, night, and weekend shifts to try the workflow on paid time. Fix placement, queues, and slow entry before starting rewards. Set the ward automatically, leave the hygiene moment for the nurse to select, and agree on kiosk cleaning with Infection Prevention. When care is urgent or the kiosk is unavailable, staff deal with the clinical need first and skip the entry if necessary.

- **Reward measure.** Use the existing monthly audit. Divide observed hygiene actions performed by observed opportunities, counting each opportunity once. Set each ward’s baseline from its three preceding comparable audits. Infection Prevention fixes the minimum sample and shift coverage before launch. If an audit falls short, carry eligibility into the next adequate audit. Keep the sampling method consistent and report the denominator alongside the rate. Behavior can change when staff know they’re being observed. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)
- **Adoption measure.** Once a month, use a brief anonymous pulse across shifts. Ask nurses whether they logged the last hand-hygiene action they completed on their most recent shift, with options for yes, no, and can’t recall. Report the share answering yes among those who recall, with response counts and shift coverage. Use existing ward-level kiosk totals to spot outages and unusual spikes. The pulse is a self-reported adoption estimate, and kiosk totals have no clinical denominator.

### What staff see

Use the existing ward huddle to share the audit result, the ward’s target, and one practical fix from staff feedback, such as “Night shift reported a queue, so we moved the kiosk.” Managers receive the same ward totals. Keep names and patient identifiers out of programme reporting and keep programme data out of performance reviews. Publish results only for groups large enough to protect anonymity.

### Timing and ending

Randomize the order in which wards enter the eight-week reward period. Start in weeks 5, 9, and 13 so each ward gets two monthly audits during its reward period. Compare earlier-starting wards with wards still receiving feedback alone, treating the comparison as directional given there are three wards.

Before each ward’s reward ends, make the kiosk the first option wherever nurses already go to record a moment, with ward selection prefilled. Keep the confirmation, ward feedback, and monthly audits. Four and eight weeks after the reward ends, repeat the adoption pulse. At week eight, **keep** the kiosk as the default at 70% or higher reported adoption, **revise** the workflow at 50–69%, and **drop** the kiosk as the default below 50%. Set minimum pulse coverage before launch and repeat an under-covered pulse before deciding. The monthly clinical audit continues throughout.

### Failure modes and ownership

Before launch, test duplicate taps, undo, downtime, and hygiene performed without a kiosk entry. A duplicate tap saves once, undo removes the entry, and an observed hygiene action counts even if the nurse skips the kiosk. The ward lead sends kiosk count corrections to the system owner. Infection Prevention resolves audit discrepancies and pauses rewards if observed compliance falls five percentage points below baseline on an adequately sampled audit. It also pauses the logging workflow promptly if it delays care or creates a hygiene hazard. Use the existing monthly audit and huddle, with one programme owner and a weekly administration budget of under an hour.
