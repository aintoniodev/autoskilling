## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

Run a four-week incentive in each participating depot or crew. The reward is **30 minutes of paid time per technician** when the crew completes valid digital checklists before at least **55% of its job starts** during its reward window. Everyone on the crew earns the time, including people with few assignments or an absence. Agree with union representatives on how technicians can take the time across shifts.

### Before launch

Walk through the checklist with technicians from different shifts, including crews with weak mobile signal. Give them paid practice time and fix the friction they identify, such as duplicate entry, login delays, or unreliable offline saving. Tell crews what changed. Put the checklist first on the assigned job screen and prefill job details the app already knows. Keep safety answers for the technician to complete.

Agree with union representatives on eligibility, reporting, and corrections. When the app is unavailable, technicians follow the existing safety procedure. Give them paid time to complete the checklist and resolve any hazards before work begins.

### What counts

**A job counts once when its completed digital safety checklist is recorded before work starts.**

Use existing checklist and job-start records, matched by job ID. The denominator is all jobs that started and required a checklist. Set any job-type exemptions before launch and exclude cancellations before work starts. Check that the existing start timestamp reflects when work begins. If it cannot establish the order reliably, fix that before offering rewards.

Test the rule before launch. Duplicate submissions count once. A checklist voided without a valid replacement contributes zero. A late checklist contributes zero. An offline submission counts if the app already preserves a reliable completion time that precedes the job start. A job that uses the fallback procedure stays in the denominator and is reported separately in aggregate so app failures are visible.

### What technicians see

After submission, show a private confirmation tied to the job. If it is still syncing, show that status and confirm credit once it is verified. Show each technician their own completed and eligible job counts, with a suggested goal scaled to their volume. A technician with five eligible jobs might aim for three. Progress accumulates through the window, and the next job is a fresh opportunity.

Post the crew’s progress toward 55% in its existing weekly briefing. Managers get aggregate results, with small crews combined where needed to protect privacy. Individual counts are private and stay out of performance reviews. The pilot owner handles disputed counts privately and corrects errors before rewards are settled.

### Timing and decision

Give every crew the app fixes, paid practice, and feedback in weeks 1–2. Record the baseline using the pilot’s counting rule and check it against the reported 35%. Assign comparable crews to reward windows in weeks 3–6, 5–8, and 7–10, randomly where practical. Compare crews receiving the reward with crews still waiting during the same weeks to estimate what the reward adds.

At the end of each window, confirm earned paid time and close the incentive. Keep the easier app workflow and private feedback. Use **the share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-start digital checklist in weeks three and four after rewards end** to decide what to do next. At **55% or above**, keep the workflow and retire the reward. At **45% to under 55%**, fix the main remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **45%**, drop the incentive and return to investigating why crews struggle to use the app. The quality rule below overrides the adoption result.

### What could go wrong

- **Rushed answers or delayed job-start entries.** The safety lead reviews five sampled jobs a week against existing records. Pause rewards for any serious safety issue or two material checklist defects in the most recent ten reviewed jobs. The safety lead decides when to resume, and the app or operations owner fixes the cause.
- **Unequal job access or poor coverage.** Review aggregate fallback rates by crew and shift before launch. Fix recurring access problems before starting an affected crew’s reward window.
- **Pressure to hide hazards or blame coworkers.** Count an accurate checklist that flags a hazard when the job later starts. A job stopped before work begins stays outside the denominator. Use the existing safety reporting route for pressure or retaliation and keep individual progress out of crew briefings.

Use one weekly report from existing records and one pilot owner. Budget under an hour each week for the report, the sample review, and routine corrections.
