**Reward audited hand hygiene.** Each ward earns one modest, staff-chosen reward, such as a hospital-funded meal available to every shift, if its two monthly audits during the reward period meet its ward goal when pooled. Every nurse on the ward shares the reward, including float staff who worked there. Kiosk entries carry zero points. The rule staff hear is “Your ward earns the reward when its routine hand-hygiene audits meet its goal.”

**Make logging fit the shift.** In month 1, give nurses from day and night shifts 15 paid minutes to try the kiosk during a normal workflow and flag where logging interrupts care. Fix the biggest barriers before starting rewards. Put the one-tap logging screen first, enable immediate “Saved” feedback and an undo, and make the workflow clear when the kiosk is down. Nurses log after completing hand hygiene, when care permits. A missed entry stays missed and the next entry starts fresh. Share fixes in the existing huddle.

**Measure with the existing monthly audit.** Infection Prevention calculates each ward’s rate as observed hand-hygiene actions performed divided by observed opportunities, counting each opportunity once. Use the same audit definitions and comparable shift coverage throughout. Freeze each ward’s baseline using its three pre-pilot audits and set the goal before launch. For wards below the hospital target, aim for a five-percentage-point improvement, capped at the target. For wards already at target, aim to stay within three points of baseline and at or above the target. Infection Prevention sets the minimum usable audit sample in advance. If the sample is too small or shift coverage is materially different, defer the reward decision to the next comparable routine audit. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241598606)

**Show ward progress in the existing huddle.** Share the ward’s audited rate, sample size, goal and next audit date. The kiosk confirms the nurse’s own submission. Managers receive ward totals only, and programme data stays out of performance reviews. A ward champion reviews existing ward-level kiosk totals and asks staff whether the workflow fits their shift. Those totals indicate usage and cannot establish what share of actual moments were logged. Before launch, test a double tap, an undo and a completed hand-hygiene action with no kiosk entry. The first saves once, the second removes the entry, and the third still counts if observed. The kiosk owner fixes logging errors and Infection Prevention corrects audit counts.

**Run two reward months per ward, then stop.** All three wards get the workflow fixes and audit feedback in month 1. Randomly assign reward starts in months 2, 3 and 4. Use the stagger to see whether audited compliance improves when rewards begin, treating the comparison as directional with three wards. Keep the one-tap default and huddle feedback after the final reward. Pool each ward’s first two post-reward audits and use the same ward goal as the decision threshold. If it holds, keep the workflow without prizes. If it falls short, use the shift feedback to fix the workflow and recheck. If logging still disrupts care after the fix, drop the kiosk workflow and continue routine audits. The ward champion uses the existing huddle and spends at most an hour a week administering the pilot.

**Pause when care or audit quality slips.** Logging can distract from care, staff may feel pressure to hide misses, and people may perform differently when observers are present. Give nurses a private route to flag problems to Infection Prevention. Infection Prevention pauses rewards on a ward if a comparable monthly audit falls five percentage points below baseline or a confirmed logging-related interruption delays care. Keep observation scheduling independent of ward managers and avoid ward rankings. Review the cause before resuming.
