# Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Make the knowledge base part of resolving a ticket. Start from the current 12% article coverage and aim for **30% of eligible resolutions** during the pilot and four weeks after rewards end.

## What earns credit

**A resolved ticket earns one credit when you link an approved article that helped resolve it or publish an approved article that fills the gap.**

Use the existing ticket resolution, article link and publication records. A new article earns credit when it’s approved, published and linked back to the ticket. Give agents protected time to write and review articles. Let them close the ticket as soon as the customer’s issue is resolved.

Count each ticket once. Multiple links and duplicate closure events add zero credit. Reopening a ticket reverses its credit. Reclosing it restores one credit if the article still applies. An article can count on multiple tickets when it helps resolve each one. An eligible ticket closed without a qualifying article stays in the denominator. Agree on narrow exclusions before launch, such as tickets where confidentiality rules prevent documenting the resolution, and apply them using existing ticket categories.

## What people see and earn

The ticket tool confirms credit as soon as it posts. Agents see their own count and a private goal of **one qualifying resolution in their next four eligible tickets**. The goal follows case volume, so a quiet week or an absence carries no penalty.

Show the team’s coverage and progress toward 30% in its existing support dashboard. Each team that reaches 30% across its four reward weeks and clears the quality checks earns **30 minutes of paid time per person**, scheduled around queue coverage. Everyone on the team earns it. Managers see team totals only. Individual counts stay out of rankings, performance reviews and pay decisions. The existing CSAT bonus keeps its current formula.

## Timing and ending

- **Weeks 1–2.** Meet with agents from different queues and shifts. Give everyone paid practice time and fix the friction they identify, such as poor search, missing articles and slow approvals. Share what changed. Turn on private feedback for everyone and check coverage using the pilot’s counting rule.
- **Weeks 3–9.** Start the four-week reward in weeks 3 and 6 across two groups of existing teams with roughly similar ticket mixes. Compare their coverage during the first three weeks, when both have the fixes and feedback and one group has the reward.
- **Before each reward window ends.** Make article search and suggested links the default in the resolution flow, with a quick skip for cases with no suitable article. Remove the reward at the scheduled end and keep the workflow and private feedback.

Four weeks after each group’s reward ends, calculate **the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article across those four weeks**. At 30% or higher, keep the workflow and retire the reward. Above the pre-reward baseline but below 30%, fix the largest remaining barrier. At or below baseline, drop the incentive programme. The quality and CSAT checks apply throughout.

## What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles inflate coverage.** The knowledge-base lead samples 10 credited tickets each week across participating teams and checks whether the article was accurate and helped resolve the issue. If two or more fail, the support lead pauses rewards for the affected teams until a fresh sample passes. Agents can flag a wrong count in the existing support channel, and the knowledge-base lead corrects the tally.
- **Agents rush customers or delay closure to earn credit.** Check team CSAT and survey response counts weekly. A drop of five percentage points from the prior four-week baseline for two weeks running triggers a pause and ticket review before rewards resume. Pending article approval never holds up closure.
- **Easy queues carry the numbers.** Review coverage by existing queue categories and check skips in queues with unusually low coverage. Use the findings to fix article gaps and search results.

Use the existing dashboard and a weekly review of the 10 sampled tickets, with a total operating budget of under one hour per week.
