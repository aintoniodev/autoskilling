## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Set up with technicians and union stewards.** Use paid time in the first two weeks to walk through real jobs with technicians from different shifts and routes. Test weak-signal sites, shared devices and jobs where a hazard stops work. Fix the friction they identify, show crews what changed, and make the checklist the first step on the mobile job card. Use the existing approved safety procedure whenever the app is unavailable. Start rewards only once crews can complete the checklist reliably in the field.

**Reward one completed safety check per job.** Give crews this rule: “A job counts once when you complete its digital safety checklist before work starts, or when you record a hazard and put the job on hold before starting.” A properly documented safety hold earns the same credit as a job that proceeds. Keep rewards independent of injury rates, hazard reports and how quickly a technician clears a hold.

**Use existing job records.** Match the checklist to the dispatch job ID and compare the recorded completion time with the existing job-start event. For an offline submission, use the saved completion time if the existing app preserves it reliably. Deduplicate repeat submissions and count the final valid record if a checklist is corrected or voided. Measure qualifying jobs as a share of jobs that started or were documented as stopped for safety, including jobs handled through the approved fallback. Exclude cancellations before any work or safety hold. Track fallback use in aggregate so app failures are visible. Validate the start record before launch. If it cannot establish the order of events reliably, fix that before attaching rewards.

**Make progress visible and attainable.** After each submission, show the technician whether it counted or is waiting to sync. Each technician sees their own progress against a goal of four out of every five eligible jobs in their reward window. A missed week simply lowers the number of jobs available that window. A crew that reaches 75% over its four-week window earns a paid team meal, with equivalent provision for different shifts. Show crew progress in the existing weekly huddle. Managers see crew totals, with small groups rolled up where individuals could be inferred. Keep programme counts out of performance reviews and disciplinary decisions. A named programme steward handles private count corrections using the existing job record.

**Stagger the reward and leave the easier workflow in place.** In weeks 1–2, validate the baseline against the reported 35% and test counting on a duplicate, a void, an offline sync, a fallback job and a safety hold. In weeks 3–4, give everyone the fixes, checklist default and private feedback. Randomly assign comparable crews to reward windows in weeks 5–8 or 7–10, so the two-week gap gives a read on what the reward adds. Remove the prize at the end of each window and keep the checklist default and feedback. Four weeks after each crew’s window ends, use its digital completion rate over those four weeks to decide what to do. At 70% or higher, keep the rollout as designed. At 50–69%, fix the biggest remaining barrier and retest. Below 50%, retire the reward format and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards.

**Watch for rushed checks, underreported hazards and unfair counts.** Have the existing safety lead review ten randomly selected credited jobs each week against the records already used for safety QA. If two or more have a material safety omission, or a sampled job started despite an unresolved stop-work hazard, the safety lead pauses rewards and works with the union steward to identify the cause before restarting. Review outages and disputed counts before deciding a crew missed its milestone. Use the existing weekly huddle and a short exception review so one owner can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
