## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward the ward for hand hygiene verified in its existing monthly audits.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal, available across all shifts, by meeting its own target across two monthly audits. Announce the rule at huddles: “Your ward earns a meal if it meets its two-month hand-hygiene target in the monthly audits.” Everyone on the ward shares the reward, including staff who were away or never used the kiosk.

### Make the kiosk useful first

Spend the first month with nurses from day, night and weekend shifts. Give them 15 minutes of paid time to try logging during a normal shift. Fix placement, access, slow entry and duplicate paperwork before starting rewards. Infection Prevention approves where the kiosk sits and how it is cleaned. Patient care takes priority, and nurses can skip an entry when logging would interrupt care.

Make the ward preselected and keep the entry to one screen. After saving, show “Hand-hygiene moment recorded” and allow an immediate undo. Use aggregate entries to guide huddle conversations about workflow barriers, such as whether supplies are within reach at a particular point in care. Post one concrete response when staff flag a problem, such as “You reported an empty dispenser on nights. We added it to the night restock round.”

### Measurement and feedback

- **Audit score.** Divide observed opportunities where nurses performed hand hygiene correctly by all eligible opportunities observed. Set each ward’s baseline from its last three comparable monthly audits. Set the pilot target five percentage points above baseline, capped at 95%. Wards already at or above 95% aim to maintain their baseline. These are pilot reward thresholds. Existing hospital standards still apply. [WHO hand-hygiene measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)
- **Count each observed opportunity once.** Before launch, walk observers through a duplicate record, a corrected record and hand hygiene performed without a kiosk entry. Dedupe the first, use the corrected audit record for the second, and count the third in full. Kiosk entries carry no reward credit.
- **Keep feedback at ward level.** At the usual huddle, show the ward’s latest audit score, number of opportunities observed and its own target. Nurses get immediate confirmation of each entry. Managers get ward totals. Configure kiosk reporting without patient details, staff identifiers, individual histories or performance-review use. There are no rankings or streaks.

### Timing and ending

Give all three wards the fixes, training and feedback in month 1. Start the two-month reward windows in months 2, 3 and 4, one ward at a time, with the order drawn before launch. Use the stagger to see whether scores move when rewards begin. With three wards, treat that comparison as directional.

End each ward’s rewards after its second monthly audit. Keep the quick-entry workflow and huddle feedback in place. At the second audit after rewards end, use **observed compliance against the ward’s original target** to decide whether to keep the workflow, change it and retest if it falls short but stays above baseline, or drop the kiosk routine if it returns to or below baseline. For wards whose target equals baseline, meeting it qualifies as keep. Routine infection-prevention auditing continues throughout.

### What could go wrong

- **Audit-day performance or easy shifts dominate.** Use the existing trained observers and a preset mix of day, night and weekend sessions. Vary visit times within that mix. Observer presence can lift measured compliance, so interpret the score as sampled performance. [Hand-hygiene practice recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015275/)
- **A thin or worsening audit gets rewarded.** Infection Prevention pauses payout if fewer than 80% of planned observation sessions are completed or if either monthly score drops five percentage points below baseline. The audit lead corrects disputed counts before payout. Infection Prevention investigates declines and decides when the ward can resume.
- **Logging becomes a quota or interrupts care.** Set no entry targets. If staff report pressure to log or a patient-care delay, the ward lead pauses kiosk use and fixes the workflow. Use existing audits and one shared tracker so a coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
