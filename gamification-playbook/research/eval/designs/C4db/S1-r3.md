## Pre-job safety checklist programme

**Reward one valid digital safety checklist per eligible job, submitted before work starts.** A checklist that documents a hazard and puts the job on hold earns the same credit as one that clears the job. The assigned technician completes it on paid time. Each crew that reaches 65% over its four-week reward window earns a company-paid meal, with an equivalent option for technicians who work alone or on other shifts. Everyone on the crew shares the reward.

### Fix the workflow first

Spend two weeks with union stewards and technicians from different shifts and routes. Give them paid time to try the app on real jobs, including low-signal sites. Fix the biggest barriers before rewards begin, and tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Agree with the union on the counting rule, reward, data access, and appeals. If the app fails, technicians use the approved safety fallback and follow the same stop-work procedure. Supervisors explicitly protect time for the check when the schedule runs late.

### Count jobs and check quality

Use existing dispatch and app records. The denominator is jobs that reached the point where a pre-job assessment was required, including jobs then put on safety hold. Exclude jobs cancelled before that point. The numerator is eligible jobs with a valid checklist linked to the job ID and submitted before the recorded work start or as part of the recorded pre-work hold. Verify on real jobs that the start record reflects when work begins and that offline checklists preserve their original completion time. Delay the reward if those records cannot establish the order reliably.

Test the rule before launch. A duplicate adds zero. A voided checklist loses credit unless it was validly replaced before work began. An approved paper fallback satisfies the safety procedure and stays in the denominator as a job without digital completion. If a repeatable app failure is driving paper use, fix it and give the affected crew a fresh reward window.

The safety lead samples 10 credited jobs each week across crews and checks timing, required fields, and whether documented hazards had an appropriate control or hold. Two failures in a sample pause rewards while the safety lead investigates. A suspected false clearance goes straight through the existing safety incident process. Technicians can dispute a count privately with the programme coordinator, who corrects it from the job record and explains the decision.

### Show people what they need

The app confirms that the checklist was saved and whether it counted. Each technician sees their own progress toward three valid checklists in their next four eligible jobs, with a smaller goal for technicians assigned fewer jobs. After a miss or absence, the next job is another chance to count. Post crew progress in the existing weekly huddle. Managers see crew totals only, with small crews rolled up where totals could identify someone. Keep programme data out of performance reviews and discipline.

### End the reward and measure what stays

Give every crew the fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Start half the crews in week 3 and half in week 5, balancing the groups by baseline adoption and job volume. Each group gets four reward weeks. Compare the groups in weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds while both have the same workflow and feedback. Before the first reward ends, put the checklist first on the job screen and prefill information dispatch already knows. End rewards after week 8.

Use the **valid pre-job digital completion rate in weeks 9–12** to decide what to do next. At 70% or higher, keep the workflow and close the incentive trial. At 50–69%, use technician feedback to fix the remaining barriers. Below 50%, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow with the union. The underlying safety requirement stays in force at every threshold.

Watch for rushed answers, delayed job-start entries, pressure on low-volume crews, and adoption dropping after the meal. Use the quality sample and private technician feedback to catch those problems. Budget one hour per week for the coordinator to review the counts, handle disputes, and share crew progress.
