## One working day for a useful code review

**Start with the review queue.** Ask five engineers, including frequent reviewers and people who regularly wait for reviews, to walk through recent delays. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected work time to try the revised workflow. Fix missing ownership, notification overload, and review time that gets displaced by sprint work. Publish what engineers reported and what changed. Set up a rotating primary reviewer and backup, with protected time and the oldest pending request at the top of the existing queue.

**Reward a useful first response.** Each review group earns a 60-minute block of paid learning time for every member if at least 85% of its eligible PRs receive a useful first human review within one working day during its four-week reward window. Agree on the reward with engineers before launch and protect the time in sprint planning. Publish one counting rule.

> A PR counts once when an eligible peer submits a useful review within one working day of the PR being ready and review requested.

A useful review is an approval after examining the change, actionable feedback, or a specific question the author needs to answer. An acknowledgement, emoji, or bot comment leaves the request open. The author can receive a useful answer even when the PR still needs work.

**Measure it from existing PR records.** Use ready-for-review, review-request, and submitted-review timestamps. Define one working day as eight hours on the owning group’s published working calendar, excluding weekends, holidays, and hours outside its review schedule. The clock starts once the PR is both ready and review requested. Reassignment, repeat requests, and reopening keep the original clock and PR ID. Exclude drafts and requests explicitly withdrawn by the author before the deadline. Keep late and unanswered requests in the denominator once due. Include eligible PRs answered through the old workflow when the response is recorded in the PR. Recalculate the historical baseline under this rule, since the current 2.6-day median gives a different view of turnaround.

**Show progress where engineers already work.** Give reviewers private confirmation when their review counts, with the same deadline visible in the PR workflow. Let each engineer set a private goal based on the requests they expect to handle that week. After an absence, the next request is a fresh chance to contribute. Post the group’s weekly result as a fraction and percentage in its existing channel. Managers see aggregate results only. Use no named leaderboard or individual drill-down, and keep programme data out of performance reviews. Use existing access controls and PR events.

**Run it in stages.** In weeks 1–2, both groups get the workflow fixes and feedback. Start the four-week reward window for one group in week 3 and the other in week 5. Use existing groups with separate review queues if available. If they share reviewers, run one team-wide window and report the before-and-after result without attributing the change to the reward alone. Before the first window ends, make automatic routing, backup coverage, and the oldest-first queue the default. End rewards on schedule and deliver earned learning time. Four weeks after the final reward window, use the same on-time rate to decide. Keep the workflow at **85% or higher**, revise it if the rate is **above baseline but below 85%**, and retire the programme if it is **at or below baseline**.

**Guard against rushed reviews and neglected hard PRs.** Each week, the review lead samples five credited reviews, including a large or complex PR when available, and checks the oldest unanswered request. If two of the five reviews fail the usefulness standard, the engineering lead pauses reward credit and resumes it after a clean sample. Before launch, walk through a duplicate request, an approval withdrawn as premature, a reassignment during leave, and a review submitted through the old workflow. A withdrawn approval loses credit unless another qualifying review arrived by the original deadline. Engineers can flag a wrong count privately to the review lead, who corrects it before the reward is calculated. Budget 45 minutes a week for sampling, corrections, and posting the aggregate.
