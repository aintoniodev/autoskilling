## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Start with two weeks of paid setup.** The safety lead, a union steward and technicians from different shifts ride through the workflow on real jobs. Fix reported problems such as weak signal, shared devices and dispatch schedules that squeeze out checklist time. Give every technician paid practice and build checklist time into each job allowance. Tell crews what they reported and what changed. Agree the counting, privacy and appeals rules with the union before rewards start.

**Reward one completed safety check per job.** Tell technicians: “Your crew gets one credit when you complete the app checklist before work begins or when you use it to document why the job must stop.” A crew that reaches 80% of eligible jobs over its four reward weeks earns a modest, company-paid meal on paid time, with an equivalent option for every shift. Every crew can earn it. Technicians can miss a week and pick up with the next job.

**Measure from existing job records.** Match the app’s checklist completion time to the work order’s existing start or safety-hold record. Count each eligible job once, including jobs held for safety. Exclude jobs cancelled before the technician reaches the pre-job check. Before launch, confirm that the records can establish the order of events. Test duplicate submissions, reopened orders, late checklists, paper checks and offline sync. Where the app preserves the original completion time, use it even if the submission syncs later.

Keep the approved paper fallback available. Record those jobs as paper checks in the rollout totals so a safe fallback stays visible. For the meal, exclude confirmed app or device outages from the crew’s score so crews can use paper without losing ground. Keep those jobs in the companywide digital adoption denominator, since outages still affect whether the app works as the default.

**Show technicians their own progress and crews their aggregate progress.** After submission, confirm which job was credited or whether it is waiting to sync. Privately show each technician completed checks against their own eligible jobs and an 80% goal sized to their job volume. Put the crew total in its existing weekly huddle or bulletin. Managers get aggregate pilot results only. Combine small groups where a total would reveal an individual’s activity. Keep pilot data out of performance reviews and discipline. The rollout coordinator corrects disputed counts, with a union steward available to help technicians raise issues.

**Watch for rushing, backfilling and pressure to start.** The safety lead uses the existing safety review to check 10 credited jobs a week for timing and credible hazard responses. Pause rewards if two of the 10 have material checklist defects or if any review confirms that someone was pressured to start an unsafe job or falsify a check. The safety lead decides when to resume after reviewing the fix with the steward. Use the existing safety reporting channel for hazards and near misses. Reporting either leaves reward eligibility intact. Automate the totals and use the existing huddle and safety review so one coordinator can run the pilot in under an hour a week.

**End the rewards on schedule and keep the easier workflow.** Give all crews the fixes and private feedback first. Confirm the baseline using the same job rules, then start four weeks of rewards for half the crews. Start the other half two weeks later. Compare adoption during that overlap to see what the reward added. Before each crew’s rewards end, put the pre-filled checklist first when a technician opens a job, with the safety-hold and paper fallback routes still available.

Four weeks after the last meal is earned, use the companywide share of eligible jobs with a valid pre-job digital checklist to decide what stays. At **80% or above**, keep the app-first workflow without prizes. If adoption is **above baseline but below 80%**, fix the most common remaining barrier and recheck. At **baseline or lower**, drop the incentive and revisit the app workflow with technicians and the union. The pre-job safety check remains required in every case.
