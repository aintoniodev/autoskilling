# Knowledge-base adoption programme

**Reward a resolved ticket with a useful article.** Tell agents the rule in one sentence: “A resolved ticket earns one credit when it links to an approved article that helps solve the issue, including an article you write and get approved.” Each team earns a €15 lunch credit per person if at least 35% of its resolved tickets qualify during its four reward weeks and the quality checks pass. Everyone on the team shares the reward. Ask agents during setup whether they’d prefer an equivalent team reward.

**Make it easy to do during a shift.** In weeks 1–2, sit with agents from different shifts and watch them close tickets. Give them 30 minutes of protected time to try the workflow. Fix the biggest obstacles they identify, such as poor search, missing permissions or slow article approval, and show them what changed. Put suggested articles first in the ticket close form and make it easy to draft from the resolution notes. Let agents close the ticket when the customer’s issue is solved and finish any draft during protected time. Keep the existing CSAT bonus terms in place.

**Use the ticketing system to count.** The denominator is all distinct resolved tickets, with merged duplicates counted under the surviving ticket. The numerator is tickets with at least one qualifying article linked in the existing ticket record. Multiple links earn one credit. A reopened ticket leaves the count until it’s resolved again, and a removed or invalid link loses its credit. A useful article can earn credit on each distinct ticket where it applies. Cases without a suitable article stay in the denominator. Before launch, replay a merged duplicate, a reopened ticket, an unrelated link and a closure without a link to verify the counts.

**Show agents their own progress and the team’s total.** At closure, confirm whether the ticket counted. If a draft is pending approval, show that status and confirm the credit once it’s approved and linked. Give agents a private goal of roughly one qualifying ticket in three, scaled to their own ticket volume. Post each team’s aggregate progress in its existing support channel. Managers get team totals only, and programme data stays out of performance reviews. An agent who misses a week can pick up with their next ticket.

**Stagger the reward and set an end date.** Give everyone the workflow fixes and feedback in weeks 1–2. Randomly assign existing teams to reward windows in weeks 3–6 and 6–9. Compare the cohorts in weeks 3–5, when both have the improved workflow and only one can earn the reward. Remove prize messaging after each window and keep article suggestions, protected time and feedback in place. Four weeks after each team’s rewards end, use its average qualifying-ticket rate over those four weeks to decide what to do: **35% or higher**, keep the workflow as standard if quality and CSAT checks pass. **20% to below 35%**, fix the biggest remaining obstacle and recheck. **Below 20%**, retire the programme and investigate why the workflow is still hard to use.

**Catch bad links and slower support early.** The support quality lead samples 10 credited tickets a week for relevance, accuracy and sensitive information. Two failures in a sample, or any exposure of sensitive information, pause the affected team’s reward. The quality lead also reviews the existing CSAT report. A drop of five percentage points from the pre-launch baseline over two weeks, with at least 30 responses, triggers a review of whether the workflow is hurting customers before rewards resume. The quality lead owns pause and restart decisions. A designated support operations owner corrects disputed counts and posts the team update. Use the existing QA and reporting routines to keep weekly administration under an hour.
