## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward accurate recording of completed hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal or equivalent meal voucher for every shift, including night staff. The ward earns it when at least 80% of sampled, completed hand-hygiene opportunities have one correct kiosk entry during its two-month reward window, and observed hand-hygiene compliance stays within 5 percentage points of its pre-reward baseline. Each ward can earn the reward independently. There are no individual quotas or streaks.

**Make the kiosk easy to use before starting.** Give day- and night-shift nurses on each ward 15 minutes of paid time to try the workflow and identify what makes it slow or disruptive. Fix placement, access and duplicate entry before launch. Make the ward and timestamp automatic and put the recording screen first. Agree with infection prevention on when logging can safely happen. Nurses should clean their hands at the required moment and continue urgent care immediately. Share the fixes at shift huddles.

**Measure recording and clinical compliance separately.** During existing monthly audits, observers tally how many completed opportunities they can verify were correctly saved in the kiosk, using the existing save confirmation. Count each opportunity once, even when it covers multiple indications. An undone entry earns zero credit. A completed action with no kiosk entry counts toward clinical compliance and as a missed recording. A kiosk entry on its own establishes neither. Clinical compliance is observed hand-hygiene actions divided by observed opportunities. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

Pilot the tally before offering the reward. Use comparable audit blocks across day and night shifts and aim for at least 30 sampled completed opportunities per ward over its reward window. Report how many entries were unverifiable and withhold the reward decision if the sample is too small or observers cannot reliably connect a save to a completed action during normal care. Keep the workflow improvements if verification proves impractical.

**Show feedback where staff already look.** The kiosk immediately confirms a save and offers an undo. At the monthly huddle, share the ward’s recording coverage, clinical compliance, sample sizes and one practical improvement. Staff and managers see the same ward totals. The pilot collects no nurse or patient identifiers. Results stay out of performance reviews, and wards are never ranked.

**Stagger the reward and end it on schedule.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback in month 1. Randomly assign reward starts in months 2, 3 and 4, with two months per ward. Compare changes in recording coverage with wards still receiving feedback alone. Three wards can provide a useful signal, with limited confidence about what caused a change. Award any earned meals after each window and leave the simplified kiosk as the default. At the next monthly audit, keep the workflow if coverage holds at 80% or higher, revise and retest at 60–79%, and drop the kiosk logging pilot below 60%. Apply those decisions only to adequate samples and subject to the clinical safety guardrail.

**Pause if care or data quality slips.** Infection prevention pauses rewards if audited compliance drops more than 5 percentage points below baseline or staff report that logging delayed care. The ward champion handles tally disputes and kiosk faults. Before launch, they test duplicate taps, an undo, an outage and a completed action with no entry. If staff start logging only when observers are present, keep audit timing varied and treat the measured coverage as specific to the observed periods. Budget under an hour a week for the pilot lead to review exceptions and update the huddle figures.
