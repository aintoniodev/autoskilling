## Three-ward hand-hygiene pilot

**Reward.** Each ward earns one team meal, available across all shifts, if its two monthly audits during the reward period meet its ward target when pooled. Set the target before launch at five percentage points above the ward’s baseline, capped at the hospital’s existing hand-hygiene target. Wards already at or above the hospital target earn the same reward by maintaining it. Every ward can qualify independently. Kiosk entries carry no points and have no effect on eligibility.

**Make logging fit the shift.** Give nurses from days, nights and weekends paid time to walk through the workflow. Fix reported problems with supplies, kiosk placement or entry time before starting rewards, then report back on what changed. Put the logging screen first, prefill the ward and show an immediate “Entry saved” message with an undo option. Nurses can log after completing hand hygiene when care allows and resume at the next opportunity after an interruption. Use existing ward-level kiosk totals to spot downtime and check uptake, alongside a brief anonymous staff pulse on when logging fits and when it gets skipped. Avoid collecting patient or staff identifiers for the programme.

**Measure and show progress.** Infection Prevention uses the existing monthly audits. Divide correctly timed observed hand-hygiene actions by observed opportunities, counting each opportunity once even when several indications coincide. Use trained observers and a comparable mix of shifts and care situations across periods. Set a minimum sample before launch based on the existing audit volume. Hold the award decision if the sample is too small or the shift mix is materially different. Post each ward’s rate, sample size, target and practical fixes in its usual huddle notes. Managers see ward aggregates only. Programme data stays out of performance reviews. [WHO hand-hygiene observation manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598606)

**Timing and ending.** Give all three wards the fixes and feedback first, and use two monthly audits to establish each ward’s baseline. Randomly assign wards to start rewards one month apart. Each ward gets two reward months, then prizes end on schedule. Keep the quick-entry screen and huddle feedback in place. Check audited compliance again four and eight weeks after each ward’s reward ends. Use the pooled follow-up rate to decide what to do with the workflow. Keep it where the ward still meets its target, revise it and recheck where compliance is above baseline but below target, and retire the pilot logging workflow where compliance returns to baseline or worse. Routine hand hygiene and audits continue throughout. With three wards, treat the staggered results as a signal about what rewards added.

**What could go wrong.** Before launch, test three cases with ward staff. A duplicate tap or an undone entry changes no reward, observed hygiene without a kiosk entry counts, and a kiosk entry without observed hygiene adds nothing to the audit. Visible observers and selective sampling can inflate scores, so Infection Prevention spot-checks a small sample of audit records and reviews shift coverage. It pauses awards if a comparable monthly audit drops five percentage points below baseline or a confirmed logging-related care delay occurs. Infection Prevention corrects audit totals and decides when to resume. The kiosk owner fixes logging faults. Assign one coordinator to handle exceptions and huddle updates in under an hour a week alongside the existing audit process.
