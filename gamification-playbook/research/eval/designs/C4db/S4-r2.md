## Six-week knowledge-base pilot

**Goal.** Raise the share of resolved tickets with a useful knowledge-base article from 12% to at least 35%, and see whether agents sustain that rate four weeks after rewards end. Keep the existing CSAT bonus separate from the pilot.

### Before rewards start

Spend two weeks with agents from different queues and shifts. Give them 30 minutes of protected time to close real tickets using the tool. Ask where search fails, where linking slows them down, and which cases would produce low-value articles. Fix the biggest friction before launching rewards, then post what agents reported and what changed. Give every team the same fixes, protected learning time, and feedback before its reward window begins.

### What counts

Tell agents the rule in one sentence: **“A resolved ticket counts once when you link an approved article that helped resolve it, or when a reusable article you wrote for the ticket is approved and linked.”**

Use existing ticket closure, article-link, and publication records. The denominator is all unique resolved tickets, including tickets closed the old way and one-off or sensitive cases where an article would be inappropriate. Start with the expectation that agents check for relevant knowledge on every ticket and skip the link when it adds no value.

Test the counting rule on five cases before launch:

- Multiple links or merged duplicates earn one count for the canonical ticket.
- A ticket reopened within seven days loses its provisional count. Its final resolution can qualify once.
- An article awaiting approval earns provisional credit. Once approved, it counts toward the ticket’s original closure week, with a seven-day approval grace period after the reward window closes.
- An existing article linked after closure qualifies only if the agent used it to resolve the ticket.
- A ticket closed without a useful article stays in the denominator and earns zero credit.

### What agents see and earn

Give agents immediate private confirmation in the ticket workflow, such as “Article linked. Credit pending the reopen window.” In their existing workspace, show each agent their own qualifying tickets and a personal target scaled to volume, roughly one in three resolutions. A missed week has no penalty. The next ticket is a fresh opportunity.

Post team progress weekly where agents already look. Managers see team totals only. Combine very small groups in reporting so a total cannot reveal an individual’s activity. Use no named leaderboard, and keep individual counts out of performance reviews and bonus calculations.

When a team reaches 35% across its four-week window and passes the quality check, everyone on that team gets a 30-minute paid break, scheduled across shifts. Teams can each earn it. Ask agents during setup whether they would prefer an equivalent team meal.

### Timing and decision

Stagger existing teams into two groups with roughly comparable queue mix and baseline usage. The first group earns rewards in weeks 1–4, the second in weeks 3–6. Compare their change from baseline during weeks 1–2 to estimate what the reward adds beyond the fixes and feedback.

Before week 4 ends, put article search first in the resolution workflow for everyone, with relevant suggestions where available. Agents confirm the link themselves and can close a ticket without one. End each team’s reward after four weeks. Measure the qualifying resolution rate again in each team’s fourth week without rewards.

Use that rate for the decision: **35% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback with prizes retired. **20–34%**, fix the biggest remaining friction and recheck. **Below 20%**, drop the incentive and revisit whether the workflow saves agents enough time to stick.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, duplicate articles, or sensitive details.** Each week the support knowledge owner samples 10 credited tickets across queues for relevance, reuse value, and privacy. Two failures in 10, or one privacy breach, pauses reward payouts while the owner fixes the issue and checks a fresh sample. The same owner corrects disputed counts through the existing support channel.
- **Slower resolution or worse customer experience.** Review existing team-level handling time, reopen rates, and CSAT alongside adoption. If handling time rises more than 10% or CSAT falls more than 3 percentage points for two consecutive weeks against the pre-pilot baseline, with enough responses to interpret the CSAT change, pause the affected team’s reward and inspect cases before restarting.
- **Unequal opportunity.** Check adoption by queue during setup. If a queue has few reusable cases, agree on a reachable team milestone before launch and keep measuring it with the same all-ticket denominator.

Reuse existing reports and reserve 45 minutes a week for sampling, corrections, and the team update.
