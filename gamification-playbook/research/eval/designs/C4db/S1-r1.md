# Safe Start pilot

Give technicians paid time to complete the digital safety checklist before each job. Run a four-week incentive per team, with staggered starts, and keep the checklist built into the normal job workflow after the reward ends.

## What earns the reward

**One job counts when its required checklist is completed in the app before work starts and any safety issue is handled under the existing stop-work procedure.** Count each job ID once, even if the checklist is reopened or resubmitted. A checklist completed after work starts still belongs in the safety record and earns no pilot credit.

Agree the reward with union representatives and a small group of technicians from different shifts. Offer each participating team **30 minutes of paid time of their choosing**, scheduled with coverage, if the team reaches **70% on-time completion across its four weeks**. Everyone on the team gets the reward, including people who were on leave. There are no individual quotas or rankings. Each new job is another opportunity to contribute.

## How it’s measured

Use existing checklist completion and job-start records. Divide the number of jobs with a qualifying checklist by all jobs that started and required one. Exclude canceled jobs that never started. Review stop-work cases through the existing safety process and recognize correct stops in team communications without naming technicians or linking recognition to the reward.

Before launch, verify that job-start timestamps reflect when work actually begins. Test a duplicate, a late submission, an offline sync, and a stop-work case. Credit offline completions when the app preserves a reliable completion timestamp. During outages or device failures, technicians use the approved fallback. Those jobs stay in the denominator and are reported separately so an app problem is visible. If existing records cannot establish the order reliably, fix that before starting the reward.

## What people see

Put the checklist first when a technician opens a job, prefill job details, and allocate time for it in the schedule. After submission, show the technician whether the checklist counted, is awaiting sync, or needs correction. Each technician can see their own record and ask the pilot owner to correct a count.

Post each team’s running percentage and 70% goal in its existing weekly huddle or crew channel. Managers see team totals only. Agree with the union that pilot counts stay out of performance reviews and discipline, and keep individual safety records under their existing access rules.

## Timing and ending

- **Weeks 1–2.** Ride along with technicians, test the app in low-signal locations, and fix the biggest sources of delay. Give all teams the improved workflow and submission feedback, then measure their completion rate. Use this post-fix baseline alongside today’s estimate of 35%.
- **Weeks 3–8.** Start half the teams in week 3 and half in week 5, with comparable mixes of shifts and job types where possible. Each team gets four reward weeks. Compare the two groups during weeks 3–4 to estimate what the reward adds once both have the same workflow.
- **Four weeks after each team’s reward ends.** Measure the share of eligible jobs with an on-time digital checklist over those four reward-free weeks. Set the decision rule before launch. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow as configured. At **50–69%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **50%**, retire the incentive design and revisit how the app fits field work. The pre-job safety requirement stays in force throughout.

## What could go wrong

- **Rushing, copying answers, or delaying the recorded job start.** The safety lead checks a random sample of 10 credited jobs a week against existing records. Pause rewards if two or more have a material quality defect, or if any case shows work proceeded despite an unresolved stop-work issue. The safety lead decides when to resume. The pilot owner corrects counts and routes app defects for repair.
- **Crews feel pressured to work through hazards or lose out because of poor coverage.** Protect paid checklist time, repeat that stopping unsafe work takes priority, and review fallback rates and stop-work patterns by team with the union. Use that review to fix scheduling or app problems before closing out the pilot.
