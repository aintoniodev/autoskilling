## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

Reward teams for resolving customer issues with knowledge that another agent can reuse. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged. Every resolved ticket should include a relevant article or an approved exception, such as a duplicate or a case whose details cannot be safely documented.

### What earns credit

Tell agents the rule in one sentence. **A resolved ticket earns one credit when it links to an existing article or a newly published article that supports the resolution.**

Use the ticketing system’s resolution and article-link records. Measure credited tickets as a share of all resolved tickets, excluding only documented exceptions from existing disposition codes. Show the exception rate alongside coverage. Before launch, recalculate the reported 12% using this definition so the comparison is consistent.

Count each ticket ID once. Two links on one ticket earn one credit. An existing article can earn credit on multiple distinct tickets if it applies to each. A draft earns credit only after publication. A reopened ticket loses its credit until it is resolved again with a valid link. A ticket resolved through the old workflow stays in the denominator unless it has an approved exception.

### What agents see and earn

At resolution, show the agent a private confirmation such as “Article linked. This ticket counts.” Give each agent a private weekly target based on their normal eligible ticket volume, adjusted for leave or queue changes. For example, someone who usually resolves 20 eligible tickets sees a goal of 12. A missed week carries no penalty.

Show team coverage and progress toward the milestone in the existing support dashboard or team huddle. Managers see team totals only, with small groups rolled into the next reporting level. Individual counts stay out of performance reviews. There is no named leaderboard.

Each team that reaches **60% coverage across its four reward weeks** earns one paid 30-minute team break, staggered to preserve queue coverage. Give the same paid time to team members who are off shift when the group takes it. Confirm the reward after the final quality check.

### Timing and ending

- **Weeks 1–2.** Give every team the same workflow improvements, private feedback, and protected time to practise. Ask agents from different queues to show where search, permissions, or writing slows them down. Fix the main blockers and tell agents what changed. Use these weeks to establish coverage after the fixes and before rewards.
- **Weeks 3–8.** Randomly assign comparable teams to start in week 3 or week 5. Each gets four reward weeks. Compare the change in coverage during weeks 3–4, when both groups have the same workflow and feedback and only the first group can earn the reward.
- **Weeks 9–12.** End rewards for everyone. Keep article suggestions and the link field visible by default in the resolution form, with a quick path for legitimate exceptions. In weeks 11–12, use **eligible-ticket coverage** to decide what to keep. At **60% or above**, keep the workflow and feedback as standard. At **40–59%**, fix the remaining friction and remeasure. Below **40%**, retire the incentive design and revisit the workflow. Keep prizes off in all three cases.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links, thin articles, or inflated exceptions.** Each week the support QA lead reviews 12 tickets across teams, including credited tickets and exceptions, as part of routine QA. Pause rewards for the affected team if two sampled tickets fail the rule or one article exposes customer information. The QA lead decides when rewards resume. The support operations owner corrects wrong counts and tells the affected agent privately.
- **Agents rush closure or spend too long writing while the queue grows.** Give agents protected authoring time and encourage reuse whenever an article fits. Review existing CSAT and reopen trends alongside coverage. If either deteriorates, the QA lead checks the affected tickets and pauses rewards if the incentive is contributing. Agents continue to earn their CSAT bonus under the existing rules.
- **The lift disappears when prizes stop.** Use the post-reward coverage check to make the decision. If both groups improve similarly before the later group starts earning rewards, credit the workflow and feedback for the shared improvement and avoid repeating the prize by default.

Use existing reporting and fold the sample into routine QA so one programme owner can run the pilot in under an hour a week.
