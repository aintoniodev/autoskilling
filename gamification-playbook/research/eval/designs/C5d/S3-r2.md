## Three-ward hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward accurate recording of hand hygiene that happened.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded meal across all shifts, with the nursing director thanking staff in person at shift huddles. Name the meal at launch and confirm that day, night and weekend staff can all take part. Each ward earns it independently.

**Launch rule:** “Your ward earns a team meal when it reaches its target for correctly logging hand-hygiene actions verified in our usual audits, while maintaining observed compliance.”

### Set up the workflow

Before starting, walk through a shift with nurses from days, nights and weekends. Give them 15 minutes of paid practice and ask where logging would interrupt care, cause queues or make corrections difficult. Fix those problems before offering the reward. Put the kiosk on the normal workflow route, open it directly to the logging screen, and make corrections easy. Post the fixes in the existing ward huddle notes under “You said, we fixed.” If the kiosk is down or care takes priority, proceed with care and resume logging at the next eligible moment.

### Count verified work

Use the existing monthly observer audits and the kiosk’s existing records. The reward measure is **the percentage of observed, completed hand-hygiene actions with a matching valid kiosk record**. Infection Prevention checks that the existing records can support one-to-one matching by ward, moment type and time without adding staff identifiers. If they cannot, run the workflow pilot with feedback and hold the reward until the measure can be verified.

Count each completed action once. A duplicate tap earns one count, a voided entry earns zero, and a corrected entry can count once. An observed action completed without a kiosk entry still belongs in the denominator. A kiosk entry outside the observed sample earns no reward credit. Rehearse those cases, plus an outage and two similar actions close together, before launch.

Set each ward’s target after its feedback-only baseline. Close half the gap to 80% verified logging, or maintain the baseline if it is already 80% or higher. Require at least 30 observed, completed actions across the two reward-period audits to make an award decision. Keep the audit mix of shifts and locations comparable and show the sample size alongside the rate. Observed behavior can change when an auditor is present, so treat the results as a sample of practice. [WHO hand-hygiene observation method](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20004812/)

### Show progress and protect care

The kiosk confirms the specific moment saved immediately and offers an undo. The personal goal is to record the next eligible completed action when care permits. Anyone returning after leave or a missed shift starts with the next action. The existing ward huddle board shows the ward’s verified rate, sample size, target and latest fix. Managers see ward aggregates only. Keep individual records out of rankings and performance reviews.

At each audit, Infection Prevention checks **observed compliance**, calculated as completed actions divided by observed opportunities. Pause the award if compliance falls **5 percentage points or more below that ward’s baseline**, or immediately if logging delays care or staff report pressure to backfill entries. Infection Prevention owns the pause and restart. The ward educator takes private correction requests, and the kiosk administrator fixes record errors. Pause for insufficient audit coverage and investigate a rise in kiosk entries without a corresponding rise in verified logging. Budget up to 45 minutes a week for the pilot lead, using the scheduled audits and existing huddles.

### Run it and end it

Give all three wards the fixes and feedback in month 1. Randomize the ward order and start the eight-week reward windows in months 2, 3 and 4, aligned with the audit cycle. Compare each ward with its own feedback-only period and, where windows overlap, with wards still on feedback alone. Three wards provide an early signal about what the reward adds.

After each ward’s second reward-period audit, deliver any earned meal and close the reward window. Keep the kiosk’s direct-to-log default and check verified logging again at the first monthly audit after the reward ends. Use that same rate to decide the kiosk’s future, subject to the compliance guardrail. **Keep** the workflow if the ward still meets its target. **Change** it if logging remains above baseline but falls short of target. **Drop** the incentive approach if logging returns to baseline or lower, and review whether the kiosk is worth the work it adds. If the follow-up sample is too small, defer the decision to the next routine audit.
