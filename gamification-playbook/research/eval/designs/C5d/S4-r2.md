## Knowledge-base adoption pilot

**Reward one useful knowledge contribution per resolved ticket.** An agent earns one credit when they resolve a ticket and link an article that helped answer it, or write a new article that passes the normal knowledge-base review and is linked to that ticket. The team earns a leader-hosted lunch during paid work time if it reaches **30% qualifying coverage** across its four reward weeks and passes the quality check. Announce the lunch, the target and the scoring rule at launch. Keep the existing CSAT bonus unchanged.

### Before launch

Give agents paid time to try the workflow on real tickets. Ask a few agents across shifts and queues where searching, linking or writing slows them down. Fix the biggest friction first. Put article search and linking in the resolution screen and make “no applicable article” a one-click choice. Tell agents what changed based on their feedback. Keep that choice available throughout the pilot so agents can resolve a ticket promptly when the knowledge base has a gap.

### Measurement and feedback

Use existing ticket-resolution and knowledge-base records. Divide the number of qualifying resolved tickets by all resolved support tickets, excluding only spam, tests and duplicate records already identified by the ticket system. A ticket resolved without an article stays in the denominator, including when no article applies. Track the share with no applicable article at team level to surface gaps in the knowledge base. Use the reported 12% as the starting reference and remeasure it under the pilot’s counting rule.

Count each ticket ID once. A reopen reverses the credit until the next resolution. Linking two articles still earns one credit. Reusing a good article on two distinct tickets can earn two credits. A draft earns credit once it passes review and is linked. Give agents a private confirmation at resolution or when the draft qualifies. Show each agent their own count and a weekly goal scaled to their usual ticket volume. A missed week simply resets the weekly goal. Show the team’s coverage and progress toward lunch in its existing weekly channel. Managers see team totals only, and pilot data stays out of performance reviews.

### Timing and decision

Give everyone the workflow fixes and private feedback for two weeks. Then randomly assign comparable existing teams to start four reward weeks either immediately or two weeks later. Use the two-week overlap between rewarded and waiting teams to estimate what the lunch adds beyond the shared fixes and feedback. At each team’s finish, award any earned lunch and end prize tracking. Keep article suggestions visible by default when agents resolve a ticket.

Use **qualifying coverage four weeks after each team’s prizes end** as the decision metric. Keep the workflow if pooled coverage is at least **10 percentage points above the two-week post-fix baseline**, revise it if it is **5 to under 10 points above**, and retire the incentive programme if the gain is **under 5 points**. Use the staggered comparison when deciding whether the reward is worth repeating.

### What could go wrong

- **Agents attach irrelevant links or write thin duplicates.** The support operations lead reviews 10 randomly selected credited tickets each week using existing QA access. If two or more fail for relevance, accuracy or duplication, pause reward scoring for the affected team and review a fresh sample before resuming. A confirmed customer-data exposure in an article triggers an immediate pause and the existing incident process.
- **Agents delay a reply or close early to get credit.** Start with linking during normal resolution and let article approval happen afterward. Check existing CSAT and reopen reports weekly. Investigate a five-point CSAT drop against the post-fix baseline once there are at least 30 responses, and pause rewards if ticket review ties the decline to the programme.
- **A count is wrong or a queue has few relevant articles.** Agents can flag errors privately to the support operations lead, who corrects counts and communicates the result. Compare article availability across queues before assigning start dates. Use gap data to prioritize knowledge-base fixes and size personal goals to the agent’s volume.

One support operations lead owns the weekly export, QA sample, corrections and team update. Budget 45 minutes a week using existing reports and a saved sample query.
