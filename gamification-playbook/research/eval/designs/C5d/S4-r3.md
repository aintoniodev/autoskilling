## Make useful knowledge part of closing a ticket

Run a six-week incentive for each support team. When a team reaches **40% article coverage across any two consecutive weeks**, it earns a paid team lunch with the head of Support. Announce the lunch at launch and schedule it in shifts so everyone can attend. The existing CSAT bonus stays in place. Every ticket remains an opportunity to capture knowledge, with 40% as the first milestone from today’s 12%.

### What counts

Tell agents at launch: **“A resolved ticket counts once when its record includes a published article that helps solve the customer’s issue, whether you linked it or wrote it.”**

Use the ticket system’s existing resolution records and article links. Divide qualifying tickets by all distinct resolved tickets, including tickets handled the old way. Extra links and repeated closes earn no additional credit. A draft earns credit when it’s published and linked within seven days of resolution. Finalize the weekly tally after seven days so a ticket reopened during that window can count when it is resolved again. If there’s no useful article to link and no reusable knowledge to publish, agents can close the ticket as usual.

Before launch, the Support Ops lead tests the tally against a duplicate link, a reopened ticket, a draft that gets published, and a resolution without a link. The lead corrects disputed counts privately and records the correction in the next tally. Use existing ticket and article records for the programme.

### What people see

Give agents immediate confirmation in the close flow that their article link was saved. Their private view shows credited tickets and a goal scaled to their own volume, such as two qualifying tickets out of five or eight out of twenty. Agents can pick up on the next ticket after a missed week. Managers see team totals only. Post team progress in the usual support channel, and keep individual counts out of performance reviews and CSAT bonus calculations.

### Rollout and ending

Before rewards start, ask agents from different shifts to show where finding or writing an article slows them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of paid practice. Fix the biggest bottleneck, such as poor search or a slow approval queue, and tell agents what changed because of their feedback. Give every team the workflow improvements and private feedback at the same time. Start the six-week reward window two weeks apart by team and compare teams on the same calendar weeks to estimate what the lunch adds.

Before the first reward window ends, put article search and linking directly in the normal close flow, with suggested articles where available. Keep that workflow after the lunches end. Decide what to do next using **article coverage over the four weeks after each team’s rewards end**. At 40% or higher, keep the workflow and feedback as standard practice. At 25–39%, fix the biggest remaining friction and measure again. Below 25%, retire this incentive design and investigate why coverage fell before testing another reward.

### What could go wrong

- **Irrelevant links or duplicate articles.** The QA lead samples 10 credited tickets across active teams each week through the existing QA process. If two or more contain irrelevant, inaccurate, duplicative, or privacy-unsafe content, the QA lead pauses reward qualification for the affected teams, gets the content corrected, and resumes after a fresh sample passes. Remove any exposed customer information immediately.
- **Slower closes or pressure on CSAT.** Use the existing QA review to check whether agents are delaying resolutions to finish articles. If CSAT drops five percentage points from the team’s pre-launch baseline for two consecutive weeks with at least 20 survey responses across those weeks, the Support lead pauses that team’s reward qualification and checks whether article work contributed. Agents can close the ticket first and finish the article during paid time.
- **Too much administration.** Have Support Ops use the existing report to publish totals and handle count disputes in one weekly pass. Budget 30 minutes for the QA sample and 20 minutes for totals and corrections.
