## Eight-week safety checklist pilot

**Fix the workflow first.** Spend two weeks with union stewards and technicians from different shifts, including crews working in low-signal areas. Give them paid time to try the checklist on real jobs. Fix slow sign-in, missing job details and offline failures before starting rewards. Make the checklist the first action on the mobile job screen, prefill job details and leave safety answers blank. Publish what technicians flagged and what changed. Agree on the reward, data access and correction process with union reps before announcing the pilot.

**Reward a real pre-job safety check.** Tell crews the rule in one sentence. “Your crew gets credit once per job when you complete the digital safety checklist on site before work starts.” Use the existing job ID, checklist completion time and dispatch or work-order start record. Before launch, verify that the start record reflects when work actually begins and that offline submissions preserve their original completion time. If either record is unreliable, fix it before using it for rewards.

Measure the percentage of started jobs requiring a checklist that have a qualifying digital completion. Exclude cancelled jobs. A duplicate earns no extra credit, a voided submission loses credit, and a checklist entered after work starts earns no credit. An approved paper fallback still satisfies the safety process and stays in the denominator so outages remain visible. Never delay a stop-work call to complete the app. If a hazard is found, follow the existing stop-work and escalation procedure.

**Show progress where crews already work.** After saving, show “Pre-job checklist saved for Job 4821,” or “Saved offline, credit pending sync.” Each technician sees their own count and a private milestone based on the jobs they actually work. For someone with five jobs, the first milestone is four completed digitally, with a checklist still required on all five. An absence resets nothing. Show crew progress at the regular safety huddle and combine small crews where totals could identify someone. Managers see crew totals only. Pilot counts stay out of performance reviews. Use neither named rankings nor streaks.

**Announce the reward up front.** Every crew that reaches 80% during its four reward weeks earns a company-paid meal on paid time, with shift coverage so everyone can attend. At the meal, the service lead thanks the crew for specific improvements they made to the workflow. Every crew can qualify, and the reward depends on checklist completion even when the checklist finds a hazard.

**Stagger the start and end on schedule.** In weeks 1–2, give everyone the fixes and feedback and establish the baseline using the pilot’s counting rule. Assign comparable crews by lot to start rewards in week 3 or week 5. The first wave earns through week 6 and the second through week 8. Compare the waves in weeks 3–4 to see what the reward adds while both have the same workflow. Keep the app default and feedback after week 8. Use one decision metric four weeks after the last reward ends, the company-wide qualifying digital completion rate. At **80% or higher**, keep the workflow and retire the prize. At **60–79%**, fix the biggest remaining barrier and recheck in four weeks. Below **60%**, drop the incentive design and revisit the workflow with technicians and stewards. The safety checklist remains required throughout.

**Watch for rushed checks, inflated counts and uneven access.** Before launch, test a duplicate, a void, a late entry, a paper fallback and an offline sync. Each week the safety lead reviews six credited jobs using existing safety records, looking for issues such as a recorded hazard with no required follow-up. **One material safety miss in the sample pauses reward sign-off** for the affected crew while the safety lead and steward review it. Technicians can raise a wrong count privately with the steward or safety lead. The safety lead decides disputed credit with the steward, and the app admin corrects the tally before sign-off. Automate tallying and budget 45 minutes a week for the sample, corrections and huddle figures.
