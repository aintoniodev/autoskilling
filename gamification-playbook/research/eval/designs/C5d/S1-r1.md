## Pre-job safety checklist pilot

**Reward.** Each crew earns one extra paid hour off per technician when at least 90% of its eligible jobs have a completed digital safety checklist before work starts over its four-week reward window. Tell crews the reward and the rule at launch. Thank each qualifying crew at its regular safety huddle and let technicians schedule the hour through the usual leave process. Everyone assigned to a qualifying crew shares the reward, including technicians on leave. The safety checklist remains required on every job covered by the safety procedure.

**Before launch.** Have the union steward, safety lead and a small group of technicians from different shifts and routes agree on the rules, privacy limits and correction process. Give technicians paid time to try the app on real routes. Fix slow loading, missing job details and poor offline access before the reward starts. Show crews what changed because of their feedback. Put the checklist first when a technician opens a job, prefill job details and leave safety answers blank. Keep the approved fallback available when the app cannot be used.

**Measurement.** Divide the number of eligible jobs with an on-time digital checklist by all eligible jobs that actually started, including jobs completed through the fallback. Use the existing job ID, checklist completion timestamp and dispatch work-start record. Verify that work-start records are reliable before launching the reward. Count each job once. Exclude cancellations before work starts and jobs outside the existing checklist requirement. A late checklist or one voided without a valid replacement before work starts earns no credit. An offline checklist counts if the app already records its original completion time and it syncs within two working days. A checklist that identifies a hazard counts if the technician follows the existing stop-work or escalation procedure. Never reward a clean result or a low incident count.

**What people see.** After submission, the app confirms that the checklist was saved and shows whether it is waiting to sync. Technicians can see their own count and eligible jobs. The regular crew huddle shows the crew’s aggregate rate, job count and progress toward 90%. Managers get crew totals only. Keep reward data out of performance reviews and discipline, with no named leaderboard. Technicians can report a wrong count privately through the existing support channel. The dispatch systems owner corrects verified record errors, and the safety lead and union steward resolve disputed reward decisions before payout.

**Timing and ending.** Give all crews the fixes, paid practice and feedback for two weeks. Then randomly assign comparable crews to start their four-week reward windows two weeks apart. Compare their rates during those two weeks to estimate what the reward adds once both groups have the improved app. After each crew’s window, end the reward and leave the checklist first in the app. Four weeks after the last reward window closes, review one measure: the share of eligible jobs with an on-time digital checklist. Set the decision bands before launch. At **90% or higher**, keep the workflow and close the reward programme. At **75–89%**, fix the largest remaining barrier and remeasure. Below **75%**, retire the reward design and reopen the workflow with technicians and the union. The checklist requirement stays in place.

**What could go wrong.** Technicians might rush answers, delay recording job starts or feel pressure to work through a hazard. Use the existing safety review to spot-check 10 credited jobs per week across crews. Pause reward credit for the affected crew if two of the 10 have missing or implausible safety answers, or immediately if any review finds work proceeded despite a stop-work requirement. The safety lead investigates with the union steward and verifies the correction before credit resumes. Report fallback jobs separately so a connectivity problem is visible, and extend an affected crew’s window by the time lost to a verified widespread outage. Use an automated weekly tally and the existing safety review so one coordinator can run the programme in under an hour per week.
