# One-working-day code review pilot

**Give every ready PR a reviewer with time to review it.** Start with a 30-minute working session with engineers who regularly open and review PRs. Ask where reviews wait, what makes them risky to pick up, and what would make a shared reward worth earning. Give the reviewer on rotation a protected 30-minute block each working day and a named backup for absences. Put the oldest unanswered PR first in the team’s existing queue. Show the team what changed in response to their feedback. If the rotation still lacks capacity after two weeks, fix coverage before starting rewards.

## What earns the reward

Tell the team at launch: **“When 80% of your group’s ready PRs receive a substantive first review within one working day over two weeks, everyone in the group earns a paid hour to use as they choose.”** Confirm the reward with engineers during setup, announce it before counting starts, and have the engineering lead award it in person at an existing team meeting. Each group can earn it once. A missed day or week carries no personal penalty.

A substantive review is a submitted approval or change request that names what the reviewer checked or identifies a concrete issue the author can act on. An empty approval, emoji, or “looking now” leaves the PR in the queue. Authors can keep working while waiting, and reviewers can request changes without losing credit.

## How it is measured

Use the PR system’s existing ready-for-review and submitted-review events. Start the clock when a human-authored PR first becomes ready. One working day is eight hours within the team’s agreed working hours, excluding weekends and team holidays. The backup covers individual absences. Count each PR once, at the first qualifying review by someone other than the author. Reassignment, reopening, and new commits do not reset the clock. PRs that reach the deadline without a qualifying review count as misses. Exclude genuine withdrawals before the deadline and spot-check them for close-and-reopen workarounds.

Before launch, replay the rule against a Friday-afternoon PR, a duplicate approval, a dismissed review, an old-style review delivered in chat, and a PR still unanswered at its deadline. The rotation owner handles disputed counts, checks the PR record, and corrects the tally. A dismissed review that failed the stated rule loses credit.

Reviewers use the existing submission confirmation and can see their own queue and deadlines. The team sees the aggregate percentage and eligible PR count in its existing engineering channel. Managers get those same totals. Keep individual scores, rankings, and pilot data out of performance reviews.

## Timing and what happens afterward

- **Weeks 1–2.** Give everyone the protected time, queue defaults, and feedback. Record the baseline percentage under the same rule. The current median of 2.6 days gives context but does not establish the baseline percentage.
- **Weeks 3–6.** Use two groups based on existing review ownership, if the team has reasonably distinct queues. Randomly choose which group starts its two-week reward window first. Keep cross-group help available and treat the comparison as directional if reviewers overlap. If there is one shared queue, use one team-wide two-week window and compare it with the baseline.
- **Four weeks after rewards end.** Keep the rotation, backup, and oldest-first queue in place. Use the four reward-free weeks to decide. At **80% or higher**, keep the setup. At **65–79%**, adjust coverage and recheck. **Below 65%**, drop the pilot target and revisit review capacity with the team.

## What could go wrong

The rotation owner samples five submitted first reviews each week for evidence of a real check or actionable feedback. If two fail, the engineering lead pauses rewards, corrects the tally, and addresses the cause before resuming. Look for rushed approvals, easy PRs jumping the queue, and authors leaving work in draft or moving it to chat. Review those patterns through the same sample and the existing team meeting. Budget 45 minutes a week for the tally, sample, and corrections.
