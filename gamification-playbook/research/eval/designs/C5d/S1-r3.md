## Six-week pre-job safety checklist programme

**Fix the field workflow first.** Have the safety lead and a union steward spend paid time with technicians from different shifts and routes. Walk through a job with poor reception, a shared device, and a last-minute dispatch change. Fix the barriers they find, give everyone paid practice time, and tell crews what changed because of their feedback. Put the checklist first on the job screen and prefill the job details. Keep the approved fallback available when the app fails. If neither route is available, follow the existing stop-work procedure.

**Reward completed checks.** Each crew earns one hour of paid release time per member when it reaches **70% of eligible jobs with a valid digital checklist completed before work starts** across its four-week reward window. Announce the exact reward at launch. Let crews choose from paid release slots with dispatch coverage, and have the field-service lead thank each qualifying crew in person. Everyone on the crew shares the reward, including people who had few jobs or were on leave. A missed week leaves the next job as a fresh opportunity.

**Use the job records already in place.** The denominator is every started job that requires a checklist under the existing safety procedure, including jobs completed using the fallback. The numerator is one valid checklist per job ID, with all required fields completed and a saved time before the recorded work start. An offline checklist counts after sync if the app preserves a reliable original save time. Exclude cancelled jobs that never started. Count duplicate submissions once, remove voided submissions, and give no digital credit for a late entry or a paper fallback. Show fallback use separately at crew level so outages are visible. Before launch, test these cases against real dispatch records. If the existing records cannot establish the order reliably, fix that before tying pay to the measure.

**Show technicians their own progress.** After a checklist saves, confirm that it was recorded for that job and show whether it counted. Mark offline submissions as pending until sync. Give each technician a private goal based on their assigned volume, such as seven of their next ten eligible jobs. Post crew totals and progress toward 70% in the existing safety huddle or dispatch channel. Managers see crew totals only. Agree with the union before launch that programme data stays out of performance reviews and that individual counts stay private. A technician can take a disputed count to the steward, the safety lead decides eligibility, and the app owner corrects the record.

**Stagger the reward and check what lasts.** Give all crews the app fixes, paid practice, and feedback together. Record two weeks of baseline data after those changes. Randomly assign comparable crews to start their four-week reward windows in weeks 1 and 3, so the last window closes in week 6. Compare the groups during the first two weeks to estimate what the reward adds. Announce the end date at launch and keep the checklist first in the app after the payout. Four weeks after each crew’s reward window ends, compare the company-wide valid pre-job digital completion rate with the post-fix baseline. Use that one measure to decide the programme’s future. At **70% or higher**, keep the workflow and end the reward as scheduled. Below 70% with improvement over baseline, fix the remaining barriers. At or below baseline, drop the incentive design.

**Watch for rushed checks, shifted timestamps, and peer pressure.** Each week the safety lead samples eight credited jobs across crews using existing job records, looking for missing hazard controls, work proceeding despite an unresolved hazard, or conflicting timestamps. A single critical safety defect or two invalid credits in the sample pauses reward decisions. The safety lead and steward review the cause, the app owner fixes any counting error, and the safety lead clears the restart. Crew-only reporting and shared eligibility reduce pressure on individuals. Use the existing weekly export and huddle so one coordinator can handle the count, sample, and update in under an hour in a routine week.
