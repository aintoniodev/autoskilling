## Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

**Reward accurate recording of real hand hygiene.** Each ward can earn one hospital-funded team meal, with food available to every shift. Announce it at launch and have the nursing director deliver it at shift huddles with specific thanks for what the ward improved. Each ward has its own target and can earn the meal. There are no individual quotas or rankings.

**Make logging easy first.** In the first month, walk through the workflow with nurses from day, night and weekend shifts. Give them 15 minutes of paid time to practise. Fix kiosk placement, sign-in delays and any workflow that pulls nurses away from care. Share the fixes at huddles. Make the ward preselected and recording accessible from the first screen. If logging still interrupts care, fix the workflow before the reward window opens.

**Use the existing monthly audit.** During routine observation blocks, the infection prevention team tallies completed hand-hygiene actions and which of those it can unambiguously match to a kiosk entry using the existing timestamp and moment category. Keep the tally at ward level, with no names, badge IDs or patient identifiers. Count one valid entry per observed action. Continue the existing compliance measure separately: observed actions performed divided by all observed opportunities. Spread audit blocks across comparable shifts, keep the schedule independent of reward dates and show the sample sizes with the results. [WHO hand-hygiene monitoring guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

**Set the target before rewards start.** The award rule is “Your ward earns the meal when the audit confirms that you reached your logging target and hand-hygiene compliance held steady.” Set the target 15 percentage points above the ward’s setup-month logging rate, capped at 90%. Wards already at or above 90% aim to maintain their baseline. Pool the two reward-month audits and require at least 40 observed completed actions for a payout decision. If the sample is smaller, defer the decision to the next routine audit without extending the incentive window. These are pilot thresholds.

**Show nurses what they can act on.** The kiosk confirms each save immediately and offers an undo. At the usual ward huddle, share the ward’s latest audited logging rate, target, compliance rate and sample size. Managers see ward totals only. Pilot data stay out of performance reviews. A missed shift or week has no penalty. A nurse can resume with the next eligible moment.

**Run two reward months per ward.** Give all three wards the workflow fixes and feedback in month one, then draw the rollout order. Start reward windows in months two, three and four so each ward gets two months. Use the stagger to see whether logging improves when the reward starts or had already improved with the fixes. With three wards, treat differences as a pilot signal. Keep the kiosk as the default after the prizes end and check each ward again at its first routine audit four to six weeks later. The decision measure is the **audited share of completed actions accurately logged**. Keep the rollout if the ward still meets its target, revise it if logging remains above baseline but below target, and drop the incentive if logging returns to baseline or worse. The hospital’s hand-hygiene requirements continue throughout.

**Protect care and handle disputes.** Before launch, test a double tap, an undo, an entry with no observed action, two overlapping actions that cannot be matched confidently, and a completed action during an outage. A duplicate earns at most one count, an undone or ambiguous entry earns none, and an outage is flagged as a workflow failure. If audited compliance falls by more than five percentage points from baseline, or staff report that logging delays care, the infection prevention lead pauses awards and investigates. If routine audits cannot verify entries without tracking individuals, hold the award and fix the measurement. The kiosk owner corrects disputed counts, and the infection prevention lead signs off on the result. One coordinator posts the audit update and handles exceptions in under an hour a week.
