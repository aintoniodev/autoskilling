## Six-week knowledge-base rally

**Reward one useful article on a resolved ticket.** A ticket earns one credit when the agent links an existing article or writes an approved article that accurately documents the fix. Reuse and authorship earn the same credit. Agents can close tickets while a new article awaits approval, and credit applies to the original resolution date once approved. The existing CSAT bonus stays in place. Agents should resolve the customer’s issue first, then link or document the fix during paid wrap-up time.

**Make the workflow easier before launch.** Spend two weeks with agents from different queues and shifts to find what slows them down. Give everyone 30 minutes of protected practice. Fix the biggest blocker and show agents what changed because of their feedback. Put article search and linking in the resolution screen, suggest relevant articles where the tool supports it, and provide a short draft template for missing fixes. Resolve any access or approval bottleneck before starting rewards.

**Count tickets using existing records.** The denominator is all resolved support tickets, excluding spam, merged duplicates, and categories already marked as ineligible for knowledge capture, such as sensitive cases. Agree on these categories before launch. Count each ticket ID once. A duplicate link adds no credit, a removed link reverses it, a reopened ticket is counted only after re-resolution, and an eligible ticket closed without an article stays in the denominator. Test these cases against the report before launch. Use the help desk’s existing ticket and article records. If a valid link or approval cannot be verified there, fix the integration before starting the rally.

**Show progress privately and reward the team.** Confirm credit in the resolution screen and show agents their own progress toward one credited ticket per four eligible resolutions during their team’s reward window. Adjust the count to their actual ticket volume so leave or a quiet queue does not create a catch-up penalty. Post team totals and percentages in the usual support channel each week. Managers see team aggregates only, and rally data stays out of performance reviews. Announce the reward at launch. Each team that reaches **25%** over its four-week window earns **one extra paid hour off per team member**, recognized at a huddle and scheduled across shifts to maintain coverage.

**Stagger the reward and keep the workflow.** Give everyone the workflow fixes and private confirmation at the same time. Assign existing teams to two groups with comparable ticket mix and starting adoption. The first group earns rewards in weeks 1–4 and the second in weeks 3–6. Compare the groups during weeks 1–2 to estimate what the reward adds beyond the fixes and feedback. Make article search the default next step in the resolution screen before rewards end, with an easy skip for exceptions. Four weeks after the final reward window, use the share of eligible resolved tickets with a qualifying article to decide what to do. At **25% or higher**, keep the workflow and feedback. At **15–24%**, fix the largest remaining blocker. Below **15%**, drop the rally and revisit whether the workflow saves agents time.

**Watch for weak links and customer harm.** Each week, the support ops lead samples 10 credited tickets across queues and checks whether the article would help another agent resolve the same issue. If **two or more fail**, pause reward accrual for the affected teams, retain valid credits, and resume after a fresh sample passes. Agents can flag a missed or reversed credit privately, and the support ops lead resolves it within two business days. Review team CSAT and reopen rates alongside adoption. A **three-percentage-point CSAT drop** or **three-point rise in reopens** against the four-week prelaunch baseline triggers a review of ticket mix and whether article work is delaying or weakening resolutions. Pause rewards if that review finds customer harm. Use the existing report and cap routine administration at 45 minutes a week.
