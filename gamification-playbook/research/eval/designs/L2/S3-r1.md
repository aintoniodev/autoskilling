## 1. Diagnosis

Test whether nurses can log hand hygiene without interrupting care. Before launch, walk a routine bedside case from hand hygiene through kiosk entry, then walk an urgent-care case where the kiosk is unavailable or unsafe to reach. Time the routine entry and ask day- and night-shift nurses what it saves them. The proposed benefit is a quick record they can check and correct before handover.

The ward charge nurse owns blocker reports. Clinical IT triages kiosk and access issues within one shift and posts the fix or workaround within two working days. In urgent care or during downtime, perform hand hygiene as usual and skip the kiosk if necessary. If logging adds work without a benefit nurses value, or most opportunities lack a safe logging path, fix the workflow before starting the incentive.

## 2. Credit rule

After performing hand hygiene at a hospital-defined opportunity, the nurse records it at the first safe pause in care. One team credit requires one accurate, same-shift kiosk entry matched to an independently observed, correctly timed hand-hygiene action. Multiple indications covered by the same action count once. Monthly observers count all sampled opportunities, including missed hygiene, regardless of kiosk use. [WHO hand-hygiene observation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Correct hand hygiene, accurate log, unique audit match | One credit |
| Two entries for the same action | One credit |
| Entry withdrawn or corrected | Recheck the match and remove any unsupported credit |
| Entry submitted after shift end or timestamp edited | Use the original submission time and edit history. Late entries earn zero |
| Correct hand hygiene with no kiosk entry, including downtime | Counts toward observed hygiene compliance. Zero logging credit |
| Missed hygiene, with or without a log | Counts as a missed opportunity. Zero credit |
| Entry outside the audit sample or match is ambiguous | Nurse can see the saved entry. Zero verified credit |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at the kiosk:** “After cleaning your hands, log the moment here when care allows. You can check and correct your entries before handover. The team reward ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation:** “Saved for this shift. Tap ‘Correct’ if the time or moment is wrong.”
- **Private progress:** “2 of your 3 practice entries saved. Log your next completed moment when care allows.” Nurses choose a practice goal of one to three based on their shift. The goal is a starting point. Nurses with no safe opportunities see “No practice goal this shift.”
- **Restart:** “Start with your next completed moment when care allows.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries. Staff and managers see pooled three-ward logging coverage and observed hygiene compliance against baseline. Suppress any breakdown that could identify a nurse. Only the designated audit reviewer can access temporary nurse-level matching codes for verification or nurse-requested corrections. Log that access, delete the pilot linkage 30 days after the final decision, and prohibit disciplinary use. Collect no patient identifiers.

## 5. Reward

Test a hospital-funded team meal with nurses from every shift before launch. Offer equivalent meal vouchers to staff who cannot attend. Everyone on the three wards shares the reward if verified logging coverage closes at least a quarter of the gap between baseline and 100%, and observed hygiene compliance stays within five percentage points of baseline on every ward. Fund kiosk fixes from day one and post what changed.

## 6. Operator card

Infection Prevention owns scoring. Before attaching stakes, confirm privacy approval, reliable kiosk edit history, and one-to-one matching by temporary nurse code, action time, and indication. If matching fails, run a feasibility pilot without a prize.

Use the existing monthly audits and kiosk records. Sample at least 30 opportunities per ward per month across day, night, and weekend shifts using the same sampling plan in each phase. Logging coverage is verified credits divided by all observed completed hygiene actions. Hygiene compliance is observed completed actions divided by all observed opportunities.

Budget two hours a week for reconciliation and corrections, plus any extra observation needed. Nurses can flag errors privately to Infection Prevention. The lead freezes scoring and reward release if a ward falls more than five points below baseline, fewer than 30 opportunities are observed on a ward that month, or more than 5% of kiosk entries in sampled sessions are duplicate or demonstrably inaccurate. Investigate and correct before resuming. Address any care delay immediately.

## 7. End and decision

Use January–February 2027 as a supported kiosk baseline, March–April for the reward, and May–June for follow-up without prizes. By April 30, put the one-tap form first on the kiosk and retain corrections, private feedback, and the safe fallback. Release the meal after April verification.

Expand only if May–June still meets both reward thresholds with adequate samples and no unresolved care delays. If coverage fades while hygiene stays steady, simplify logging and retest. Stop the incentive if hygiene worsens beyond the guardrail or logging disrupts care. The baseline comparison supports a feasibility decision. Audit visibility can change behavior. [WHO guidance on observation bias](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)

## 8. Main risks

- **Care interruption.** Nurses queue or detour to the kiosk during care.
- **Inflated adoption.** Nurses duplicate entries or log mainly when observers are present.
- **Surveillance concerns.** Nurses infer that linked records could be used to rank or discipline them.
