## 1. Diagnosis

**Working hypothesis · Some agents haven’t tried the new workflow. Others may be blocked by access, article gaps, or publishing delays.** The reported 12% is a starting point. Recalculate it from January 4–31 ticket history using the rules below.

Before launch, shadow two real resolutions with agents. For a routine password reset, check whether the agent can find and link the approved article from the close screen faster than composing the answer from scratch. For a novel issue with no approved article, close the ticket normally and test the knowledge team’s review queue. The Support Ops lead owns access and workflow blockers, acknowledges them within one business day, and fixes them or posts a workaround within two. Hold launch if most cases are blocked or the supported path adds material work.

## 2. Credit rule

At ticket resolution, an agent earns **one credit per ticket** for linking a relevant, current, approved article or writing an article that is approved, published, and linked within seven days of resolution. Attribute credit to the resolving agent. A draft gets a pending receipt. Agents can close any ticket on time and route a knowledge gap to the review queue.

Measure verified coverage as credited tickets divided by eligible resolved tickets. Start from the ticket system’s resolution log, including tickets with no knowledge-base activity. Exclude only predefined categories such as spam, merged duplicates, and issues whose details cannot be safely documented. Report exclusions and all-ticket coverage alongside eligible coverage. Missing articles and tool failures stay in the eligible denominator.

| Case | Counting rule |
|---|---|
| Routine resolution with a relevant approved article | One credit |
| Two links or a duplicate event | One credit for the canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopens or article approval is withdrawn during verification | Remove provisional credit and recheck on valid re-resolution |
| Ticket due date is edited or article is published late | Use the recorded resolution timestamp. Credit closes after seven days |
| Agent resolves the ticket using the old workflow | Eligible ticket stays in the denominator. Award credit if it meets the same article rule |
| Ticket is omitted from the knowledge-base feed | Recover it from the resolution log and count it as uncredited unless verified |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at resolution** · “Search for the fix you used and link the approved article. It can save you from typing the same steps next time. If there’s a gap, close the ticket and send it to Knowledge Review. Rewards end February 28.”
- **Confirmation** · “Password Reset linked to ticket #4821. One provisional credit, verified within seven days.” For a draft, show “Sent to Knowledge Review. Credit follows approval, publication, and linking.”
- **Progress** · “2 of 3 verified this week. On your next eligible ticket, search for the fix you used.” Set the weekly target at 30% of each agent’s typical eligible volume, rounded up and capped at actual opportunities. With zero, show “No eligible tickets this week.” With one, the target is one.
- **Restart** · “This week starts fresh. On your next resolution, check whether the fix has an article.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own credits, pending items, and corrections. A weekly support-channel post shows the participating team’s verified rate against its own baseline. Managers see aggregates. Suppress any breakdown with fewer than five contributing agents or that exposes an individual. Grant named, logged, time-limited access for requested coaching or disputes. Calculate the existing CSAT bonus separately under its own formula.

## 5. Motivator

If the participating team reaches **30% verified coverage over February** and clears the quality gate, every team member gets **45 minutes of paid flex time**, scheduled with queue coverage. Confirm appeal with several agents and secure staffing approval before launch. Support Ops reserves time for fixes and posts what agents reported and what shipped.

## 6. Operator card

Support Ops owns the ticket and article-history reconciliation and accepts corrections privately by ticket ID. An independent Support QA reviewer samples 20 credited tickets and 10 uncredited or excluded tickets per arm each week. Check article relevance, accuracy, sensitive content, and eligibility. At **three material defects in 20 credited tickets** or **one sensitive-data exposure**, the Support Ops lead pauses awards, corrects affected records, and requires a clean recheck before resuming. Budget four staff-hours weekly, plus article review. Confirm audit history, reviewer capacity, and paid-time approval before launch.

## 7. End and decision

Run February 1–28, 2027. Pair comparable support pods and randomly assign one per pair to incentives. Give both arms the same workflow fixes, private feedback, and article-first close screen with a skip option. Verify and award by March 8. Keep workflow and feedback in place. Measure March 9–April 5.

Scale if pilot coverage reached 30%, follow-up coverage stays at least 10 percentage points above the comparison arm after baseline adjustment, no quality pause remains open, and CSAT falls by no more than two percentage points relative to comparison. Check CSAT response counts before interpreting the result. Revise if the lift fades or the comparison is inconclusive. Stop if quality or CSAT worsens beyond the gate. With too few comparable pods, treat the pilot as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Risks

- Agents link marginal articles to earn credit. Check relevance independently.
- Review queues or extra close steps consume time and put CSAT at risk. Keep closure available and track review turnaround.
- The reward creates a February bump that disappears. Base the scale decision on real tickets after awards end.
