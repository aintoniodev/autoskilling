## 1. Diagnosis

The reported digital completion rate is about 35%. Recalculate it from dispatch records before using it as the baseline. Shadow one technician through a supported job and another through a job with poor signal, missing access, or an incorrect template. Compare time and rework with the approved fallback, and ask what happens when the checklist flags a hazard. If the app adds work or risk, fix that first. If most jobs are blocked, defer the pilot. If the app works and technicians simply haven’t tried it, run the pilot.

The field operations lead owns blockers, responds within one business day, and posts fixes in the crew briefing. Assume reliable dispatch and checklist history, an approved fallback, and union agreement on paid time, data use, and the reward before launch.

## 2. Credit rule

When a technician opens an assigned job, award **one crew credit per dispatch job** for completing the required digital checklist before starting work, with any hazards handled under the existing safety procedure. A completed checklist that correctly records a stop-work decision also earns credit. Give immediate provisional confirmation and verify credits before they count toward the reward.

The crew score is verified credits divided by all app-supported jobs that reached a pre-work assessment opportunity, whether or not a checklist was submitted. Keep blocked jobs and fallback use visible in aggregate alongside the score. Use dispatch records and independently logged outages to determine exclusions.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal job or properly recorded stop-work | One credit for a qualified checklist. |
| Duplicate, reassignment, or reopen | One credit total per dispatch job ID. |
| Checklist recalled or invalidated | Remove provisional credit. Correct the score after review. Paid time already awarded stays paid. |
| Late submission or edited schedule | Verify against original completion and actual start records. A schedule edit cannot make a late checklist timely. Accept delayed offline sync when the original completion time is verifiable. |
| Approved paper fallback | Preserve the safety record. Zero digital credit. Keep the job in the denominator unless independently verified as blocked. |
| Checklist omitted or job missing from the app | Reconcile against dispatch. Keep app-supported jobs in the denominator, with zero credit. Exclude independently verified cancellations before the assessment opportunity. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at job open:** “Open the safety checklist before you start. The job details are already filled in. If the app is unavailable, use the approved fallback. Follow the usual stop-work procedure if you find a hazard. The crew challenge ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation:** “Checklist saved for job 4821. It will count once verified. If you recorded a stop-work decision, follow the usual procedure.”
- **Progress:** “You’ve completed 3 of the 4 eligible jobs you’ve had this week in the app. Open the checklist on your next assigned job.” Show “No eligible jobs yet” at zero. With just one opportunity, show its status without a percentage or minimum target.
- **Restart:** “Start fresh with your next eligible job. Open the checklist before work begins.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing crew briefing shows weekly crew completion against its own baseline, with no names or rankings. Combine or suppress identifiable groups. Managers see aggregates. A designated reviewer can access an individual record for a correction, dispute, or technician-requested coaching. Log access, close it when the case ends, and exclude programme data from discipline. Agree retention and access with union representatives before launch.

## 5. Reward

Test the reward and target with technicians and union reps before launch. Proposed reward is **30 minutes of paid release time for every crew member**, including people with few or no eligible jobs, if the crew finishes **15 percentage points above its verified baseline** and clears the quality review. Schedule coverage so the time is usable. Use the weekly briefing to show progress and report which app problems were fixed.

## 6. Operator card

Field operations reconciles dispatch, checklist timestamps and revisions, and the blocker log. The safety lead independently samples 10 credited jobs each week across pilot crews, checking timing and hazard handling against existing records. **Pause scoring and hold unpaid rewards** if two sampled jobs fail or one review confirms work began despite a recorded stop-work condition. Resume after the safety lead verifies the fix. Technicians can dispute a credit through the existing support channel or a union rep. Field operations responds within two business days. Budget three staff-hours weekly for reconciliation, review, and corrections.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline **January 4–31, 2027**. Pilot **February 1–28**. Follow-up **March 1–28**, with no new rewards. Randomly assign comparable crews to the programme or to the same app support and private feedback alone. Before February 28, make the prefilled checklist the first step in both groups, with the approved fallback available.

Scale if the programme crews reach **60%** in follow-up and improve **10 percentage points more than comparison crews** from baseline, with no unresolved quality pause. Change and retest if the pilot lifts use but misses the follow-up thresholds. Stop if there’s no pilot lift or safety quality deteriorates. If there are too few comparable crews, treat results as feasibility evidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Rushed or backfilled checks.** Verify timing and hazard handling independently, and credit valid stop-work decisions.
- **Peer pressure or perceived surveillance.** Use private progress, unnamed crew results, and a union-approved correction route.
- **A lift that fades when the reward ends.** Fix field friction during the pilot and base the scale decision on real jobs after rewards end.
