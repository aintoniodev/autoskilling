# Hand-hygiene kiosk pilot

## 1. Diagnosis

**Working hypothesis: nurses haven’t tried the kiosk, or it adds friction during care.** Before launch, the ward educator walks through two real cases with day- and night-shift nurses. On a routine round, the nurse cleans their hands and records the moment at the next safe break in care. During an urgent response or kiosk outage, the nurse follows normal hand-hygiene practice and continues care. A missed entry carries no individual penalty.

Ask nurses whether the kiosk replaces an existing documentation step and time the full workflow. The nursing informatics lead owns access and usability fixes, acknowledges reports within one working day, and reports back at huddles. Defer incentives if most eligible cases lack safe access or the kiosk adds work without a benefit nurses value. **Assumptions:** the hospital approves the pilot and can support coded audit matching, correction history, and break coverage.

## 2. Credit rule

A nurse’s cue is the next safe break after completing a hand-hygiene action. The ward earns one credit when a sampled, observer-confirmed action has one matching kiosk entry with the correct moment and event time, saved by shift end. The reward measure is **matched entries ÷ all sampled completed actions**, including omissions and outages. Clinical compliance is measured separately as **actions performed ÷ opportunities observed**. Raw kiosk totals carry no reward.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Completed action, accurately logged by shift end | One credit when matched |
| Duplicate or several indications for the same opportunity | One credit total |
| Entry withdrawn or matched in error | Remove credit before results are finalized |
| Late entry or edited event time | Late first saves earn zero. Timely entries with corrections require observer confirmation and preserved edit history |
| Completed action documented through the old route | Zero kiosk credit. Counts as performed in the clinical audit |
| Completed action omitted from the kiosk | Zero credit. Stays in the denominator |
| Hand hygiene missed but an entry was filed | Zero credit. Counts as a missed opportunity in the clinical audit |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation, at the kiosk and shift huddle** — “After cleaning your hands, record the moment at your next safe break in care. Save it before your shift ends so you can check what you’ve recorded. Patient care comes first. The pilot ends April 30.”
- **Confirmation** — “Entry saved for hand hygiene before patient contact. You can correct it in My Entries.”
- **Private progress** — “You’ve saved 1 of 1 practice entries this week. Keep logging completed moments when care allows.” One practice entry is enough for the milestone, regardless of shift load. Staff with no eligible moments have no goal that week.
- **Restart** — “Start with your next completed moment. Earlier weeks don’t affect this one.”

## 4. Visibility

Nurses see their own entries and practice progress. At existing huddles, staff and managers see their ward’s verified logging rate against its own baseline, updated monthly. Suppress results with fewer than 30 sampled completed actions or contributions from fewer than five nurses, and omit shift breakdowns that could identify someone. A designated steward can open individual records for a correction, dispute, or requested coaching. Log and time-limit that access and prohibit disciplinary reuse.

## 5. Motivator

Test a hospital-funded catered break with nurses from all three shifts before launch. Each ward earns it if April’s verified logging rate reaches **80% or improves by 15 percentage points from baseline**, with the clinical safety gate met. Fund relief coverage so nights and weekends can participate. At huddles, report the kiosk fixes made in response to staff feedback.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner:** Infection Prevention lead. **Sources:** timestamped kiosk records and monthly observer audits. Sample day, night, and weekend shifts on a consistent schedule across phases. Match records one-to-one using a temporary staff code, ward, event time, and moment, without patient identifiers. Resolve ambiguous matches before awarding credit. Nurses submit corrections privately to the ward educator.

**Safety gate:** If observed compliance drops by 5 percentage points or more from baseline, or a credible report links kiosk use to delayed care, Infection Prevention pauses reward eligibility, investigates, and authorizes restart after the fix is verified. Sparse audit data defer payout. Budget one hour per ward per week for reconciliation and fixes, plus observer time.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January–February 2027** as the kiosk-with-support baseline, **March–April** for incentives, and **May–June** for reward-free follow-up. Before April 30, make the kiosk the easiest approved logging route and retain the fallback, workflow fixes, and private feedback.

Expand if each ward meets the April logging target again in June, retains at least 75% of its April improvement where it improved, and clears the safety gate. Revise and retest if uptake fades or audit coverage is insufficient. Stop incentives if safety problems or unreliable matching persist. The baseline comparison supports a rollout decision with limited causal confidence.

## 8. Main risks

- **Fabricated or duplicate entries.** Only independently matched actions earn credit.
- **Care disruption or uneven access.** Allow delayed entry within the shift, preserve the fallback, and fix barriers across shifts.
- **Observer bias or gains that fade.** Sample comparable shifts throughout and judge persistence two months after rewards end. [WHO guidance on hand-hygiene monitoring](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK144028/)
