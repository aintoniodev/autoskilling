## 1. Diagnosis

The team’s reported median review response is 2.6 days. Use the baseline period to measure how many reviews meet the one-working-day target and find out what causes the delay. Walk two real pull requests with engineers. In a supported case, trace a ready PR from its review request through the queue to a substantive response. In a blocked case, trace a PR assigned to an absent reviewer and test whether the backup can take it. The engineering lead owns coverage and access fixes and responds to reported blockers within one working day.

Ask five engineers whether the queue, protected review time, and proposed reward would make the pilot worth trying. Launch if delayed reviews are largely actionable and engineers can review them within normal hours. Fix capacity or access first if most cases are blocked.

## 2. Credit rule

A designated reviewer earns one credit per eligible PR for submitting its first substantive peer review within eight working hours of the first ready-for-review request. A qualifying review either approves the PR with a specific note on what was checked or requests changes with an actionable finding. Use a published team working calendar that excludes weekends and holidays, with backup coverage for absences.

The team measure is the share of **all eligible PRs** that receive a qualifying review by the deadline, including unanswered PRs in the denominator. Assume repository event history can reconstruct readiness, requests, submissions and dismissals. Check that assumption before attaching a reward.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Ready PR gets a substantive review after six working hours | One credit and one on-time PR |
| Duplicate request, reassignment or second review | Same PR and original clock. At most one credit |
| Review dismissed or PR recalled | Verify the reason. An invalid review loses credit and the original clock applies. An author recall before review is logged separately for audit |
| Review is late or someone edits the due date | The original deadline stands. A late review earns no credit |
| Engineer replies in chat | The reply counts if the engineer records a qualifying review on the PR by the deadline |
| Request is missing from the queue | Reconcile ready PRs against repository events. Restore the original timestamp and count any miss |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation:** “This PR is ready for your review. Pick it up from the review queue during your review block. If you’re tied up, hand it to the backup here so the author gets an answer today. The pilot ends March 5.”
- **Confirmation:** “Review recorded within one working day. This PR counts toward the team milestone.”
- **Progress:** “You answered 3 of 4 assigned PRs on time this week. Your goal is 75%. Open your next review.”
- **Restart:** “Start with the next PR in your queue. You can hand it to the backup if you’re at capacity.”

With one to three opportunities, show the actual count and skip the weekly personal target. With zero, show “No reviews assigned this week.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel shows the weekly on-time rate against baseline, the team milestone, and fixes made in response to feedback. Suppress breakdowns that could identify an individual. Managers see aggregates. The pilot owner can access an individual record for a requested correction, dispute, or coaching session. Log that access, expire it when the case closes, and exclude it from performance management.

## 5. Reward

If the team reaches **80% on time** across the four-week pilot and passes the quality check, everyone gets one paid hour back, scheduled with coverage. Test the reward with the five engineers before announcing it. Give the engineering lead time to fix queue and routing issues during the pilot, and post the fixes in the team channel.

## 6. Operator card

The engineering lead runs the pilot from repository events and owns corrections through a private message. Budget 30 minutes weekly for reconciliation, corrections and the team update, plus 45 minutes for a rotating senior engineer to audit up to 10 randomly selected on-time reviews they did not perform. The auditor checks for specific evidence of review and material misses. Apply the same check during baseline and follow-up. The engineering lead pauses reward accrual if two sampled reviews in a week are materially deficient or one confirmed miss is critical. Fix the cause and pass a fresh audit before resuming.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 11–February 5, 2027** as the baseline with the queue, backup and protected review time in place. Run rewards **February 8–March 5**, then keep those supports and private feedback through the reward-free follow-up **March 8–April 2**. Read results after the final deadlines mature.

Keep the workflow and extend it to another team if follow-up reaches **80% on time**, improves by **at least 15 percentage points** over baseline, and stays within the quality guardrail. If follow-up improves but misses either threshold, adjust routing or capacity and retest. End the incentive experiment if follow-up shows no gain or quality repeatedly trips the pause rule. The before-and-after comparison supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamp reviews.** Audit substance independently and pause rewards when quality slips.
- **Hard PRs get avoided.** Keep unanswered requests in the denominator and preserve the clock through handoffs.
- **Scepticism grows or the lift fades.** Let engineers test the reward, make feedback private, and judge success after the paid reward ends.
