## 1. Diagnosis

Agents currently link or write an article on 12% of resolved tickets. That figure gives us a starting point. The cause is unverified. Before launch, shadow an agent closing a ticket with a relevant article and one closing a ticket without a safe, reusable answer. Time the lookup, linking and authoring steps. Ask where the tool adds work or risks delaying a customer response.

The KB lead owns access, search and publishing blockers and triages them within one business day. Add protected writing time for missing reusable answers. If most cases are blocked or the supported path adds material handling time, fix the workflow before starting rewards. **Assumptions to verify** include reliable ticket history, KB review capacity, comparable support teams and approval for paid reward time.

## 2. Credit rule

When an agent resolves a ticket, they earn **one credit per unique ticket** by linking a published article that matches the actual resolution or by writing an approved, published article that documents a reusable fix. Attach the article at resolution. New articles can finish review within seven days. Agents can close promptly using the normal workflow if they encounter a blocker. They can record the reason in the close form.

The primary metric is qualified tickets divided by all eligible resolved tickets, including eligible omissions. Define exclusions before launch, such as spam, merged duplicates and cases with no safe, reusable answer. Also report coverage across **all** resolved tickets and audit exclusion reasons.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Normal resolution with a matching published article | One credit |
| Duplicate link, second article or merged ticket | One credit maximum on the canonical ticket |
| Ticket reopens for an incorrect fix or article is recalled | Remove pending credit. Recheck a corrected resolution against the original ticket |
| Late entry or edited due date | Use the original resolution timestamp and recorded article history. Due-date edits never change the window |
| Eligible ticket resolved the old way | Zero credit. Include it in the eligible denominator |
| Agent omits the KB field | Zero credit. Include it unless a documented exclusion is verified |

Verify credits after seven days. Correct later reporting errors and honor rewards already awarded. If ticket and KB history cannot support these checks, run the pilot without prizes.

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation at ticket close** — “Link the article that solved this ticket so the next agent can reuse the fix. If a reusable answer is missing, draft it for KB review. Close the ticket normally if you’re blocked. Team rewards run through 14 February.”
- **Confirmation** — “Article linked to ticket #4821. Your credit is pending the seven-day check.” For a draft, show “Sent for KB review.”
- **Progress** — “2 of 3 verified this week. On your next eligible ticket, link the article that solved it.” Set the weekly goal at one credit per three forecast eligible tickets, rounded up and capped at actual opportunities. For zero opportunities, show “No eligible tickets this week. No goal due.”
- **Restart** — “Fresh week. Start with your next eligible ticket.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own tickets and progress privately. Post each team’s verified rate against its own baseline in the usual support channel. Managers see aggregates. Suppress breakdowns that could identify an agent, including any with fewer than five contributing agents. The pilot owner can grant logged, time-limited access to individual records for corrections, disputes or agent-requested coaching. Programme records are excluded from discipline and CSAT bonus calculations.

## 5. Motivator

Each pilot team that reaches **45% verified coverage** over the four weeks earns **30 minutes of protected paid time per agent**, including agents with few or zero eligible tickets. Test the reward and threshold with agents across shifts before launch. Fund KB fixes from the pilot budget and post the fixes in the support channel alongside team progress.

## 6. Operator card

Support Operations owns the pilot and reconciles ticket history with KB versions and approvals. Each week, an independent QA reviewer randomly checks up to 20 credited tickets per arm and 10 uncredited tickets across both arms, including exclusions. Check that the article matches the resolution, can be reused and contains no restricted information. At three material credit failures in an arm’s sample or one restricted-data exposure, Support Operations pauses new awards, investigates and reruns QA before resuming. Agents request corrections through the existing QA channel. Budget about five staff hours per week for QA and administration, plus KB remediation.

## 7. End and decision

Recalculate the baseline for **4–17 January 2027**. Pair comparable teams by baseline and ticket mix, then randomize within pairs. Both groups get the same workflow fixes, private feedback and KB suggestions at ticket close, with manual closure available. Only the reward arm gets the milestone and weekly team posts.

Run rewards **18 January–14 February**. Continue the workflow and private feedback through **15 February–14 March**, with no further rewards. Decide **22 March** after verification. Scale if reward teams sustain at least **45% coverage** and improve at least **10 percentage points more than comparison teams** from baseline, with QA passing and CSAT falling by less than **3 points relative to comparison**. Stop rewards if quality failures persist, CSAT crosses the threshold or there is no sustained advantage. Revise and retest intermediate or inconclusive results, including low CSAT response counts. A baseline-only pilot can establish feasibility.

## 8. Risks

- **Irrelevant links or inflated exclusions** can earn easy credit. Audit both.
- **Longer closes can hurt customers and put CSAT bonuses under pressure.** Preserve prompt closure and give agents protected writing time.
- **Coverage can fade when paid time ends.** Use the post-reward comparison to decide whether the incentive warrants repeating.
