## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently have a digital checklist. That figure shows an adoption gap. Before launching, the safety lead and a union steward should shadow one job with working access and one with poor signal or failed access. Compare the time and rework involved in the app and the existing process. Fix recurring friction before launch. If most jobs remain blocked, or the app adds substantial work on supported jobs, defer incentives.

The safety lead owns blockers, with same-shift triage and a workaround or resolution within two working days. Technicians with app trouble use the approved fallback before work starts. Existing stop-work rules apply throughout.

## 2. What earns credit

When a dispatched job visit opens, the assigned technician submits the required job-specific checklist before the independently recorded work start. A completed checklist that records a verified safety hold also earns credit. Award one credit per visit. The team score is credited visits divided by eligible visits, drawn from dispatch records. Eligibility rules are set before launch. Report documented app outages and access exclusions separately.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete checklist before work starts | One credit |
| Duplicate or resubmission | One credit total |
| Checklist withdrawn as invalid | Remove credit. A verified safety hold keeps its credit. |
| Start time edited or checklist synced late | Use original event history and a trustworthy offline completion time. Hold ambiguous cases for review. |
| Approved fallback used | Safety obligation met. No digital credit. Count the visit in the denominator unless a documented exclusion applies. |
| Eligible visit missing a checklist | Zero credit. Dispatch still supplies the visit. |

## 3. What technicians see

- **Invitation in the job screen** — “Before you start, open this job’s safety checklist. It’s already tied to the visit, so you can record your checks and any hold in one place. If the app is unavailable, use the approved fallback. The pilot ends 28 February.”
- **Confirmation** — “Checklist recorded before work started. This visit counts toward your pilot progress.” Offline submissions show “Pending sync” until verified.
- **Progress** — “2 of 3 toward your personal goal this week. Open the checklist on your next visit before starting.” Set the goal at up to three based on scheduled eligible visits. With no visits, show “No eligible visits this week.” The safety check remains required on every visit.
- **Restart** — “Your next visit is a fresh chance. Open its checklist before starting.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own progress. The existing crew briefing shows verified team adoption against its own baseline, plus fixes made in response to technician reports. Managers see aggregates only. Pool groups with fewer than 10 technicians or 20 eligible visits and suppress breakdowns that could identify someone. A designated reviewer can access individual pilot records for corrections, disputes or requested coaching. Log access, expire it when the case closes and prohibit disciplinary reuse.

## 5. Reward

Test **30 minutes of paid release time** with a few technicians and the union steward before launch. Everyone in a pilot reporting group earns it if the group reaches **60% adoption and improves at least 15 percentage points** over its baseline, with the quality check cleared. Stagger release time to maintain coverage. Agree on eligibility and scheduling with the union before announcing it.

## 6. Operator card

- **Data and owners** — The pilot coordinator reconciles dispatch, app submissions, job-start history and exclusions weekly. Launch with rewards only after verifying those records and agreeing on data access and reward terms with the union.
- **Quality and pause** — A safety reviewer independent of the reward checks 20 randomly selected credited visits each week against job records for timing and substantive completion. Any critical miss or two defective visits in the sample pauses reward qualification. The safety lead investigates and clears the pause after a clean recheck.
- **Corrections and effort** — Technicians can flag a visit privately to the coordinator, with steward support if requested. Respond within two working days. Budget three staff hours weekly for reconciliation, review and corrections.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline **4–31 January 2027**. Run rewards **1–28 February**. Randomly assign matched crews to workflow support with or without the reward and team progress display. Give both groups the same fixes and private feedback. Before February ends, put the prefilled checklist first in the job screen for both groups and retain the fallback.

Settle the reward by **5 March** and measure persistence **8 March–4 April**. Scale the programme if pilot crews sustain at least **65% adoption** and improve at least **10 percentage points more** than comparison crews from baseline, with quality cleared. Revise if adoption improves but misses either threshold. Stop incentives if there is no incremental improvement or a critical quality issue remains unresolved.

## 8. Main risks

- **Box-ticking under time pressure.** Audit actual job checks and count verified safety holds.
- **Unequal access skews results.** Review exclusions by reporting group and fix recurring device or connectivity issues.
- **Crew pressure exposes individuals.** Keep personal progress private and award paid time to the whole qualifying group.
