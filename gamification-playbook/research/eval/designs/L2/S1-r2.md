## 1. Diagnosis

About 35% of jobs currently get a digital checklist. That figure alone can’t tell us whether technicians lack access, find the app slower than the existing procedure, or simply haven’t tried it. Before launch, walk through one connected job and one poor-signal job with technicians and a union steward. Time both paths and check whether the app handles the actual safety procedure. The Field Operations lead owns fixes, acknowledges blockers by the next working day, and publishes fixes in the existing crew channel. If most jobs on a crew are blocked, hold that crew’s launch.

**Launch assumptions.** Dispatch can supply reliable job IDs and work-start records. The app can preserve completion time offline, and the existing approved checklist remains available. Agree the reward, data access and correction rules with union representatives before launch.

## 2. Credit rule

When dispatch opens a job, the assigned technician earns **one provisional credit per job** by completing the required digital safety checklist before work starts and following the existing escalation procedure for any hazard. A reported hazard or stop-work decision preserves credit for a valid checklist. If the app is unavailable, use the approved fallback before starting. If neither is available, follow the existing stop-work procedure.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Completed before start, synced later | Count if the original offline timestamp verifies completion. |
| Duplicate submission or multiple technicians | Count once against the job ID, for the designated checklist owner. |
| Checklist withdrawn or invalidated | Remove provisional credit. A valid pre-start submission can restore it. |
| Late completion or edited schedule | Use the actual start and preserve timestamp history. A schedule edit cannot create credit. |
| Approved fallback used | Record compliant fallback separately. No digital credit. |
| Started job omitted from app | Reconcile against dispatch and retain it in the adoption denominator. Unverifiable timing earns no credit. |

## 3. Worker copy

**Invitation:** “Before you start this job, tap Safety Checklist in your job card. The app fills in the job details for you. If it’s unavailable, use the approved fallback. Follow the usual stop-work procedure if you find a hazard. The reward period ends February 28.”

**Confirmation:** “Job 417’s checklist was saved before work started. Credit pending verification.”

**Progress:** “3 of 4 eligible jobs had a verified digital checklist this week. Open Safety Checklist on your next job.” The private goal uses each technician’s actual eligible volume. One opportunity shows one job. Zero shows “No eligible jobs this week.”

**Restart:** “Start with your next job. Open Safety Checklist before work begins.”

## 4. Visibility

Technicians see their own jobs and credits. The crew channel shows verified crew adoption against its own baseline. Managers see the same aggregates. Suppress figures where a small crew or narrow breakdown could identify someone. The named Safety lead can access individual records for sampled checks, requested coaching or disputes. Log access, close it when the case ends, and bar disciplinary reuse of programme data.

## 5. Motivator

Offer a **€25-equivalent voucher per pilot crew member** if the crew’s verified digital completion rate improves by at least 15 percentage points from baseline and passes the safety review. Include crew members with few or zero eligible jobs. Test the reward and target with technicians and the union steward before launch. Publish blocker fixes alongside crew progress.

## 6. Operator card

**Owner and workload.** Field Operations runs the dispatch-to-app reconciliation and corrections. Budget three staff-hours weekly across Operations and Safety. Technicians can flag a job ID privately through dispatch or their steward. Operations responds within two working days and logs the resolution.

**Measure and verify.** Divide verified pre-start digital checklists by all started jobs eligible for the app under predeclared rules. Publish fallback and excluded-job rates alongside adoption. Safety independently samples 20 started jobs weekly across both groups, including fallback and missing records, and checks timing and hazard handling against existing records. One confirmed unsafe start linked to programme pressure, or two materially invalid credited checklists in a weekly sample, pauses rewards and standings pending Safety and steward review. The safety procedure continues throughout.

## 7. End and decision

Use **January 4–31, 2027** as baseline, **February 1–28** for rewards, and **March 1–28** for reward-free follow-up. Pair crews by baseline and job mix, then randomly assign one in each pair to the reward and crew-progress programme. Both groups get the same fixes and private feedback. Before February ends, put the prefilled checklist first in both groups’ job cards. Keep the fallback and private feedback afterward.

Scale the programme if the pilot’s improvement over baseline exceeds the comparison group’s by **at least 10 percentage points during follow-up**, with no unresolved safety pause and no increase in sampled safety failures relative to the comparison group. If the gap is positive but smaller and safety holds, revise and retest. If the gap disappears or safety worsens, end incentives and retain verified workflow improvements.

## 8. Risks

- **Rushed checks or hidden hazards.** Preserve credit for valid stop-work decisions and make the safety pause immediate.
- **Unequal access or missing jobs.** Track fallback and exclusions, reconcile against dispatch, and resolve disputes before payout.
- **Surveillance concerns.** Agree access rules with the union before launch and keep individual progress private.
