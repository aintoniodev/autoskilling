## 1. Diagnosis

Today, 12% of resolved tickets have an article linked or written. That figure alone can’t tell us whether agents forget, can’t find a suitable article, or skip the step to protect response time and their CSAT bonus.

Before launch, have a support lead and two agents walk through two recent tickets from resolution to documentation. For a repeat troubleshooting issue, test whether the agent can find and attach the article that actually solved it. For a customer-specific issue with no reusable answer, test whether the agent can close the ticket normally and record the reason. Time both paths and check for access, search and publishing delays. The KB lead triages blockers within one business day. If most cases are blocked, defer the incentive. If documentation consistently adds rework or puts CSAT pay at risk, fix that first.

## 2. Credit rule

At resolution, the assigned agent links an existing article that covers the fix or submits a reusable, customer-safe article by the end of their next working day. A new article counts once the KB reviewer approves and publishes it. One ticket earns at most one credit. Agents can resolve tickets on time while documentation is pending.

The team adoption rate is verified credits divided by eligible resolved tickets from the ticketing system, including tickets agents skipped. A KB reviewer validates exceptions for cases with no reusable answer or a documented tool blocker. Report exceptions and all-ticket coverage alongside the eligible rate. Recompute the 12% baseline using the pilot rules before setting the comparison.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Article matches the fix | One credit. |
| Two links, resubmission or duplicate ticket | One credit per canonical ticket. |
| Ticket reopens for the same issue or article is withdrawn as incorrect | Suspend credit pending review. Restore it if the corrected resolution qualifies. |
| Late submission or edited due date | No credit for late work. Use the recorded resolution event to determine the deadline. An on-time draft can qualify after review. |
| Agent resolves the ticket without KB work | Zero credit. Ticket stays in the denominator if eligible. |
| Ticket is missing from the KB report | Reconcile against the ticketing system. Include it and count zero until qualifying evidence appears. |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation** “Resolved this ticket? Attach the article that solved it so the next agent can find the fix. If there’s a reusable answer missing, submit it by the end of your next working day. Close the ticket as usual. The reward period ends February 28.”
- **Confirmation** “Article linked to ticket #4831. You have 2 of 3 toward your goal this week.” For a draft, show “Article submitted for review. It counts once published.”
- **Progress** “2 of 3 this week. On your next eligible resolution, attach the article that solved it.” Set the weekly goal at 40% of each agent’s eligible volume, rounded up, capped at three. With one opportunity, the goal is one. With zero, show “No eligible tickets this week.”
- **Restart** “Fresh week. Start with your next eligible resolution.”

## 4. Visibility

Agents see their own ticket-level progress. The existing support huddle shows the pilot group’s verified rate against its baseline, without names or rankings. Hide cuts with fewer than 10 agents or 30 tickets, including cuts that reveal a hidden cell by subtraction. Managers see aggregates. The pilot owner grants logged, time-limited individual access for corrections, disputes or coaching the agent requests. Keep individual records out of discipline and compensation decisions.

## 5. Motivator

If the pilot group reaches 40% verified adoption and clears the quality check, everyone in it gets one paid hour of their choice, scheduled with queue coverage. Test the reward with a few agents before launch. Keep CSAT bonus rules in place and confirm coverage protects agents’ bonus opportunity. Use the huddle to share which reported blockers were fixed.

## 6. Operator card

The support ops lead reconciles ticket events with KB audit and publishing logs. An independent QA lead samples 20 credited tickets weekly, or all if fewer, for relevance, accuracy and customer-data exposure. At three failures in 20, a 15% failure rate in a smaller sample, or any customer-data exposure, the QA lead pauses reward verification until fixes pass a fresh review. Agents send the support ops lead a ticket ID for corrections and get a response within two working days. Budget two staff-hours weekly for reconciliation, QA and corrections, plus article review and blocker fixes.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed 2027 dates are January 4–31 for baseline, February 1–28 for rewards and March 1–28 for follow-up. Assuming comparable queues, assign by queue to reward or comparison. Both get workflow fixes and private feedback. Before rewards stop, put article suggestions in the resolution flow with a skip option. Allow one week for pending reviews before paying out. Scale if follow-up adoption stays at or above 40%, improves at least 15 percentage points more than comparison, and CSAT falls by no more than two points on a 100-point scale relative to comparison, with no unresolved quality pause. Change the design if adoption fades. Stop if quality or CSAT breaches persist. Without comparable queues, treat the pilot as a feasibility test.

## 8. Risks

Agents attach loosely related articles to hit the target. Documentation crowds out customer care because the CSAT bonus still matters. Adoption falls when paid time ends because the article search still takes too long.
