## 1. Diagnosis

The median review wait is 2.6 days. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the engineering lead walks two recent PRs with their authors and reviewers. On a review-ready PR with an available reviewer, check whether the request was noticed, whether the next action was clear, and what the reviewer had to interrupt. On a PR delayed by missing access, context, or coverage, trace who could unblock it and how long that took. Confirm the pattern against two weeks of queue history.

Run the pilot if engineers say earlier reviews would reduce waiting and requests routinely get buried. If most requests are blocked, or reviewers lack capacity, defer the incentive and fix that first. The engineering lead owns blockers and restores access, assigns a backup, or gives the author a clear next step within one working day.

## 2. Credit rule

A review-ready PR entering the team’s review queue starts an eight-working-hour clock. Use the team’s published coverage hours and holiday calendar. The assigned reviewer earns one credit for the first timely, substantive submitted review. That means an approval that states what was checked, requested changes with actionable feedback, or a specific blocking question grounded in the diff. Authors can flag missing context, and reviewers can hand off to the duty reviewer with the original clock intact.

The team metric is **eligible PRs with a timely qualifying review ÷ all eligible PRs whose deadline has passed**. Count only the first review cycle per PR. Drafts enter when marked ready and review is requested. Retain ready requests even when routing fails or the author closes the PR before a qualifying review. Report those reasons alongside the metric.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Reviewer submits actionable feedback in six working hours | One credit and one timely PR |
| Two reviewers respond, or the author requests review again | First qualifying review gets credit. Same PR and clock |
| First review is dismissed as invalid | Use the next qualifying review’s actual timestamp. Reverse the provisional credit if needed |
| Reviewer is reassigned, or a due date is edited | Original deadline holds. A late answer earns zero |
| Reviewer uses the existing GitHub review flow | Same credit if the submitted review qualifies |
| Request is missed, or someone replies “looking now” in chat | PR remains in the denominator. Zero credit |

## 3. Worker copy

- **Invitation in the existing review notification.** “PR #482 is ready. Open the diff from your review queue and leave the author a decision or a concrete next step by 15:00 tomorrow. If you’re at capacity, hand it to the duty reviewer now. The pilot ends 21 February.”
- **Confirmation.** “Your review on #482 was submitted with two working hours to spare. One provisional review credit.”
- **Private progress.** “You’ve answered 1 of your 2 review requests on time this week. #495 is next, due Thursday at 14:00.” The personal goal is to answer up to two assigned requests on time. With one opportunity, the goal is one. With zero, show “No review requests this week.”
- **Restart.** “Start with your next request. #501 is ready and due tomorrow at 11:00.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel gets one weekly update with the verified team rate, its baseline, and any routing or access fixes shipped. Publish the update when the underlying requests span at least five reviewers. Managers receive the same aggregate. The engineering lead may access individual records for a requested coaching session, correction, or dispute. Log the access, expire it when the case closes, and prohibit disciplinary use.

## 5. Motivator

Offer **one meeting-free afternoon of paid focus time**, available to the whole team and staggered for review coverage, if at least 80% of eligible PRs receive timely reviews across the four-week pilot and the quality checks pass. Test the reward and notification copy with five engineers before launch. The engineering lead books the time once the final week’s results are verified.

## 6. Operator card

Assume GitHub review and request history is available, the engineering lead can arrange backup coverage, and the sponsor can approve paid focus time. Reconcile the ready PR list against review events weekly. An engineer who is neither the author nor the reviewer samples 10 credited reviews each week, or all if fewer, and checks each against the diff. If two sampled reviews are perfunctory or one misses a material defect visible in the diff, the lead pauses the reward, corrects credits, and fixes the cause before resuming. Engineers send disputed PR links privately to the lead for resolution within two working days. Budget one team-hour weekly.

## 7. End and decision

Baseline **11–24 January 2027**, pilot **25 January–21 February**, and reward-free follow-up **22 February–21 March**. Recalculate the baseline using the same clock and eligibility rules. Put the oldest-due review first in the existing queue before rewards end, then keep routing, handoffs, and private confirmations.

Keep the workflow if follow-up is **at least 80% timely and 15 percentage points above baseline**, with no quality pause. Adjust and retest if timeliness improves but falls short. End the incentive if gains disappear or quality deteriorates. The baseline comparison measures feasibility for the combined workflow and incentive.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamping** buys speed at the cost of defects. Audit against the diff.
- **Easy PRs get picked first.** Keep missed requests in the metric and surface the oldest deadline.
- **The pilot feels like surveillance or extra work.** Keep progress private, fix routing problems visibly, and check whether improvement survives the reward.
