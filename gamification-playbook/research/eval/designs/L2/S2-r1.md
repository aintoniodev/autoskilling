## 1. Diagnosis

The 25-engineer team’s median review wait is 2.6 days. The cause is unverified. Before launch, the engineering lead walks through one PR with an available reviewer and one stalled PR with the authors and reviewers. Check whether each reviewer knew they owned it, had access and context, and had time to review it. If ownership is easy to miss, test the incentive. If reviews routinely lose to other commitments, protect review time first. If most reviews are blocked, postpone the reward phase. The engineering lead owns blockers and resolves or reroutes them within four staffed hours.

## 2. What earns credit

When a human-authored team PR becomes ready for review, the review queue assigns an available reviewer. The PR earns **one credit** if a non-author reviewer submits a substantive review within **eight staffed hours** of the first ready event. A substantive review approves the actual change, requests specific changes, or raises a concrete issue that needs an answer before merge. An acknowledgment alone earns zero. For urgent or specialist work, use the existing escalation route. The response can still earn credit, and the original clock keeps running.

The team metric is **credited PRs ÷ all eligible ready PRs**. Use the published team working calendar, including holidays. Exclude drafts until first marked ready, bot PRs and PRs outside the team’s ownership.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Reviewer checks the diff and approves in six staffed hours | One credit |
| Two reviewers respond, or the PR is reopened | At most one credit for that PR |
| Reviewer retracts a mistaken approval | Remove credit unless another qualifying review met the original deadline |
| The due date is edited or the reviewer changes | Use the deadline calculated from the original ready event |
| Reviewer uses GitHub directly | Same credit if the review qualifies |
| Queue misses the PR, or it’s merged or withdrawn without review | Keep it in the denominator with zero credit |

## 3. What engineers see

- **Invitation in the existing review notification** “You’re up for PR #418. Open the diff and leave a review by Tuesday at 14:00 so the author can keep moving. If you’re blocked or at capacity, hand it back to the queue now. The team reward pilot ends 5 March.”
- **Confirmation on submission** “Review submitted for #418 within one working day. It counts toward the team target, subject to the weekly quality check.”
- **Private progress** “You answered 2 of your 3 assigned reviews within one working day. Next up is #425, due tomorrow at 11:00.” Show “No reviews assigned” when there are zero. With one assignment, show its deadline and outcome without a percentage.
- **Restart** “Next up is #425, due tomorrow at 11:00. Open the diff or hand it back if you’re blocked.”

## 4. Visibility

Engineers see their own progress. The existing team channel gets one weekly update with the verified team rate, baseline and progress toward the reward. Suppress updates based on fewer than 10 PRs or five reviewers and roll them into the next update. Managers see the same aggregate. The engineering lead can access individual records for a correction, dispute or engineer-requested coaching. Log the purpose, expire access when the case closes and prohibit use in performance reviews or discipline.

## 5. Reward

If the team clears the adoption and quality thresholds by 5 March, everyone gets **one protected paid hour back** the following week. Reduce planned work by that hour and stagger it for coverage. Test the offer with five engineers before launch. Use the existing team channel to show which review blockers were fixed from their feedback.

## 6. Operator card

**Assumptions** · GitHub records ready events, review submissions and dismissals. The queue records assignments and handoffs. The sponsor can approve the paid hour and give the engineering lead time to fix routing.

The engineering lead reconciles GitHub ready events against the queue weekly and handles private corrections through the existing team channel’s direct messages. A rotating senior engineer who did not author or review the sampled PRs checks 10 randomly selected credited reviews each week, or all if fewer, against the diff and review record. Budget 90 minutes total per week. If two sampled reviews are superficial or one skips a required check, the lead pauses reward qualification, corrects affected credits and resumes after fixing the cause and passing a fresh audit. Verify event history before attaching stakes.

## 7. End and decision

Proposed 2027 dates. **11 January–5 February** establishes the baseline with the same routing, notifications and feedback. Make the review queue the default during baseline, with GitHub still available. **8 February–5 March** adds the reward. **8 March–2 April** keeps the workflow and private feedback and measures four weeks without rewards.

Award the hour if at least **80%** qualify, at least **10 percentage points** above baseline, with **40 eligible PRs** and no unresolved quality pause. Keep the workflow and consider rollout if follow-up meets those same thresholds. If baseline already meets 80%, skip the reward. Extend measurement if volume is low, fix routing if improvement fades, and stop the incentive if quality worsens. The before-and-after comparison supports a feasibility decision.

## 8. Risks

- **Rubber-stamping** to beat the clock. Audit reviews against the diff.
- **Cherry-picking** easy PRs or resetting clocks. Reconcile against GitHub and keep original deadlines.
- **Resentment or a temporary lift.** Protect the paid hour, keep progress private and base rollout on the reward-free follow-up.
