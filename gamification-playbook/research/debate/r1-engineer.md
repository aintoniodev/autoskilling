# Round 1 — Engineer

**Verdict:** Spend the first days proving the workflow works and the score means what the sponsor thinks it means. Use an existing queue, approved data and private feedback. Let the agent draft and test the rules; don't let it assume production access, HR approval, or an operator's time. If any is missing, deliver a conditional design or a no-stakes diagnostic, not a launch plan disguised as a checklist.

## Ranked by deployment value per unit of effort

Ranks reflect a few days to design/build for 20–300 workers, not evidence grades. “Check” means observable evidence **in a proposed design** for the blind eval; none of these checks measures employee behaviour in that eval.

## Write the skill as a launch card, not a treatise

Start with a one-page **minimum viable programme** and three states: **blocked**, **no-stakes feasibility only**, **eligible for launch subject to approvals**. Ask for one evidence row per gate: source, owner, pass test, fallback. Use “if missing, do X” branches; mark assumptions, separate day-one deliverables from sponsor commitments, and estimate operator minutes per week from review volume. Existing restricted reports, manual samples and a shared correction inbox may suffice; build automation only when the existing path cannot meet the turnaround, privacy or traceability requirement. Put mechanics, exhaustive test matrices and evidence in references.

Use the frozen rubric on paired held-out designs with/without the skill, same brief and output budget, shuffled labels and both judges. Use the table as a map to observable evidence in judge reasons, not new scoring criteria. Reward executable omissions rather than verbosity; report rubric scores, scenario/run spread and nonlaunchable flags separately. A bundled win cannot establish which hypothesis caused it, whether the sponsor will staff it, or whether employees change behaviour.

## Minimum viable programme (seven things)

1. **Workflow and benefit:** one role, one real work cue, a supported path checked end to end, safe fallback and blocker owner.
2. **Outcome contract:** stable unit, eligible set and denominator, one qualified credit at most, source and timing sufficient to verify the actual behaviour.
3. **Private feedback:** baseline, reachable goal, n/N and next action; handle zero/uneven opportunities and allow restart without penalty. No required prizes, ranks or streak.
4. **Safety gate:** one independent quality check, one displacement check, an explicit pause trigger and decision owner; a severe verified incident can stop a low-volume pilot.
5. **Trust and corrections:** least-privilege views, no discipline, retention/approval boundaries, versioned rules and source, small independent prelaunch replay and a named correction route.
6. **Bounded operation:** sponsor, operator, admin time cap and end date. If prizes are approved, distinguish acknowledgement from verified settlement.
7. **Scale-or-stop readout:** baseline, follow-up on subsequent opportunities with incentives gone, and predeclared quality/cost/coverage criteria. Compare to plain support where feasible; otherwise call it a feasibility result.
