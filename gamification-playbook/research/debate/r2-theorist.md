# Round 2 — Theorist

**Kill the case-as-checklist, not every mechanism in it.** The bundle lost 7.3 points (62.6 vs 69.9). On equal-length designs, holistic judgments without the rubric also fell: launch −8.5, employees notice −3.2, lasting −12.1. Run SD <1 says the aggregate gap is repeatable on these briefs, not that four distinct briefs establish a population effect. One plausible mechanism: listing 13 commitments shifts the agent from choosing an intervention to implementing a template, spending scarce answer space on rules that don't solve the bottleneck. Equal length doesn't rule out this allocation problem. The bundle comparison can't prove it or identify the harmful ingredient.

## Rebuttals

**Empiricist.** Concede H2 moves up: broad evidence for specific goals and self-monitoring warrants a usable target and feedback, not fixed percentages or rank. But evidence grade isn't design priority. H1/H3 come first because better motivation amplifies whatever earns credit and cannot move an unsupported case. Beshears varied qualifying hours, not the cue: an event cue is a testable implementation, not an established effect. Agree with splitting proxy response from gate validity (H1/H10), exercise carryover from a workplace sunset (H4), and a tested framing from a settlement schedule (H9). H11 ranks above H7 because comparing against the same support is necessary to *attribute* the increment, not because pilots motivate anyone. Rank evidence changes sign; the streak result among school users who connected doesn't identify a repeat-use effect for everyone assigned; neither settles a default. Keep H8's power distinction without claiming restricted access improves performance; H6's contingency evidence doesn't order praise and money.

**Engineer.** Agree on existing workflow and staffed channels, independent shadow checks, and blocked / feasibility-only / conditional launch states. Don't mandate an event platform, fine-grained personal dashboards or payout machinery in a no-prize pilot. No trustworthy event history means no stakes on that measure, not no learning: hand-check cases and show directly verifiable feedback. A reproducible score can still reward the wrong thing; an independent customer outcome and a displaced-work measure remain separate. I rank H11 higher: allow an honestly noncausal feasibility pilot, but make the incremental comparison mandatory for a causal scale claim. A launch card alone can still be an operator-facing checklist; show what a worker will actually encounter on the next eligible case.

**Contrarian.** Agree that temporary lift can buy enough practice to uncover durable value. That requires the first try to teach something that makes the *second* try worthwhile without more payment; excitement alone is not that mechanism. I'd require the actual invitation, first credible payoff and safe next move where activation is the bottleneck. I still reject H7 as a lead: short team contests and high-performer recognition support candidates, not mandatory individual exposure; even top-only recognition may tell most workers they aren't in the running. If the barrier is uncertainty about feasibility, a peer demonstration can transmit the useful information without rank. Money can cover an actual cost, but may instead buy the cheapest qualifying action. Keep immediate factual feedback, not instant entitlement; protected loss framing, repair and celebration each need lift over otherwise identical feedback or a simple restart. Mentoring supports a concrete unblock action, not telemetry access. H12's evidenced beneficiary payoff is a promising way to make value credible; a story that falsely credits the tool is worse than operational feedback. H13's norm may correct a false belief, but the antibiotic trial bundled messenger, comparison and advice; a low, selective or irrelevant norm may legitimise non-use.

## Updated ranking and skill cut

Ranked by teaching priority, not estimated effect: **H1 > H3 > H2 > H11 > H4 > H10 > H8 > H5 > H7 > H12 > H6 > H9 > H13.** H2 rises because safety without a reachable payoff won't recruit effort; H10 remains essential where stakes exist but doesn't need its own workflow.

**Five core principles:**

1. **Diagnose before incentivising (H3).** Observe a supported and blocked case, including QA/rework. Fix feasibility, cue the action in existing work, and preserve a safe fallback.
2. **Reward the result you actually want (H1/H10).** Define target, credit and denominator; trace a plausible exploit and late correction. Check independent quality and omitted work; shadow-score and agree a pause rule before stakes.
3. **Make the next good action worth trying (H2, parts of H5/H9).** Show a reachable opportunity-adjusted goal, a concrete invitation and prompt, honest payoff. Handle zero opportunities and provide a normal restart without treating excuses as wins.
4. **Separate help from surveillance (H8).** Private progress by default; disclose narrow coaching or dispute access with a real action, bounded retention and no disciplinary reuse. If prizes exist, separate tentative credit from verified settlement.
5. **Learn what survives (H11/H4).** Compare one justified add-on to equal plain support where credible; otherwise report feasibility only. Stop rewards as planned and measure quality-qualified use on subsequent opportunities after settlement.

**Dead as defaults:** required ranks, bracket/target formulas, blanket named dashboards, calendar streaks or graduation, repair bonuses, badge catalogues/confetti, instant prizes, fixed hold periods, and loss framing. They add pressure or ceremony without solving a demonstrated constraint and can steer work toward the wrong proxy. H7/H6 are optional contrasts, not recommendations; retain H12 as a candidate example under principle 3, not a sixth commandment. H13 stays out of core: test only a true, relevant norm against the same message without it.

## Rubric and consequential disagreements

**Yes, caution-weighted; no, not a sufficient explanation.** C4/C8 or C9/C10 can double-credit a generic safeguard; C5/C7 protect proportionality and privacy but don't require an offer workers notice. C3/C6 already ask for a next action, so the gap is the visibility and credibility of the first payoff, not the total absence of motivation. Keep the frozen primary and launch flags. Use a separate blind invitation/payoff/re-entry diagnostic that private feedback can pass. The holistic drops weaken a rubric-only explanation, but judges can share priors; neither rating observes a worker.

Three choices could change the skill: **mandatory launch example or only when activation is uncertain** (compare short variants with/without it, same budget); **teach an explicit social/reward menu or one mechanism-neutral conditional** (compare against the same core; look for better invitations without worse safety, especially where a peer signal is plausible); **require full shadow/replay for all plans or only material stakes** (compare proportionate gate wording; count executable checks, not named infrastructure). Test variants against no skill on fresh blind briefs. Promotion of any mechanic still needs incremental worker evidence, not merely higher design scores.
