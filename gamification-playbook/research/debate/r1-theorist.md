# Round 1 — Theorist

Ranked by confidence that the **skill should teach the rule**, not by effect size or evidence grade. Some rules are prerequisites for learning rather than claims about motivation. C# refers to a frozen rubric criterion. The eval can show whether the skill changes what agents specify on held-out briefs. It cannot show that employees behave differently, or that one rule caused a score gain: the intervention is the whole skill. H1–H5, H8, H10 and H11 map to distinct rubric requirements; H6, H7 and H9 are only partially observable through shared criteria, not separate tests of recognition, competition or timing. Literature verification is the only available check on what actually happened to participants in the cited settings; unanswered transfer questions require a workplace check outside this investigation.

## Ranked hypotheses, failure points and checks

1. **H1 — Reward quality-qualified work.** Highest confidence: what earns credit should advance the stated outcome even if people pursue credit by the cheapest route. **Break:** a manual first send followed by an AI replacement earns credit under Rally's original rule without improving first-send adoption; requiring on-time sends can instead buy premature releases. Factory throughput and bank misconduct show opposite sides of proxy response, not the right gate for quoting. **Eval (C1, C4):** does the design handle both send sequences, duplicate and reversed sends, independent defect sampling and displaced work? Only an audit of live cases can establish that the gate catches harm without suppressing legitimate work.

2. **H3 — Fix the workflow and cue the next action.** High confidence: rewards cannot create access, safe outputs or available opportunities. **Break:** entering the queue may precede knowing whether a case is supported; an unconditional tool-first cue adds rework. Flexible gym scheduling supports avoiding rigid clock windows, not making a complex task automatic. **Eval (C2):** look for an observable eligibility cue, safe fallback, named blocker owner and a comparison of tool vs manual work including QA. Actual workflow observation must confirm net value. Keep deliberate verification even if opening the tool becomes routine.

3. **H11 — Compare against plain support before scaling.** High confidence: if training, management attention and rewards launch together, a lift cannot be attributed to rewards. **Break:** reps share tips or see a common scoreboard across arms. With only a few teams, cluster assignment does not manufacture precision; hundreds of quotes are not hundreds of assignments. Gym heterogeneity supports testing, not a particular workplace rollout. **Eval (C10):** require a credible incremental comparison with equivalent basic support and measurement, realistic assignment and uncertainty, or an explicitly noncausal feasibility pilot. The pilot itself, not its judge score, would settle local incremental value.

4. **H10 — Shadow-test and replay.** High confidence as a measurement control, not a behaviour lever. **Break:** a nightly replay of current records can be perfectly deterministic and still lose who made the first send; an audit against its own source repeats the error. Hand-hygiene sensing failures transfer as a warning about ground truth, not evidence for one architecture. **Eval (C8):** look for independent human-labelled true/false cases, event-time policy versions, explainable changes and an appeal. Actual reconciliation establishes accuracy. Keep identifiable history only for the justified window (H8).

5. **H4 — Withdraw the prizes and observe what remains.** High confidence in the inference rule, not in carryover. **Break:** retention after a contest with the tool still set as the default may reflect workflow policy or learning, not habit; delayed payout and promised relaunch can extend the treatment. Gym follow-ups show carryover can decay, not how long workplace follow-up should be. **Eval (C9):** check an explicit post-settlement, opportunity-based window, retained support and no success claim from live scores alone. A comparable untreated trajectory with seasonal and case-mix checks is needed to attribute carryover.

6. **H8 — Separate self-feedback, coaching and surveillance.** High confidence as a default against unnecessary exposure. **Break:** a hidden individual can be inferred from a small group's total and visible peers, or a manager can turn coaching access into a performance record. Factory concealment is a plausible analogue for hiding tool failures, not proof that restricted access increases reporting. **Eval (C7):** walk each role through access, inference, opt-out and reuse; include a narrow, legitimate coaching or appeal path. Verify the monitoring literature's outcomes separately from the untested access policy.

7. **H2 — Give achievable goals and actionable feedback.** Moderate-high confidence; progress helps only if people can change what happens next. **Break:** a rep can improve their feasible-opportunity rate by classifying borderline cases as blockers, or hit 100% from one easy case. Public goal-setting isn't public rank (H7) or employer visibility (H8). **Eval (C3):** check zero opportunities, tiny books and unequal complexity; retain all-due counts and predeclare eligibility independently. Goal/monitoring syntheses support the broad rule; no result here selects 30/60 milestones or a bracket formula.

8. **H7 — Default to personal progress; test competition separately.** Moderate confidence: workplace effects plausibly differ because rank changes both information and the payoff to helping. **Break:** even private rank can discourage; book-size matching doesn't equalise complexity, and public opt-in makes participants self-selected. A personal benchmark, shared aggregate and named tournament are distinct. **Eval (C7, partly C3):** inspect whether the design distinguishes those choices and protects cooperation, comparability and lower-ranked workers. **Literature check:** compare rank information and cash tournament arms against their actual comparators; neither those nor step bundles establish a winning visibility default.

9. **H5 — Make re-entry easy; streak repair is optional.** Moderate confidence in a no-cliff rule, low confidence in repair as a lever. **Break:** a missed gym visit is often a choice; an outage, leave or an unsupported renewal is not. Paying to return can make a deliberate miss profitable, while treating “excused” as “completed” corrupts H1. **Eval (C6):** replay no opportunity, legitimate fallback, voluntary miss and return; an ordinary restart can pass without a streak. **Literature check:** distinguish repair after a displayed break from return bonuses and inspect matched comparators; no workplace lift is identified.

10. **H6 — Prefer informative recognition to higher stakes.** Moderate-low as a comparative claim. **Break:** useful feedback transmits information; praise also signals status or managerial expectation. A privately useful error explanation is not the public star treatment in the sales field study. Lab free-choice after payment does not match required work already under a pay plan. **Eval (shared C5):** judge whether private feedback is useful without the add-on and the latter has a testable rationale. **Literature check:** separate initial interest, reward contingency, audience and post-withdrawal outcome; the eval cannot establish crowd-out or that recognition beats cash.

11. **H9 — Acknowledge promptly; settle when verified.** Low as a standalone behavioural rule; useful transaction design when prizes exist. **Break:** a provisional badge shown as won can feel like a loss when corrected, despite no cash clawback. Animal reinforcement and teacher loss-framing don't validate settlement timing. **Eval (shared C5/C8):** check that receipt, tentative status and final award are distinguishable, reasons contestable and no wages at risk. **Literature check:** verify what was actually paid and when; the local window requires the observed adverse-event latency distribution, not a universal 14 days.

## What earns space in a short skill

- **Measure once (H1 + H10, settlement portion of H9).** Define success and independent harm checks, then prove the event record can reproduce them. Split prompt informational feedback from conditional payout; fold the former into H2. Keep no wage clawback as a safeguard, not a motivational claim.
- **Make safe work easy (H3 + H2 + re-entry portion of H5).** Fix feasibility before raising difficulty; give a credible next eligible action. Cut streaks, repair bonuses, fixed milestone percentages and calendar-based graduation as defaults.
- **Use the lightest feedback that works (H8 + conditional H7/H6).** Separate audience, information and reward stakes. Cut default named ranks, recognition-over-cash claims and decorative requirements. Treat additions as optional tests, not expected wins.
- **Learn what lasts (H11 + H4).** One comparison and exit protocol; score in-programme increment and post-settlement persistence separately. A gain on a directly mapped rubric criterion supports teaching the design behaviour; it does not verify the mechanism or identify which paragraph helped.
