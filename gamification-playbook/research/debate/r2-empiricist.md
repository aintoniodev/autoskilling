# Round 2 · Empiricist

The 13-principle bundle lost to no skill, 62.6 vs 69.9 (−7.3 points, run SD <1). At equal length, the rubric-free judge agreed on launch (71.2 vs 79.7), employee notice (52.8 vs 56.0) and especially lasting use (40.3 vs 52.4). That weakens rubric bias as the sole explanation. Four unseen briefs, three repeats and two rubric judges give four distinct tasks. This result identifies a weaker bundle on those tasks. Individual rules, the proposed replacement and employee outcomes remain untested.

## Rebuttals

- **Theorist.** Agree on H1’s first-send mismatch and premature-send risk, H3’s need for a safe eligible cue, and H4/H11’s post-settlement comparison with equal support. H2 belongs above H10 on evidence for behaviour. A replayable ledger is a control whose value depends on independent ground truth. Keep deliberate QA even if tool initiation becomes routine. Fold restart and settlement into the relevant steps. Keep audience, rank and stakes independently variable.
- **Engineer.** Agree on existing workflows, independent reconciliation, staffed corrections, inferred identity through overlapping totals and feasibility-only pilots when assignment is weak. The seven gates can become another checklist that crowds out the worker’s first use. Require replay and settlement in proportion to stakes. One walkthrough can expose a blocker. Any frequency or savings claim needs representative cases. Keep H11 above optional mechanics, since generic launch language can hide what the game adds.
- **Contrarian, H7/H6.** Delfgaauw’s six-week store tournaments bundled weekly team ranks with competition. Effects varied by manager/team gender composition and estimates were similar with and without money. Bradler’s surprise cards to three of eight lifted final-hour data entry by 7.3%, mainly among nonrecipients. Kosfeld’s announced best-performer card raised short-project output about 12%. Friebel’s threshold team bonus increased sales and customers served by 3% without rank. These support testing distinct interventions, with live named individual standings and routine badge collections still untested by these trials. [Delfgaauw](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/09069.pdf), [Bradler](https://docs.iza.org/dp8311.pdf), [Kosfeld](https://doi.org/10.1257/mic.3.3.86), [Friebel](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20160788)
- **H9/H5/H8.** Hossain–List’s factory framing contrast was about +1% team output. Levitt’s published school paper reports suggestive loss-over-gain evidence and no detectable lift with delayed rewards in that setting. Neither tests a virtual shield or delayed verified workplace payout. Duolingo reports +1.7% day-seven retention for new learners after an animation change (unit unspecified) and +0.38% relative daily active learners with two freezes. Vendor evidence warrants a test at most. Sandvik’s working paper says +17% daily revenue in the first two months under assigned mentoring. The published estimate is +19%. This strengthens the case for testing support, with dashboard access still untested. [Hossain–List](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1120.1544), [Levitt](https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20130358), [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/), [Sandvik](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.07524)
- **H12/H13.** Grant’s field experiments support beneficiary-impact stories for fundraising and lifeguard work (B per contrast). Tool-specific stories need truthful attribution and their own test. Hallsworth’s 3.3% lower antibiotic dispensing rate came from a letter to high prescribers that combined peer comparison, a trusted messenger, action steps and a leaflet (B for the package). Whether recipients held mistaken beliefs remains open. Milkman also found a high-and-rising exercise norm message promising. Test truthful norms against the same ask without that message. [Grant](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.93.1.108), [Hallsworth](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)00215-4), [Milkman](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)
- **H1–H4/H10–H11.** Agree on qualified credit, real workflow value, an achievable next move and equal support. I rank H4 higher because a burst of practice has two outcomes worth separating, near-term net value and later use. Agree on a frozen salience diagnostic that checks actual worker copy, first payoff and a move after a miss. Even a private cue can pass. Independent audit and comparison belong in the plan even when they contribute little to the launch message.

## Updated ranking and decisions

**H2 > H1 > H4 > H8 > H3 > H11 > H6 > H12 > H7 > H13 > H5 > H10 > H9.** H1 moves up because invalid credit can defeat the goal before motivation matters. Directed tests earn space where transfer is uncertain.

1. **H2.** Give a credible goal and usable private progress with a next action. **A** for goal setting and monitoring, **D** for local targets, denominators and timing.
2. **H1 + H10.** Credit qualified work, reconcile against independent evidence and pause on harm. **B** for proxy response and displacement, **D** for the gate, replay and correction design. In Hong’s factory trial the quantity bonus reduced quality under flat base pay, with no detectable quality decline under piece-rate base pay. [Hong et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/iere.12278)
3. **H4 + H11.** Measure later opportunities after final payout and compare against equal plain support. **A** for variable exercise carryover, **D** for the workplace protocol. Baseline-only pilots can assess feasibility. Credible causal claims need a credible counterfactual.
4. **H8.** Limit individual access to a justified purpose. **A** for monitoring risk, **D** for access controls.
5. **H3 + H5.** Fix blockers, cue real eligible work and make restarting easy. **D** for this workflow and restart design. Flexible gym reward windows and return incentives each have **B** evidence in their own setting.

Drop default H7 ranks, H6 recognition-over-cash, mandatory streaks, badge catalogues, celebrations and fixed brackets or habit clocks. Their effects or transfer depend on the specific design. Drop H9 as a behaviour rule. Keep verified settlement as a **D** control when prizes exist. Keep H12/H13 as candidate tests. Open disagreements are whether beneficiary impact or visible competition earns the first add-on, and when proactive coaching justifies named access. Test a compact core against no skill on fresh briefs, then vary one add-on at a time. Use employee data to settle adoption and persistence.

## Corrections to the draft skill

- **Framing and diagnosis.** Replace the adoption promise, “most designs,” inevitable loss of benefit and guaranteed help for unnoticed workflows with “Diagnose workflow value, distorted credit, monitoring and possible decay. Use an incentive to test whether more people try a feasible workflow. Measure carryover.” Keep an all-due denominator alongside predeclared eligible cases. Label the one-mechanic cap and blocked-case cutoff as **D** design choices.
- **Credit and badges.** “Incentives can shift effort toward credited activity.” Kerr and the Wells Fargo enforcement case are **D** illustrations. GitHub streak removal was followed by fewer long streaks, weekends with activity and single-contribution days (**B**, code quality unmeasured). Stack Overflow activity shifted around badge thresholds (**C**). Catalogue complexity and gaming remain separate **D** concerns. [Kerr](https://doi.org/10.2307/255378), [OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2016/nr-occ-2016-106.html), [Moldon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02371), [Anderson](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/www13-badges.pdf)
- **Feedback and goals.** Harkin’s monitoring interventions improved goal attainment (*d* = 0.40, CI 0.32–0.48, trim-and-fill 0.19, **A**). The comparison was monitoring interventions versus controls. Epton’s goal-setting effect was *d* = 0.34 (CI 0.28–0.41, **A**). Locke–Latham support specific, challenging goals subject to ability and commitment. Prompt confirmation and personal n/N are **D** prescriptions here. [Harkin](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/), [Epton](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29189034/), [Locke–Latham](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705)
- **Restart and habit.** Silverman–Barasch found consumer streak repair attenuated the broken-streak penalty (**C** for the cited analogue, **D** for workplace policy). Milkman’s highest estimated during-programme lift was +27% weekly gym visits versus placebo. The return microbonus added an estimated 0.266 visits/week versus planning, reminders and visit incentives alone (**B**). The ranking is by point estimate. The contrast tests a bonus after a missed planned visit. Lally modelled 18–254 days to 95% of self-reported automaticity among 39 good fits from 84 observation days (**C**). Delete “the one real-world study.” [Silverman–Barasch](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029), [Milkman, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/), [Lally](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- **Visibility.** “Ravid’s 94 independent samples are the total. Monitoring presence showed no detectable average broad performance gain (41 samples) and was associated with greater strain (23, **A**).” Team aggregate salience and anonymity require local checks (**D**). [Ravid, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12514)
- **Rewards and rank.** Ashraf’s public stars more than doubled condom sales versus other arms. Sales commissions showed no detectable average effect, with gains among the poorest agents (**B**). Barankay’s private rank removal raised furniture sales. Blanes i Vidal–Nossol found gains from relative feedback. Bandiera’s sequential farm regimes estimate −14% for team ranks vs piece rates and +24% for a cash tournament vs ranks, with team composition changing (**B** for specific field contrasts, **D** for display defaults). [Ashraf](https://www.lse.ac.uk/marshall-institute/assets/Documents/Publications/nomarginnomission-jpube.pdf), [Barankay](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/07/rankincentives_1.pdf), [Blanes i Vidal–Nossol](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1383), [Bandiera](https://doi.org/10.1111/jeea.12028)
- **Exit.** Exercise carryover varied (**A**). Charness–Gneezy found some gains, Royer small gains from incentives alone and Carrera no detectable later lift. In Milkman, 8% of 53 treatments showed statistically significant increases in weekly visits versus placebo during the four weeks after withdrawal (**B**). Other arms’ effects remain uncertain. Choose follow-up by opportunity volume and decision horizon (**D**), dropping “at least as long again.” [Charness–Gneezy](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA7416), [Royer](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130327), [Carrera](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/10874/can-financial-incentives-help-people-trying-to-establish-new-habits-experimental-evidence-with-new-gym-members), [Milkman](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)
