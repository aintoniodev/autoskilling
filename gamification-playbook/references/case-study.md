# Case study: a renewal quoting programme, before and after the evidence

An anonymised real design, what the evidence changed, and how a design built from its
original principles scored against no guidance at all.

## The setting

A B2B software vendor's renewal sales team, about 40 reps in several groups, can quote
renewals with an AI quote builder inside their CRM or by hand. The sponsor wanted the AI
quote to become the default way of working: 40% of renewals due should get their first
quote from the tool. The plan was an eight-week programme, then retirement.

## The original 13 principles, and what happened to each

| # | Original principle | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One credit per renewal whose quote in play is an AI quote the rep sent; never clicks or drafts | Modify | Right unit. But "the quote in play" differs from the target "first quote", so a rep could earn credit by replacing a manual first quote. Align credit and target, or say why they differ. |
| 2 | One policy object holds every rule, date, threshold and prize | Keep | Good governance; not a motivator. Freeze rules per cohort. |
| 3 | Milestones at 30% and 60% of each rep's book, brackets by book size | Modify | Personal goals sized to volume are well supported (A). The exact 30/60 numbers and brackets are not. |
| 4 | Streak of on-time weeks, frozen and excused weeks, blocker reports feed "You said, we fixed" | Modify | Re-entry beats a streak that can break (B/C). Keep "you said, we fixed": it is the visible half of fixing the work. |
| 5 | Leaderboard visibility as a sponsor switch, top 5 only | Modify | No named board by default; opt-in only, with comparable groups (B, mixed). |
| 6 | Managers see every rep's activity and score | Drop | Monitoring shows no performance gain and more strain (A). Managers get team totals. |
| 7 | Eight weeks, graduation at a six-week streak, early retirement | Modify | Keep the time box. Eight weeks is not a habit clock (C). Measure after the end. |
| 8 | Provisional credits, final check 14 days after the end, swag on send | Modify | Confirm fast, pay only verified work. No irreversible prizes on unverified sends. |
| 9 | Nightly recompute from the system of record | Keep | Replayable and auditable. |
| 10 | Seven badges, including "fixer" and "coach" | Modify | Few, meaningful milestones at most (C). |
| 11 | Team finish line and group lines | Modify | Keep the team line, the most visible safe signal. Base it on the team's own baseline. |
| 12 | Quality alarm when credits and recalls both rise | Modify | Name the threshold, the owner and the pause. |
| 13 | Confetti when a credit lands | Drop as a requirement | No evidence it changes behaviour. Keep the specific confirmation. |

## What the measurement showed

Each skill variant was given to a design agent for four unrelated held-out scenarios
(field safety checklists, code reviews, hand hygiene, knowledge-base articles), three runs
each. Two blind judges scored each design against a frozen ten-criterion rubric. A third
judge, with no rubric, scored how well it would launch, be noticed and last.

| Variant | Rubric (0–100) | Launch | Noticed | Lasting | Words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No guidance (B0) | 69.9 | 79.7 | 56.0 | 52.4 | 722 |
| The original 13 principles (B1) | 62.6 | 71.2 | 52.8 | 40.3 | 725 |
| Full evidence workflow (V1) | 74.5 | 65.6 | 46.9 | 35.3 | 896 |

Pooled later over more batches, no guidance scored 65.6 / 76.5 / 56.9 / 48.1 and the
shipped skill 70.2 / 74.6 / 62.3 / 55.8 (`../research/report.md`).

The same three arms were then run on this case itself (three to six designs each, same
judges):

| Arm | Rubric | Launch | Noticed | Lasting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design from the original 13 principles | 44.3 | 52.0 | 63.3 | 22.0 |
| No guidance | 62.4 | 77.0 | 50.0 | 40.0 |
| With the skill | 68.4 | 78.3 | 55.2 | 51.7 |

The original design was the most noticed (badges, a board, a streak) and the least
lasting. The judge called it over-built for the brief and expected reps to drift back to
quoting by hand after eight weeks of small prizes.

The original principles made designs worse than no guidance on both judges. They pushed
mechanics (brackets, streaks, badges, boards) into scenarios where they did not fit. The
full evidence workflow won on the rubric but lost on the holistic judge. Its designs were
rigorous but heavy to run, hard to explain, and nobody would notice them. The shipped
skill is the result of closing that gap. `../research/report.md` has the full loop.

## Lessons

- **Principles that worked in one place are hypotheses elsewhere.** Brackets, streaks and
  badges fitted the original sponsor's taste, not the evidence.
- **Rigour that nobody notices fails too.** A design needs one thing people will feel: the
  team's visible progress and a real modest reward, tied to verified work.
- **The visible fix list was the strongest original idea.** "You said, we fixed" makes
  fixing the work, the best-supported lever, visible.
